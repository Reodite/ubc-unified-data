From graduate student to department leader, Dr\. J9 Austin has spent nearly two decades advancing the field of medical genetics at UBC\.

After completing a PhD in neuropsychiatric genetics, Dr\. Austin earned a genetic counselling graduate degree at UBC’s Faculty of Medicine, became a clinical faculty member in 2004, and then moved into the academic track\. Recognized as a leading researcher and teacher in the field of medical genetics, their work focuses on advancing a justice\-oriented approach to personalized medicine, helping to ensure diverse populations have access to the potential benefits of genomic medicine\.

Now, as the Head of UBC’s Department of Medical Genetics, and a professor in both medical genetics and psychiatry, Dr\. Austin is focused on supporting people across the department to thrive and collaborating with partners across B\.C\. to help move the implementation of genomic medicine forward in an equitable and accessible way\.

We spoke with Dr\. Austin to hear more about their aspirations and what they’ve learned from a mentor\.

---

## Who do you admire, and why?

The people around the world who are out in the streets raising their voices and doing what they can to protect each other and democracy\. It’s an act of hope and courage — what’s more inspiring than that? Watching this bravery inspires me to be courageous in taking actions in ways that align with my values, and to focus on building community in my role as department head\.

## For you, what is special about the UBC Faculty of Medicine?

There are so many people here who truly care and are trying to do good things the right way\.

## What are your main goals or aspirations as Head of the Department of Medical Genetics?

We have some exciting things happening within the department — after our recent expansion, we are now home to the largest genetic counselling training program in the country, and are soon to open the first PhD program in genetic counselling\. I am looking forward to enrolling our first students in this new program, this year\.

My goal is to build an environment in which *all* the members of the department can contribute and succeed to their fullest potential\. Where people feel like they belong, they are valued, and they’re proud to be a part of the department\.

I would love for the department to be recognized across B\.C\. as the key resource for consultation around implementation of genomic medicine and to be recognized nationally and internationally as a leader in this field\.

## What is one of your favourite quotes?

Maya Angelou said *“Do the best you can until you know better\. Then when you know better, do better\.”* I live by this quote\.

## What’s one thing you’ve learned from a mentor?

Science is for everyone\. Given opportunities, anyone who is motivated to do so — regardless of things like their history and identity — can contribute to science in important ways\. This was something I learned from my PhD supervisor\. He didn’t say these things out loud, but I learned this from him through his actions\.

## What was your first job?

I worked in a stationery shop on Saturdays when I was 15\. I wore a horrible uniform, made photocopies for people, sold pens, paper and stationery, and earned 10 UK pounds per day\. My boss was very kind and patient, and made it feel safe to learn and try new things \(like making calligraphy signs for the shelves\) — these are things I try to emulate\.

## How do you like to spend your downtime?

Being active in nature — hiking, biking and kayaking — eating good food, lifting weights and reading\.

## What is your favourite spot in B\.C\.?

My apartment in downtown Vancouver with my partner and cat\. Or any B\.C\. beach on a rainy day\.
