## Building meaningful relationships to advance Indigenous health

In 2021, the UBC Faculty of Medicine released its action plan and official [Response](https://www.med.ubc.ca/files/2021/07/FoM-Response-to-the-TRC-21-07-05-Final.pdf "UBC Faculty of Medicine Response to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada Calls to Action") to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada Calls to Action, reaffirming its commitment to advancing reconciliation and working together with First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples to transform health education, research and care\.

**Rhonda Carriere** \(Métis\), Manager, Indigenous Initiatives, is part of a passionate team helping to implement those commitments\. By building meaningful relationships with Indigenous communities across B\.C\., Rhonda and her team are embedding Indigenous perspectives and culturally safe approaches to health care into all elements of the Faculty’s Strategic Plan\.

In this Q&amp;A, Rhonda shares what she does to invest in her own health and how her team is engaging with Indigenous partners to collaborate on systemic changes across the province and beyond\.

### Title:

Manager, Indigenous Initiatives

### Department/unit:

Dean’s Office, Health Engagement

### Campus:

Vancouver

### How long have you worked at the Faculty of Medicine?

Five and a half years at the Faculty, and seven years at UBC\.

### Tell us about your role in the Faculty of Medicine:

Part of my work with the Indigenous Engagement team is to centre the Faculty’s commitments to the [Truth and Reconcilation Calls to Action](https://ehprnh2mwo3.exactdn.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Calls_to_Action_English2.pdf), [UBC Indigenous Strategic Plan](https://isp.ubc.ca/) and recommendations from Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls \(MMIWG\), United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples \(UNDRIP\) and In Plain Sight reports to impact systems\-level change\. Our team facilitates relationship building with Indigenous communities and organizations to infuse Indigenous ways of knowing and culturally safe approaches to health care into the Faculty’s core pillars of Education, Research, Organization and Partnership\.

This work matters to me as a Métis person because it’s time to take major strides toward fulfilling the commitments we have made to Indigenous people and communities\. We recently passed the five\-year anniversary of the Faculty’s formal apology for its role in perpetuating past and present harms to Indigenous peoples and its commitment to seek change through action\. We vowed to forge a new path and it is time to check in on our progress\. The Faculty’s recently formed Indigenous Partnership Executive Advisory Committee \(IPEAC\), co\-chaired by Dr\. Nadine Caron and Derek Thompson, gets to the heart of this work\. Departments and divisions represented on IPEAC have been encouraged to focus on an Indigenous topic — such as Indigenous concepts, wellness, or health — through grand rounds, a conference or another activity over the coming year\.

### What’s your favourite thing about your work?

Working with a small team that is passionate about engaging Indigenous communities and organizations beyond a one\-sided relationship\. I am excited to learn about the great work being done with Indigenous peoples in good ways that give back to their communities\. We want to elevate that work and encourage others to join in\.

### What’s one personal experience you would encourage others to do/try?

If you have not visited an Indigenous community or attended a local event, make it a goal for 2026\. There are many opportunities on campus and beyond to learn about First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples\. In September, the [Indigenous Speakers Series](https://redi.med.ubc.ca/indigenous-initiatives-speakers-series/), [Orange Shirt Day](https://irshdc.ubc.ca/orange-shirt-day/) events and [Come Toward the Fire](https://www.cometowardthefire.com/), an annual celebration presented by the Chan Centre for the Performing Arts and Musqueam, on whose land we are priviledged to work, learn and play at UBC Vancouver, provide great opportunities to connect with and learn about Indigenous neighbours\.

### What do you do to invest in your mental and/or physical health?

I’ve experienced a small health set back from a Zumba injury, so I am making an effort to adjust to more low\-intensity activities and still have fun\! Gentle stretching and daily walking clears the mind and allows me to come back refreshed\. I recently got back on my bike and enjoy finding hidden corners of the city when riding — the Renfrew ravine is a beautiful forested area in an urban setting\.

### What trait do you most admire in others?

Commitment and perseverance to make positive change within systems\.

### Share an accomplishment that you are proud of from the past year:

I am proud of working with the team to build a partnership with the [Éyameth’ Health Centre](https://eyameth.com/), an Indigenous\-led primary care facility recently opened by the Sts’ailes First Nation in Harrison Mills\. Last winter we brought a team from the Faculty of Medicine, together with other invited guests, to visit Éyameth and meet the team, tour the facility and learn more about the centre\. It was a great day spent getting to know each other and finding ways we might work together\.

We learned that the opening of the Éyameth’ Health Centre was the culmination of the vision and hard work of many Sts’ailes people over decades and across generations\. The dream to blend traditional healing with modern medicine for Indigenous and non\-Indigenous people living on Stó:lō territory, known in Halq’eméylem as ***S’ólh Téméxw*** \(meaning “our world, our land”\), in the eastern Fraser Valley is being realized\. Visiting their traditional medicines room was inspiring\. I look forward to the ways the Faculty will collaborate with Éyameth’ and other Indigenous initiatives going forward\.
