[Dr\. Keerat Sidhu](https://med-fom-mednet.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/01/KeeratSidhu-2026_800x450-940x529.jpg)

It all began with a letter to her future self\. “Dear Keerat…”

The elementary school writing assignment was the first time Dr\. Keerat Sidhu recorded her passion for science and aspirations of becoming a doctor\.

After opening the letter years later at her high school graduation in North Delta, Dr\. Sidhu went on to fulfil the promise to her younger self\. She completed her medical degree, internal medicine residency and general internal medicine fellowship, all at the UBC Faculty of Medicine\.

Since then, she has built her career at UBC and in the Fraser region, serving as a clinical assistant professor in the Department of Medicine and a general internal medicine physician at Surrey Memorial Hospital, where she has spent years helping to train and mentor UBC MD students and resident doctors\.

Now, as the recently appointed Associate Dean, Fraser, Dr\. Sidhu is well\-positioned to provide strategic leadership for the Faculty’s medical and health professions education activities in the region where she grew up\.

We spoke with Dr\. Sidhu to learn more about her vision for her new role and what makes the UBC Faculty of Medicine unique\.

---

## Who do you admire, and why?

I admire anybody who is willing to take a risk or do something outside of their comfort zone\. It is easy to talk yourself out of something or overthink \(something I have been guilty of\!\)\. I admire those who can sit with and tackle uncertainty head\-on\. This is a mindset and practice that I am trying to incorporate into my life more often\.

## For you, what is special about UBC, and the Faculty of Medicine?

UBC and the Faculty of Medicine offer many broad opportunities for educational and professional growth\. The world can be your oyster here because there are innumerable fantastic and diverse leaders who can guide you as you pursue your goals\. The culture is supportive, collaborative and always inclusive\. I feel lucky to work with so many excellent colleagues every day\.

## What’s the most impactful lesson or piece of advice you’ve gained from a mentor?

Do not take what you’re told at face value — always evaluate and examine the problem yourself\. This is not to say don’t trust anybody, but rather to always use your own critical thinking skills when confronted with a challenge\. Adopting this mindset has helped me avoid cognitive errors during busy clinical days and improved my capacity to problem\-solve outside of medicine as well\.

## What was your first job?

I worked in retail at H&amp;M\. Because of that job, I am very particular about how I fold clothes and I wince every time I see messy clothes left in a fitting room\.

## What are your main goals or aspirations as Associate Dean, Fraser?

As someone who was born and raised in the Fraser, and now lives and works in the region, I know first\-hand the wealth of opportunity and potential here\.

UBC already has a strong presence in the Fraser region, and I’m inspired every day by the incredible clinical faculty and medical and health professions learners whose dedication to serving local patients and communities is unwavering\.

I hope to continue growing our undergraduate and postgraduate footprint in the region, whether that be through helping develop new residency programs or working with sites across the region to develop new clinical rotations for learners\.

I am excited to collaborate with leaders in Indigenous communities, health authorities, government and the medical education space to identify key needs and to co\-create tangible solutions to meet those needs together\.

## How do you like to spend your downtime?

I love spending time with my two little boys — they are the definition of joy\. Otherwise, you can find me trying out a new restaurant in the city, getting a sweaty workout session in, or on a walk listening to a podcast\.

## What is your favourite spot in B\.C\.?

This is the hardest question of them all\! If I had to pick, nothing beats a walk around the downtown Vancouver seawall on a warm summer evening\.
