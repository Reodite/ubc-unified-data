[Gabriel Rose](https://med-fom-mednet.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/01/GabrielRose_20220528_PJ_3210_960x540-940x529.jpg)

Since 2009, Gabriel Rose has supported the UBC Faculty of Medicine community in various roles through his commitment to enhancing strategic partnerships and delivering meaningful results\.

Raised in Vernon, B\.C\. on the traditional and unceded territory of the Syilx Okanagan People, he studied business and international development before working for a non\-governmental organization in Vietnam\. After returning to B\.C\., he dedicated his career to advancing strategic initiatives in the public and not\-for\-profit sectors, with a particular focus on health and post\-secondary education\.

In previous roles with the Faculty of Medicine, including Director of Strategic Initiatives, he led and collaborated on various projects, including [refreshing the Faculty’s strategic plan](https://stratplan.med.ubc.ca/), developing multiple program expansion and distribution proposals, renewing the MD curriculum and redesigning the Faculty’s governance structure\.

Now, as Senior Advisor, Government Relations for the Faculty of Medicine and Office of VP Health, Mr\. Rose is looking forward to building on the university’s deep and collaborative relationships with the provincial government to support UBC’s health education and research programs in service to the people of B\.C\. and beyond\.

---

## Who do you admire, and why?

I admire people who work in healthcare\-related roles, because they choose, every day, to apply their skills and knowledge to meet the foundational needs of others\.

## For you, what is special about the UBC Faculty of Medicine?

Our vision—to transform health for everyone—is special because it is relevant, inclusive and unifying\.

Relevant, because health is vital to each of us, to our families and to society as a whole\. Inclusive, as it reminds us that our work is for everyone and that health is multidimensional\. Unifying, because we can all see how our day\-to\-day work contributes to realizing our vision\.

## What’s one thing you’ve learned from a mentor?

It’s not enough to do the right thing, nor is it enough to do the thing right—we need to do the right thing right\. My former employer and mentor, Dan Zollmann, taught me about the power and importance of upfront strategic analysis to clarify the intended outcome and identify the actions needed to get there, all with a focus on delivering solutions that work both technically and for the people who need them\.

## What was your first job?

As a child I had a paper route delivering the Morning Star newspaper in Vernon\. In addition to daily paper delivery, once a week, I would go door\-to\-door to collect payments and return the cash to the office\. I learned the importance of kindness and customer service, clarity of communication and fulfilling commitments, all of which have been relevant in every job I’ve had since\.

## What are your main goals or aspirations as Senior Advisor, Government Relations?

The Faculty of Medicine and the provincial government share a common commitment to serve society, and our success is interdependent\. We rely on the government to fulfill our mandate and we play a key role in helping government achieve its goals and service plans\.

We are fortunate to have strong, collaborative working relationships with people and teams across the provincial government, due in large part to my predecessor, Cindi Valensky, and other leaders at UBC\.

Within that context, my goal is to build and maintain respectful, collaborative and productive relationships with our provincial government colleagues, while advising the Faculty and Office of the Vice President Health on program planning, proposals and implementation\. Ultimately, I hope to enhance and sustain support for UBC’s health education and research programs in alignment with the priorities set by the provincial government\.

This work is deeply collaborative and I look forward to working closely with units across the Faculty, UBC Health and UBC Government Relations to support government advocacy and stewardship activities\.

## How do you like to spend your downtime?

During the limited downtime that this stage of life affords, I like to read, run and play beach volleyball\. I hope to get back into Ultimate Frisbee and skiing\. In the meantime, I’m lucky to be able to take my son to soccer and swimming, and we like to play tennis and hike together\.

## What is your favourite spot in B\.C\.?

Having grown up in Vernon, Kalamalka Lake Provincial Park will always hold a special place in my heart\.
