From RN to fast\-track midwifery student, Violet Ignace brings clinical expertise, leadership and a deep commitment to equitable care to her training\. Earlier this year, she was recognized with the Amy E\. Sauder and Jean Craig Smith Scholarships through UBC’s highly competitive Premier Undergraduate Awards program and was named a [Wesbrook Scholar](https://students.ubc.ca/finances/awards-scholarships-bursaries/premier-undergraduate-wesbrook-scholarships/) —one of the university’s highest honours for students who demonstrate exceptional academic achievement, leadership and community engagement\.

We spoke with Violet about her journey to midwifery, the values that guide her practice and her hopes for the future\.

*Photos courtesy of Violet Ignace\.*

[Woman smiling over a large flower\.](https://med-midwifery-2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/processed-4C6FE491-BA49-4D12-8A82-F78AB7C8FC2E-720x960.jpeg)

## **“Leadership looks like seeing a leader in everyone I come across\. Humility to recognize my achievements while continuing to see gifts in others and uphold community\.” —** Violet Ignace

## **What does receiving the Amy E\. Sauder and Jean Craig Smith Scholarships mean to you?**

I am so honoured and grateful to be selected for such a prestigious award\. It makes me feel so proud of myself\. My late mother always encouraged me to take my academic endeavours seriously\.

## **You entered midwifery as an RN through the fast\-track pathway\. How has your nursing background shaped your approach to midwifery?**

I have a background in nursing and focused my master’s in nursing on Indigenous cultural safety\. This path has led me to appreciate the improvements that are currently happening and midwives across BC advocate for equitable healthcare access\.

## **Your leadership and community engagement were recognized through these awards\. How do you understand your role in supporting communities through midwifery care?**

I have volunteered at an inner\-city community centre in downtown Vancouver, supporting the Indigenous diaspora and some host\-nation members\. I am looking forward to being a midwife where I can be a vessel for people to communicate their needs and priorities\.

## **What does leadership look like to you in midwifery?**

Leadership looks like seeing a leader in everyone I come across\. Humility to recognize my achievements while continuing to see gifts in others and uphold community\.

## **What are your hopes for your future practice?**

My personal values and philosophies involve maintaining a strong commitment to supporting improved outcomes for Indigenous Peoples\. I hope to learn from families and members of the healthcare team to be a well\-rounded practitioner\. As I grow as a midwife, I will use my Master of Nursing education to advocate on a wider provincial scale for research and advancement of the profession\.

**We congratulate Violet on being named a Wesbrook Scholar and recipient of the Amy E\. Sauder and Jean Craig Smith Scholarships, and extend our sincere thanks to the donors whose generosity recognizes and supports outstanding student leaders across UBC\.**
