The Michael Smith Laboratories is fortunate to have a strong group of associate faculty members whose expertise further strengthens the linkages between the Lab and the academic departments at UBC\.

[https://www\.bccrc\.ca/dept/mo/people/samuel\-aparicio](https://www.bccrc.ca/dept/mo/people/samuel-aparicio)[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Sam-Aparicio-head-shot_crop.jpg)

[Dr\. Sam Aparicio](https://www.bccrc.ca/dept/mo/people/samuel-aparicio)

Dr\. Aparicio’s research program encompasses the fields of cancer genomics, mouse genetic models, high throughput screens, small molecule chemical probes and translational breast cancer research\.

---

[https://www\.bcgsc\.ca/people/steven\-jones](https://www.bcgsc.ca/people/steven-jones)[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Steve-Jones-grey_crop.png)

[Dr\. Steven Jones](https://www.bcgsc.ca/people/steven-jones)

Dr\. Steven Jones’ lab undertakes computational cancer research leveraging advanced algorithms and data analysis techniques to gain insights into the complexity of cancer, unravel its underlying mechanisms, and devise innovative strategies for diagnosis, treatment, and prevention\.

---

[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Maths-Madzvamuse2013-crop-800x800.jpg)

[Dr\. Anotida Madzvamuse](https://www.math.ubc.ca/user/3665)

Dr\. Madzvamuse’s research lies at the interface between fundamental disciplines and experimental sciences, and seeks to formulate new mathematical and computational models to study single and collective cell migration through non\-isotropic environments\. His goal is to create artificial synthetic cells for biomedical research\.

---

[http://www\.phas\.ubc\.ca/\~andre/](http://www.phas.ubc.ca/~andre/)[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Andre-Marziali-head-shot_crop.jpg)

[Dr\. Andre Marziali](http://www.phas.ubc.ca/~andre/)

The aim of Dr\. Marziali’s Applied Biophysics research is to develop new technologies for genomics and life sciences ranging from engineering design of novel instruments for BC genomics groups, to development of automation for tissue microarray analysis, rapid genotyping, single molecule analysis methods using nanopores, and novel electrophoresis technologies\.

---

[https://measday\-lab\.landfood\.ubc\.ca/](https://measday-lab.landfood.ubc.ca/)[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Vivian-Measday-head-shot_crop.jpg)

[Dr\. Vivien Measday](https://measday-lab.landfood.ubc.ca/)

Dr\. Measday’s lab studies yeast diversity associated with vineyards and wineries in British Columbia and also uses Saccharomyces cerevisiae as a model to study mobile genetic elements\.

---

[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/PS_Headshot_cropped_2.jpg)

[Dr\. Peter Stirling](https://www.bccrc.ca/dept/tfl/people/peter-stirling)

Dr\. Stirling’s research uses yeast models and human cell cultures to study the causes of genome instability, which can be a factor in the formation of tumours\. The goal of his work is to suggest novel therapeutic options that can target these causes to overwhelm genome stability in cancer, blocking tumour growth\.

---

[https://biochem\.ubc\.ca/person/natalie\-strynadka/](https://biochem.ubc.ca/person/natalie-strynadka/)[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Nadia-Strynadka-head-shot_crop.png)

[Dr\. Natalie Strynadka](https://biochem.ubc.ca/faculty-2/natalie-strynadka/)

Resistance to standard antibiotic therapies to treat disease is a global health concern\. The ultimate goal of the research in the Strynadka laboratory is the structure\-based design of novel, therapeutically useful antibiotics and inhibitors of antibiotic\-resistance mechanisms\.

---

[https://www\.msl\.ubc\.ca/people/dr\-stephen\-withers/](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-stephen-withers/)[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Stephen_Withers-head-shot_crop.jpg)

[Dr\. Stephen Withers](https://withers.chem.ubc.ca/)

Research in the Withers lab primarily focuses upon enzymes involved in glycoside cleavage and synthesis, with special emphasis upon their mechanisms\. Research also focuses upon exploitation of the understandings developed\. Applications range from development of new enzyme\-based synthetic methodologies to new therapeutics and imaging agents\.
