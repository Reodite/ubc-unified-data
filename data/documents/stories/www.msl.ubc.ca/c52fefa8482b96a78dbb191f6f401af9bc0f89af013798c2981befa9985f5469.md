### The Biotechnology Laboratory officially opens

In 1987, UBC made a bold investment by establishing Canada's first interdisciplinary biotechnology unit\. Dr\. [Michael Smith](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/about-us/#dr-michael-smith) was named the founding Director of the Biotechnology Laboratory where he recruited some of the brightest minds\. This unit quickly became a resource for the entire university, encouraging biotechnology advancements across the campus and in the wider scientific community\.

[The Biotechnology Laboratory officially opens](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/1987/01/timeline-smith-biotech-lab.png)

### Prominent scientists and engineers recruited to the Biotechnology Laboratory

With the Biotechnology Laboratory officially open, Dr\. Michael Smith made it his first priority to recruit some of the finest up\-and\-coming scientists and engineers to UBC\. The first professional appointments to the Biotechnology Laboratory from 1988\-1989 included Drs\. [Brett Finlay](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-brett-finlay/), [Wilfred Jefferies](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-wilfred-jefferies/), [Jim Kronstad](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-jim-kronstad/), [James Piret](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-james-piret/), [Terry Snutch](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-terry-snutch/), [Robin Turner](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-robin-turner/), John Carlson, and Louise Glass\. Academic appointments span across five faculties including Science, Medicine, Applied Science, Forestry, and Land and Food Systems, emphasizing a cross\-disciplinary approach to the full range of exciting biotechnology issues\.

### Dr\. Michael Smith awarded Nobel Prize in Chemistry

In 1993, Dr\. [Michael Smith](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/about-us/#dr-michael-smith) received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1993/summary/) for his research on site\-directed mutagenesis, a technique where a specific DNA sequence could be altered in a targeted manner\. Smith shared the Prize with Dr\. Kary Mullis for his invention of the PCR method\. Smith's research opened the door to entirely new fields such as genetic engineering and synthetic biology\.

### Dr\. Wilfred Jefferies discovers iron transport as a potential readout for Alzheimer’s detection

Dr\. [Wilfred Jefferies](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-wilfred-jefferies/) and collaborators discovered that the iron transport protein, P97, acts as a molecular marker for Alzheimer's disease \(AD\)\. [Previous research](https://rdcu.be/bA2RE) by Dr\. Jefferies showed that iron levels were elevated in the brains of AD patients\. Furthering this research, Dr\. Jefferies and his collaboratories found that P97 was increased in the serum but not in various control groups, making it a potentially powerful biomarker for AD\. This potential biomarker would allow for non\-invasive medical testing for AD\. This [research](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/FBL/7/5/10.2741/ujiie) was published in *Frontiers in Bioscience*\. 

[Dr\. Wilfred Jefferies discovers iron transport as a potential readout for Alzheimer’s detection](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2003/12/timeline-iron.png)

### Drs Brett Finlay and Marco Marra sequence the novel SARS virus in record time

As an emerging pandemic began sweeping the globe, Drs Brett Finlay, Marco Marra and their research teams work ed around the clock to sequence the novel Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome \(SARS\) virus in just six days\. Th is impressive milestone paved the way for another enormous accomplishment – the development of three possible vaccines for SARS in under a year in collaboration with many other scientists as part of the SARS Accelerated Vaccine Initiative \(SAVI\) program\. The innovative scientific approaches used to solve the SARS crisis would go on to inform the process es of combatting [future pandemics like COVID\-19 in 2020](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/how-sars-vaccine-development-can-inform-covid-19-response/)\.

### The Terry Project is born

[Dave Ng](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-dave-ng/), the [Terry Project](http://www.terry.ubc.ca/about/) is a cross\-faculty program designed to educate undergraduate students on pressing global issues like climate change, poverty, conflict, and disease\. ### Bioinformatics program established

The Bioinformatics Graduate Program was established in 2006, offering M\.Sc\. and Ph\.D\. degrees to students doing research in the field of bioinformatics\. This interdisciplinary graduate program, administered by the Faculty of Science at the University of British Columbia and Simon Fraser University, combines training in both biological and computational methodologies\. The program integrates academic centres in computer science, statistics, molecular biology, and biotechnology, with translational research groups at hospitals and at the clinical interface\. Members of the Genome Science &amp; Technology Graduate program \(GSAT\), the Department of Statistics, the Bioinformatics, Empirical &amp; Theoretical Algorithms Laboratory in Computer Science, the Genome Sciences Centre at the BC Cancer Agency \(GSC\), and the Michael Smith Laboratories have been instrumental in developing the current program\. The program also integrates other departments at UBC in the faculties of Medicine, Science, Forestry, and Land and Food Systems that broadly support bioinformatics research in the life sciences\.

### First tree genome decoded

Michael Smith Laboratories researchers, Drs\. [Jörg Bohlmann](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-jorg-bohlmann/) and [Brian Ellis,](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-brian-ellis/) are involved in decoding the first tree genome of the black cottonwood or *Populus trichocarpa* tree\. ### First *E\. coli* vaccine for cattle developed

Research by Dr\. [Brett Finlay](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-brett-finlay/) has helped develop [Econiche](https://science.ubc.ca/news/ubc-microbiologists-work-leads-first-e-coli-vaccine-cattle-0) \- the world’s first vaccine designed to reduce cattle shedding of a particularly dangerous strain of *E\. coli*\. Econiche was developed by the Canadian biopharmaceutical company Bioniche in partnership with UBC, the Alberta Research Council \(ARC\), and the University of Saskatchewan\. Most strains of *E\. coli* are harmless, but some can cause severe illness and even be fatal when ingested by humans from contaminated meat, vegetables or water\. The vaccination of cattle can help reduce the risk of food and water contamination by a strain that is dangerous to humans: *Escherichia coli* O157:H7\.

### Dr\. Christian Kastrup develops platelets with synthetic nuclei \(RNA\)

### Microbes involved in asthma are different depending on the environment

### Hemorhage \(bleeding\) is the leading killer of humans

Blood platelets have one main job: Stop bleeding by forming clots\. Sometimes, however, these tiny cell fragments fail when they are needed most – when a person is experiencing massive bleeding, usually due to trauma\. Dr\. [Christian Kastrup](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/people/dr-christian-kastrup/) developed a potential strategy for endowing platelets with extra powers so they can rise to the occasion and continue coagulation\. If it’s proven to work in clinical situations, such “superplatelets” might become a standard part of emergency department supplies, along with bandages, oxygen, and saline\. The [research](https://doi.org/10.1111/jth.14006) was described in the *Journal of Thrombosis and Haemostasis*\.
