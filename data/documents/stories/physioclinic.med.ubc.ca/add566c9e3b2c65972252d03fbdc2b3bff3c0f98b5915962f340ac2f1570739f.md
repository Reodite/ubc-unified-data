## Name and Title\(s\):  

Gavin Tonkin

Physiotherapist

## Clinics:  

[Physio At Your Doorstep](https://www.physioatyourdoorstep.ca/)

[The UBC Physical Therapy and Research Clinic](https://physioclinic.med.ubc.ca/)

## Where do you work?

I’m one of the lucky physiotherapists who get to work at the UBC Physical Therapy and Research Clinic \(PTRC\)\. At the clinic, I manage my own caseload while also supervising physiotherapy students during their clinical placements\. Additionally, I operate a mobile and virtual practice called Physio At Your Doorstep, providing care directly to clients in Vancouver and the surrounding areas\.

## What is the most rewarding part of your job?

The most rewarding aspect is empowering individuals to regain their independence\. Whether it’s helping a runner return to the seawall after an injury or assisting a senior in navigating their home safely after a joint replacement, seeing a client transition from frustration to confidence is incredibly powerful\. Helping people understand their bodies and how they can take control of their recovery is such an amazing process to be a part of\.

## Why does physiotherapy matter?

For lots of reasons\. Most importantly, I believe physiotherapy is an excellent approach to help people get the most out of their lives\. Whether that means getting on the ground to play with grandkids, running a marathon, returning to sport, or recovering from surgery and managing chronic conditions, the list goes on\. It is through movement that we interact with the world, and physiotherapy provides the tools to overcome or adapt to the limitations that hold us back\.

## What drew you to physical therapy?

I have worked in healthcare for over 15 years, a journey that began in Australia as an Exercise Physiologist\. While I loved helping people reach their mobility goals in that role, moving to Canada and working as a Kinesiologist sparked a desire to deepen my clinical impact\.

I saw that becoming a physiotherapist would give me the clinical tools to take a lead role in assessing and treating a much wider range of people\. It’s been the perfect way to combine my love for movement with my goal of helping people get back to their daily lives\.
