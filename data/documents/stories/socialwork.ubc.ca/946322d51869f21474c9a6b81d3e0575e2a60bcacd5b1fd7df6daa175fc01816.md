## About

Allen Alvarez supports healthcare professionals in building moral wellness through ethics education, evaluation, analysis of ethical issues and moral distress consultation\. He engages applied ethics scholarship to understand moral experience and standards that define good healthcare practices and decisions that help patients, families, healthcare professionals and communities to flourish in their moral wellness and resilience\.

Using philosophy as a tool to increase understanding of complex concepts and issues as well as promote clarity of thought and communication, Allen partners with healthcare professionals and academic scholars to build knowledge through research and translate this to practical life\. As an ethicist embedded in healthcare practices, Allen uses systematic methods in making sense of and addressing challenges people face in their quest to live well with meaning and purpose\.

## Research

### Ongoing Projects

-   **Review of effectiveness of interventions in addressing moral distress of healthcare professionals\.** This narrative review aims understand which of the interventions currently being used show promise of effectiveness in addressing moral distress of healthcare professionals\.
-   **Avoiding risk of virtue signaling through evaluation\.** This project will review literature related to virtue signaling and conduct case studies of success and barriers encountered by organizations in enacting their EDI, cultural safety, and T&amp;R commitments through evaluation\.
-   **Levels of moral distress in healthcare\.** This project invites health professionals to assess their moral distress using Epstein and Hamric’s Measure of Moral Distress for Healthcare Professionals \(MMD\-HP\) and use the results to provide participants a sense of the average moral distress experienced by their community\. The intended direct impact is educational for participants as well as to inform interested healthcare leaders and policy makers about the average levels of moral distress experienced by the participating population\.
