*Linda Wu was a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Stone Centre for Wealth and Income Inequality\. **She spoke with communications research assistant Bhavya Dubey\.***

**Looking back at your year as a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Stone Centre, what research goals did you have upon joining the Stone Centre at UBC, and where did you make the greatest progress?  
**Upon joining the Stone Centre, my primary goal was to advance my research on how taxation affects wealth accumulation and intergenerational transfers\. Over the past year, I made the greatest progress on my job market paper concerning estate taxation, as well as a broader project examining family businesses\. Presenting this work at both UBC’s internal seminars and external conferences was incredibly valuable\. The feedback I received from these presentations helped me significantly refine my empirical strategies and clarify the broader economic implications of my findings\.

**While working at the Stone Centre, whom among the affiliate faculty did you collaborate with and what research came from it?**During my time here, I engaged with many faculty members and participated in the seminar series\. I started collaborating with Terry Moon, working extensively with Canadian administrative data\. This partnership resulted in our recently circulated working paper, “*Income and Tax Consequences of Selling Ownership Stakes: Evidence from Major Shareholders of Private Firms in Canada\.*” In this paper, we find large reductions in major shareholders’ overall income and taxes after selling their company\. Additionally, Terry and I have another exciting work in progress examining the “*Impacts of Stock Options on Innovative Firms’ Growth and Performance\.*“

**Which forms of support from the Stone Centre were most useful for your research?**Without a doubt, the people at the Stone Centre, the faculty, staff, and students, were the most vital source of support for my research\. The Centre provided an exceptional intellectual community where I was able to connect with individuals who quickly became mentors, colleagues, and friends\. I received feedback that helped not only my research but also my overall career trajectory\. In addition, the research funding provided me the chance to participate in external conferences, allowing me to expand my professional network within the broader academic field\.

**As an inequality scholar, what specific dimensions of the field have you been able to explore or refine during your time here? How has the centre’s focus on inequality influenced your methodology?**  
My time at the Centre allowed me to explore the dynamics of wealth concentration at the very top of the distribution, particularly through the lens of private business ownership and capital taxation\. The Centre’s focus on inequality challenged me to think more holistically about how tax policy shapes economic disparities across generations\. Methodologically, this environment pushed me to refine my approach to leveraging large\-scale administrative tax records\. 

**Looking to next year at the University of Michigan, how do you see the work you’ve started here influencing your future research agenda?**  
As I transition to the University of Michigan, the foundation I’ve built at the Stone Centre will anchor my future research agenda\. The collaborative projects I started here, particularly those utilizing administrative data to study corporate owners, will transition into my pipeline of projects\. I look forward to bringing these experiences into my new academic community in Michigan\.

**For the Postdoctoral researchers and graduate fellows who will follow in your footsteps at the Stone Centre, what advice would you give them on how to best utilize the Centre’s resources and community of researchers?**My biggest piece of advice is to lean into the Centre’s exceptional intellectual community from day one\. Do not hesitate to share and present your work in its early stages; the faculty and peers here are incredibly supportive, and their early feedback can save you months of misdirected effort\. Take full advantage of the specialized resources available, particularly the access to administrative datasets and the conference funding that can elevate your external visibility\. Finally, take the time to build genuine relationships—the conversations you have in the hallways or over karaoke are often just as valuable as the formal seminars\.
