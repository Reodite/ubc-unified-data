**Oluwaseyi Ezekiel  
**Mastercard Foundation Scholar

**Q: Can you tell us about your experience at the McGill Africa Umoja Summit?**

**A:** I had the opportunity to attend the McGill Africa Umoja Summit in Montreal, a gathering focused on exploring African Indigenous knowledge systems and their relevance in addressing today’s global challenges\. The summit brought together scholars, students, and changemakers to reflect on Africa’s rich cultural heritage and how traditional knowledge can contribute to innovative solutions for the future\. For me, it was an opportunity to learn, engage, and reflect on how knowledge systems shape the way we understand and interact with the world\.

**Q: What was one of the highlights of the summit for you?**

**A:** One of the highlights was participating in the Sankofa Challenge\. This interactive experience aided my learning about African innovation, music, history, and cultural diversity across the continent\. Beyond being a competition, the challenge encouraged participants to reconnect with African heritage and appreciate the depth of knowledge and creativity embedded within African societies\. It sparked my curiosity and prepared me for the deeper conversations that followed during the summit\.

**Q: What key lesson from the summit stayed with you the most?**

**A:** The discussion that resonated with me most was the importance of “Sankofa”—looking back at Indigenous Knowledge Systems in African agriculture and food systems to inform the future\.

A particularly powerful concept introduced during the summit was **Two\-Eyed Seeing**, which emphasizes the value of viewing the world through both Indigenous and Western knowledge systems\. Rather than seeing these approaches as competing, Two\-Eyed Seeing recognizes that they can complement each other to create more holistic solutions\.

This perspective challenged me to rethink innovation\. Scientific advancement does not have to replace the knowledge that African communities have developed and preserved for generations\. Instead, we can build stronger solutions by respecting and integrating multiple ways of knowing\.

**Q: How has this experience influenced your work and research interests?**

**A:** My work sits at the intersection of geospatial technology and environmental management, and the summit encouraged me to think more critically about how knowledge influences the systems we create\. It made me reflect on important questions: Whose knowledge informs the data we collect? Whose experiences shape the environmental solutions we design? How can we ensure that innovation is culturally aware and inclusive? Moving forward, I hope to approach my research and professional practice with greater openness, collaboration, and appreciation for diverse knowledge systems\.

**Q: What does the future of sustainability look like to you?**

**A:** The summit reinforced my belief that sustainable solutions must be built through collaboration\. The future of environmental management cannot rely on a single perspective; it requires the integration of scientific research, Indigenous wisdom, community experiences, and innovative technologies\.

The three phases of the summit, Reclaim, Apply, and Imagine, captured this journey beautifully\. It entails reconnecting with our roots, applying knowledge to present challenges, and imagining a future shaped by inclusive and sustainable solutions\.

**Q: Who would you like to thank for making this experience possible?**

I am grateful to the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at UBC and McGill University for providing this opportunity\. I also appreciate the speakers and panelists whose insights and stories expanded my understanding of African knowledge systems and reaffirmed their global significance\.

I look forward to applying the lessons from this experience in my research, professional journey, and engagement with the communities I serve\.
