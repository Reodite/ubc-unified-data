**Beyond the Conference Room: Four Scholars Reflect on Leadership, Wellbeing, and Resilient Agriculture**

In March 2026, three Mastercard Foundation Scholars from the University of British Columbia traveled to Calgary, Alberta, to attend the Advancing Women in Agriculture \(AWC West\) Conference, an annual gathering of leaders, practitioners, and changemakers working to shape the future of agriculture across Canada and beyond\.

For **MaryJane Onyechesi, Deborah Akande, Safiya Adamu, and Eunice Darko**, the conference was more than a professional event\. It became a space for reflection on leadership, well\-being, and the complex systems that underpin global food security\.

While each scholar engaged with the conference in unique ways, their reflections converge around three powerful themes: **the human side of agriculture, the practice of intentional leadership, and the need for systems\-based approaches to resilience\.**

## **Centering the Human Side of Agriculture**

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/WhatsApp-Image-2026-05-04-at-21.15.42-2-edited-768x432.jpeg)

*Deborah Akande and Maryjane Onyechesi at the AWC West conference at Calgary*

Across sessions and conversations, one message stood out clearly: agriculture is not just about production systems; it is about people\. For **MaryJane Onyechesi**, a graduate student in the Master of Land and Water Systems program, discussions on farmer mental health revealed a dimension of agriculture that is often overlooked\.

> **“Agricultural sustainability must extend beyond technical solutions to encompass the well\-being of farmers\. Resilience is not solely ecological; it is also social and human\-centered\.”**

This perspective was reinforced for **Deborah Akande**, a graduate student in the Master of Food Science, during a Rural Watch workshop focused on farmer mental health and community support\.

> **“Food is more than a product or a system; it is deeply connected to people’s lives, stories, and well\-being\. Behind every plate is a farmer navigating not only agricultural seasons but emotional and personal ones\.”**

Also, through practical tools such as active listening and trauma\-informed engagement, Deborah began to see how small, intentional actions can meaningfully support farming communities\. Collectively, these reflections exemplify a key insight that **sustainable agriculture must prioritize the well\-being of those who sustain it\.**

## **Rethinking Leadership: Communication, Energy, and Authenticity**

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/WhatsApp-Image-2026-03-13-at-20.15.43-2-edited-1-768x433.jpeg)

*\(Left\) Safiya Adamu and Eunice Darko \(Right\) at the AWC West conference at Calgary*

Another defining theme was leadership not as a title but as a practice rooted in self\-awareness, communication, and intentionality\. Deborah Akande reflected on how effective leadership begins with how we communicate and show up for others\.

> **“Impact depends on how effectively we communicate what we know to the people who need it\.”**

Workshops on communication frameworks and energy management shifted her approach to well\-being and productivity, emphasizing that burnout should not be the cost of impact\. Similarly, Eunice Darko’s experience challenged conventional ideas of visibility and leadership\.

> **“It’s not about saying yes to everything to be seen\. It’s about being strategic and authentic in how you show up\.”**

For Eunice, this insight marked a turning point, one that strengthened her commitment to building confidence, embracing intentional growth, and focusing on meaningful contributions rather than performative visibility\. MaryJane’s reflection complements these perspectives by highlighting the importance of professional resilience and leadership capacity within agricultural systems\.

Collectively, their insights reframe leadership as something deeply human: **grounded in clarity, sustained by well\-being, and expressed through authentic engagement\.**

## **From Technical Solutions to Systems Thinking**

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/WhatsApp-Image-2026-03-12-at-22.58.04-2-edited.jpeg)

*Eunice Darko with co\-participants at the AWC West conference at Calgary*

Perhaps the most significant shift across all three reflections is a move toward systems thinking\. **MaryJane Onyechesi** articulated this most explicitly, noting how the conference reshaped her understanding of resilience:

> “I now see resilience as a multidimensional system that integrates human, environmental, and policy dimensions, rather than a purely biophysical outcome\.”

Discussions on climate variability, supply chains, and global food systems emphasized that agricultural challenges are interconnected and must be addressed through integrated approaches\. This shift has direct implications beyond Canada\. For regions such as Sub\-Saharan Africa, where smallholder farmers face increasing climate pressures, these insights underscore the need for solutions that combine **technical innovation, inclusive policies, and strong support systems for farmers\.**

Deborah’s commitment to engaging rural communities and Eunice’s focus on intentional leadership both reflect this broader systems perspective, where impact is not isolated but relational and far\-reaching\.

## **Looking Ahead: From Reflection to Action**

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/WhatsApp-Image-2026-05-04-at-21.15.44-edited-768x432.jpeg)

*Mastercard Foundations Scholars at UBC repre*sentatives

While each scholar’s experience at AWC West 2026 was distinct, a shared commitment emerges: to translate learning into action\. MaryJane plans to integrate these insights into her research on soil–water resilience and climate\-smart agriculture, ensuring that social and institutional dimensions are embedded within technical frameworks\.

Deborah is taking her learning beyond the conference space, engaging with rural communities, fostering conversations around mental health, and supporting farmers through practical, human\-centered approaches\. Eunice is applying her reflections to her personal and professional journey, with a renewed focus on intentional leadership, confidence, and meaningful impact\.

Together, their experiences reflect the broader mission of the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program: to equip young leaders not only with knowledge but also with the perspective and purpose to drive change in their communities and beyond\.

The scholars extend their gratitude to the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at the University of British Columbia for making this opportunity possible and to the organizers and leaders of AWC West 2026 for creating a space that fosters learning, connection, and growth\.
