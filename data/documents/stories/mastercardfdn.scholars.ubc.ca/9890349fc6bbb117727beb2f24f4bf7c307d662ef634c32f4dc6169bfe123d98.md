**Reflection by IDRIS AYAB  
Mastercard Foundation Alumni, Class of ’25**

I am deeply grateful to the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at the University of British Columbia for supporting my participation in the **Africa Mindset Reset Forum 2026**, organised by the African School of Governance \(ASG\) in Kigali, Rwanda, from 25–26 August 2026\.

Themed **“From Pan\-Africanism to Meta\-Africanism,”** the forum brought together current and former African leaders, policymakers, business leaders, academics, entrepreneurs, civil society actors, young people, and other changemakers from across the continent\. The gathering created a valuable platform for reflecting on Africa’s development journey, the limitations that continue to hold the continent back, and the mindset, institutional and structural changes required to translate Africa’s enormous potential into tangible progress\.

For me, the experience went beyond attending a high\-level conference\. It was an opportunity to learn from diverse perspectives, engage with people working across different sectors of African development, and reflect more deeply on my own responsibility as a young African and Mastercard Foundation Scholar/alumnus\.

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Idris-3-768x432.jpg)

### Key Reflections and Lessons

#### 1\. From imagining the Africa we want to building the Africa we work for

One of the most profound lessons from the forum was the need to move beyond aspirations and rhetoric towards deliberate action\.

There was a strong emphasis on the fact that Africa’s future cannot simply be something we hope for or describe\. It is something that must be actively built through commitment, sacrifice, innovation, and collective responsibility\.

This challenged me to think beyond asking what kind of Africa I want to see and instead ask, **“What am I personally doing to contribute to that Africa?”**

As young Africans, we have an important role in this process, not only as beneficiaries of development but also as active participants in shaping the continent’s future\.

#### 2\. Effective governance and the importance of execution

Another recurring theme was the importance of effective governance and institutional capacity\.

Africa has no shortage of ideas, policies, strategies, and development ambitions\. However, the ability to translate these ambitions into timely and measurable results remains a major challenge\.

The discussions emphasized the need to reduce unnecessary bureaucracy, improve institutional efficiency, and create systems that enable good ideas to move from conception to implementation at the right time\.

This reinforced for me that **good governance is not only about developing policies; it is also about the capacity and willingness to implement them effectively\.**

#### 3\. The evolving meaning of Pan\-Africanism

The central theme of moving from Pan\-Africanism to Meta\-Africanism provided an important framework for understanding the next phase of African integration and development\.

The discussions clarified that Meta\-Africanism should not be viewed as a replacement for Pan\-Africanism but rather as an extension that seeks to advance some of the aspirations that have long been associated with African unity\.

This includes thinking beyond political solidarity towards deeper economic integration, stronger institutions, interconnected markets, greater mobility, and practical collaboration among African countries\.

The conversations around open borders and continental integration particularly highlighted the importance of making it easier for Africans to move, trade, collaborate, and create opportunities across national boundaries\.

#### 4\. Moving beyond the export of raw materials

The forum also placed significant emphasis on Africa’s natural resources and the need to capture greater value from them\.

Rather than continuing to primarily export raw materials, African countries need to strengthen industrialization, develop local processing capacity, and create value chains that generate greater economic benefits within the continent\.

Agriculture was similarly highlighted as a sector with enormous potential for transformation, particularly through value addition, industrialization, technology, and improved productivity\.

This resonated strongly with my own interests in sustainable land management, agroforestry, and climate\-resilient livelihoods\. It reinforced my belief that Africa’s natural resources should not only be conserved but also sustainably harnessed in ways that create dignified livelihoods and contribute to local economic development\.

#### 5\. Africa’s greatest asset: its young population

The forum also reinforced the enormous importance of Africa’s youth\.

With a predominantly young population, Africa possesses a significant demographic advantage\. However, this advantage will only translate into development if young people are properly supported, equipped, and given meaningful opportunities to participate in the continent’s transformation\.

This requires investment in education, skills, entrepreneurship, innovation, leadership, and institutions that enable young people to contribute meaningfully\.

As a Mastercard Foundation Scholar and young African, this was particularly meaningful to me\. The Foundation’s investment in young Africans is an example of how supporting young people’s capabilities can contribute to broader societal transformation\.

The discussions reminded me that being a young African comes with both an opportunity and a responsibility: to acquire the knowledge and skills needed to contribute, while also actively participating in building the future we envision\.

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Idris-4-768x432.jpg)

### Positive Representation of Africa

Another important dimension of the forum was the discussion around how Africa is portrayed on the global stage\.

Africa’s story is often presented through the lens of challenges, crises, and deficits\. While these realities should not be ignored, they do not represent the entirety of the African experience\.

There is a need for Africans to take greater ownership of the narratives surrounding the continent by highlighting our innovations, achievements, opportunities, people, and solutions\.

This does not mean ignoring Africa’s challenges\. Rather, it means presenting a more balanced and authentic picture of the continent, one that acknowledges its problems while also recognizing its immense potential and the people working to realize it\.

### Networking and the Mastercard Foundation Community

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Idris-2-768x433.jpg)

One of the highlights of my participation was the opportunity to connect with fellow Mastercard Foundation Scholars and alumni who were also present at the forum\.

We had the opportunity to take a group photograph together, which was a meaningful reminder of the growing community of young Africans connected through the Mastercard Foundation’s investment in education, leadership, and transformation\.

Beyond the formal sessions, I was able to interact with participants from different countries and professional backgrounds\. These conversations provided opportunities to exchange ideas, learn from different African contexts, and build relationships that I hope will continue beyond the forum\.

### Personal Takeaways

My participation in the Africa Mindset Reset Forum has strengthened my conviction that Africa’s transformation will require more than political declarations or ambitious visions\. It will require **a mindset shift accompanied by the capacity to execute\.**

I leave the forum with a stronger appreciation of the importance of:

-   Developing institutions that can effectively deliver results;
-   Supporting and empowering Africa’s young population;
-   Promoting regional integration and open borders;
-   Moving from raw\-material exports towards industrialization and value addition;
-   Harnessing agriculture and natural resources sustainably;
-   Improving Africa’s representation and narrative on the global stage; and
-   Moving from conversations and aspirations to measurable action and impact\.

I also appreciated the commitment by the African School of Governance to make the forum an annual gathering, with an emphasis on ensuring that subsequent editions can demonstrate measurable progress and impact from previous conversations\.

### Conclusion

Participating in the Africa Mindset Reset Forum in Kigali was a valuable and transformative experience\. It exposed me to diverse perspectives on Africa’s development and challenged me to think more critically about the role that individuals, institutions, and young Africans must play in shaping the continent’s future\.

I am particularly grateful to the **Mastercard Foundation for making this experience possible**\. The Foundation’s support enabled me not only to participate in an important continental dialogue but also to connect with other members of the Mastercard Foundation community and bring back new perspectives that will continue to influence my work and leadership journey\.

The forum reinforced a simple but powerful message for me:

**Africa’s reset must begin in our minds, but it must ultimately be demonstrated through our actions\.**

I look forward to applying the lessons and perspectives gained from Kigali to my continued work and engagement in contributing to a more sustainable, prosperous, and inclusive Africa\.
