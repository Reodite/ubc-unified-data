**Metro Vancouver is a vibrant, multicultural hub** and home to more than 60 per cent of the population of the province of British Columbia \(B\.C\.\)\.

Stretching from Squamish to Richmond and into the Fraser Valley, the region’s diverse and fast\-growing communities also have unique healthcare needs\. That’s why UBC’s Faculty of Medicine takes a community\-based approach to training medical students across the region\.

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/CaryCuncic_780x780.png)

Dr\. Cary Cuncic

“We’re helping UBC medical students deepen their understanding of the diverse healthcare needs of communities across B\.C\., and that includes the communities right here in Metro Vancouver,” says Dr\. Cary Cuncic, who serves as the Faculty’s Regional Associate Dean of the Vancouver Fraser Medical Program \(VFMP\), representing the largest of the Faculty’s four regional MD training sites across the province\.  

Today, first\-year medical students in the VFMP join one of three distinct learning communities — Vancouver’s North, South or Fraser learning community — where they will learn the wide spectrum of medicine while building strong connections with their peers, mentors and the communities that many of them will go on to serve\.

Meet just a few of the more than 800 medical students training across Metro Vancouver and discover what they have to say about the benefits of region\-wide community\-based training\.

---

## “I like consistency and the idea of training in the same hospitals so that I can get to know the doctors and my future colleagues\.”

### **–** **Idiris Kabel, UBC MD student**  
**South Learning Community**  
**Vancouver Coastal Health region**

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/VFMP-PE-1.png)

Idiris Kabel likes to keep busy — running, composing slam poetry and organizing student events as the class president\. He also prefers large bustling cities and is looking forward to training at the Vancouver General Hospital as part of the Southern Learning Community, which also includes communities such as Kitsilano, Kerrisdale and Richmond\.

---

## “People are more comfortable expressing their health concerns in their mother tongue\. It’s important to me to provide care in both Farsi and English\.”

### **– Niki Shahraki, UBC MD student**  
**North Learning Community**  
**Vancouver Coastal Health region**

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/VFMP-PE-2.png)

Niki Shahraki is a passionate advocate for global health equity\. Growing up in North Vancouver and Iran, she volunteered at rural Iranian schools and raised funds for children with chronic illnesses who need specialized care\. This experience shaped her understanding of medicine as a profession grounded in long\-term relationships and focused on making a tangible impact\.

As a medical student in the North Learning Community — which includes West Vancouver, the North Shore and downtown Vancouver — Niki is particularly drawn to supporting women and children, and is excited to explore obstetrics and gynecology, pediatrics and family medicine as potential career paths\.

### ******– Mira Lail, UBC MD student******  
******Fraser Learning Community******  
**Fraser Health region**

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/VFMP-PE-4.png)

## “I transitioned from physics to medicine to have a direct impact on helping people through difficult times\.”

### ******– Sarah Wang, UBC MD student******  
******South Learning Community******  
**Vancouver Coastal Health region**

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/VFMP-PE-7.png)

For Sarah Wang, pursuing medicine at UBC offered her the comfort of learning in a smaller class while training in her favourite community, Richmond\. She recently finished her first family medicine placement and was moved by how welcoming and grateful patients were to have a medical student present\. The experience reaffirmed her decision to pursue medicine and provided an opportunity to expand her medical vocabulary in Mandarin\.

“My preceptor was fluent so I learned a lot from him,” she says\. “Which is one reason why I chose the South Learning Community, so that I can learn how to serve the community well\.”

---

**Over the next four years, more than 800 UBC medical students just like these ones** will train in communities across Metro Vancouver, building lasting connections with the patients and families they will one day serve as family physicians, cardiologists, neurologists, oncologists and more\.

Alongside them are hundreds more UBC medical students who are doing the same training in urban, rural, remote and Indigenous communities across the province — mentored by nearly 13,000 dedicated UBC clinical faculty\.

These are the relationships that help to shape and inspire where medical students eventually practice and the type of care they provide\.

And together, they are shaping the future of health care here in B\.C\. and beyond, one that is rooted in community and compassion\.
