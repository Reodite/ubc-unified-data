More UBC medical students are training — and staying on to practice as family doctors — in B\.C\.’s northern rural communities than ever before, thanks to a new undergraduate program

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/NRIC_1920x1080.jpg)

*Rural family doctor and UBC clinical faculty member Dr\. Aryn Khan \(left\) guides UBC medical student Jason Van Zyl through a patient examination*\.

One short training visit to Vanderhoof, B\.C\., during his first year in UBC’s [Northern Medical Program](https://mdprogram.med.ubc.ca/admissions/before-you-apply/pathways-to-medicine/northern-rural-pathway) \(NMP\) was all it took to convince Jason Van Zyl to consider rural family medicine as a career\.

“I was taking a patient’s history in the clinic when my supervisor Dr\. Aryn Khan bursts in and says, ‘We have to go to the hospital now, there’s a C\-section happening,’” recalls the now third\-year NMP student\.

“We dash across the street to the hospital, scrub in, and there’s one family doctor performing the C\-section, another family doctor is doing the anesthetic and Dr\. Khan and I are there to catch the baby,” Van Zyl continues\. “They pulled the baby out and asked me to listen to its heartbeat, and it was really slow\. We ended up doing a 30\-minute resuscitation\. In the end, everyone was fine\.”

But there was little time to soak in the elation\. “We congratulated the mom, handed off the baby, ran back across the street and started doing clinic again\.”

Van Zyl’s head was spinning, but Dr\. Khan just shrugged it off\. “She said, ‘This is the life of a rural doctor\. This is what I do every day\.’ And I thought, ‘Okay, I’m sold\.’”

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/NRIC-Students-1_500x400.jpg)

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/NRIC-Students-2_500x400.jpg)

First image: Dr\. Khan observes as NRIC MD student Justin Grenier assesses a pregnant patient\. Second image: Grenier and fellow student Jason Van Zyl at St\. John Hospital in Vanderhoof, B\.C\.

Van Zyl is now back in Vanderhoof for six months as one of seven students piloting the new Northern Regional Integrated Clerkship \(NRIC\)\. Officially launching in the summer of 2026, third\-year NMP students in the program spend six months training at the University Hospital of Northern British Columbia \(UHNBC\) in Prince George and six months training in a northern community\.

The blended clerkship model builds on the success of UBC’s Integrated Community Clerkship \(ICC\), in which students spend an entire academic year training in a rural community\. With NRIC, UBC has doubled its network of rural clerkship sites to 20, with more UBC students than ever training in underserved areas of the north, such as Daajing Giids, Chetwynd, Terrace and Hazelton\. Many will build close connections in these communities, inspiring them to stay and practice after graduation\.

“In a small town, people know you as a real person, and I love that,” says Dr\. Khan, who has been practicing and training MD students in Vanderhoof for 10 years\. “My kids go to school with kids that I delivered, or kids whose grandmother’s hand I held as she was dying\. In rural medicine, you can really see how much of an impact you make, and the students can see the impact they’re making\. It brings medicine back to the basics of humanity\.”

Immersive MD training in rural communities

UBC’s year\-long integrated community clerkships place undergraduate MD students in northern and rural communities across B\.C\., where they train in clinics and hospitals under the mentorship of practicing doctors\.  

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/NRIC-Map-B2-940x720-1.png)

20 rural training sites

At 20 rural clerkship sites located throughout B\.C\., UBC MD students gain hands\-on training while building strong connections in communities such as Daajing Giids, Comox, Cranbrook, Chilliwack and more\.  
  

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/NRIC-Slide3-940x720-1.png)

Practicing medicine where they train

Ninety\-three per cent of UBC MD graduates who attend UBC for postgraduate medical training remain in B\.C\. to practice, with many staying in the communities where they trained\.  

---

NRIC trainee Justin Grenier understands the appeal\. A member of the Nak’azdli Whut’en First Nation community, Grenier grew up in nearby Prince George and spent 12 years working as a paramedic and firefighter in Vanderhoof before starting medical school\.

“I love seeing Elders and other people from the community who recognize that I’m First Nations and are excited to see me,” says Grenier, who is also training in Vanderhoof\. “They’ll ask who my mom is — and everyone knows my mom\. It feels really wonderful\.”

While he envisions working as a rural family physician, Grenier says he’s enjoyed his hospital rotations and is considering applying to the new [Indigenous Internal Medicine Pathway](https://education.pathways.med.ubc.ca/snapshots/bringing-an-indigenous-perspective-to-internal-medicine/)\. Whatever type of medicine he ends up practicing, he’s certain that he wants to continue providing care in or near his home community\.

“My main goal as a physician is to help the Dakelh people throughout the B\.C\. Interior,” he says\. “It’s the reason why I’ve wanted to become a doctor, and I can’t wait to actually start practicing medicine\.”
