## Project Details

**Overview**

Altermagnetism is a novel form of magnetic order featuring spin\-split electron bands \(characteristic of conventional ferromagnets\) and zero net magnetization, similar to antiferromagnets\. The interplay between altermagnetism and superconductivity is an active topic of contemporary research, from topological phenomena like robust surface states to potential device applications, like new types of Josephson junctions that can generate tunable spin supercurrents\. However, altermagnetic superconductors have not been observed in nature yet, and many proposed devices have not been studied in realistic models\.

**Project Objectives**

The goal of my project was to study the Josephson effect in altermagnetic superconductors, where a supercurrent flows across a thin barrier between two superconductors\. First, I developed different models of altermagnetic materials along with studying potential forms of superconductivity that could be realized within them\. We then modelled a Josephson junction constructed from these altermagnetic superconductors and computed the resulting currents, studying their dependence on device parameters like barrier thickness and doping\.

**Research Outcomes**

We found zero\-energy topological surface states in our model, which enhance the Josephson current across the metallic barrier but were only available to one of the two electron spin channels\. From our calculations, a combination of these surface states and strong altermagnetic splitting enable a large spin\-polarized supercurrent, where the vast majority of the current arises from one spin channel\. This was expected from previous minimal models, but we were able to concretely study both factors and conclude that they could contribute a roughly equal amount to the large spin polarization\.

## My Co\-op Experience at MPI

**Why did you apply for this placement?**

I really enjoyed a previous co\-op placement where I worked on theoretical condensed matter physics, since it was fun to see how interesting physics could emerge from relatively simple models of solids\. It seemed like I could learn a lot from this placement because of the wide variety of expertise and cutting\-edge research in solid\-state physics at the MPI\-FKF as well\. I have also never lived outside of Canada for a long time, so I thought that it could be a great opportunity to travel\!

**What recommendations do you have for students who wish to participate in this exchange program?**

My advice is to first and foremost find things that make you happy and make time for friends and/or travel\! It can be challenging moving to a new country at first, so that should be a priority\. At work, it really helped to ask a lot of questions about my project; even though I was a bit scared of people thinking they were dumb questions, I found that everyone was very willing to share their experiences and go over physics concepts with me\. In a similar vein, keep an open mind about your interests\! There is a lot of interesting research going on at the Max Planck Institutes, a lot of which I wasn’t exposed to beforehand\. Also, try the German Fanta and half/half cola\-orange soda drinks \(Spezi, Mezzo Mix, etc\.\)\! It tastes more like real fruit and doesn’t have as much artificial colour\.

**Tell us about your travels and cultural experiences**

I had the opportunity to travel almost every weekend around Germany, so I visited a lot of places near Stuttgart and Munich, along with making a trip to Paris in August, which was really beautiful\! Some of my favourite places to visit were Heidelberg and Tübingen, which are small cities around an hour away from Stuttgart\. They had some beautiful historic buildings, city squares, greenery, and lots of fascinating academic heritage\.
