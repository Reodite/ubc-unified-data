| **HOW WRITING 101 &amp; WRITING 201 ARE ORGANIZED** |

**Pre\-class tutorials:** If you would like individual feedback on your in\-class writing, staff and volunteers are available from 5:15 to 6:15 p\.m\.

**What a typical class looks like:**

-   **6:30 p\.m\.:** Class starts\. At the door, you’ll find a table with readings for next class and optional groundwork assignments\.
-   **6:30–7:00 p\.m\.**: We’ll begin the first 30 minutes of class with writing exercises to warm ourselves up for the class\. You’ll have the option to share your writing with the class for peer feedback\.
-   **7:00–8:30 p\.m\.**: The guest teacher arrives and will present on the topic of the evening\. You’ll have the opportunity to learn about a new genre, practice writing through exercises, and share your writing with the class\. There’s a short break around 7:45 p\.m\.
-   **8:30 p\.m\.:** The lecture ends at 8:30 p\.m\. To respect our many volunteer guest teachers, we ask that you give them your full attention until that time\. The next week’s bus and meal tickets are given out at the end of class\. \(Stick around to the end for the book draw\!\)

| **LEARNING NEW KINDS OF WRITING** |

In Writing 101 and Writing 201, we learn about and practice writing in many different forms, styles, and genres\. Some of these forms will be familiar and others may be new\. There will be many opportunities throughout the semester for you to pick up your pens and try that kind of writing for yourselves\. Overall, by practicing these new genres and styles, you’ll learn that “how” you write something affects the meaning of “what” you say\. Some techniques or forms may not work for you, which is okay\. Part of our classes will involve discovering which kinds of writing resonate with *you\.*

| **LOVING SENTENCES, WITH GILLES CYRENNE** |

The series is optional and is focused on the nuts and bolts—and hammers\!—of English grammar\.

| **FEEDBACK AND SUPPORT** |

There will be multiple ways for you to receive support in your writing and classroom learning, from feedback during class and group activities to 1:1 tutorial sessions\. You will also receive written feedback on your writing, including constructive comments, from Hum staff\. You can submit your writing either handwritten or typed, or email it to h\.u\.m@ubc\.ca if you would like us to print it out for you\. Just come by the office before class to pick it up\.

 **TUTORIALS:** These optional sessions will give you one\-on\-one feedback with a staff member or volunteer on your writing\. These sessions are a great opportunity to ask specific questions about what you learned in class or about a specific piece of writing you’re currently working on\. The writing you share during these tutorials should be relevant to the course\.

 **GROUNDWORK:** Every evening, you’ll get the chance to review the genre and concepts introduced in class through “groundwork” exercises\. You can take these optional exercises home with you and reflect on them over the week\. You can share your responses by handing in your writing in\-person or by email\.

**PUBLICATION**: We encourage you to submit pieces throughout the term\. You’ll also be able to choose and revise one piece of writing to be included in Hum’s annual publication, which will be published in Spring 2026\. We’ll share more details later in the term\.

|  |

| **REQUIREMENTS** |

In order to graduate from the course, you need to submit at least three pieces of class writing\. These pieces can be something that began from a writing exercise, class activity, groundwork, or any other kind of class\-related writing \(that is, you should not submit writing developed outside the course\)\. You’ll receive feedback on the pieces that you turn in\.

You need to attend at least nine out of twelve classes to graduate from this course\.
