### **About the Presenters:**

[Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/miles-208x300.jpg)  

**Dr\. Miles Marchand** serves as the cardiologist for the Musqueam and Squamish Nations, as well as Carrier\-Sekani Family Services, and has developed innovative, community\-driven models of care to improve cardiovascular outcomes for Indigenous Peoples\. He is the founder of the *One Heart at a Time* program and established the first on\-reserve, Indigenized cardiac rehabilitation program in Canada—*Strong Hearts*—in partnership with the Musqueam community\. His work emphasizes culturally safe care, strong community partnerships, and a two\-eyed seeing approach that brings together Indigenous and Western ways of knowing\.

He is the Director of the Centre for Indigenous Cardiovascular Health, where he leads initiatives focused on advancing equity in cardiovascular care through clinical innovation, research, and system transformation\. As an active clinician\-scientist, he contributes to national research and policy efforts aimed at optimizing Indigenous cardiovascular health and evaluating culturally responsive models of care\.

Dr\. Marchand completed his medical degree and residencies in internal medicine and cardiology at the University of British Columbia, serving as Chief Resident in both programs\. He subsequently pursued advanced fellowship training in cardiac rehabilitation and prevention, with a focus on Indigenous cardiovascular health, as well as a research fellowship in First Nations, Inuit, and Métis Communities Experiencing Heart Function Inequities, supported by the Canadian Cardiovascular Society, Pfizer, and the CHF Alliance\. He has also completed a Graduate Certificate in Indigenous Public Health through the UBC Centre for Excellence in Indigenous Health\.

Dr\. Marchand has worked alongside Indigenous communities across Canada in urban, rural, and remote settings, from the west coast of British Columbia to Iqaluit in the North\. In 2023, he was awarded the Canadian Medical Association Young Leaders Award in recognition of his leadership in advancing equity in cardiovascular care and outcomes for Indigenous Peoples\.
