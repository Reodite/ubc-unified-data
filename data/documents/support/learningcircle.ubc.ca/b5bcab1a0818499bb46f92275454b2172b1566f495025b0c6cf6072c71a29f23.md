# [Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/UBCLC-Promo-banners-1.png)

This webinar will provide an overview of the Women’s Mobile Primary Care \(WMPC\) program at Vancouver Aboriginal Health Society \(VAHS\), an Indigenous\-led, low\-barrier primary care initiative serving street\-entrenched women and gender\-diverse populations in Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside\. Speakers will discuss the broader Indigenous health landscape, the importance of culturally safe and relationship\-based care, and how WMPC integrates clinical, social, and cultural supports to improve access and continuity of care\.

### **About the Presenters:**

  [Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Screenshot-2026-05-13-at-3.02.27-PM-229x300.png) 

**Jasheil Athalia \(she/her\)**

**Senior Manager, Program Development**

Jasheil Athalia is a Senior Manager at Vancouver Aboriginal Health Society \(VAHS\), where she supports the strategic development and expansion of culturally grounded health and wellness programs serving urban Indigenous communities in Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside\. She oversees the Women’s Mobile Primary Care \(WMPC\) program, a low\-barrier, trauma\-informed mobile healthcare initiative serving street\-entrenched women and gender\-diverse populations\. Jasheil also manages VAHS’ Indigenous Garden program\. Her work spans Indigenous health research, program management, partnership development, and strategic funding initiatives focused on improving access to culturally safe and integrated models of care\.

  [Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Screenshot-2026-05-13-at-3.02.36-PM-295x300.png) 

**Jenn Wong**

**Registered Social Worker**

Jenn is a dedicated social worker known for her grounded and compassionate work\. Jenn holds a bachelor’s degree in social work from UVIC and has worked in the social services sector since 2012\. She has worked in various roles from frontline work, to management, and program development\. Jenn is committed to living and working with a Two\-Eyed seeing approach as a multiracial person and as a settler living on Turtle Island\. She currently works with Vancouver Aboriginal Health Society on the Women’s Mobile Primary Care Team\. With the team, Jenn strives to provide a safe space for community members to be who they are without judgement and work together to meet each person’s needs\.

[Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Screenshot-2026-05-13-at-3.02.59-PM-223x300.png)  

**Roberta Watt  
Registered Nurse**

Roberta Watt is a Registered Nurse with nearly a decade of frontline and leadership experience serving Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside\. She is deeply committed to sex worker advocacy, supporting marginalized communities, and advancing health equity through trauma\-informed and relational practice\. Roberta’s career has focused on harm reduction outreach, increasing access to care, and championing community\-based approaches for those facing significant barriers\. She is recognized for her collaborative leadership and dedication to education, advocacy, and holistic team wellness\.

[Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/IMG_6826-261x300.jpg) 

**Keira Lumley  
Cultural Outreach Worker**

Keira is Plains Cree on her mother’s side and Irish settler on her father’s side, and is a member of Sucker Creek First Nation on Treaty 8 territory\. Born and raised on Treaty 6 territory, Keira now resides on the unceded xʷməθkʷəy̓əm, Sḵwx̱wú7mesh, and səlilwətaɬ territory, while completing her Bachelor of Science at UBC, and continuing into the UBC MD Program this fall\. She joined the WMPC team in September and was excited by the opportunity to integrate her cultural knowledge into primary care with a program that emphasizes meeting community members where they are at and reducing as many barriers to healthcare as possible\. Through her teachings, Keira views our health and wellbeing wholistically and enjoys connecting with the DTES community in conversations on Indigenous medicines, diverse traditional knowledge, and through practices such as smudging\.
