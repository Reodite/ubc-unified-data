The UBC Learning Circle is partnering with the SFU First Nations Student Association and the [SFU Public Interest Research Group](http://www.sfpirg.ca/) to present the third and final installment of the “Aboriginal Youth in the Criminal Justice System” series\.  Join renowned Canadian criminologist Dr\. Kim Rossmo for an evening talk on the missing women’s inquiry, his experience with the Vancouver Police Department, the process of creating geographic profiling, and his experiences at SFU\.

**About the Presenter**

[https://med\-fom\-learningcircle\.sites\.olt\.ubc\.ca/files/2013/09/Dr\.\-Kim\-Rossmo\.jpeg](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2013/09/Dr.-Kim-Rossmo.jpeg)[Dr\. Kim Rossmo](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2013/09/Dr.-Kim-Rossmo-214x300.jpeg)Dr\. Rossmo received his Masters degree from SFU and later became the first police officer to obtain a Doctorate in criminology\. His dissertation research resulted in a new criminal investigative methodology called geographic profiling\. In 1998, his analysis of cases of missing sex trade workers determined that a serial killer was at work, a conclusion ultimately vindicated by the arrest and conviction of Robert Pickton in 2002\. Dr\. Rossmo is currently based at Texas State University – San Marcos, where he holds the Endowed Chair in Criminology and is director of the Center for Geospatial Intelligence and Investigation\.
