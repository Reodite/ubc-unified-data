# [Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/1.jpg)

How are our relationships with land and water linked to our personal well\-being? This discussion delves into eco\-anxiety, environmental change, and how our connections to place and community can enhance mental health and well\-being\. By drawing on Indigenous Knowledge and storytelling, we will examine how our relationships with water, the environment, and each other can help us cope with the emotional challenges of a changing world\.

### **About the Presenters:**

  [Image](https://med-fom-learningcircle.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Screenshot-2026-09-23-110903-285x300.png) 

**Amy Charles** is an Indigenous Knowledge and Relations Specialist at Ocean Wise, where she works to highlight the important connections between people, water, place, and wellbeing through environmental education and building relationships\. Her focus is on creating meaningful connections with communities, as well as with the land and water\. She believes that these relationships can greatly influence how we experience and respond to environmental changes, including feelings of eco\-anxiety and worries about the future of our communities and ecosystems\.

Amy takes a community\-centred approach to environmental education, using storytelling, place\-based learning, and Indigenous perspectives\. This approach not only promotes curiosity and connection but also encourages action\. She is passionate about empowering young people to see themselves and their communities reflected in STEM and environmental fields, while also crafting learning experiences that nurture a sense of belonging and connection to place\. In addition, Amy actively participates on the boards of the Canadian Network for Ocean Education \(CaNOE\) and the Society for Canadian Women in Science and Technology \(SCWIST\)\.
