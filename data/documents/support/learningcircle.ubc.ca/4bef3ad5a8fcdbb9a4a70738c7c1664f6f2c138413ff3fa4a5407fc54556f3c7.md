This is a special Research Circle on Culturally Safe Dementia Care \(CSDC\) and how to build nursing capacity to care for First Nations Elders with memory loss\. The facilitators include the researchers, Dr\. Wendy Hulko \(principal investigator, [Thompson River University](http://www.tru.ca/)\) and Danielle Wilson \(practitioner co\-lead, [Interior Health](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/Pages/default.aspx)\) and also elder advisors Cecilia DeRose \([Esketemc](https://www.esketemc.ca/)\) and Jean William \([St’exelcemc](http://www.williamslakeband.ca/)\) from Three Corners, Williams Lake, B\.C\.

\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

**Why a Teaching Session led by Elders?**

Dementia is becoming of increasing importance to First Nations communities and there has been a call for culturally relevant education for nurses specific to dementia care so that nurses can better care for Elders with memory loss\. To meet this need, a collaboration of Secwepemc Elders, academic and community\-based researchers, health authority decision\-makers, and practicing nurses was formed to undertake an innovative community\-based research project on culturally safe dementia care\. This project used quantitative and qualitative methods of data collection and analysis\. 34 Elders from six Secwepemc communities in British Columbia participated in a series of roundtables to share their views on memory loss and memory care in later life and to develop a teaching session for nurses\. The data were analyzed thematically to answer the research questions and create four teaching stories\. 38 nurses \(health care aides, licensed practical nurses and registered nurses\) working in residential and/or home and community care in Williams Lake and Kamloops were recruited as research participants; 23 completed three pre\-test measures, 15 of whom took part in the education program that included the Provincial Health Services Authority \([PHSA](http://www.phsa.ca/)\) Indigenous Cultural Competency \([ICC](http://www.culturalcompetency.ca/)\) modules and a teaching session with Elders, followed by post\-test assessment and scoring of the three measures\. The data were analyzed statistically and thematically\. The results indicate that our educational offering improved the knowledge, skills, and values of the nurse participants with respect to cultural safety and dementia care based on their scores having increased from per to post and discussion of the Elders’ teachings in the debriefing sessions\. Further, nurse participants indicated the teaching session with the Elders was very effective and an integral part of this education program\. Presenters will describe the research design, present the results from analyses of the two data sets \(nurses and Elders\), and discuss the implications for practice, research and education involving nurses and Elders\.

All are welcome to attend and participate in this FREE live videoconference and computer webinar session\.

**Purpose:**

-   The purpose of this research is to build skills and teach nurses &amp; community caregivers to care for First Nations Elders with memory loss in a culturally safe way\.

**Session Topics:**

-   To turn lessons from earlier work with Elders into a teaching session for nurses, all guided by Elders
-   To find out what nurses know about cultural safety and dementia care and the skills they have before and after the teaching sessions; and,
-   To find out how well the teaching sessions improved the ability of nurses to care for Elders with memory loss
