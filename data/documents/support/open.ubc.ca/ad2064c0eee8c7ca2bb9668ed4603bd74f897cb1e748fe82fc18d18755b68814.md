UBC is committed to making education more affordable and accessible by supporting the integration of open educational resources \(OER\) into credit courses\. This year, over $220,000 in [OER Affordability Grants](https://oerfund.open.ubc.ca/oer-affordability-grants/) were awarded to support [10 projects across six Faculties](https://oerfund.open.ubc.ca/announcing-the-2026-oer-affordability-grant-recipients/), with the goal of lowering the cost of learning materials and improving access for UBC students\. Once developed and integrated into courses, the OER from these projects are expected to be used by approximately 4,995 students each year, generating an estimated annual cost savings of $681,000 for students as long as the resources are in use\.

The funded initiatives span a range of activities, with a primary focus on developing open\-access digital textbooks and interactive learning resources as alternatives to expensive commercial materials\. These projects use contemporary pedagogical approaches that include antiracist teaching methods, strategies for teaching AI\-resilient coding, and “born open” practices where new courses use OER from the start\. The initiatives aim to make educational resources in Indigenous Law, Biochemistry, Statistics, Film Theory, Cantonese, Korean Language, Education, and Computer Science both affordable for UBC students and broadly accessible to a wide audience\.

### **Funded Projects:**

-   **An Interactive Open R Textbook for Forestry Biometrics**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Suborna Ahmed; Faculty of Forestry
    -   This project will transform FRST 232 into a zero\-cost course by replacing expensive commercial manuals with an interactive, open\-access mastery learning platform\. In an era when Generative AI often encourages code generation, this initiative addresses a critical pedagogical gap by shifting the focus to AI resilient debugging skills\.  
          
        
-   **Open Pathways to Korean: An Open Textbook Bridging Classroom and Everyday Korean**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Ho Jung Choi, Faculty of Arts
    -   This project will develop an open textbook for Korean language courses at UBC\. The OER will integrate online textbook content with guided self\-study materials, interactive practice activities, and clearly structured learning pathways aligned with learning outcomes\. Beyond foundational language skills, the materials will emphasize real\-world communication tasks across interpretive, interpersonal, and presentational modes\.  
          
        
-   **A Companion Guide to Film Theory**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Christine Evans; Faculty of Arts
    -   This project will develop an open\-access digital textbook designed to demystify the complex landscape of classical film theory for undergraduate students \.The Guide will offer clear, accessible, yet academically rigorous explanations of major theoretical movements – ranging from early pioneers to semiotics and feminist film theory\.  
        
-   **Indigenous Peoples and Law: Open Scholarly Textbook**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Scott Franks, Allard School of Law
    -   This project will create an open\-educational textbook on Indigenous and Aboriginal Law in Canada\. The scholarly textbook will offer an overview of this content related to Indigenous legal orders and Aboriginal law in social, political and historical context\. It will be used in new Law course which is delivered across both terms in the first\-year curriculum to all of Allard’s 200 first\-year students\.  
          
        
-   **Biochemistry: Let’s get Molecular**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Eden Fussner\-Dupas, Faculty of Science
    -   This project team will develop detailed mechanistic schematics and molecular modeling videos that highlight the exciting biochemical processes involved in information transfer and these will be accompanied by expanded figure legends and text to guide the user through these incredible processes\. The team will build H5P interactive mini lectures, alongside additional practice questions\. Combined, this multi\-media, focused learning resource package will replace the currently used traditional textbook in BIOC 302 and if successful, in BIOC 303\.  
          
        
-   **Free online textbook for CPSC\-V 210**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Reid Holmes; Faculty of Science
    -   A cornerstone of the curriculum renewal process for CPSC 210 will be integrating an online reader for the course, consisting of a free textbook custom designed for 210 learners\. This reader will have 1:1 mapping to each 210 lecture, and will carefully transition students both into the course from its predecessor, CPSC 110 but also out of the course so they are best prepared for its follow\-up course, CPSC 310\. These readings would be accessible to students on any device without cost or registration\.  
          
        
-   **Health Data Science in the AI Era: An OER for Reproducible Research &amp; Knowledge Translation**
    -   *Principal Investigator:*  Ehsan Karim, Faculty of Medicine
    -   This project will develop a comprehensive, open\-access textbook and interactive problem bank for SPPH 381H\. The OER will uniquely integrate the core pillars of health data science: \(1\) navigating complex health data sources; \(2\) applying data privacy, security, and Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles; \(3\) performing side\-by\-side “polyglot” analyses \(R and Python\) via AI\-assisted workflows; \(4\) auditing AI\-generated code for bias and accuracy; and \(5\) mastering “Last Mile” knowledge translation skills, from dynamic reporting to deploying interactive dashboards for policymakers\. By converting these materials into OER, this project ensures SPPH 381H is zero\-cost while arming students with essential workforce\-ready skills\.  
          
        
-   **dsa\-explain: Interactive modules for Algorithms and Data Structures**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Elham Khoda; Faculty of Science
    -   The objective of this project is to develop dsa\-explain, a user\-friendly and accessible open repository with OER interactive modules covering foundational topics in algorithms and data structures\. The resource will combine intuitive explanations with static or interactive visual components, practical applications, browser\-executable code examples, and integrated formative reflections, such as short multiple\-choice questions, to support active learning and self\-assessment\. dsa\-explain will consist of approximately ten interactive modules covering essential concepts in algorithms and data structures\.  
          
        
-   **An Antiracist Framework for Teaching French**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Marika Kunnas; Faculty of Education
    -   This project will create a text\-based professional learning book created to educate current and future French teachers on implementing antiracist pedagogy in Canadian French language programs\. The framework will be available in English and French via UBC Pressbooks, with the option of a downloadable PDF or eBook\.  
          
        
-   **Building an Open and Interactive Cantonese Textbook for Beginners**
    -   *Principal Investigator:* Zoe Lam; Faculty of Arts
    -   This project will develop an open online textbook with accompanying interactive activities for CNTO 311 Basic Cantonese for Mandarin Speakers to replace the current physical textbook which will soon be out\-of\-print\. The open\-access textbook will consiste of ten units organized around core communicative themes such as greetings, shopping, transportation, and hobbies\. Each unit will integrate text, audio, video, and interactive activities to promote multimodal learning and learner autonomy\.  
        

Student wages make up more than 60% of the total budget allocations, supporting students in project team roles for core content development and project coordination\. Other budget allocations include faculty course releases \(17%\), staff support \(9%\), and peer reviews and Indigenous consultations \(5%\) to ensure academic rigour and cultural integrity\.

**Please see the [OER Fund: Funded Projects](https://oerfund.open.ubc.ca/funded-projects/) page for more information and to access the full list of projects supported by the OER Fund\.**

### **About the OER Fund**

The UBCV OER Fund aims to support affordable and inclusive access to learning materials through the development, adaption, and use of open educational resources in UBCV credit courses\. The OER Fund consist of two grant pathways: [OER Affordability Grants](https://open.ubc.ca/oer-fund/oer-implementation-grant/) of up to $25,000 which are awarded on annual cycle and [OER Rapid Innovation Grants](https://open.ubc.ca/oer-fund/oer-rapid-innovation-grant/) of up to $2,000, which are open on a rolling basis\.
