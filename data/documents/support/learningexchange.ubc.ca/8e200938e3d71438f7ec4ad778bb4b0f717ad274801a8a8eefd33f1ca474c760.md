### Engaging faculty from across UBC, the advisory will support the Learning Exchange’s academic goals\.

[Nine pictures of the individual members of the Academic Advisory in a 9x9 grid\.](https://learningexchangeaug2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Academic-Advisory-Committee-v3-940x468.png)

May 27, 2026

This May the UBC Learning Exchange launched its inaugural Academic Advisory committee to help guide and champion its work as an academic unit\.

The new advisory is comprised of nine faculty members from diverse departments across the university\. See this page for the [list of committee members\.](https://learningexchangeaug2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/?page_id=17547&amp;preview=true)

The group will meet three times a year to develop and support the academic priorities of the Learning Exchange as outlined in the [Learning Exchange Action Plan](https://learningexchange.ubc.ca/about-us/actionplan2025/?login) by sharing ideas, offering advice and building connections\.

Group members have been invited to bring their expertise, experience, and connections to strengthen and expand the Learning Exchange’s strategic objectives, such as enhancing experiential education, partnering for purpose, and supporting research that leads to greater impact\.

“This is a really exciting development in the evolution of the Learning Exchange,” said Simon Bates, Vice\-Provost and Associate Vice\-President, Teaching and Learning, and chair of the advisory\. “The Academic Advisory can help support and champion the Learning Exchange as a unit in service of the University’s academic mission: teaching, learning, and research\. It’s heartening to know there is such interest in this opportunity, and I want to thank these faculty members for the contributions they will be making\.

“The Learning Exchange has been supporting UBC to connect with Downtown Eastside communities for more than a quarter century now,” said Melanie Stewart, Vice President, External Relations\. “I expect this group will enhance and deepen these two\-way connections, making it easier to work together on the pressing issues that face people not only in Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside, but also across British Columbia\.” 

“The Learning Exchange has collaborated with faculty and staff from across UBC Vancouver and has learned a great deal about how to operate in the Downtown Eastside,” said Kathleen Leahy, Director of the UBC Learning Exchange and staff lead on the advisory\.

While the Learning Exchange does not offer courses or graduate programs, it contributes to many courses across a range of disciplines\. It offers experiential learning through student staff positions as well as placements and projects\.  Student learning occurs on\-site, in collaboration with Downtown Eastside community partners, and in collaboration with faculty and student groups on campus\.

Research and knowledge exchange activities include leadership of the Making Research Accessible Initiative, support for community\-based research in the Downtown Eastside, scholarship \(research and evaluation\) at the Learning Exchange, and advice to community partners on how to effectively engage the broader academic community\.
