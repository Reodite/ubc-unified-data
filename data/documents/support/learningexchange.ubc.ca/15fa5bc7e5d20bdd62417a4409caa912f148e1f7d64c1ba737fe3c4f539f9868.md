### Indyanna Vanderlaan reflects on her time at the Learning Exchange and the craft of professional communications\.  

---

**Name:** Indyanna Vanderlaan  
**Pronouns:** she/her  
**Learning Exchange role:** Communications Assistant Work Learn  
**Faculty:** Arts  
**Program:** Bachelor of Media Studies  
**Hometown:** Edmonton, Alberta

---

#### How did you hear about the UBC Learning Exchange?

I honestly didn’t know about it until I found the job listing on the UBC Careers Centre website\! I was looking for a Work Learn position for the summer, found the Communications Assistant position, and went from there—and then stayed two extra terms\!

#### What did you do at the Learning Exchange?

[Portrait of Indyanna Vanderlaan smiling at the camera in a collared white shirt and brown sweater vest\.](https://learningexchangeaug2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/DSC01729.jpg)

I started out running the social media—a lot of reading reports that were being added to the [Downtown Eastside Research Access Portal](https://dtesresearchaccess.ubc.ca/), making infographic\-style posts about them to make them easier to consume via social media, and taking photos of different programs and events\. But my favourite projects were the videos\. My supervisor let me take charge on a few video projects, including [an interview with a community member](https://learningexchange.ubc.ca/2025/01/29/watch-weaving-rugs-and-sharing-stories/) who was taking part in a weaving workshop that was part of the English Conversation Program, and a much more ambitious video explainer \[*see below*\] where we interviewed many different people to show what exactly the Learning Exchange is and does\!

#### Did your understanding of the Downtown Eastside change?

It didn’t exactly change, but deepened\. I always knew there was more to it than the picture that is painted in the media or the general public’s opinions, but working at the Learning Exchange allowed me to see the deeper sense of community that is present here\. 

When I first moved to Vancouver, I held the same opinion of the overall city that many do: people are cold, they don’t care about you, they are always in a rush\. But in Chinatown and the Downtown Eastside, that isn’t the case\. There is a real community: people take care of each other and care very deeply about one another\. I feel very privileged to have been allowed into that space in any capacity and feel a similar loyalty to and passion for defending and taking care of its rich history and culture, and the people that live here\.

#### How did your experience at the Learning Exchange influence your academic or career path?

Working at the Learning Exchange gave me super valuable experience in both media production and ethics\. It was a really fun opportunity to practically apply what I learned in the Bachelor of Media Studies, and apply that to the craft of professional communications\. But most importantly, I learned how to approach media creation in a much more careful way, with an emphasis on consent and transparency, for example, ensuring people are fully informed of how their image will be used\. It was a bit of a shift from a way of thinking where the final product, the content, is king\. Now, everything I make is much more intentional\. 

#### What do you think the Learning Exchange may have learned from you?

I hope the Learning Exchange learned that they can use their social media in a way that makes their valuable work more visible and recognizable on a different level\. I know, for example, that my first video with them was chosen to be shown on the UBC\.ca home page, and I hope that brought in more support, volunteers, and/or participants in their programs\. It’s important to celebrate what happens at the Learning Exchange\. And in today’s digital world, it can feel more real when it is on the Internet\. And everyone at the Learning Exchange—employees, students, and community members—deserve to have their accomplishments celebrated\!
