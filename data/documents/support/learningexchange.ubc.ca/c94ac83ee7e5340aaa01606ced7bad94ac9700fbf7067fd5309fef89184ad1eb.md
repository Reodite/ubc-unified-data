### A round\-up of community\-based research updates, including a DTES Research Access Portal usage snapshot\.

September 4, 2026

[Image](https://learningexchangeaug2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Research_ethics_graphic_600px.png)

#### Understanding how to partner with community on research ethics reviews 

The Learning Exchange is partnering on a pilot project to discover how to build capacity for community research ethics reviews with Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside\.  

Called “RECOOP,” which is short for “Research Ethics Community of Practice,” the goal is to find out how to strengthen community\-based research ethics capacity at UBC and in Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside \(DTES\) by integrating community perspectives—especially those of people with firsthand knowledge of some of the challenges people in the Downtown Eastside face—into institutional research governance\.  

The plan is to assemble a group of community members with an interest in research ethics as a community of practice to review selected studies\. This group will help inform how to bring community perspectives into the institutional ethics setting\.  

The one\-year exploratory project launched in May 2026 with the creation of a dedicated project team to steer the project, and the hiring of a PhD student to support the implementation along with Learning Exchange staff\.  

RECOOP will build on and is grounded in feedback from community members involved in community\-based participatory research and people involved in developing the Research 101 Manifesto\. Drawing on these perspectives, RECOOP aims to make research processes, practices, and outcomes more accessible, relevant, and impactful for both DTES and UBC communities\. 

The project is in partnership with the UBC Office of Research Ethics, the BC Centre on Substance Use, and the SpencerCreo Foundation\.

### New Indigenous Research Ethics Resource Now Available Online 

UBC’s Office of Research Ethics \(ORE\) launched a [new online resource](https://indigenous.researchethics.ubc.ca/) in May that provides links and guidance for researchers and communities preparing and submitting Indigenous\-focused research ethics applications at UBC and potentially beyond\. The website links to resources around the world, as well as to information related specifically to ethics applications submitted to the Behavioural Research Ethics Board at UBC\.  

It’s organized by phases of the research life cycle, from “Preparing and Learning” to “Collecting, Analyzing and Sharing Research Results\.” There is also a page for Community Connections, which in the future could include information from First Nations and Indigenous Communities about their research priorities and protocols\.  

Considered a work\-in\-progress, the resource was informed by many researchers, staff, and community members, and supported financially by the UBC Office of Indigenous Strategic Initiatives\.

### Downtown Research Access Portal surpasses 2,200 items 

A [recent snapshot of the Downtown Eastside Research Access Portal \(DTES RAP\)](https://dtesresearchaccess.ubc.ca/news/details/2253) revealed that 488 new items have been added since 2023, pushing the total past the 2,200 item mark\.  

As of 2026, 88 per cent of the items are freely available and do not require payment to access\. Locating these types of resources and linking to them from the RAP is a large part of the work of the Making Research Accessible initiative\.  

As the portal has grown, so has online engagement\. From 2025\-2026, Google Analytics indicates there were 6,306 website users, about double the amount from the previous year\. Those users triggered 44,035 pageviews this year, the most the RAP has experienced since 2023\.   

See the full snapshot with more interesting RAP stats [here](https://dtesresearchaccess.ubc.ca/news/details/2253)\. 

The DTES Research Access Portal \(DTES RAP\) is a free portal anyone can use to access research findings and community\-generated materials related to Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside\. It was created by the Making Research Accessible in the Downtown Eastside initiative \(MRAi\)\. The MRAi is led by the UBC Learning Exchange and UBC Library’s Irving K\. Barber Learning Centre in collaboration with SFU CERi, SFU Library, UBC iSchool, and Vancouver Public Library\. 

Learn more about the MRAi [here](https://learningexchange.ubc.ca/community-based-research/making-research-accessible-initiative/)\.
