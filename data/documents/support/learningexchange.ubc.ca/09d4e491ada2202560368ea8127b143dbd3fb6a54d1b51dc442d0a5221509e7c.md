### One from a community partner and two from UBC, the recognitions highlight the dedication and skills of the Learning Exchange team\.

[Image](https://learningexchangeaug2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/11/Kathleen_Dionne_Matt-940x706.png)

Clockwise from left: Kathleen Leahy \(image courtesy of Alastair Bird Photogaphy and Central City Foundation\), Matt Hume, and Dionne Pelan\.

We don’t talk about our fabulous staff at the UBC Learning Exchange often\. But it’s also not often they receive three different honours in the course of three consecutive months\.

This fall, a long\-time Downtown Eastside community organization recognized one of our team for her community leadership, and UBC recognized two others, one of which was for the prestigious President’s award\.

“Honestly, I’m not surprised,” said Melanie Stewart, Vice\-President of UBC’s External Relations Portfolio, the UBC portfolio responsible for the Learning Exchange\. “The Learning Exchange supports UBC to connect with the Downtown Eastside on pressing community\-identified priorities relevant to all of British Columbia\. This is important work\. Making it happen takes humility, dedication, initiative and integrity\. It’s so nice to see their hard work recognized\.”

#### Kathleen Leahy: A Profile in Leadership

Central City Foundation recently included Kathleen Leahy, the Director of the Learning Exchange since 2011, in its annual [Change Can Happen: Profiles of Community Leadership series](https://centralcityfoundation.ca/change-can-happen-profiles-in-community-leadership-kathleen-leahy/) for her community leadership\.

The letter informing her of the selection read:

“Under your guidance the Learning Exchange has established itself as a valued community asset that makes learning accessible to community but even more importantly for inspiring students, academics, and other partners to come and learn from community\. By turning the idea of experts on its head you’ve been able to ensure the voices of those in the DTES have been afforded the respect and value they deserve\. 

“As a seasoned leader, you have honed the art of connecting and arranging resources and talents to deliver tangible results\. Your extensive experience in community development has made you a sought\-after collaborator in innovative projects in the DTES bringing together diverse partners who might not otherwise collaborate\. You’ve done all of this while maintaining core programming on your site, offering expertise to community, academics, and university staff alike and mentoring your team of dedicated community developers\. 

“You have truly set a high standard for how post\-secondary institutions can contribute to community and we deeply appreciate this unwavering commitment\.”

Founded in 1907, the [Central City Foundation](https://centralcityfoundation.ca/) is a leader in Canadian [social purpose real estate](https://centralcityfoundation.ca/about-us/social-purpose-real-estate/)\. The Profiles of Community Leadership series is designed to “celebrate examples of extraordinary community leadership and innovation” that demonstrate that change can happen\. The Profiles of Community Leadership series is available on the [Central City Foundation website](https://centralcityfoundation.ca/change-can-happen-profiles-in-community-leadership-kathleen-leahy/) and will be published in the Globe and Mail, Business in Vancouver, Macleans Magazine and BC Business\.

#### Matt Hume: President’s Choice

For his work as the Student Learning Coordinator, Matt Hume was awarded the President’s Staff Award for Enhancing the UBC Experience in September\.

The announcement read:

“Matthew Hume \(he/him\) creates meaningful intersections between student experience, academic objectives, and the needs of Vancouver’s Downtown Eastside \(DTES\) communities\. Beginning as a student staff member at the UBC Learning Exchange, Matt now introduces students to the DTES through workshops, guided walks, and events that foster reciprocal learning and lasting connections\. He has developed innovative programs that give students hands\-on, community\-engaged learning to gain valuable transferable skills while contributing to the priorities of DTES partners\.

“Grounded in humility, positivity and respect, Matt exemplifies reciprocity and partnership with a strong emphasis on working with historically, systematically, and persistently marginalized populations\. He serves students, faculty, staff and community members with authentic and empathetic support and cultivates a culture of inclusive and mutually beneficial learning\. In 2024, 95% of students reported positive experiences with the Learning Exchange\.”

“Through his dedication and compassion, Matt fosters a unique and transformative UBC experience for students, staff, and community partners\.”

Matt arrived at the Learning Exchange after leaving a career as a high school drama teacher to do a Master’s degree in UBC’s Language and Literacy Education department in the Faculty of Education\. He started as a Work Learn student, was hired as the Student Learning Coordinator two years later, and has remained in the role for eight more years\.

Created in 1991, the President’s Awards are given annually to a small number of UBC staff who demonstrate outstanding achievement and excellence in the UBC community\.

#### Dionne Pelan: 25 years\!

This fall UBC recognized Dionne Pelan’s long service and dedication by officially welcoming her into its [25\-year club](https://ceremonies.ubc.ca/honours-recognition/25-year-club/?login), an honor for employees who have been at the university for more than a quarter of a century\.

In the year 2000 when Dionne applied to work at the Learning Exchange, she had to print her résumé and hand deliver it because companies were not yet accepting resumes digitally, which wasn’t hard as she was living just around the corner and already working in the community\.

At the time, the Learning Exchange didn’t have any computers for public use, just one room, a large boardroom table, and a mandate to connect with and listen to the Downtown Eastside\. Dionne’s extensive connections gave the Learning Exchange a level of credibility as it was finding its way\. One of the first things the Learning Exchange team heard was talk of the emerging digital divide between those who had access to new personal technologies and those who did not\.

Soon the Learning Exchange had a drop\-in and computer access space with six computer kiosks—three for Internet access and three for word processing\. Twenty\-five years on, it has a robust digital literacy program that includes drop\-in access to computers, free computer workshops on a range of topics, and a Pop\-Up Tech Café program led by community ambassadors \(in conjunction with the Downtown Eastside Literacy Roundtable\)\. The Learning Exchange also works in collaboration with other organizations to host forums on digital literacy and the digital divide, which continues to widen as more and more services close their storefront outlets and move online\.

As the Digital Literacy Coordinator, and also the founding coordinator of the Drop\-In and Computer Program, Dionne has been instrumental in these initiatives—building strong relationships to address community priorities, many of which she developed and has helped maintain over the last 25 years\. Her contributions have been instrumental to the many successes the Learning Exchange has earned\.

Dionne also received the President’s Service Award for Excellence in 2013\.
