In Brief:

Academic Director Elisa Baniassad shares her vision for UBC’s new Learning Technology Innovation Center and why universities must shape their own educational future\.

I have worked in the intersection of pedagogy and technology since around 2002, when I developed my first pedagogical tool: the Teaching Oriented Language Development environment — a tool that let students write the grammar for a domain specific language, enter the semantics for each language element, write some sample code, see an abstract syntax tree \(a visual representation\) of their code, watch the execution of that code, and \(\!\) when running the code would see the nodes of the abstract syntax tree highlight\. I don’t have a reference for that work, because in 2002, as a pre\-tenure discipline specific Software Engineering research faculty member at the Chinese University of Hong Kong, I felt almost no motivation to disseminate learning technology innovation or inquiry\.

I was basing this erroneous belief on my experience as a graduate student in the late 90’s, often sitting in the UBC Computer Science lounge right next door to the lab where Murray Goldberg was developing Web CT — one of the first Learning Management Systems\. Murray did not, I believe, get *research* credit for this work – he was \(excellent\) teaching\-oriented faculty\. WebCT work counted as software innovation alone\. And ultimately, he left our department, and sold WebCT, and had profound impact on the way teaching is delivered across the world, and that’s a positive story *for him*, but UBC CPSC lost an amazing and visionary educator\.

But a lot has changed since the early oughts\. Learning Technology is now a field in its own right, and there are top\-tier publication venues for discipline specific education research that accept tool development as publishable units\. Not that technology is an end in itself – but is recognised as a potential vehicle to improved learning outcomes, and as a tool for inquiry into the essential nature of the practice of learning\.

Learning Technology itself has penetrated the teaching and learning ecosystem\. Is there a single program that involves absolutely no software? No online discussion forum? No autograder? No learning management system or web equivalent for distribution of materials? Unlikely \(though strangely appealing\)\. And now AI is prompting a sea change in many fields, including in education\. It will not affect every academic sphere at once, but the wave is on the move\. My home program, Computer Science, is by necessity an early adopter\. CS educators are being provoked to both incorporate AI into their teaching, and also into the learning outcomes for courses and whole programs — even the ones that formerly had nothing to do with machine learning or artificial intelligence\. While for years there was a kind of affectionate attitude of “AI is the future and it always will be”, we are now all recognising: that future is now\.

And so, it becomes increasingly imperative that a university as vast and influential as UBC takes a careful and yet courageous approach to providing faculty and students with the right teaching and learning tools, and with the opportunity and resourcing to inquire, interrogate, and innovate, about the tools themselves\. With learning technology, the medium defines the message, and we can’t let industry determine how we will educate\.

The vision for the Learning Technology Innovation Centre is grounded in this foundational belief: that it is our responsibility, as a world leading research and teaching institution, to define and invent our own academic future\. UBC, both collectively and each individual member of faculty, needs to be able to find their own voice in this new world\. LTIC’s role will be to provision, prototype, or even develop tools, so that UBC educators can speak with fluency, with creativity, and without friction, in their own pedagogical language, and to provide technology and tech expertise to help faculty innovate and inquire into the fundamental and of the moment questions about teaching, learning, and technology\.
