Find information and resources for soliciting referees for clinical professor appointments and promotions\. 

---

Letters of reference are a key component in assessing candidates for clinical professor appointments and promotions\. The process must uphold fairness, confidentiality, and institutional standards\. 

## Guidelines

Candidates must submit a list of **at least six potential referees \(at least two must be external to UBC\)**, with the following information:  

-   names and contact information 
-   disclosure of the relationship 
-   rationale for each suggestion 

Referees should represent a range of perspectives and be able to speak to different areas of the candidate’s academic contributions and how they meet the criteria for appointment or promotion, including:  

-   The candidate’s significant contributions to professional practice in the hospital, agency, professional organization, FoM and/or UBC through their teaching/educational leadership activities\. 
-   The candidate’s impact in service to their hospital, agency, professional organization, the FoM, UBC, or community, including related leadership in committee, administrative or policy\-making decisions\. 
-   The candidate’s impact in scholarly activity as referenced in the criteria\.

Referees **do not need to be arm’s length** but must not have a **close personal relationship** with the candidate \(e\.g\., relative/family, close friends\)\. 

Referees are contacted by the Head/ Director or their delegate [using the Letter of Request for Reference template\.](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/?page_id=33292#templates) 

-   The Head/ Director reviews and prioritizes list of references, consulting with CARP as needed\.
-   Only two letters will be solicited by the Department/School from the list of names provided by the candidate\. Ideally, one is from someone who is external to UBC\.  
-   Candidates **must not** contact referees directly to solicit the letters\. 

Letters of reference **must remain** **confidential\.** 

Members of the review committees **must not** disclose the identity of referees or the contents of their letters\. 

**Please Note:** For more information about the confidentiality of letters of reference in a faculty member’s personnel file, please refer to UBC Human Resources’ [Guidelines for personnel files](https://hr.ubc.ca/managers-admins/managing-and-leading/faculty-relations/guidelines-personnel-files) \(CWL login required\)\.
