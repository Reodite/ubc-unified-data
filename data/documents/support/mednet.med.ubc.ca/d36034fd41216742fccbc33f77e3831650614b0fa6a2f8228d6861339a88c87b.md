The MS Research Network was proud to support five summer students who were awarded studentships through the [Faculty of Medicine Student Summer Research Program](https://www.med.ubc.ca/current-learners/summer-student-research-program/) 2025, working on interdisciplinary research projects\. Below is a summary of them\.  

This project found that autotaxin levels were lower in patients with cGvHD across both sexes, suggesting that targeting it may be beneficial for male and female transplant patients and established a basis to evaluate autotaxin therapy to block development of cGvHD and inform similar strategies across other autoimmune conditions\.  

This project collected patient and families’ preferences for engagement with genetic testing information and their overall understanding of the process\. The students identified knowledge gaps around costs, genetic counsellors and access to results, highlighting areas for improving patient education and family\-based risk communication for future genetic testing and research\.  

This project aimed to improve methods for preparing and analyzing immune cells from spinal cord tissue and identified several critical steps to help refine methodology to characterize immune cell populations that will enable understanding of inflammation and repair processes for spinal cord injury or other similar neurological diseases\.   

This project evaluated and compiled existing research using established guidelines and criteria for a systematic review that aims to understand how medications influence the risk of developing MS\. Once complete, the review will support early identification of MS risk factors and inform preventative clinical strategies\.  

This project identified functional brain networks activated during the pain empathy task to support and improve pre\-surgical planning and as a benchmark to detect subtle deficits in patients who need brain surgery or suffer from cognitive defects\. 

The MS Research Network’s goal is to use the latest advances in cell and immuno\-engineering to develop, manufacture and test novel therapies for multiple sclerosis and other autoimmune diseases by drawing on integrative expertise across neurology, immunology, biomedical engineering and regenerative medicine\. Supporting student involvement in this initiative not only advances cutting\-edge research, but also fosters interdisciplinary collaboration that is critical to transforming patient care\. 

The students and researchers expressed their gratitude for the support that enables them to engage in meaningful research, develop critical skills, learn from exceptional mentors and pursue opportunities without financial burden, making these impactful experiences possible\. With the support of the VPRI and Office of Research, we look forward to fostering future collaborations that advance our shared goal of transforming patient care\.
