## Getting Started with Generative AI

While AI includes many technologies, Generative AI is currently having the greatest impact on day\-to\-day work, making it a practical starting point for building AI literacy across the Faculty of Medicine\.

### Common Use

Generative AI is one of the most practical and widely accessible ways to begin applying AI in your day\-to\-day work\. It can support routine administrative tasks, including:  

-   Brainstorming
-   Summarizing information
-   Drafting written materials

#### **Common Copilot Use Cases**

-   Simplified communication: Drafting clear, professional emails or summarizing key points from long email threads\.
-   Meeting efficiency: Generating succinct meeting summaries, action items, and follow\-ups\.
-   Project support: Organizing project tasks, timelines, and shared documents\.
-   Content creation: Outlining or drafting reports, presentations, and instructional materials\.

#### Zoom AI Companion Use Case

-   Meeting summaries: automated summaries, key action items, and structural takeaways from your Zoom calls\.

## PRO\-TIP: Prompt Engineering

To get the most accurate and useful results from an AI tool, use structured prompts like the Role, Task, Format \(RTF\) framework:

| Element | What it Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R**ole | *Who* the AI is | *“You are an administrative assistant…”* |
| **T**ask | *What* you need it to do | *“Draft a follow\-up email regarding the committee timeline…”* |
| **F**ormat | Desired *output* | *“In bullet points…”* or *“As a formal memo…”* |

*More resources about prompt engineering are available on the [CTLT site](https://ai.ctlt.ubc.ca/prompt-engineering)\.*

## **Oversight and Transparency**

While AI tools save time, you remain the expert in the room\. Always apply these core rules to your work\.

#### **Human Oversight**

-   Always review and verify AI\-generated content before sharing or publishing\.
-   Treat GenAI as a support tool, not a decision\-maker\.

#### Practice Transparency

-   Be clear with colleagues when content has been AI\-assisted, especially in formal communications\.
-   Maintain accountability for final outputs\.

## Privacy &amp; Security: Dos and Don’ts

Keep these boundaries in mind before inputting any text into an AI tool\.

### **Do**

-   **Authenticate** your M365 tools using your official UBC staff account \(firstname\.lastname@ubc\.ca\) to ensure data protection\.
-   **Use** [**tools** that meet UBC’s privacy and security standards](https://genai.ubc.ca/privacy-and-risk/)\.
-   **Limit what you input** into AI tools and manage data carefully – only share what’s necessary\.
-   **Review AI\-generated outputs** for accuracy and alignment with UBC policies\.
-   **Engage with privacy and security teams early** when planning to use AI\.

### **Don’t**

-   **Don’t enter personal or confidential information** into unapproved AI tools\.
-   **Don’t reuse your CWL password** in any AI service\.
-   **Don’t accept vendor terms** without reviewing privacy and security clauses\.
-   **Don’t assume** that anything you input into an AI tool is private\.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Copilot Chat can be used with **low to medium\-risk data**\. To help you self\-assess, [UBC classifies electronic information](https://cio.ubc.ca/information-security/policy-standards-resources/U1) into four tiers: 

-   **Low Risk**: Publicly available information, e\.g\., names and work contact info of UBC staff and faculty\. 
-   **Medium Risk**: Proprietary information, confidential financial info and records, etc\. 
-   **High Risk**: Personally identifiable information, including student names, student and employee IDs, face ID photos, grades, home addresses, etc\. *\(Do NOT input\)*\.
-   **Very High** **Risk:** Social Insurance Numbers \(SIN\), official government IDs, bank account details, Protected Health Information, Date of Birth, etc\. *\(Do NOT input\)*\.  
      
    

You can currently use two options:

**Zoom AI Companion – Meeting summary**: Automatically generates summaries when enabled\. Review the [terms of service and user guide here](https://it.ubc.ca/services/teaching-learning-tools/zoom-video-conferencing/zoom-ai-companion-terms-service)\.

**Copilot Chat**: Meeting transcripts can be obtained from Zoom or MS Teams if the meeting was recorded\. The current version of Microsoft Teams \(including Copilot chat\) available to all UBC users is approved for **low to medium risk data**\. This means it’s acceptable to use Copilot chat with **employee names** and to generate transcripts that include staff\-related information\. However, if your content includes **learner information**, it must be **de\-identified** before using Copilot chat, due to current licensing and data protection standards\.

*Note: UBC is actively reviewing and testing a more advanced version of Copilot that may support **High\-Risk data** applications in the future, pending licensing agreements*\.

Some units, programs and departments may classify certain types of data \(e\.g\. schedules\) as higher risk than UBC’s general standards\. This is especially true in the Faculty of Medicine, where health\-related data is common\. In such cases, **follow the specific guidelines set by your unit**, even if they are more restrictive than UBC\-wide policies\.
