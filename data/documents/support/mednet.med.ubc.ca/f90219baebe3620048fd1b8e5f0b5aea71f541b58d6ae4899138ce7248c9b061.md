Find information on the key requirements for establishing a department or school\-level standing committee to review Clinical Appointment, Reappointment,  and Promotion \(CARP\) files\. 

---

Each department or school is responsible for establishing a standing **Clinical Appointment, Reappointment, and Promotion \(CARP\) Committee** to deliberate and make recommendations on the appointment, reappointment, and promotion of Clinical Faculty, as well as creating a [Terms of Reference](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/hr/cf-hr/resources/#templates) document outlining the roles and responsibilities of members, membership term and quorum\.

 It is **strongly recommended** that this be a dedicated standing committee for the following reasons: 

-   **Policy compliance:** The [Clinical Faculty Policy](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/hr/cf-hr/resources/#policies) sets out specific membership, quorum, and voting eligibility criteria that are difficult to manage in a joint committee with tenure\-stream faculty; 
-   **Confidentiality:** Only those eligible to vote should review and deliberate on files; a dedicated committee ensures this standard is maintained; 
-   **Efficiency:** A dedicated committee focuses solely on Clinical Faculty files, avoiding the need to navigate two sets of criteria – those outlined in the [Clinical Faculty Policy](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/hr/cf-hr/resources/#policies) and those in the [Collective Agreement](https://hr.ubc.ca/working-ubc/faculty-resources/faculty-collective-agreement-and-policies); 
-   **Process integrity:** Reduces the risk of procedural errors or appeals; 
-   **Recognition and engagement:** Underscores the value of clinical faculty by having their work reviewed by peers who understand their contributions\. 

Each CARP committee will be responsible for deliberating and making recommendations on the appointment, reappointment,  and promotion of clinical faculty, specifically: 

-   new appointments at all ranks above clinical instructor; 
-   reappointments where non\-reappointment is being considered; 
-   promotions to all ranks\. 

## **Committee Compositio**n

The Head/ Director must consult with a committee where membership reflects a diverse mix of perspectives and expertise\. 

-   Committees **should** consist of 4\-8 elected clinical faculty; 
-   Further considerations for representation include: [Clinical Faculty Advisory Council Representatives,](https://www.med.ubc.ca/clinical-faculty/contacts/#council) ranks, expertise, distributed sites, educational programs, and Division \(if applicable\)\. 

### **Representation:** 

-   In cases where fewer than four eligible clinical faculty from the Department/School are available to serve, the Head/Director will need to supplement the Committee membership\. This issue most commonly arises for clinical professors, as small units may not have sufficient members at that rank willing or available to serve; 
-   When supplementation is required, it is recommended that additional members be appointed in the following order: 

1.  Existing tenure\-stream faculty from the Department/School that work in a clinical or health system setting;
2.  Clinical faculty outside the Department/School that serve on their unit’s CARP Committee\.  

**The Chair** is responsible for ensuring all conflicts of interest between the Chair, committee members and the candidates are identified and managed\. Chair and committee members are not to be in personal or external reporting relationships with the candidate\. Reference UBC’s [COI Policy \(SC3\) found on the Office of the University Counsel website](https://universitycounsel.ubc.ca/policies/coi-policy/)\. If there are questions, please contact the [Faculty HR team](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/hr/contacts/)\. 

## **Establishing Quorum and Voting Requirements** 

To be eligible to vote on:  

-   **appointments**, members must be of a **rank equal to or higher** than that of the candidate; 
-   **reappointment** and **promotion**, members must be of a **rank higher** than that of the candidate except for clinical professors where the rank must be equal\. 

Refer to the [CARP Members Eligible to Vote](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/hr/cf-hr/resources/#information-guides) chart for a breakdown of members eligible to vote by rank\.

Eligible members in a conflict of interest with a candidate should not participate\. 

A majority vote \(&gt;50%\) constitutes the recommendation of the CARP Committee\. Accordingly, quorum is when the number of members in attendance can constitute a majority vote in relation to the size of the committee\. 

## **Materials for Deliberation** 

The Committee considers only the candidate’s file, as obtained following recognized procedures, in supporting a recommendation\. Opinions submitted in writing by a member of the CARP who has reviewed the file but cannot participate in consultations may also be considered\.  

## **Handling Concerns** 

If the review of a file raises concerns relative to the [criteria in the Clinical Faculty Policy](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/hr/cf-hr/resources/#policies), the Committee should not proceed to vote until the Head/Director provides the clinical faculty member \(or applicant\) with concerns in writing and provide them with an opportunity to either \(i\) respond to those concerns before their file moves forward to a vote and recommendation by the CARP, or \(ii\) request that the review be stopped \(withdraw\)\.  

Clinical Faculty members \(or applicants\) reviewed at CARP shall not be informed of the specific results of a CARP Committee vote\.

## **Committee Conduct** 

Conduct expected of committee members in the Faculty of Medicine is outlined on the [Criteria &amp; Responsibilities for Standing Committees](https://mednet.med.ubc.ca/office-of-the-dean/governance/committees/criteria-responsibilities-for-standing-committees/#role-of-member) page\.
