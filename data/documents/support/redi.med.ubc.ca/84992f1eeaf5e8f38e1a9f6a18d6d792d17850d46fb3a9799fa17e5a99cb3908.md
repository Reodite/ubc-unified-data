This tip sheet provides a practical, step\-by\-step approach for navigating difficult conversations with respect and clarity\. By following the O’DEAR model—Open, Describe, Explain, Ask, and Request—you can express concerns, listen actively, and co\-construct solutions to address sensitive issues\.

[Download the PDF Guide](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2024/08/EDI-ODEAR-Conversation-Framework-2.pdf)

# The O’DEAR Framework for Difficult Conversations

## When to Use This Framework

Do you need to give someone feedback about something they said or did that may have been sexist, racist, ableist, oppressive, or otherwise insensitive?

The **O’DEAR framework** offers a structured approach to navigate difficult conversations\. It helps you:

-   Share concerns clearly and respectfully
-   Listen to the other person’s perspective
-   Maintain psychological safety where possible
-   Exit the conversation in a constructive way

---

## “O” is for OPEN

Begin by opening the conversation in a respectful and intentional way\. Whenever possible, choose a private setting and a time when the other person is not rushed or distracted\.

### Examples

-   “I’d like to speak with you about…”
-   “Do you have a few minutes to talk about…?”
-   “We have a situation we should discuss…”

---

## “D” is for DESCRIBE

Describe the behaviour in a specific, neutral, and objective way\. Focus on what was said or done, not interpretations of intent\.

### Examples

-   “When you…”
-   “When you said…”
-   “When I did \_\_\_ and you…”
-   “I heard from a student that…”
-   “In a faculty meeting, you said…”

---

## “E” is for EXPLAIN

Explain the impact the behaviour had on you, others, or the work environment\. Use clear, honest language about the effect\.

### Examples

-   “I felt belittled / confused / frustrated / excluded\.”
-   “I experienced \_\_\_ when it happened\.”
-   “Some colleagues were concerned / saddened / shocked\.”
-   “I had difficulty refocusing after that\.”

---

## “A” is for ASK

Invite the other person to share their perspective and help you better understand their intentions or reasoning\.

### Examples

-   “What was going on for you?”
-   “What were you trying to accomplish?”
-   “How did you feel in that moment?”
-   “I’m curious to better understand what you were thinking\.”

---

## “R” is for REQUEST

Make a clear request for a different behaviour or approach going forward\. Aim for constructive, actionable change\.

### Examples

-   “I would appreciate it if we could speak directly about issues so we can work through them together\.”
-   “I would appreciate more care in how words or actions are used moving forward\.”
-   “We are all working to improve, and this is one area to focus on\.”
-   “Let’s think about next steps together\. What strategies could help repair relationships with colleagues?”
