# Creating Belonging &amp; Building Relationships Through Ice Breaker Activities

Ice breaker activities can help foster connection, trust, and engagement in classes, meetings, workshops, and team environments\.

---

## Benefits of Ice Breakers for Classes and Meetings

### Building Connection and Trust

-   Builds social connection, trust, and rapport\.
-   Helps colleagues get to know each other beyond their professional roles\.
-   Reveals similarities and shared experiences despite demographic differences\.
-   Reduces power dynamics so that more voices can contribute to discussions and decisions\.
-   Fosters confidence to speak up later in a class or meeting\.
-   Supports an environment where people feel more comfortable expressing different perspectives\.

### Supporting Well\-Being

-   Fosters well\-being\.
-   Promotes laughter and relieves stress\.
-   Creates opportunities for participants to step outside their comfort zones\.

### Enhancing Performance and Innovation

-   Improves performance and innovation\.
-   Encourages the social cohesion necessary for creativity and collaboration\.

---

## Diversity Ice Breakers

Use these questions to encourage reflection, understanding, and connection across differences\.

-   Based purely on how you look, talk, or present yourself, what is an incorrect assumption people sometimes make about you?
-   What is your strategy for connecting across difference? *\(Credit: Inside\-Out Prison Exchange Program\)*
-   What feelings come up when you think about equity, diversity, and inclusion?
-   What is a positive interaction you have had with someone you did not know?
-   What is something you would like to share about yourself that people might not otherwise know?

---

## Teaching Ice Breakers

These prompts can help participants reflect on learning, teaching, and belonging\.

-   Looking back over your life, who is a teacher who inspired you?
-   Why did they inspire you?
-   Describe a time in a learning environment or workplace when you felt a sense of inclusion\.
-   What factors contributed to that feeling of inclusion?

---

## Academic Job Ice Breakers

These questions are particularly useful for academic departments, faculty retreats, and professional development events\.

-   How does your research or teaching connect you to place?
-   What is one unexpected aspect of your professional journey that brought you to your current position?
-   If you could open any business, what would it be?
-   What is your dream job?
-   If money and practicality were not concerns, what would you be doing with your life?
-   What is one thing you need from your teammates in order to do your best work?
-   What is something we should know to work most effectively with you?

---

## Just\-for\-Fun Ice Breakers

These prompts are designed to create connection through storytelling, humour, and shared experiences\.

-   What is an object on your desk or in your workspace that is meaningful to you?
-   What is one thing you have done once that you will never do again? *\(Credit: Inside\-Out Prison Exchange Program\)*
-   What does “home” mean to you?
-   **Two Truths and a Lie:** Share three statements about yourself\. The rest of the group guesses which statement is the lie\.
-   Who is one famous person you would like to meet, and why?
-   If you could have any superpower, what would it be?
-   Find someone you do not know very well and identify 10 things you have in common\. \(These should be somewhat surprising and go beyond obvious similarities\.\)

---

## Tips for Facilitators

-   Choose ice breakers that align with the goals of the meeting or class\.
-   Consider the comfort level of participants and avoid questions that may feel overly personal\.
-   Provide the option to pass on any question\.
-   Model participation to help create a welcoming environment\.
-   Allow sufficient time for discussion and reflection\.
