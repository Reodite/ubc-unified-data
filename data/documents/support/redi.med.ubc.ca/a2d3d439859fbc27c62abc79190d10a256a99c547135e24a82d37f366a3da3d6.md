# Best Practices for Mitigating Cognitive Biases in the Hiring Process

Cognitive biases can significantly inhibit equity in hiring by influencing decision\-making and contributing to unconscious discrimination\. It is important for hiring committees to be aware of potential biases and take steps to mitigate their effects when screening and interviewing candidates\.

---

## Common Cognitive Biases in Hiring

### Affinity Bias

**Definition**  
The tendency to prefer candidates who are similar to ourselves in terms of background, race, gender, interests, experiences, or other characteristics\.

**Example**  
“I like a candidate because I relate to them\. We were born in the same area, attended the same schools for undergraduate and medical education, and majored in similar subjects\. We just ‘click’ in a way that I don’t with the other candidates\.”

---

### Bandwagon Effect

**Definition**  
The tendency to prefer candidates based on the preferences of others rather than independently assessing their qualifications and contributing to a genuine consensus\.

**Example**  
“I vote for the candidate who already has the most support among committee members rather than considering each candidate’s merits and advocating for the person I believe would be the best fit\.”

---

### Confirmation Bias

**Definition**  
The tendency to search for, interpret, favour, and recall information in ways that confirm our existing beliefs or assumptions\.

**Example**  
“It is better to be safe than sorry\. Therefore, I view the candidate with a traditional academic background as a safer and stronger choice than a candidate with diverse experiences and a non\-traditional academic path\.”

---

### Recency Effect

**Definition**  
The tendency to favour candidates whom we have met or reviewed most recently\.

**Example**  
“After interviewing several candidates, I can more easily recall reasons for preferring the final candidate\. I may forget many of the strengths of earlier candidates, even if those strengths were more relevant or compelling\.”

---

### Halo Effect

**Definition**  
The tendency to form an overall positive impression of a candidate based on one particularly favourable characteristic or achievement\.

**Example**  
“I notice on a candidate’s résumé that they were once an Olympic skier\. As a result, I assume their talent and work ethic in this position are equally exceptional, even though that achievement may not be directly related to the role\.”

---

### Fundamental Attribution Error

**Definition**  
The tendency to overlook situational or environmental factors influencing a candidate’s behaviour while overemphasizing personal characteristics\.

**Example**  
“Although the candidate had a legitimate reason beyond their control for rescheduling the interview, I still \(consciously or unconsciously\) view them as less reliable\.”

---

## Practices to Mitigate Cognitive Biases in Hiring

### Rubrics

#### Best Practices

-   Create a common evaluation rubric before reviewing candidates\.
-   Apply the rubric consistently throughout the process\.
-   Minimize the influence of factors that are unrelated to the established criteria\.

---

### Personal Note\-Taking

#### Best Practices

-   Take detailed and objective notes throughout the process\.
-   Recognize that details may be forgotten over time and document observations promptly\.
-   Review notes regularly to accurately recall your impressions and assessments\.

**Note:** The Office of the University Counsel’s guidelines provide additional recommendations regarding the content, use, retention, and handling of personal notes created during the hiring and interview process\.

---

### Reminders

#### Best Practices

-   Regularly remind committee members about common cognitive biases\.
-   Encourage committee members to revisit their notes before discussions and decisions\.

---

### Transparency

#### Best Practices

-   Clearly define and communicate the genuine needs of the position\.
-   Be transparent with candidates about the purpose and goals of each stage of the hiring process\.

---

### Reflection

#### Best Practices

-   Reflect on the hiring process and identify opportunities for improvement\.
-   Consider keeping a journal to document observations, priorities, challenges, and outcomes throughout the process\.

---

### Shuffling

#### Best Practices

-   Vary the order in which candidate applications and interviews are reviewed\.
-   Rotate presentation sequences throughout the process to reduce the impact of recency effects and other ordering biases\.
