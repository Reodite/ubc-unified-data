# Four ways job postings can appeal to prospective applicants with strong equity, diversity &amp; inclusion skills

> Crafting job postings that resonate positively with people who have strong equity, diversity, and inclusion skills can result in strong candidates being willing to apply\.

## 1\. The job posting conveys a clear vision for the role\.

**Make it clear:** What are the specific qualifications, duties and responsibilities?

-   Don’t inflate minimum education, certification or experience requirements\.
-   Be transparent, as required by policy, law or a relevant licensing body\.
-   Use [plain language](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/services-for-government/service-experience-digital-delivery/web-content-development-guides/web-style-guide/writing-guide/plain-language)\.
-   Avoid abstract verbs such as, “supports,” “participates in,” “works well with\.”
-   Avoid–or at least clarify–jargon, technical terms, acronyms, or abbreviations\.

*Good examples:*

-   *\[…\] interactions with diverse clients; ability to lead diverse teams*
-   *Demonstrated commitment to enhancing one’s own awareness, knowledge, and skills related to equity, diversity, and inclusion*
-   *\[X\] degree in a relevant discipline; minimum of \[X\] years of related experience, or the equivalent combination of education and experience*

## 2\. The job posting showcases a culture in which EDI skills are essential\.

**Make it clear:** How do the role and candidates’ skills connect to supporting and advancing EDI efforts in the Faculty and/or within the program or department?

-   Indicate how the role intersects with groups doing inclusion work \(e\.g\., Faculty of Medicine Indigenous Engagement, REDI Team, Office of Faculty Development, Office of Organizational Development, UBC Equity &amp; Inclusion Office, Centre for Teaching, Learning &amp; Technology, etc\.\)\.
-   Highlight any EDI committees, professional development, affinity groups and relevant communities of practice connected to your EDI efforts\.

*Good examples:*

-   *This role contributes to Faculty culture transformation primarily through \[…\]*
-   *Understanding of the principles of Truth &amp; Reconciliation and supporting Faculty’s commitments in response to the Truth &amp; Reconciliation Committee’s Calls to Action*
-   *High emotional intelligence when dealing with conflict*
-   *Racialized, Indigenous and other members of historically marginalized groups are encouraged to apply*

---

## 3\. The job posting indicates it will be a fair hiring process, one in which all candidates can succeed\.

**Make it clear:** What features emphasize that [inclusive excellence](https://equity.ubc.ca/resources/inclusive-excellence/) is valued? Which groups are under\-represented within the unit or within the Faculty overall?

-   Be expansive about the range of credentials, education, or experience that can be considered valid preparation for the position\.
-   Indicate that there are multiple ways to meet the job’s qualifications\.
-   Identify transferrable skills and contexts that may be of value in the position\.

*Good examples:*

-   *Understanding of and commitment to principles of equity, diversity &amp; inclusion, anti\-racism, and anti\-discrimination*
-   *Willingness to respect diverse perspectives, including perspectives that may be in conflict with one’s own*
-   *Demonstrated commitment to ongoing learning in an evolving landscape of EDI, anti\-racism and Truth &amp; Reconciliation*

## 4\. The job posting is welcoming and supportive\.

**Make it clear:** How will the person hired be supported to succeed in the role?

-   Use a flexible and welcoming tone\.
-   Indicate accommodations are available for candidates with disabilities\.
-   Where appropriate, highlight applicable inclusive workplace culture features: prioritizing work\-life balance, being highly diverse, having an EDI committee, valuing multiple perspectives and ways of thinking, etc\.
-   Avoid using exclusionary language \(e\.g\., use the pronoun “their” instead of “his” or “her”\); avoid words that may be seen as gender\-coded \(e\.g\. “decisive” or “nurturing”\)\.

*Good examples:*

-   *Ability to work effectively/productively and build relationships with diverse, intercultural and interdisciplinary groups*
-   *Comfortable engaging in conversations about unconscious bias, microaggressions, privilege and decolonization*
-   *Poor \[performance descriptor\] could undermine trust in the \[program, department, etc\.\] or negatively impact psychological safety within learning environments\. Sound professional judgment and \[additional key skills\] are essential\.*

## Job Postings &amp; [Indigenization](https://opentextbc.ca/indigenizationinstructors/back-matter/glossary/): Word Choice Matters

“Purposefully using the best and most respectful words is being [respectful, accurate, and responsible](https://opentextbc.ca/indigenousdigitalliteracies/chapter/words-matter/)\.

The language we use \[in job postings\] is important\. It can create a sense of empowerment, identity, and pride in people\.

It can also do the opposite\. Language and words can promote stereotypes and isolate people\. It can continue outdated narratives and cause harm to both individuals and communities\.”¹
