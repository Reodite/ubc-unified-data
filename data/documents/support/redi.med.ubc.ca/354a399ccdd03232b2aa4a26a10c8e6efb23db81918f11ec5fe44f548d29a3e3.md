Case studies shape how future clinicians understand patient care, but do they reflect the diversity of the people they will serve? This guide offers strategies for writing inclusive cases that challenge stereotypes, highlight systemic factors affecting health, and ensure all patients are seen as complex individuals\. By integrating these principles, educators can foster more equitable and effective clinical learning\.

# Writing Inclusive Case Studies

## Why Is This Important?

This guide was created in response to a request from UGME for support in reviewing case studies\. It was developed in collaboration with Dr\. Saleem Razack, Senior Faculty Advisor to the REDI Team\.

Inclusive case studies help learners challenge stereotypes, practice inclusive clinical perspective\-taking, and better understand the diverse histories and experiences of the people they will encounter\.

As short stories with real\-world lessons, inclusive cases provide both:

-   **Windows** for members of dominant groups to see into the lives of people who are historically, systemically, and persistently marginalized \(HSPM\)
-   **Mirrors** that allow members of HSPM groups to see themselves, their families, and their histories represented

---

## Inclusive Cases Have Serendipity

Inclusive cases reflect that diversity is normal and that every person and body is unique\. A person’s identity may sometimes have clinical relevance, but not always\. People should be represented as complex, multidimensional individuals\.

---

## Representing HSPM Groups in Meaningful Ways

Members of HSPM groups should be portrayed in ways that are not limited to their identity\.

### Consider:

-   Are there cases featuring transgender people where gender identity is not the focus?
-   Are Indigenous people only shown in cases involving trauma, or also in routine care \(e\.g\., psoriasis, strep throat\)?

---

## People as Unique Individuals

Inclusive cases should reflect individuality rather than grouping people into broad categories\.

-   Distinguish between groups that are often generalized \(e\.g\., 2SLGBTQIA\+, BIPOC\)
-   Include variety in names, naming traditions, and pronouns

---

## Represent Diverse Family Structures

-   Include multi\-generational and extended family involvement in healthcare decisions
-   Avoid centring only nuclear family structures \(e\.g\., “mother, father, two children”\)

---

## Stereotype Awareness

Inclusive cases actively avoid reinforcing stereotypes and aim for balanced representation\.

### Counter\-stereotypical examples:

-   A Black woman with a stress fracture from a squash tournament
-   An Indigenous, neurodiverse law school graduate receiving a flu vaccine
-   A supportive immigrant family navigating a young person’s gender transition

---

## Intersectionality in Case Design

Cases should reflect the reality that individuals may hold multiple identities\.

### Examples:

-   A Black, queer woman caring for a disabled adult child
-   A formerly incarcerated elderly White man who uses a wheelchair and communicates through a translator
-   A highly educated immigrant woman in financial distress due to licensing barriers in Canada
-   A child of two divorced fathers, one receiving inpatient recovery support

---

## Balanced Representation Across Cases

Across a curriculum:

-   Everyone should have opportunities to be portrayed in “neutral” or everyday contexts
-   Members of HSPM groups should not only appear in stigmatized or trauma\-focused narratives
-   All groups should be shown experiencing both strengths and challenges

---

## Specific Examples That Illuminate Universal Health Principles

When identity is included in a case, it should serve a **learning objective**, not define the individual\.

### Examples:

-   A case involving a nun with endometrial cancer can teach about cancer risk factors related to reproductive history, not about religion\.
-   A Japanese\-Canadian patient case can illustrate dietary transitions and changing health outcomes across generations, not cultural essentialism\.
-   A refugee patient presenting with varying symptoms can illustrate trauma responses and healthcare access challenges, not assumptions about immigrant behaviour\.

---

## Systemic Connections

Inclusive cases should highlight **determinants of health**, including social, economic, environmental, and structural factors\.

For more information, visit the Government of Canada’s webpage on Social Determinants of Health and Health Inequalities\.

### Guiding Questions

-   How do structural determinants \(distribution of power, money, resources\) shape health outcomes?
-   Are racism and other forms of oppression being framed as determinants of health rather than biological traits like race?
-   Are addiction, poverty, and homelessness framed as policy outcomes rather than individual failings?
-   Are disability and inequity framed as outcomes of inaccessible systems rather than individual deficits?
-   Are we acknowledging stigma and discrimination within healthcare systems?

---

## Bonus Tips for Case Developers

### Consider Societal Needs

-   Observe current societal issues and ask: What kinds of cases does society need right now?

### Conduct a Sensitivity Review

-   After drafting, invite feedback from members of relevant communities
-   Compensate contributors appropriately for their time and expertise

### Adopt a Stance of Humility

-   Strive for progress, not perfection
-   Be open to feedback and revisions
-   Use critique as a tool for improvement
