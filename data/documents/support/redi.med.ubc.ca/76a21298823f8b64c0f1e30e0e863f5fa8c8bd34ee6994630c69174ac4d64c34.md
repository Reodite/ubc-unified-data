How can we make our research more equitable? This REDI Best Practice tip sheet introduces co\-creation as a collaborative approach to research that centres the voices and priorities of marginalized and underserved communities and leads to more impactful and inclusive research\.

[Download the PDF Guide](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/05/REDI-Version-Equity-in-Research-CoCreation-1.pdf)

## Question: How Can We Make Our Research More Equitable?

Co\-creation is a collaborative approach to creative problem\-solving between diverse participants at all stages of a research initiative\. It can increase the impact of research on complex health problems, particularly when working with participants from marginalized and underserved communities\.

Below are key considerations and strategies for supporting co\-creation with communities in research\.

---

## Reframe the Research Relationship: Be a Partner Who Is Fully Present

### Be a Partner

-   Do research *with* the community, not *to* the community\.
-   Do not assume the community shares the same priorities or values as the researcher\.
-   Listen to community\-identified health priorities and align research to support them\.
-   Invest time in repairing and healing relationships that may have been harmed by past negative research experiences\.

---

### Be Fully Present

Engaging in co\-creation requires relational presence beyond a strictly technical or professional role\.

#### Reflective Questions

-   What brings you to this relationship?
-   Where do you come from?
-   What motivates you to do this research?
-   How has your lived experience shaped your research journey?

---

## Foster Autonomous Motivation

According to Richard M\. Ryan and Edward L\. Deci \(self\-determination theory\), autonomously motivated individuals experience improved well\-being, flourishing, creativity, prosocial behaviour, and care for others \(Ryan &amp; Deci, 2000\)\.

Autonomous motivation is supported by three core elements: **connection, capacity, and choice**\.

---

### Connection

Connection is about belonging, trust, and validation\.

#### Examples of supportive statements:

-   “I am here if you need me\.”
-   “I value our relationship and would love to work with you\.”
-   “I understand why you might feel this way\.”
-   “No rush\.”

---

### Capacity

Capacity refers to self\-efficacy, empowerment, and confidence\.

#### Examples of supportive statements:

-   “What supports are most important to you?”
-   “You are doing great work in this field\.”
-   “How can I help make this feel less overwhelming?”

---

### Choice

Choice is about consent, autonomy, and authenticity\.

#### Examples of supportive statements:

-   “What are the challenges your community is facing?”
-   “What is most important for me to understand about your community?”
-   “What approaches feel right for you and your community?”

---

## Reference
