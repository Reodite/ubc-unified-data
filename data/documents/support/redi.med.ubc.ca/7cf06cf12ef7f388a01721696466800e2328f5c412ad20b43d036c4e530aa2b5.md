## Speaker Bio

[Image](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Mark-Matthew-Health-Quality-BC-edited.jpg)

**Mark Matthew, Simpcw First Nation  
Director, Indigenous Health, Health Quality BC**

Mark joined Health Quality BC in September 2022 and is the Director of Indigenous Health\.

Mark Matthew grew up on the reserve of the Simpcw First Nation near Barriere, B\.C\. and has observed the consequences of colonization in his family, friends, and community\. He continues to reflect on his unearned privilege of a white presenting male due to mixed parentage\.

Mark’s career in health began in 2007 as the Aboriginal Contracts Advisor at Interior Health\. Just over a year later he joined the FNHA as a Community Development Liaison and eventually took on the role of Acting Director of Community Engagement in 2011 supporting communication, collaboration, and planning and health with First Nations throughout BC\. After nearly a decade working in engagement, he moved to a new Quality team at FNHA in 2019 as the Manager of Quality Initiatives and Partner Relations\. Here he was able to support efforts like the revision of the BC Health Quality Matrix, BCs Standard on Cultural Safety and Humility and participate on an Indigenous Advisory Committee for Patient Centred Measurement and the Cultural Safety and Humility Complaints Collaborative\. This work has given him the opportunity to explore various opportunities for change across the health system\.

Mark continues to be motivated to pursue a career where he can be part of positive change in First Nations and Indigenous communities\. He has been an effective facilitator, relationship builder and successful at finding the commonalities between the different realities faced by First Nations and non\-Indigenous health leaders/providers\.

Currently Mark lives on Tk’emlúps te Secwépemc \(Kamloops\) with his wife and two kids\. He likes to keep active and enjoys a good friendly competition for time to time\.

## **Description** 

*Written by Derek K Thompson – Čaabať Bookwilla \| Suhiltun , Director, Indigenous Engagement*

For many years, health\-care organizations, educational institutions and health professionals across British Columbia have been learning about cultural safety, cultural humility and Indigenous\-specific racism\. Reports have been written, recommendations have been made, courses have been developed and commitments have been announced\. Yet an important question remains:  **What should all of this learning actually change?** In this important conversation, I am honoured to welcome **Mark Matthew**, a member of the [Simpcw First Nation](https://simpcw.com/) and Director, Indigenous Health with Health Quality BC, to explore [*A Refreshed Approach: Framework for Effective Cultural Safety and Humility and Indigenous\-Specific Anti\-Racism Education and Training*](https://healthqualitybc.ca/resources/a-refreshed-approach-framework-for-effective-cultural-safety-and-humility-and-indigenous-specific-anti-racism-education-and-training/)\. Together, we will consider how education can move beyond awareness and understanding toward meaningful changes in behaviour, relationships, organizations and health systems\.

The conversation will invite us to examine the distance between **knowing and doing**\. Knowing about colonialism is important, but knowledge alone does not dismantle racism\. Knowing the history of Indian residential schools does not automatically create culturally safe health care\. And knowing the language of reconciliation does not necessarily change the experience of an Indigenous person entering a hospital, clinic or health\-care relationship\. Cultural safety and humility require continuing reflection on assumptions, biases, relationships and power, while Indigenous\-Specific Anti\-Racism requires us to recognize and confront racism not only between individuals, but within policies, practices, institutional cultures and systems\. *A Refreshed Approach* challenges us to ask whether our education is actually changing how we behave, how organizations exercise responsibility and, most importantly, how Indigenous peoples experience health care\.

The **UBC Faculty of Medicine Indigenous Speakers Series** provides an important place for this conversation\. Grounded in Indigenous voices, perspectives and oral traditions, the Series creates space for truth\-telling, learning, dialogue and the advancement of reconciliation\. Our conversation with Mark will ask us to move beyond the question, **“What have we learned?”** toward more difficult questions: **What are we doing differently because of what we have learned? How are Indigenous peoples experiencing that difference? And how will we know that meaningful change has occurred?** Because ultimately, the measure of Indigenous\-Specific Anti\-Racism education cannot simply be how many people have completed the training\. **The measure must be whether Indigenous peoples experience something different because they did\.**

### What Will I Learn?

By the end of this session, participants will be able to reflect on the principles and purpose of *A Refreshed Approach: Framework for Effective Cultural Safety and Humility and Indigenous\-Specific Anti\-Racism Education and Training*; explore the distinction between awareness, learning, behavioural change and systemic transformation; and identify individual and organizational responsibilities for translating Cultural Safety and Humility and Indigenous\-Specific Anti\-Racism education into meaningful, measurable and sustained improvements in the experiences of Indigenous peoples within health care\.
