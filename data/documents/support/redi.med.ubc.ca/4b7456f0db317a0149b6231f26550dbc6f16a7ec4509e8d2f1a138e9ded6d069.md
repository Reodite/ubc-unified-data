## Speaker Bio

[Angela Paul– Qwenot, MBA Executive Director, Éyameth’ Health Centre, Sts’ailes](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/image0-edited.jpeg)

**Angela Paul– *Qwenot*, MBA  
Executive Director, Éyameth’ Health Centre, Sts’ailes**

Angela Paul carries the ancestral name **Qwenot**\. Her late mother was from Sḵwxwú7mesh and her late father is from Sts’ailes, and she is a proud mother and grandmother to Tsleil\-Waututh children and grandchildren\.

Angela is a Coast Salish weaver, scholar, and Indigenous health leader whose work is grounded in an understanding that health and wellness are deeply connected to culture, land, relationships, identity, and ancestral knowledge\. She holds an MBA in Indigenous Business Leadership from Simon Fraser University and is currently pursuing her PhD in Arts Education\.

For her MBA capstone, Angela designed and created a 10\-foot **Weaving Governance** panel as part of her research into traditional laws of the land and Coast Salish weavings as **Holders of Knowledge**\. Her weaving practice continues to inform her approach to leadership: bringing together people, knowledge systems, partnerships, and ways of knowing with integrity and responsibility\.

Today, Angela is **Weaving Wellness** through her leadership with **Éyameth’ Health Centre** in Harrison Mills, British Columbia\. She has been instrumental in championing the development of this innovative, Nations\-led primary care initiative and its vision for transforming health and wellness by weaving together Indigenous knowledge and traditional approaches with Western medicine and contemporary primary care\.

Through Éyameth’, Angela is helping advance a community\-driven model of care rooted in relationships, cultural safety, collaboration, and the understanding that meaningful transformation in Indigenous health requires more than improving access to services\. It requires creating space for Indigenous knowledge, leadership, and community priorities to shape the health system itself\.

Across her work as a weaver, researcher, leader, mother, and grandmother, Angela carries a commitment to creating healthier pathways for the generations that follow\.

## **Description** 

*Written by Derek K Thompson – Čaabať Bookwilla \| Suhiltun , Director, Indigenous Engagement*

**We are excited to announce that this event will be the first\-ever virtual session hosted in a BC First Nations community\!** I will be on\-site at the Éyameth’ Health Centre in Sts’ailes territory for this special session, and have the incredible opportunity to engage in a meaningful conversation with *Qwenot* – Angela as she also provides a virtual tour of the health centre and staff\.

For generations, First Nations peoples have understood that health is about far more than the treatment of illness\. Health lives within our relationships — with family, community, culture, language, land, ceremony, identity and the generations who came before us\. Long before hospitals, clinics and contemporary health systems existed on these lands, Indigenous peoples possessed sophisticated systems of knowledge about what it meant to care for one another and what was required for individuals, families and communities to live well\. Colonization attempted to disrupt these systems, but it did not erase them\.

I am honoured to welcome ***Qwenot* – Angela Paul**, Executive Director of the [Éyameth’ Health Centre](https://eyameth.com/), for a conversation about what becomes possible when Indigenous peoples are not simply invited into existing health\-care systems, but are empowered to imagine, lead and create health systems grounded in Indigenous knowledge, relationships and understandings of wellness\. Éyameth’ means *weaving health together* in the Halq’eméylem language, and the newly opened [Sts’ailes](https://www.stsailes.com/)\-led Health Centre represents the culmination of decades of vision, determination, community leadership and relationships\.

For Angela, however, Éyameth’ is more than a building\. Its story reaches across generations\. As a teenager, she heard her father speak about restoring health and wellness and returning to the knowledge systems that have always helped Indigenous peoples remain well, whole and healthy\. Decades later, standing within the completed Éyameth’ Health Centre, Angela witnessed that vision become something tangible — a place where contemporary medicine, Indigenous knowledge, traditional wellness and healing, primary care, mental health, Elder care and community relationships can exist together\. The Centre asks us to consider a profound question: **what might health care become when Indigenous peoples are able to design it according to their own knowledge, values, responsibilities and aspirations?**

The story of Éyameth’ reminds us that reconciliation is not only about looking backward at what has been taken away\. It is also about looking forward toward what Indigenous peoples are rebuilding, reclaiming and imagining for themselves\. Like the strands of a Coast Salish weaving, those possibilities become stronger when they are brought together with care, purpose and responsibility\. And perhaps that is one of the most important lessons Angela Paul and Éyameth’ offer us: **the future of Indigenous health will not simply be found within the systems we have inherited — it will be woven from the knowledge, relationships, courage and dreams we choose to carry forward together\.**

### What Will I Learn?

By the end of this session, participants will be able to reflect on the significance of First Nations\-led health care and Indigenous self\-determination in transforming health systems; explore how culture, identity, traditional knowledge, relationships and community leadership contribute to wholistic wellness; and consider how the principle of *Éyameth’ — weaving health together* can inform our collective responsibilities toward cultural safety, reconciliation and a more equitable future for health care\.
