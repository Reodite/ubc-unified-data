# Best Practices for Managing Power Dynamics in Deliberation in Hiring Committees

## Why Is This Important?

Being able to manage the influence of power dynamics on decision\-making is an essential skill in hiring committees\. Within any committee, members may hold different levels of power based on factors such as their roles, communication styles, lived experiences, and length of service at UBC or in Canada\.

For example, one committee member may be responsible for evaluating another member, which can raise concerns about retaliation or discomfort in expressing disagreement\. Power dynamics can also limit open communication and reduce the diversity of perspectives needed for strong, fair decisions\.

Using inclusive chairing practices and intentionally structured discussions can help reduce the influence of power imbalances and support better decision\-making\.

---

## Acknowledge and Reframe Differences in Power During Initial Meetings

### Best Practices

-   Create a collegial, warm environment where diverse perspectives are framed as strengths\.
-   Remind committee members that they represent the interests of the department, faculty, and university as a whole\.
-   Use facilitation techniques that support participation, such as:
    -   Giving people time to think before responding
    -   Inviting input directly \(e\.g\., “What are your thoughts?”\)
-   Open meetings with ice\-breaker activities that help build rapport beyond committee roles\.
-   Ensure all members are properly introduced and have their names correctly pronounced\.

---

## Manage Logistics So Everyone Can Participate in Decisions

### Best Practices

-   Communicate key dates early so members can prepare adequately for each meeting\.
-   Provide a clear timeline or forecast of each stage of the hiring process\.
-   Schedule meetings at times that accommodate as many members as possible\.
-   Share a clear agenda for each meeting and adhere to it\.
-   Discourage avoidable absences during key decision\-making periods\.
-   Ensure sufficient time for reviewing applications, particularly when members have significant teaching or workload demands\.

---

## Create Multiple, High\-Quality Channels for Input

### Best Practices

-   In larger committees, use online surveys or shared documents to allow all members to contribute input on candidates\.
-   Structure the process so members independently review applications before group discussion\.
-   Use standardized rubrics, then share results with the full committee before deliberation meetings\.
-   Encourage reflection on participation levels to ensure balanced contribution:
    -   Avoid over\-contributing or under\-contributing
    -   Support equitable engagement across members
-   Actively invite counter\-perspectives to ensure diverse viewpoints are considered\.

---

## Create Supports to Ensure All Voices Are Heard

### Best Practices

-   Pair students, junior faculty, or less\-experienced members with a buddy who can help them navigate the process and expectations\.
-   Assign an equity advisor to:
    -   Support inclusive practices
    -   Help ensure accountability in decision\-making
    -   Raise equity\-related concerns during the search process
-   Encourage a shared responsibility for equity by:
    -   Recognizing it as a collective effort
    -   Encouraging members to amplify voices from historically marginalized groups or those with less power
