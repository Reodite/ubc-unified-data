# Best Practices to Avoid the “Minority Tax” on New Colleagues from HSPM Groups

## Why Is This Important?

It is important that newly hired colleagues who are members of historically, systemically, and persistently marginalized \(HSPM\) groups find their new working environment engaging, supportive, and welcoming\.

When challenges arise, colleagues from HSPM groups often find themselves doing the emotional labour of helping others understand and uphold equity, diversity, and inclusion principles\. This additional burden, often referred to as the **“minority tax,”** can contribute to burnout and lower retention rates, particularly for individuals hired through preferential or restricted hiring initiatives\.

A supportive onboarding experience can help new team members feel valued, connected, and positioned for success\.

---

## Is Your Team Ready to Welcome New Colleagues and Help Them Thrive?

### Be Proactive and Create a Soft Landing

The first week should not feel overwhelming\. It should balance excitement, relationship\-building, settling in, and accomplishing achievable early successes, even if they are small\.

#### Best Practices

-   Take time to get to know the person\.
-   Do not assume they will want to participate in equity, diversity, and inclusion committees or initiatives\.
-   Ask about their career goals and aspirations\.
-   Discuss accessibility requirements and support needs\.
-   Treat onboarding as an ongoing process rather than a one\-time event\.
-   Be prepared to adapt onboarding plans based on feedback, conversations, and lessons learned\.

---

## Provide Mentoring and Community

Creating opportunities for connection and support can help new team members feel welcomed and reduce feelings of isolation\.

#### Best Practices

-   Connect new hires with relevant affinity groups across the university\.
-   Establish a buddy system within the team or unit\.
-   Create mentoring opportunities that emphasize active listening and relationship\-building\.
-   Foster environments where new colleagues feel heard, supported, and able to seek guidance when needed\.

#### Why This Matters

Mentorship and community\-building can strengthen retention by helping new team members develop a sense of belonging and connection\.

---

## Invest in Ongoing Learning and Growth for the Whole Team

Creating an inclusive workplace is a shared responsibility\. Existing team members should continue developing their knowledge and skills before and after a new colleague joins the team\.

#### Best Practices

-   Invest in ongoing learning about supporting colleagues from HSPM groups\.
-   Learn evidence\-informed practices for creating inclusive and welcoming work environments\.
-   Encourage upstander training and skill development\.
-   Build confidence in responding to exclusionary, biased, or inappropriate comments and behaviours\.
-   Promote healthy approaches to conflict that strengthen relationships and team cohesion\.
-   Build understanding and support for the rationale and goals of restricted or preferential hiring initiatives\.
-   Ensure managers and team members understand relevant UBC policies, procedures, and resources that support equity, diversity, and inclusion\.

---

## Support New Colleagues to Thrive

Managers and leaders play an important role in helping newly hired HSPM team members navigate workplace culture and build a sense of belonging\.

#### Best Practices

-   Advocate for new team members when needed\.
-   Foster an environment of inclusion and belonging\.
-   Recognize and celebrate contributions and achievements\.
-   Help remove barriers to participation and success\.
-   Create opportunities for professional growth and advancement\.

#### Why This Matters

When people feel supported, valued, and included, they are more likely to thrive, contribute meaningfully, and remain engaged within the organization\.
