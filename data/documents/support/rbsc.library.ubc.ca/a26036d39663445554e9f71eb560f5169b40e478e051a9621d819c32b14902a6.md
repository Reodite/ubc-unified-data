“Words are inadequate to express the joy that thrilled my heart upon the reception of the long looked\-for letter from my sweet little lady love,” wrote Kate Edmonds, in January 1884\. “When I love you so dearly—just a line penned by your fair hand will ever interest me \[…\] For I assure you, my mad, wild, passionate love for you is no jesting matter\.” Addressed to Kate’s “own precious darling,” Miss Mattie A\. Pickens, this letter provides a window into a rather intimate exchange between two young women nearly 150 years ago\.

[https://library\-rbsc\-2017\.sites\.olt\.ubc\.ca/files/2026/07/RBSC\-ARC\-1825\-01\-19\-AR\-06\-scaled\.jpg](https://library-rbsc-2017.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/RBSC-ARC-1825-01-19-AR-06-scaled.jpg)[A t\-shirt from the 1990s declaring “If I can't dance I don't want to be part of your revolution”\.](https://library-rbsc-2017.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/RBSC-ARC-1825-01-19-AR-06-227x300.jpg)

T\-shirt\. Lesbian collection\. RBSC\-ARC\-1825\-01\-19\-AR\-06

Kate Edmonds’ letter to Mattie Pickens is just one of many records left behind by women who loved women that can be found in the [Lesbian Collection at Rare Books and Special Collections](https://rbscarchives.library.ubc.ca/lesbian-collection)\. RBSC is thrilled to announce these and many more new acquisitions to the Lesbian Collection, made available to the public just in time for Vancouver’s Pride Week\. From letters to photographs, from postcards to sheet music, the materials in the Lesbian Collection span nearly four centuries, encompassing a wide array of subjects\. The collection takes an expansive view of gender and sexuality, pulling together materials that resonate with queer experiences, aesthetics, and identities through the ages\.

The Lesbian Collection speaks to a sense of community, seeking to document queer lives across centuries\. A t\-shirt from the 1990s declaring “If I can’t dance I don’t want to be part of your revolution” finds its match in dance cards from a 1919 “Lesbian Reception\.” A cryptic postcard asks, “If blue were red and red were blue, and you were I and I were you, and this world held but just us two, and you knew, nobody knew\. Would you?” These objects and thousands more allow for a glimpse into queer cultures and signifiers of the past, their authors’ voices echoing forward into our present\.

\[Would you?\]\. Lesbian collection\. RBSC\-ARC\-1825\-08\-06\-07

While the bulk of the material in the Lesbian Collection comes from everyday women, there are a handful of more recognizable names and faces\. The collection includes over 150 letters belonging to British suffragette Mary Blathwayt \(1879\-1961\) and her family, as well as letters written by the American actress Charlotte Cushman \(1816\-1876\)\. Eleanor Roosevelt makes an appearance photographed alongside dancer Mayris Chaney, and Sarah Bernhardt stares out from a pair of postcards\. These postcards are just two out of over 1,300 in the collection newly available for research\. The subjects of these postcards include early drag kings and queens, scenes from plays and operas, holiday and celebratory wishes exchanged between friends and lovers, coded messages, early 20th century depictions of sapphic relationships, and many more\.

Together, these records tell a story of queer existence, resistance, love, celebration, solidarity, and community\. All too frequently relegated to the margins of archival materials, love between women takes centre stage in the Lesbian Collection\.
