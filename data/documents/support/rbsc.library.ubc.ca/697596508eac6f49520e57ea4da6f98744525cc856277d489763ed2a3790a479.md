## [The R\. Mathison Printing Collection](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/05/16/the-r-mathison-printing-collection/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on May 16, 2011

As extensive as the Chung Collection is, there are a number of related collections here at UBC that are complimentary\- this is part of what makes the Chung Collection such a great fit here at UBC\. One modest but important example is the [R\. Mathison Printing Collection](http://digitalcollections.library.ubc.ca/cdm4/index_mathison.php?CISOROOT=/mathison)\. Acquired in 2009 by the [Rare Books and Special Collections](http://www.library.ubc.ca/spcoll/) division of the library, these 54 ephemeral items were all printed by R\. Mathison Jr\., a job printer who operated in Vancouver from ca\. 1886 to 1890\.

There are many examples of early B\.C\. printing in the Chung Collection as well, but a few items in the R\. Mathison Printing Collection are also relevant to the study of [Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/rm_1_39.jpg)Chinese\-Canadian history\. For example the item to the left is an advertising card for a Chinese business called the Hop Yick Drug Store\.  In addition to selling tea, sugar, rice, nut oil and Chinese merchandise, it advertises that it is “prepared to contract for the supply of first\-class Chinese labor to any extent on public or private works, at lowest current rates\.”

There are also two examples of laundry advertising cards: one Chinese \(Tong Yuen Laundry\) and one white \(Pacific Steam Laundry\)\. Note that the Pacific Steam Laundry advertises that they employ white labour only:

[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/rm_1_47.jpg)

Both laundries were on Dupont Street \(now Pende[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/rm_1_40.jpg)r Street\)\- could they have been each other’s competition?

The R\. Mathison Printing collection is completely digitized and is available through [UBC Library Digital Collections and Services](http://digitalcollections.library.ubc.ca/cdm4/index_mathison.php?CISOROOT=/mathison)\.

## [Student research on display](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/05/04/student-research-on-display/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on May 4, 2011

[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/cap_posters1.jpg)We are very excited to be displaying posters created by students in the [Coordinated Arts Program](http://cap.arts.ubc.ca/), just outside the Chung Collection exhibition room\. These students were part of the Law and Society stream of CAP and came to the Chung Collection in January for a tour and an introduction to archival research\. The students were all assigned to choose an item from the Chung Collection and use it to inspire a poster presentation, an essay or a video\.  They presented their posters at the [CAP conference in April](http://cap.arts.ubc.ca/program/cap-conference-201011.html), where several of the students met Dr\. Chung who came to see their posters\. We have selected a number of the posters for this display and the variety of topics is very interesting:

-   Chinese\-Aboriginal relations in B\.C\.
-   The history of drug law enforcement in Vancouver
-   Civil rights history in Canada
-   Funeral and death rites in different cultures
-   Traditional Chinese medicine
-   Chinese in B\.C\. during the gold rush
-   Relationship between the Japanese and Chinese communities
-   The history of interracial marriage

Congratulations to the CAP students for their very interesting research\! We hope that you found your experience using the Chung Collection to be a rewarding one\.

We will keep the posters on display in the reading room until May 20th\. They can be viewed during our regular summer opening hours \(Monday to Friday, 9\-5\)\.

## [We make mistakes; you can help us fix them\!](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/05/03/we-make-mistakes-you-can-help-us-fix-them/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on May 3, 2011

The cataloguing of the Chung Collection has been done over a number of years, by a number of very hard working professionals and student assistants\. However, mistakes do happen, and while we endeavor to make the records as accurate as we can, we are always happy to hear from an expert who can help us make them even better\. Here are a couple of recent examples:

We had thought that [this photograph](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/collection/details/8921) was of Chin Shee, one of Yip Sang’s wives who moved to be with him here in Vancouver, but a researcher informed us that it is actually Wong Shee, another of Yip Sang’s wives\. The researcher was able to confirm this with descendants of Yip Sang\. If you see a correction like this that needs to be made, you can contact us at chung\.collection@ubc\.ca\.

Another way to communicate with us is through the digital collections, where you can leave a comment about each item\. For example, [this photograph](http://digitalcollections.library.ubc.ca/u?/coll0803-7,419) of the Kuo Min Tang was recently commented on by a researcher, pointing out that we had the photographer wrong\- we had catalogued it as W\.H\. Wand, which the researcher very logically thought should have actually been the well\-known Chinese Canadian photographer C\.B\. Wand\. On closer inspection of the photograph, the typo turned out to be in the last name, not the first\- the photographer is William Henry Wills, who was active in the Kelowna area in the first quarter of the 1900’s\.

Thanks to our researchers for their help and expertise, and if you have information about an item in the Chung Collection, we’re always happy to hear from you\!

## [UBC students getting ready to vote](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/04/27/ubc-students-getting-ready-to-vote/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on April 27, 2011

[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/jung.jpg)A few months back we had the pleasure of [hosting students](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/news/coordinated-arts-program-visit-chung-collection) from the [Coordinated Arts Program](http://cap.arts.ubc.ca/) Law and Society stream for a tour of the Chung Collection and an introduction to researching with primary sources\. As we were discussing the civil rights of Chinese Canadians \(and how the right to vote was taken away from Chinese Canadians in 1872\) it struck me\- these students being mostly 18 or 19 years old now were not old enough to vote in any past provincial and federal elections\. The right to vote suddenly became even more important to emphasize \(and as their professor told them, “political apathy is not allowed in this class\!”\)\.

UBC students are taking their right to vote to heart and the proof is in a [Youtube video of a “vote mob”](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4o6tRuFp9NY&amp;feature=player_embedded) held on campus\. Vote mobs are being held on campuses across the country after comedian Rick Mercer encouraged Canada’s youth to shock the political parties by actually showing up and casting their ballots\.

Chinese Canadians finally won back the right to vote in this country in 1947\. Learn more in the Chung Collection by searching under the subject heading [Chinese–Civil rights](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/advanced/results/as_subject/Chinese--Civil%20rights/subject/767) or using keywords such as [“vote”](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/simple/results/term/vote) or [“voting”](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/simple/results/term/voting)\.
