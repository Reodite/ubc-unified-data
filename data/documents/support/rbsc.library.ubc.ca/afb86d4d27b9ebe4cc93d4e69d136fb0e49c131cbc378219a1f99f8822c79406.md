## [Happy 125th birthday Vancouver\!](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/04/06/happy-125th-birthday-vancouver/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on April 6, 2011

Happy birthday Vancouver\! Today, April 6 2011, marks the 125\[th\] anniversary of the incorporation of the City of Vancouver\.

[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/village.jpg)Another birthday celebration in Vancouver’s past was the Golden Jubilee celebrations in 1936\.  The local Chinese Canadian community was very spirited, creating a “Chinese village” with a big, beautifully constructed bamboo arch, located at the corner of Pender and Carrell\.  The Chinese village was accompanied by a souvenir pamphlet produced by Quene Yip \(son of the “unofficial mayor” of Chinatown [Yip Sang](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/collection-themes/immigration-and-settlement/yip-family-and-yip-sang-company)\) called “Vancouver Chinatown\.” These were concerted [Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/arch.jpg)efforts on the part of Chinese Canadians to “open up” their community to the rest of the city and to tourists, trying to break down the barriers of fear and misunderstanding that existed in the early years of Vancouver\.  A letter from his sister Susan Yip praised Quene’s efforts:

“It was an excellent idea to have composed such a booklet, giving the foreign visitors an insight into “Vancouver Chinatown”… for the right kind of publicity is very necessary for the Chinese and China nowadays in this world of international relationship\!”

[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/susanletter.jpg)

Search the Chung Collection for the keywords [“Golden Jubilee”](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/simple/results/term/golden%20jubilee) or [“Chinese Village”](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/simple/results/term/chinese%20village) for records of these events\.

Vancouver 125 links:

Our friends at the City of Vancouver Archives have summarized the activities of the [Golden Jubilee Society](http://www.vancouverarchives.ca/2011/03/vancouver-golden-jubilee-society-records/) in 1936\.

The [Vancouver Sun](http://www.vancouversun.com/news/vancouver-125/index.html) has excellent coverage today of Vancouver 125 events\.

Don’t forget to vote for the places in Vancouver that matter to you with the [Vancouver Heritage Foundation](http://www.vancouverheritagefoundation.org/projects/placesthatmatter.html) \(voting closes at midnight tonight\!\)\.

And of course, check out the [City of Vancouver anniversary site](http://vancouver.ca/commsvcs/cultural/celebrate125/index.htm) for complete listings of events and activities\.

## [Dr\. Chung on model ship restoration, and the importance of hobbies](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/04/05/dr-chung-on-model-ship-restoration-and-the-importance-of-hobbies/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on April 5, 2011

[Click here](http://blogs.ubc.ca/libnews/files/2011/04/4-1-MONTE-Artifacts_Antiques-2.pdf) to read an article in the Spring 2011 Montecristo Magazine in which Dr\. Chung talks about the importance of having a hobby:

## [Chinese Canadian legal history](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/03/31/chinese-canadian-legal-history/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on March 31, 2011

[“Road to Justice”](http://www.roadtojustice.ca/) is a new web resource outlining the early legal history of Chinese Canadians\. Presented by the Metro Toronto Chinese &amp; Southeast Asian Legal Clinic with funding from the Community Historical Recognition Program, the website gives background on all aspects of Chinese Canadian legal history including laws and legislation, key court cases, the first Chinese Canadian lawyers and the redress campaign\.  The site includes text, case study documents, video interviews, and a graphical timeline which is a great teaching resource\.

Related material in the Chung Collection includes material related to [Dock Yip](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/category/chung/page/12/search/collection/advanced/results/as_term/any%7Cdock%20yip%7CAND), the first Chinese Canadian lawyer called to the bar\. Also try searching under subject headings such as [Chinese–capture and imprisonment](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/category/chung/page/12/search/collection/advanced/results/as_subject/Chinese--capture%20%2526%20imprisonment/subject/759), [Law &amp; legal affairs](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/category/chung/page/12/search/collection/advanced/results/as_subject/Law%20%2526%20legal%20affairs/subject/766), and [Emigration &amp; Immigration](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/category/chung/page/12/search/collection/advanced/results/as_subject/Emigration%20%2526%20immigration/subject/277)\.  Researchers of this topic should also be sure to consult the finding aid for the [John Keenlyside Legal Research Collection](http://www.library.ubc.ca/spcoll/AZ/PDF/KL/Keenlyside_John.pdf), which like the Chung Collection, is held in the [Rare Books and Special Collections](http://www.library.ubc.ca/spcoll/welcome.html) division of the UBC Library\.

## [Cantonese opera in the Chung Collection and at MOA](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/03/21/cantonese-opera-in-the-chung-collection-and-at-moa/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on March 21, 2011

[Cantonese Opera troupe](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/opera.jpg)

A favourite stop in the Chung Collection exhibition is always the [photograph of the Cantonese Opera troupe](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/collection/details/8912) in case 5\. It is a beautifully detailed photograph, taken by Cecil B\. Wand in 1923\.

Those who are looking for more on Cantonese Opera must also stop at the [UBC Museum of Anthropology](http://moa.ubc.ca/), where they have a collection of over 400 Cantonese opera costumes, props and instruments\. For reasons unknown, travelling troupes sometimes left costumes and other items behind in Vancouver with the Jin Wah Sing Musical Association, who stored the costumes until they were acquired by the Museum of Anthropology\.

Do not despair if you cannot make it to campus\- through the MOA website you can view high quality photographs of all of these objects\. Visit the [MOA Collection Online](http://moa.ubc.ca/collection-online/) and type “Cantonese opera” as your search terms and this is what you will see:

[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/moasearchresults.JPG)

You can click on each item and zoom  in to see the amazing detail that went into these costumes:

[Image](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/moazoom.JPG)

Information on visiting MOA is available on their [website](http://moa.ubc.ca/visitor/)\. It’s definitely worth a trip\!

## [Archives of the Yip family and company](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/03/16/archives-of-the-yip-family-and-company/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on March 16, 2011

[Yip Sang with four of his children](http://library.ubc.ca/spcoll/images/cc/yipfamily.jpg)I was recently asked where all of the archival material related to the Yip Family and the Wing Sang Company \(later the Yip Sang Company\) is kept\. Because Yip Sang and his family were so prominent in the early days of Vancouver’s Chinese\-Canadian community, they are now of great interest to researchers\. It seems like a good time to highlight the various places where this archival material can be found:

The two main institutions with archives originating from the Yip family are here in the Chung Collection at UBC Library and also at the [City of Vancouver Archives](http://vancouver.ca/ctyclerk/archives/index.htm)\.  In the Chung Collection, we have a number of family photographs and other family documents, such as correspondence between the siblings, Christmas cards, wedding and party invitations; personal documents of some of the siblings and their various career and personal interests; and extensive business records of the Wing Sang/Yip Sang Company, including records of steamship ticket sales, and records related to their exporting and cannery businesses\. We also have a number of artifacts from the Wing Sang building, mostly related to business, such as clipboards, a lantern and rubber stamps\.

At the City of Vancouver Archives, there are similar documents, including family photographs and business records\. What makes the material at the City of Vancouver really special are more than 600 undelivered letters sent to and from Chinese immigrants and Chinese family and friends in China\. The Yip family acted as unofficial postmasters for the Chinese\-Canadian community, because letters addressed with Chinese characters could not be delivered by the Canadian postal system\. The undelivered letters in the fonds are written in older style Chinese, dating from ca\. 1903 to 1919\.  Through a joint digitization project between UBC Library and the City of Vancouver Archives, these letters are now digitized and [available online](http://digitalcollections.library.ubc.ca/cdm4/search.php?CISOROOT=/yipsang)\.

Finally, there are over 300 artifacts related to the Yip family at the [Museum of Vancouver](http://www.museumofvancouver.ca/index.php)\. You can see some of their [Chinatown artifacts](http://www.museumofvancouver.ca/collections.php?tag=6) online, and see some artifacts in person in their Gateway to the Pacific exhibition\.

How did the archives of one family end up in two institutions? When the Wing Sang building renovations were started in the 1980’s, one family member offered some of the historical material to Dr\. Chung, and another family member sent other material to the City of Vancouver Archives \(you can read the really interesting story of their salvage operation in the Wing Sang building on their [website](http://vancouver.ca/ctyclerk/archives/digitized/Yip_Sang/index.htm)\)\. The good news is, both halves of the collection are now in public institutions, freely available to any researcher who wishes to view them\! You can even search both the digitized material from Chung Collection and the undelivered Yip letters through our joint project [The Chinese Experience in B\.C\. 1850\-1950](http://www.library.ubc.ca/chineseinbc/index.html)\.

You can read more about the Yip family through the [Chung Collection](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/collection-themes/immigration-and-settlement/yip-family-and-yip-sang-company), and also [browse](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/advanced/results/as_term/category%7CThe%20Yip%20family%20and%20Yip%20Sang%20Company) all of the Yip family and company items in the collection; you can also access online a [finding aid](http://vancouver.ca/ArchivesSearch/Results.aspx?BU=http://vancouver.ca/ctyclerk/archives/search/advsearch.aspx&amp;QB0=AND&amp;QF0=PRI%20REC%20NO&amp;QI0=Add.MSS%201108&amp;TN=records&amp;RN=0&amp;RF=WebHierarchy&amp;DF=WebFullImages&amp;RL=0&amp;DL=0&amp;NP=255&amp;ID=&amp;MF=GENERICENGWPMSG.INI&amp;MQ=&amp;TI=0&amp;AC=QBE_QUERY) to the collection at the City of Vancouver Archives\.

## [This day in Vancouver: Greenhill Park explosion](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/03/07/this-day-in-vancouver-greenhill-park-explosion/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on March 7, 2011

Thanks to [The Dependent Magazine](http://thedependent.ca/news-and-opinion/history/this-day-in-vancouver-march-6th/) for the reminder that yesterday was the anniversary of the Greenhill Park explosion in Vancouver Harbour on March 6, 1945\.  The S\.S\. Greenhill Park was a freighter and the explosion, initially blamed on improper storage of combustible materials, killed 8 longshoremen and injured 19 other workers\.

Sources much later told late\-Vancouver historian [Chuck Davis](http://www.vancouverhistory.ca/), the explosion was to be blamed on a deadly combination of sodium chlorate, stowed whiskey, and a match struck beneath the deck\. You can read about this story on the [History of Metropolitan Vancouver](http://www.vancouverhistory.ca/archives_greenhill_park.htm)\.

In the Chung Collection, we have archival material which originated with Hugh Robinson, who was on the board of inquiry for the explosion\. The material includes inquiry proceedings, correspondence, notes, clippings, and mechanical drawings of the ship\. They can be found in the database by searching for [“Green Hill Park”](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/simple/results/term/%22Green%20Hill%20Park%22) or for [“Robinson, Hugh\.”](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/simple/results/term/%22Robinson%2C%20Hugh%22)

## [Improved search function](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/02/24/improved-search-function/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on February 24, 2011

The helpful folks in our Systems department at UBC Library have made a significant upgrade to the search functionality of the Chung Collection website: you can now choose to search only for items which have an online version currently available\. This is a great option for those who are using the collection from away and are unable to come and see the items in person \(or who prefer to do research at home in their pyjamas\)\.

From the Quick Search box on the [home page](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/), the [Search page](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/simple) or the [Advanced Search page](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/advanced) just choose “Online” in the new “Availability” box\. The database will then retrieve records for items with an online version when you perform your search\. To see the online version, click on the thumbnail view which appears in the record for each item\.

## [“Lost and Found” at the Japanese Canadian National Museum](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/02/21/lost-and-found-at-the-japanese-canadian-national-museum/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on February 21, 2011

The Japanese Canadian National Museum has opened an exhibition of photographs called “Lost and Found\.” They hope that visitors to the exhibition will recognize people or landmarks in the photographs and contribute this knowledge by leaving a note on pages posted for this purpose beside the photographs\.  To learn more about the exhibition and for location and hours, [click here](http://www.jcnm.ca/2011/02/12/lost-found-2)\.

This is a challenge in many archival and museum collections, and the Chung Collection is no exception\- for example, try searching for [portraits of Chinese men](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/advanced/results/as_term/title%7CPortrait%20Chinese%20Man%7CAND/as_media_type/8/media_type/8) or [women](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/search/collection/advanced/results/as_term/title%7CPortrait%20Chinese%20woman%7CAND/as_media_type/8/media_type/8) and you’ll see that we lack identifying information for many of them\. If you recognize someone or have other information to share, you can always leave a comment in the Digital Collection version of the photograph\. To get to the full digital version, click on the thumbnail view\.

## [Digitization and translation funding announced for collection at UVic](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/02/08/digitization-and-translation-funding-announced-for-collection-at-uvic/)

Congratulations to our colleagues at the University of Victoria Archives\-  a gift of $26,000 from the Victoria Foundation has been announced to digitize and translate the archives of the Chinese Consolidated Benevolent Association\.  Since many of the documents in this archival collection are in traditional Chinese script, this funding will make the material more accesible to scholars and students who cannot read the script\.

## [Coordinated Arts Program visit to the Chung Collection](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/2011/02/04/coordinated-arts-program-visit-to-the-chung-collection/)

By [sromkey](https://rbsc.library.ubc.ca/author/sromkey/ "Posts by sromkey") on February 4, 2011

In January, the Rare Books and Special Collections and the Chung Collection hosted almost 100 first year undergraduate students from the [Coordinated Arts Program](http://cap.arts.ubc.ca/)\. Students in CAP choose one of four themes and take core classes tailored to that theme\.

Students from the [Law and Society theme](http://cap.arts.ubc.ca/program/cap-themes-2010w/cap-law-and-society.html) are studying early immigration, racism and community in early Vancouver, and came for two sessions: a tour of the Chung Collection exhibition space, and a session to talk about doing research in an archival environment\. They will be using documents and artifacts from the Chung Collection for their research project this semester\.

Students from the [Individual and Society theme](http://cap.arts.ubc.ca/program/cap-themes-2010w/cap-individual-and-society.html) read Joy Kogawa’s novel *Obasan* so they came primarily to see the [archives of Joy Kogawa](http://www.library.ubc.ca/spcoll/AZ/PDF/KL/Kogawa_Joy.pdf), which are held in [Rare Books and Special Collections](http://www.library.ubc.ca/spcoll/)\. They also viewed documents from the Japanese Canadian Research Collection and a [sign from an internment camp](http://chung.library.ubc.ca/collection/details/6026) for Japanese\-Canadians during World War II\.

UBC faculty members who would like to bring their students to see the Chung Collection are encouraged to contact the Rare Books and Special Collections Archivist at sarah\.romkey@ubc\.ca\. It is a great opportunity to introduce students to using archival material for their research\.  

Thanks to Greg Mackie and Kathryn Grafton from the Coordinated Arts Program for coordinating these visits\!
