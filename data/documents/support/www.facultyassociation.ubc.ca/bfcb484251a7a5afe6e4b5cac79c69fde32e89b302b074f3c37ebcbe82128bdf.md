June 25, 2026

[Update on Salary Increases and Lump Sum Payments for Bargaining Unit Members](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/update-on-salary-increases/)

Faculty Relations is working with the Integrated Service Centre \(ISC\),  Human Resources and Dean’s Offices to arrange for all increases for 2025 and 2026 to be processed\.  The 2025 3% General Wage Increase \(GWI\)  and the Labour Market Salary Adjustment \(LMSA\) of 1% for EL Faculty and 1\.5% for Lecturers, will be processed at the same time as the 2026 increases \(GWI, LMSA, Career Progress, etc\)\. FR anticipates these will be processed on the August 15\[th\] paycheque\.

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/update-on-salary-increases/)

April 16, 2026

[Spring Bargaining Update](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/spring-bargaining-update/)

As you’ll remember from our [December 2025 bargaining update](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/december-bargaining-update/) with its FAQ’s, we have been waiting for the provincial public\-sector bargaining landscape to de\-fog, since it took the province until late November of 2025 to settle with the first public unions in the province\. By now, happily, most of the major unions in BC have reached tentative agreements with their employers, including BCGEU, the BCTF, and several others\. And the provincial “mandate”—i\.e\., what the province is willing to fund—is becoming far clearer and has shown itself to be consistent in each settlement\. 

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/spring-bargaining-update/)

December 12, 2025

[December Bargaining Update](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/december-bargaining-update/)

Bargaining is sometimes slow cooking food—worth it in the end, but needing a certain degree of patience\.  This is definitely one of those rounds\.  Normally, we know what the provincial funding for public sector bargaining units is by late spring, usually before our contract term is up\.  This year, however, it was mid\-November, with the BCGEU mediated agreement, before we knew what the provincial “mandate” would be\.  So that delay has definitely slowed down our work toward a tentative agreement at the table\. 

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/december-bargaining-update/)

November 26, 2025

[Workload: Crushing](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/workload-crushing/)

Workload—the increasingly overwhelming onslaught of deadlines, administrivia, course\-preps, projects, supervisions, marking, reporting, writing, meetings, experiments, editing, and endless emails—is becoming close to unbearable for many of us\. For most of us, workload is our top concern and burden\. 

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/workload-crushing/)

November 21, 2025

[Sessional Lecturers: UBC’s Precariat](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/sessional-lecturers-precariat/)

Many academics have spent time on short\-term contingent appointments; by some measures, academia is in fact one of the most highly casualized work sectors in Canada\. This is unhealthy for our institution and even less sustainable for the scholars who fulfill UBC’s public mission with very little in return\. We want to keep improving this situation for all of our sakes\.

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/sessional-lecturers-precariat/)

November 12, 2025

[UBC Lecturers: Leaning In For Better Jobs](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/lecturers-leaning-better/)

UBC’s Lecturers 400\+ are the heroes of the undergraduate curriculum at UBC\.  Without their teaching expertise and important service, UBC’s curricular offerings would be unsustainable\. And yet these academics remain precariously employed and lack many of the rights and benefits of their tenured and tenure\-track colleagues\.

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/lecturers-leaning-better/)

October 22, 2025

[Healthy Mind, Healthy Body: Benefits Bargaining](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/healthy-benefits-bargaining/)

There are three central issues that we have brought to the bargaining table this round related to our extended health benefits plan: \(1\) that any changes to our benefits plan must be approved by the Faculty Association; \(2\) an across\-the\-board increase to the specified coverage limits in our SunLife benefits plan; and \(3\) benefits for all\!

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/healthy-benefits-bargaining/)

[Promotion &amp; Tenure: Fairer Paths](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/promotion-tenure-fairer/)

One key issue for tenure\-track and tenured \(TTF\) colleagues is UBC’s tenure and promotion processes\.  These procedures have significant, sometimes hugely stressful, impact on our TTF members, and we think there are ways to improve them\.

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/promotion-tenure-fairer/)

August 06, 2025

[Librarians and Archivists: Partners in Research &amp; Learning](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/librarians-archivists-partners/)

Many of our bargaining proposals on salaries, benefits, and workload will apply to all of us in the Faculty Association, but we also seek to ensure that protections offered in the Collective Agreement recognize the vitally important work of librarians and archivists within the University, and build in parity with other bargaining unit members whenever possible\. For this round for Librarians and Archivists, we are focused on selection committee processes, recruitment and selection procedures and processes, and clarifying and expanding our criteria for appointments\.

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/librarians-archivists-partners/)

July 17, 2025

[Student Surveys: First, Do No Harm](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/student-surveys-first/)

This will be at least the third round of collective bargaining in which we have beseeched UBC to act like a university, respect the overwhelming scholarly evidence, and stop using student survey data for purposes for which they are manifestly unfit\.  Statistically unreliable, obviously not addressing the question at hand, and riddled with discriminatory, toxic, and uninformed opinion, anonymous student surveys \(SEoT’s, or SEI’s\) are deeply inappropriate for their intended purpose\.

[Read more](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/bargaining/student-surveys-first/)
