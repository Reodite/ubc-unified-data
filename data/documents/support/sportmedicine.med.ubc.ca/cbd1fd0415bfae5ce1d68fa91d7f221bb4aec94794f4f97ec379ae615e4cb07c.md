### **Training and Competence**

The physicians at UBC Sport and Exercise Medicine clinic are highly trained professionals dedicated to sport and exercise medicine\. Each has completed a minimum of 7 years of post\-secondary education, gaining experience across a wide range of disciplines while providing care to patients of all backgrounds\.

Accordingly, each physician on our team is fully qualified to provide care for musculoskeletal issues to all genders, ages, and ethnicities\. Same\-gender chaperones are available upon patient request\.

### **Service Delivery &amp; Urgency**

The UBC Sport and Exercise Medicine clinic is a specialist clinic\. As such, our service delivery and timelines may differ from a non\-specialist office \(such as a GP or walk\-in clinic\)\.

Our clinic follows the standards and guidelines set out by the College of Physicians and Surgeons\. While we acknowledge the importance of every patient’s unique situation, we rely on our medical team to assess the urgency and priority of patient requests from a medical standpoint\.

For acute medical emergencies, patients should always contact their family doctor or visit the emergency room when appropriate\. If you do not have a family doctor, it is advised that you find a walk\-in clinic for urgent concerns\. To find a walk\-in clinic or check wait times, please visit the Medimap webpage: [https://medimap\.ca/clinics/walk\-in\-clinics/bc/vancouver](https://medimap.ca/clinics/walk-in-clinics/bc/vancouver)

As a public clinic within the MSP system, we serve all patients in our community with equality and equity\. The clinic does not allow patients to pay privately to reduce wait times or to be seen faster than other patients\.

# **Our Clinical Operations Model: One\-to\-One Patient to Provider Care**

UBC Sport and Exercise Medicine physicians take personal responsibility for ensuring the health care needs of their own patients are met\. How this is done is determined by the physician individually\. This includes the timeliness of responding to written correspondence, when and how many appointments are made available, and the extent to which patients are discouraged from visiting walk\-in clinics\.

The challenge is that many of these considerations compete for the same limited resource: a physician’s time\. For example:

-   the more time a physician spends responding to written correspondence, the less time they have available for appointments;
-   the more appointment slots reserved for urgent matters, the fewer slots available to book in advance;
-   the more out\-of\-schedule appointments are accommodated, the less likely scheduled appointments are to run on time\.

An individual physician model has certain advantages, such as better continuity of care and higher overall quality of care, since one individual takes personal responsibility for your health needs\. A group physician model also has advantages, such as more convenient appointment times and greater responsiveness, since the workload is shared across a larger pool of physicians\.

We understand this approach may not be preferred by everyone\. Our goal is to do the best we can, to be transparent, to communicate our decisions and reasoning, and to enable patients to decide how they wish to proceed, including, if necessary, switching to another clinic that has adopted more of a group practice model\.

### Equality &amp; Non\-Discrimination

As an organization, we believe in and promote equality and respect for all\. Our physicians, staff, and patients come from a diverse range of backgrounds spanning cultural and ethnic origin, socioeconomic status, sexual orientation, and other aspects of identity\. With this in mind, our clinic must balance our desire and obligation to be non\-discriminatory with reasonable patient preferences related to religious, cultural, or other personal reasons\.

We do so by giving patients the opportunity to request a physician switch **prior** to their intake appointment, but **not afterwards\.** This policy helps ensure the clinic meets its legal, professional, and regulatory requirements, and applying it strictly is necessary to prevent any real or perceived bias\.

## Our **Clinic’s Policy On Switching Doctors**

A question that occasionally arises is whether patients are able to switch between physicians at the UBC Sport and Exercise Medicine Clinic\. As part of our team’s guiding principles, we do not switch patients between physicians\. There are several reasons for this, all relating to ensuring physicians working in a collaborative team environment can deliver the best long\-term patient care possible:

-   maximizing physician availability;
-   ensuring team workload distribution;
-   supporting sound physician decision\-making\.

Additional positive side effects of this policy include:

-   reducing high\-risk patient hand\-overs;
-   promoting long\-term patient\-physician relationships;
-   enabling family\-friendly staffing policies\.

Where appropriate, referrals are made to outside physicians who have specialized in certain medical issues or procedures\.

### **Maximizing Physician Availability to Provide Care**

One of the modern challenges for physicians is the amount of administrative work involved\. For every hour of direct patient care, there is at least another hour of paperwork and other administrative tasks required\. Generally speaking, the proportion of administrative work has been increasing, in line with ever\-increasing legal and regulatory requirements and the growing complexity of care\.

One particularly time\-consuming task for physicians is taking over responsibility for the care of a new patient\. On average, every new patient involves several hours of work, comprising an initial half\-hour meeting, a detailed review of prior medical records, and the construction of an optimized patient medical record\.

This investment of time and resources is entirely wasted in the event of a switch, as the new physician must redo this entire process\. In aggregate, restricting switches between physicians therefore helps maximize physician availability for direct medical care, as opposed to administrative matters\.

### **Preventing the Screening of Patients**

Different patients have different needs and expectations\. In a clinical environment, this can sometimes create an unconscious desire among physicians to screen out certain patients\. As a clinic, we do not believe this is appropriate\. We strongly uphold the professional obligation of physicians to accept patients on a first\-come, first\-served basis\.  
Allowing switches between physicians undermines this principle by creating a loophole that incentivizes subtly steering certain patients toward another physician in the hope that they request a switch\. Restricting switches therefore supports the philosophy that physicians must accept patients as they come, and helps ensure our physicians work hard to provide the best possible care for all of their patients\.

### Supporting Sound Physician Decision\-Making

As professionals, physicians have a duty to provide appropriate, quality medical care\. Fulfilling this duty sometimes means saying no to patient requests or making unpopular treatment recommendations\. In such circumstances, some patients may wish to seek a second opinion or a different approach\.

Unfortunately, in a collaborative team\-based clinic, allowing switches between physicians undermines both this duty and the patient’s ability to obtain unbiased second opinions\.

### Additional Positive Side Effects

In addition to the reasons discussed above, there are several positive side effects of this policy worth noting\.  
One positive outcome is a reduction in transfers of responsibility between physicians\. Such transfers are known to increase the risk of administrative and medical errors; minimizing their frequency therefore reduces the risk of such errors\.

Another positive outcome is the promotion of long\-term patient\-physician relationships, which research has shown reduces morbidity and mortality\. As with any relationship, there may be bumps along the way; restricting intra\-clinic switches encourages overcoming these challenges\.

Lastly, this policy supports a discrimination\-free environment\. Our policies support our providers as people first, before their role as healthcare professionals\. We take a strict approach to preventing any conscious or unconscious discrimination that switching doctors may reinforce, particularly in situations where a physician may be away for medical, parental, or other reasons\.
