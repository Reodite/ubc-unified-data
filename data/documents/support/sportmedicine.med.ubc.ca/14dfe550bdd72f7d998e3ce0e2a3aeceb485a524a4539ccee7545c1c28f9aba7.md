**MD, PhD, Dip\. Sport Med\. \(CASEM\)**  
*Professor, Division of Sport &amp; Exercise Medicine &amp; School of Kinesiology*  
*Adjunct Professor, Department of Biomedical Physiology &amp; Kinesiology, Simon Fraser University*

Dr\. Michael Koehle is a Professor in the Division of Sport and Exercise Medicine in the Department of Family Practice and the School of Kinesiology at the University of British Columbia\. He is a physician\-scientist whose work integrates clinical sport medicine, environmental physiology, and public health\.

  
Dr\. Koehle’s research focuses on how environmental stressors including: air pollution, altitude, and heat, affect human physiology, health, and athletic performance\. He is internationally recognized for his work on the interaction between exercise and air pollution, including recent reviews and original research examining the physiological and performance impacts of particulate matter and traffic\-related exposures during physical activity\. His work also explores mitigation strategies to support safe physical activity in increasingly polluted and climate\-affected environments\.

  
He directs the Environmental Physiology Laboratory at UBC and collaborates with national sport organizations and high\-performance programs\. His research spans both laboratory and real\-world settings, with applications ranging from elite sport performance to population health and urban planning\.

  
Clinically, Dr\. Koehle practices sport and exercise medicine at the UBC Sport and Exercise Medicine Clinic, where he provides care for a wide range of patients from recreationally active individuals to elite athletes, focusing on exercise\-related injury, illness, and performance optimization\. He is one of a handful of doctors who is able to conduct [dive medical](https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/education-training-certification/diver/dive-medicine-physicians) assessments\.

  
Dr\. Koehle is an active educator, teaching undergraduate and graduate courses in kinesiology and supervising trainees in clinical and research settings\. His academic contributions span environmental physiology, air pollution, and exercise, as well as leadership in interdisciplinary initiatives addressing the health impacts of climate change
