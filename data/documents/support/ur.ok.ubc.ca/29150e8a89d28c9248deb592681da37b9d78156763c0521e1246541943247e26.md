-   Navigation and menus

-   August 20, 2026

Your main menu should guide users to the website’s most important areas\. It is not a list of every page on the site and a page does not need to appear in the main menu to remain available on the website\.

Make menu decisions based on what users need to find or do, rather than how recently content was created or how important it is internally\.

The main menu is a reflection of your sitemap\. There are no shortcuts to third or fourth level pages\. When adding/removing items from the menu, these changes should also reflect the structure and hierarchy of your sitemap\.

## Add an item when it improves clarity for the user’s path

Do not add a menu item simply because a page is new, important internally or needs temporary promotion\. If an item is being added to your navigation, it is because a landing page or second level page has been built\.

New landing pages or second level pages should be considered when:

-   users are likely to look for the content under the proposed location,
-   the topic is distinct from existing menu items;
-   the content will remain useful over time;
-   the page is complete, accurate and can be maintained; or
-   the content could be reached more logically through an existing section\.

For temporary priorities, consider a homepage feature, event listing or post\.

## Choose the appropriate menu level

Top\-level navigation items are used for landing pages\. These should represent broad, distinct subject areas\.

A top\-level item may be appropriate when the content:

-   represents a user’s major purpose of the website
-   includes several substantial related pages
-   cannot be grouped clearly under an existing item

Submenu \(dropdown\) items are second level pages representing the child pages to a landing page\. A submenu or landing\-page link is usually more appropriate when the content is part of an existing subject area or serves a narrower purpose\.

### Remove an item when it no longer helps users

When an item is removed from the navigation, it is important to consider when the page should be removed from the sitemap \(i\.e\., making it a draft instead of published\)\. This is not always the case, but if a page should no longer be accessible to users then it should not be left published\.

When to remove a page:

-   The content is outdated or inaccurate
-   The information is duplicated elsewhere
-   The item overlaps with another menu option
-   The content belongs more logically under another subject
-   The label no longer represents the destination accurately
-   The content has been merged into another page

If a published page is removed or its URL changes, you must create a redirect and update links that point to the old address\. Removing only the menu item does not require a redirect because the page URL has not changed\.

**Do not remove an item based on low traffic alone\.**

-   Low use may indicate that the label is unclear or the item is difficult to find
-   Some low\-traffic content may also serve a small but important audience
-   Use analytics alongside user feedback, search terms and the purpose of the content when making menu decisions

### Keep navigation stable

Navigation should evolve as the website changes, but it should not change unnecessarily\. Users benefit from predictable navigation\.
