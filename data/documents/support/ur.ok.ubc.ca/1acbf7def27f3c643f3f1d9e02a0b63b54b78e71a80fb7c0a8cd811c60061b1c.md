-   Accessibility and usability \| Page layouts and components \| Using images and media

-   August 28, 2026

Images should add meaning, context or a useful visual connection to the content\. Choose images that accurately and respectfully represent the subject, people and UBC Okanagan\.

Whenever possible, use authentic UBC photography rather than generic stock imagery\. [UBC Brand photography](https://brand.ubc.ca/guidelines/photography/) emphasizes real people, activities and settings that are distinctive to the university\.

## Does this page need an image?

Before adding an image, consider whether it:

-   is directly relevant to the page;
-   adds information, context or a meaningful connection;
-   accurately represents the content;
-   feels appropriate for the intended audience; or
-   will remain useful when displayed in the intended layout\.

Avoid adding an image just to fill space or to make a page more visual\. Web pages don’t always need an image\.

## Represent people and communities respectfully

Images used for UBC websites should represent people appropriately and respectfully, particularly people from historically marginalized communities\.

Before selecting or publishing an image, consider its original context, who is represented and whether the way it is being reused could change or misrepresent its meaning\.

Take particular care with Indigenous imagery, cultural practices, ceremonies, regalia, artwork and community settings\. Refer to the [UBC Guidelines for Respectful Image Usage](https://brand.ubc.ca/guidelines/photography/) before using imagery involving Indigenous peoples or other potentially sensitive contexts\.

## Use approved image sources

Start with UBC image libraries when appropriate\. They provide photography created for UBC communications and can help maintain an authentic, recognizable representation of the university\.

UBC Okanagan photography is available through the [UBC Okanagan MediaValet library](https://brand.ubc.ca/guidelines/photography/)\.

### Confirm consent and usage rights

Before publishing photography of identifiable people, confirm that the image has the appropriate consent and usage permissions\.

Images from UBC’s approved photography libraries have been collected for UBC communications use, but their context still matters\.

For photography you arrange or obtain elsewhere, follow UBC’s consent requirements\.

### Use AI image tools appropriately

UBC allows AI image tools to support parts of the creative process, but they should not be used to create entirely AI\-generated images for publication\.

AI image tools may be used for:

-   Creative inspiration: Generating ideas, exploring perspectives or supporting visual brainstorming\.
-   Minor edits: Basic adjustments such as image scaling and sharpening\.

When AI image editing is used and for transparency, indicate the use of AI in the image caption, for example: Photo illustration: UBC/AI tool assist\.

Refer to UBC Brand: [Using AI tools – Basic principles for UBC communicators](https://brand.ubc.ca/guidelines/photography/) for current guidance\.
