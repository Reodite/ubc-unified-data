-   Accessibility and usability \| Page layouts and components

-   September 9, 2026

Buttons draw attention to important actions and help users understand what to do next\.

Use them intentionally\. When too many links are styled as buttons—or several buttons have equal visual prominence—it becomes harder for users to identify the most important action\.

## Choose buttons based on the page’s primary action

Not every link needs to be a button\. The visual prominence and placement of a button should reflect the importance of the action\.

### Standalone buttons

These should be used for the most important action on a page or section of content\.

### Text links

Use for supporting information on the page\. When users need to scan several destinations, a list of descriptive links is often more effective than a group of buttons\.

### Button bars

These can be used to provide a series of optional supporting actions for the content of the page\. These are generally used for sections like “additional resources”, or to navigate to supplementary child pages\.

It is generally not recommended to use multiple buttons in close proximity as it de\-emphasizes the visual importance of a button\. However, button bars can be used sparingly to create a better page experience in some layouts\.

## Place buttons where users are ready to act

Button placement should follow the user’s decision\-making process\.

-   An immediate action such as ‘Book an appointment’ may belong near the beginning of a page when users already understand what they are booking\.
-   An action such as ‘Apply to the program’ may be more useful after users have reviewed eligibility requirements, deadlines or other information needed to make that decision\.

Keep buttons close to the content that explains them, and avoid hiding important actions inside accordions, carousels, videos or other components where they may be overlooked\.

### Use buttons consistently

Consistent styling helps users recognize actions and understand their relative importance across the website\.

-   Use approved UBCO button styles\.
-   Do not introduce new colours or decorative treatments\.
-   Keep enough space around buttons for easy selection, particularly on mobile\.

Do not use a button simply to make an ordinary link more noticeable\. Improve the page hierarchy or link wording first\.

### Keep buttons accessible

Buttons should remain understandable and usable across devices and methods of navigation\.

-   Use visible, descriptive text for important actions\.
-   Do not use an icon or colour to communicate meaning\.
-   Provide enough space around buttons for touch interaction\.
-   Check that longer labels remain readable on smaller screens and at browser zoom\.

## Consider how links behave

Users should be able to predict both the destination and what will happen when they select a link or button\.

-   Open links in the same browser tab by default\.
-   For downloads, use a descriptive label and identify the file type when useful

Link to the authoritative source whenever possible rather than uploading another copy of information maintained elsewhere\.
