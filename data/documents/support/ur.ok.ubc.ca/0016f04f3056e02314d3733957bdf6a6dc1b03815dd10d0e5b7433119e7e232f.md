Clear, consistent page titles, headings and descriptions help search engines understand a page and help users decide whether it meets their needs\.

Search optimization does not require repeating keywords or writing for an algorithm\. Use clear, specific language that accurately reflects the page’s content and the terms your audience is likely to use\.

## Keep page elements aligned

The page title, headings and introductions serve different purposes, but should consistently describe the same overall topic\.

-   Page title \(H1\): Identifies the page and its primary topic
-   Section headings \(H2, H3 and below\): Organize the content and describe its main sections
-   Page introduction: Establishes the page’s purpose and provides relevant context

Search engines may use different page content when generating how a page appears in search results\. Focus on making each element accurate and useful rather than trying to control exactly how the result will appear\.

### Write a clear page title

Use a specific, distinct page title that reflects the language users are likely to recognize and search for\.

Avoid vague titles such as Welcome, Overview, Resources or Information on their own\.

### Use headings to describe the content

Headings help users scan a page and give search engines additional context about its structure and topics\.

Write headings that accurately describe the content that follows in the user’s language\. Avoid repeating the page title or using the same phrase across multiple headings—this hurts search optimization\.

Maintain the appropriate H1, H2 and H3 hierarchy rather than choosing heading levels for their appearance\.

### Make the introduction useful

Opening content can help search engines understand the page and may be used when generating a search\-result description\.

Use the introduction to establish the page’s purpose and provide important context\. Include relevant terms naturally rather than repeating phrases\.
