-   Copywriting \| Page layouts and components \| SEO \(search engine optimization\)

-   January 22, 2026

Chatbots and AI\-generated summaries are becoming more common for users accessing information online\. This means they are finding answers to their questions and learning about UBC Okanagan without visiting one of our websites\.

However, that doesn’t mean our websites are obsolete\. We need to be more mindful of how we construct our web pages and write content that feeds AI resources the information it needs\.

Generative engine optimization \(GEO\) is a common term used to describe practices that pair with SEO to help web managers respond to the growing trend of “zero click searches”\.

## Answer questions

Intent is a big piece of GEO\. While keywords are still useful, writing content that addresses the needs of your users is more valuable\. Search engines have evolved to the point where direct keyword matching is no longer needed as long as it reflects the intention of the user’s query\.

**Building page content that addresses and answers user’s needs is most important\.**

### Create specific pages

Web pages generally fall into three buckets: navigational, informational or commercial/transactional\. Determine the *primary* purpose of your page and build towards that\. For example, do you want them to find content, to learn or to perform a specific action?

Understanding your user can be challenging but it is essential to building optimized content\. Web pages that answer questions, share information on a topic, and link to similar content are more likely to be visited by users and referenced by AI\.

The more we can simplify content into giving straight information, the more likely it is to succeed\. Try to use lists or steps when practical\. Look for opportunities to change one long page into multiple pages\.

## Chunk on\-page copy

AI summaries often prioritize headings and direct language\. This means that having a clear heading with a concise answer is a great way to structure web content\.

Try to look at on\-page copy as “chunked” content\. Instead of long paragraphs of explanation, can that information be labelled with a heading and reduced into smaller pieces?
