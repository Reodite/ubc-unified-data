-   Copywriting

-   September 9, 2026

Prospective high school students may be comparing programs, exploring possible careers, checking admission requirements or imagining what university could be like\. Effective copy helps them understand their options and make informed decisions without requiring prior knowledge of university systems\.

**Write for the student’s decision, not for the organizational unit that owns the page\.** Be direct, specific and realistic, and give students useful information they can act on\.

## Don’t overcomplicate the text

Webpages should be written to the complexity of a 9th grade level\. This ensures audiences will be able to quite understand and digest the information being presented\.

[https://hemingwayapp\.com/](https://hemingwayapp.com/) can be a useful resource to test the reading level\.

### Speak directly to the student

Use “you” and “your” when the information applies directly to the prospective student\. This creates a more natural conversation and keeps the content focused on their needs\.

Use a clear, confident and respectful tone\. Plain language does not mean oversimplifying the subject or talking down to students\.

#### Avoid:

-   forced slang or attempts to sound like a teenager
-   excessive enthusiasm or exclamation marks
-   overuse of flowery descriptions \(or language that sounds like a sales pitch\)
-   telling students what they should value about their university experience

## Write for the decision the student is making

Before writing, consider what has brought a student to the particular page and what they need to decide\.

They may be trying to:

-   understand what a program is actually about
-   compare programs or areas of study
-   determine whether they meet admission requirements
-   understand costs or available support
-   imagine the academic or campus experience
-   explore where a program could lead
-   decide what to do next

### Instead of generic recruitment language, make the experience tangible

Broad recruitment claims make it difficult for students to understand what distinguishes one program or experience from another\.

Claims such as innovative, world\-class, unique, exciting or endless opportunities provide little information unless the page demonstrates what makes the experience distinctive\.

#### Replace general statements with specific examples

-   Instead of: Learn from world\-class experts\.
-   Consider: Learn from researchers working in areas such as climate adaptation, health or data science\.

-   Instead of: Gain valuable hands\-on experience\.
-   Consider: Build practical experience through laboratory work, field learning or co\-op opportunities available within the program\.

**Useful details to include in writing are:**

-   subjects and questions students will explore
-   laboratories, studios, fieldwork or other learning environments
-   opportunities for research, co\-op, exchange or community\-based learning
-   facilities or environments connected to the program
-   skills students can develop
