## [General Info](https://orca.ubc.ca/instructors/#general-info)

As you consider whether to use the ORCA in one of your courses, it’s worth thinking about the up\-front effort required, as well as the long\-term steady state effort needed to continue running your assessments in the ORCA\. Below is a schematic from a paper by [Zilles et al](http://zilles.cs.illinois.edu/papers/zilles_csedu_orca_2019.pdf) that shows the up\-front and long\-term time investments needed for running assessments in the ORCA\.

[Scaling properties of ORCA exams in terms of instructor effort\. Left: traditional exams are less effort for a small number of students, but ORCA exams become more efficient for hundreds of students in a course\. Right: ORCA exams require more up\-front effort to create pools of question generators, but are much less effort to repeat in the future\.](https://science-orca-2026.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/06/effort-940x379.png)

It’s important to try and plan ahead when considering the ORCA and it takes some planning to get scheduled in\.

# [Exams](https://orca.ubc.ca/instructors/#exams)

The ORCA is flexible enough to run a few different assessment types\. Below are two main models of assessments in the ORCA\.

## [1\. Self\-scheduled assessments](https://orca.ubc.ca/instructors/#1-self-scheduled-assessments)

In courses with a high degree of randomization and/or question banks, the most effective way to utilize the ORCA is to conduct an assessment over several days\.  
This allows students to self\-schedule a convenient time to do their assessment in an invigilated and secured environment in a dedicated testing facility\.  
For self\-scheduled exams, students will be assigned seats, and time accommodations are available\.

[Screenshot of the reservation system for a sample quiz, showing some available sessions, and some unavailable\.](https://science-orca-2026.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/06/booking_reservation-940x644.png)

## [2\. Strict\-scheduled assessments](https://orca.ubc.ca/instructors/#2-strict-scheduled-assessments) \(Phased out; pilots only\!\)

It is not often the case that courses can immediately jump from traditional pen and paper assessments to a digital one distributed over several days with randomization and a test bank \(though it has been known to happen\!\)\. While a question bank for a course is under development, as a temporary solution instructors can run a “strict\-scheduled” assessment as a pilot during class time\. The main feature of this assessment type is that all students complete the digital assessment synchronously \(at the same time\) in the minimum number of ORCA rooms to accommodate the whole class\. Currently, the maximum capacity of the ORCA is about 120 seats\.

**As of September 2026, strict\-scheduled assessments are only available for pilots \(i\.e\. instructors piloting the ORCA for the first time\)\.** Please contact the ORCA Director to discuss this if it presents a problem for your class \(orca\.director@ubc\.ca\)\.

[Screenshot of the reservation system for a sample quiz, showing an instructor assigned reservation during class time\.](https://science-orca-2026.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/06/fixed_reservation-940x644.png)

## [Other options](https://orca.ubc.ca/instructors/#other-options)

If your assessment doesn’t quite fit one of these, reach out to us and we’d be happy to discuss and brainstorm other options with you\!  
Our ultimate goal is to create ORCA rooms all across campus with sessions running from 11 AM to 7 PM six days a week to give students and instructors maximum flexibility\.
