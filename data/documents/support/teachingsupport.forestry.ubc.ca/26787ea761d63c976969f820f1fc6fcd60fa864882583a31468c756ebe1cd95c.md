                    | GenAI Guidance and Oral Exams |
                    
                    - **Row 1**
                    
                      - **Cell 1:**
                      
                        **To integrate GenAI use**, make sure to specify acceptable uses by [including language](https://academicintegrity.ubc.ca/generative-ai-syllabus/) in the **syllabus** \(e\.g\., brainstorming, data analysis, etc\.\) and to remind students to [cite following the UBC Library Guide](https://guides.library.ubc.ca/GenAI/cite) with a limit \(e\.g\., &lt;5 AI citations\)\.
                        
                        **To mitigate GenAI use,** you can consider:
                        
                        -   In\-person activities \(e\.g\., presentations, discussions\)
                        -   [Invigilating an Exam with Lockdown Browser](https://teachingsupport.forestry.ubc.ca/files/2025/10/Tips-for-using-LDB-Tipsheet.pdf)
                        -   [Lockdown Browser with  
                            Zoom](https://teachingsupport.forestry.ubc.ca/files/2021/04/Tip-Sheet-Invigilating-an-Exam-with-Zoom-and-Lockdown-Browser-Two-Options.pdf)
                        -   [Oral Exams on Canvas](https://teachingsupport.forestry.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/Tips-on-Creating-Audio-and-Video-Submission-Quizzes-on-Canvas-with-LDB-V2-1.pdf)
                        
                        **Implementing Oral Exams Using Canvas**
                        
                        Large and small classes can run [oral exams through Canvas](https://teachingsupport.forestry.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/Tips-on-Creating-Audio-and-Video-Submission-Quizzes-on-Canvas-with-LDB-V2-1.pdf), without requiring hundreds of live one\-on\-one sessions\. This approach **works especially well for large Forestry courses** to assess student ability to explain ecological processes, justify management decisions, or interpret applied scenarios, while keeping the workload manageable for the teaching team\.
                        
                        Some advantages include:
                        
                        -   Eliminating the need for individual live oral exam appointments
                        -   Maintaining the core benefit of oral assessments: to assess deeper thinking and understanding of student in the moment
                        -   Promoting academic integrity through oral exams, which is naturally resistant to AI\-assisted cheating
                        
                        Further resources can be found from this [Oral Exam article by The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/2025/12/12/ai-artificial-intelligence-college-oral-exam/) and [How Oral Exams Improve Engineering Student Performance, Motivation from UC San Diego](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/oral-exams-improve-engineering-student-performance-motivation)\.
                        
                        Please reach out to forestry\.tls@ubc\.ca if you’d like to discuss any aspects of AI in your teaching\.
                        
                        For **academic integrity cautions**, please visit the [Academic Integrity webpage](https://academicintegrity.ubc.ca/genai/)\.
