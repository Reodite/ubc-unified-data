Check out these feature recommendations along with a list of books and films selected by UBC Okanagan Library employees\.

**[The Correspondent](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2025/09/Correspondent258-768x1025.jpg)**

**The Correspondent** by Virginia Evans

“There is much to love about this novel but first and foremost is Sybil, one of the most endearing and relatable characters\. Reading her life story through letters was such a joy and felt very human and real\. There’s love &amp; loss, sadness &amp; hope, kindness &amp; humor, and in the end an uplifting message that there is still life to be lived in our final chapters\. Kudos to Virginia Evans who pulled off a plot completely through letters so effectively it felt a bit like magic\. 5 stars\!”

–Karin, Library Services Assistant, Collections

[Borrow The Correspondent](http://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=14775892,)

---

[Image](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2025/09/shared-image-30-e1785870378858-768x1004.jpeg)

**Bad Land** by Corinna Chong

*“When Regina’s estranged brother shows up unannounced on Regina’s front lawn in Drumheller AB you just know something is about to unravel\. Not only do the characters and the family secrets just under the surface tug you through this story, but the plot is unexpected and fresh\. Line\-by\-line this book sings\.* Bad Land *is like the fossil traced throughout, it’s compact, heavier than it appears, stunning, and completely unique\.”*

– Dania, Communications and Marketing Specialist

[Borrow Bad Land](https://go.exlibris.link/QF8PP1SF)

[Borrow Alchemised](https://orl.bibliocommons.com/v2/record/S111C710887)

**[cover of One day everyone will have been against this by Omar Al Akkad](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2025/09/ONeday.jpg)**

**One day, everyone will have always been against this** by Omar El Akkad\.

This was our Anti\-racism Book Club read for this year\. In this book, Omar El Akkad speaks mainly of the genocide in Gaza as well as numerous other facets of Western imperialism\. Our book club engaged in thoughtful discussion on the Egyptian\-Canadian\-American author’s thoughts and experiences as a journalist, father, human, etc\. We encourage folks to engage with the asynchronous content on our Google site: [2026 Anti\-Racism Book Club](https://sites.google.com/view/2026antiracismbookclub/ "https://sites.google.com/view/2026antiracismbookclub/")

– UBC Okanagan Library Equity &amp; Diversity Committee

[Borrow One Day](https://go.exlibris.link/1Wl8BXHt)

[A short history of nearly everything](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2025/09/A-Short-History-768x1161.jpg)

**A Short History of Nearly Everything** by Bill Bryson

“It’s been a while since I read this book, but what stuck with me was a sense of wonder for human history and experiences as Bryson makes them relatable, often hilariously so, in a way that most texts do not\.  In addition, when he comes to more modern times, you will marvel at how we in fact managed to persevere through disastrous early experiences with inventions, such as the refrigerator, to end up with the versions we know now\.” 

[Borrow a Short History](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=4185735)

–Larissa, Library Services Coordinator

[eddy's kingdom](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2025/09/eddys-kingdom-768x1138.jpg)

**Eddy’s Kingdom** a film by Greg Crompton

“In 1971 Eddy Haymour bought Rattlesnake Island, just across the lake from Peachland, intending to build a Middle East\-themed amusement park but was thwarted by the provincial government’s repossession of the land\. In 1976 he held the Canadian embassy in Beirut hostage at gunpoint to get it back\. This is a must\-watch if you want in on this piece of local lore\. And, if you want to know more after viewing, check out “From Nut House to Castle” and “Helplessness, Hostages, and Heroics” in OSC\!”

[Eddy’s Kingdom](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=11212975)

– Alex, Archivist, Okanagan Special Collections &amp; Archives

**[Image](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2025/09/Chainsaw.jpg)My Heart is a Chainsaw** by Stephen Graham Jones

*“Set in the small town of Proofrock, Idaho, the novel follows Jade Daniels, a teenager who is obsessed with slasher movies\. She believes a real\-life slasher scenario is unfolding around her\. As mysterious deaths begin to occur, Jade sees herself as the only one who understands what’s happening\. Blending her deep knowledge of horror films with her own traumatic past, she tries to prepare a newcomer, Letha Mondragon, to be the “final girl” who survives\. But as the violence escalates, Jade must confront both external threats and her own inner demons in a chilling, emotional journey of survival\.”*

– Hannah, Library Services Assistant

**[Image](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2025/09/island-X.jpg)Island X: Taiwanese Student Migrants, Campus Spies, and Cold War Activism** by Wendy Cheng

“Island X tells the stories of students who fled to the US from the martial law regime in Cold War\-era Taiwan in the 1960s and 70s\. Drawing from archives and interviews about migrants’ family histories, Cheng demonstrates how Taiwanese student activists were surveilled and politically persecuted by both their home country’s authoritarian government and by US intelligence apparatuses\. As international students in the US are facing undue scrutiny once again, this timely account of student activism and geopolitics should be of interest to readers of Cold War politics, immigrant family histories, and spy thrillers\.”

– Eric, AIM program assistant

[Borrow Island X](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=12960093)
