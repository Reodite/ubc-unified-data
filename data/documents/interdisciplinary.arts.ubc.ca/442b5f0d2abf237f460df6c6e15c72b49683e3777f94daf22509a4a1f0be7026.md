> Every year, IDST students in ASTU 401 design and construct a research project based on their own interests and the academic disciplines in their unique program\. They are provided a range of options for their research project, but they are also free to suggest their own approach \(with Program Chair approval\)\.

Below are a few examples of previous student projects to help prospective students to the course imagine what they could accomplish in ASTU 401D\.

---

Image **Izzie Bjonness\-Jacobsen, BA ’24**

Title of Project: Caught in the Web: GenAI and Inequalities in the United States and Beyond

My report and accompanying system map weave together academic and grey literature to answer the question, “How will generative AI tools \(e\.g\. ChatGPT\) impact social and economic inequalities in the U\.S\.?” It’s a big, complex topic I chose because I wanted to make sense of it myself\. My perspective on it is evolving to this day\. I’m glad to have given myself the chance to deep\-dive on something that interested me through this class\!

My work takes the format of a report and accompanying “system map” made with the data mapping tool Kumu\. This fits into the entry format of the Map the System research competition, where I was also able to submit my work\.

Disciplines Included in Project: In its finished form, I think my project falls best under the interdisciplinary category of Science and Technology Studies, which examines relationships between science, technology, and society with the tools of philosophy, history, sociology, and cultural students\.

Image **Eden Colley, BA ’23**

Title of Project: Write Your Heart Out: A Psychology\-based Creative Writing Workshop

A psychology based creative writing workshop developed for facilitation in youth departments in public libraries\. The project’s intention was to create a transferable program that blends the psychological benefits of creative writing, while also using libraries as trusted spaces in the community\.

The format of the project was a research paper and a sample workshop facilitation brochure\.

Disciplines Included in Project: Notable research focuses included were developmental psychology, sociology of public library space, and creative writing facilitation\.

**Jared Khalifa, current IDST student**

Title of Project: Barriers Athletes Face in Accessing Postoperative Rehabilitation and Returning to Sport Following Gender\-Affirming Top Surgery

Access to appropriate care, professional guidance, and online resources for athletes undergoing top surgery remains limited, reflecting broader systemic stigmas surrounding gender\-affirming care\. The project proposes that disciplines such as sports physiotherapy, strength and conditioning, athletic therapy, and sports psychology can be well\-positioned to meaningfully support athletes returning to sport following top surgery\.

The format of the project was a research paper\.

Disciplines Included in Project: Social Justice, Medicine, Kinesiology, Sociology, and Psychology

**[https://interdisciplinary\.arts\.ubc\.ca/wp\-content/uploads/sites/68/2026/05/Mikaela\-Cochrane\.jpg](https://interdisciplinary.arts.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/68/2026/05/Mikaela-Cochrane.jpg)ImageMikaela Cochrane, BA ’25**

Title of Project: Hurt People: Monstrous Narratives and Individual Responsibility

A self\-conscious media literacy exercise and philosophical exploration of the monster/victim dichotomy using the framing device of my in\-development story concert *Hurt People*, wherein a legitimate victimhood narrative is harnessed as justification for acts of harm

The format of the project wasa a ‘Research Project Blueprint’, intended to function as both a theoretical outline and a research reading list to support the creative writing process\. It includes an in\-depth explanation of key project themes and a curated list of corresponding philosophical and cultural works which invoke and/or problematize the concept of ‘individual responsibility’\.

Disciplines Included in Project: Creative Writing, Philosophy, and Cultural Studies
