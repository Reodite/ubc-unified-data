In part two of our interview with Dr\. Clayton Ashton, Program Chair of the Interdisciplinary Studies \(IDST\) program, he describes what makes the program unique\. He also highlights some work he’s seen in teaching a capstone course for IDST students\.

---

### In your opinion, what makes the IDST program unique?

The most obvious answer is that the IDST program allows for a remarkable amount of freedom and creativity for students to build their own distinctive Bachelor of Arts degrees based on their interests and goals\. UBC has so many incredible departments and options for different majors and minors, but some students have a bunch of seemingly unrelated academic interests; or maybe they have a particular research or professional focus that doesn’t easily fit into one of these traditional disciplinary specializations\. IDST offers these students a way to build links between these different disciplines and to create an academic program that is entirely unique to them\. Now when I look back at my own undergraduate degree, I can see how all my varied interests were leading towards my eventual PhD research, but at the time it was a challenge to find an available major that would let me do everything I wanted while also allowing me to graduate in a timely manner\. The IDST program is an opportunity to do exactly that\.

The other answer to this question is that the students are what make this program unique\. IDST is home to some of the most ambitious, creative, and impressive students you can imagine\. I am often blown away by the conversations I have with IDST students, and I’ve learned so much about a wide range of topics and issues\. We’ve also seen a growing focus within the IDST student community on topics of immediate relevance to our current world, such as the climate crisis\. Providing a space for these impressive students to direct their energies towards these complex and interdisciplinary issues is a real privilege\. At the same time, I know there are students out there who may not yet realize how impressive or unique their potential contributions are because they haven’t yet found themselves a comfortable academic home at UBC\. It it my hope to spread the word about IDST so that more students can at least explore this option and decide if it would give them the place they need to succeed\.

### You’ve been teaching a capstone course for students in IDST called ASTU\_V 401\-D\_201 – DIRECTED STUDY \(D\)\. What’s been the most interesting research project or experience you’ve had with the students?

This is a tough question because there have been so many fascinating projects and experiences in this capstone course\! Because they are all so unique and diverse, it’s hard to choose a representative example\. As the course instructor, I feel like I’ve somehow managed to get free tickets to dozens of incredible Ted Talks\. To give just a few examples: a presentation on fungi and lichen that made me question my understanding of how we even define “life”, a talk about depictions of disabilities in cinema, a proposal for a student\-directed seminar on Indigenizing academia, a blueprint for a program in public libraries that would use creative writing as a mental health aid for teens, a spec script for the pilot of a children’s cartoon series\. These are my best attempts to describe the projects briefly, but this really doesn’t do the work justice\. What makes them so exciting is how students combine disciplines together to create their unique and sophisticated approaches to these complex topics and questions\. This is where IDST really shines\. Topics range from climate crisis to creative writing, and have included issues such as the global economy, jurisprudence in the U\.S\., bullying in schools, generative AI, second language acquisition, and many more\.

### What would you tell a student considering applying for IDST?

For the application process, I would suggest looking at the IDST website to get some basic information about the program and how it works, and then consider if you need to speak to Arts Academic Advising to get some advice about general BA degree requirements\. And, of course, please reach out to us at the IDST program with any further questions\. Because the program is so unique and open\-ended it can take a little more thought to put together a workable program, but we’re here to help with that process\. The application process is not designed to weed out application, but rather to help students build a program that fits our program’s requirements \(and the general BA degree requirements\) while also helping students realize their goals\.

Second, I would suggest spending some time thinking about what you want to get out of your bachelor’s degree and where you want to go next\. You don’t need to have your future figured out, but I do think students are likely to get the most out of this program if they spend a bit of time reflecting on what they hope to get out the program and the courses that they choose to build into their IDST degree\. Even if some of those courses change over the course of their time in the program, those changes will be more informed by this reflection\.
