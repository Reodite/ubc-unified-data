UBC Faculty of Forestry &amp; Environmental Stewardship members Terry Sunderland and Peter Wood recently reflected in The Conversation on the findings of the Global Forest Goals Report 2026, which evaluates global progress toward the UN Strategic Plan for Forests\. They note that while many countries have strengthened forest conservation efforts, improved monitoring and reporting systems, and advanced sustainable forest management, overall progress remains uneven\. Most of the UN’s forest targets are still only partially met, and the goal of reversing deforestation is notably off track\.

Reporting from Forests News echoes these concerns, emphasizing that despite policy improvements and isolated success stories, global forest loss continues at a troubling pace—especially in primary forests that are vital for biodiversity and climate stability\. Together, these assessments underscore a clear message: achieving the UN’s forest goals will require far deeper international cooperation, stronger implementation, and sustained global commitment to safeguarding the world’s forests\.
