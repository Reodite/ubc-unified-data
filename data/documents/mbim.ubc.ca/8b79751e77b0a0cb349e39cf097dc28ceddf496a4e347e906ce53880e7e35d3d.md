**Defining the maternal microbial legacy in early life: linking breast milk, areola skin, and the infant gut microbiomes**

Infancy is a critical window for microbiome development with potential implications for childhood health, yet maternal microbial contributions remain incompletely understood\. Breast milk is increasingly being recognized to harbor its own microbiome, but its composition, origins, and contribution to the infant gut microbiome remain unclear\. My research examines the breast milk microbiome in early lactation in over 500 mother\-infant pairs from the Maternal Microbiome Legacy Project\. Using *cpn* 60 amplicon sequencing, I investigate breast milk microbial patterns at 10 days and 3 months postpartum and associations with perinatal factors\. I also examine its relationships with the maternal areola skin microbiome to better understand potential origins of bacteria detected in breast milk, and with the infant stool microbiome to assess potential contributions to early gut colonization\. Together, this work advances our understanding of maternal microbial influences in early life\.
