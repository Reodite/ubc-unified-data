## **Tatiana Lau**

**[Tatiana Lau](https://mbim.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-images/First-Gen%20-%20Tatiana_0.png)What are some of your favourite experiences from this mentorship program?** 

I’ve really enjoyed talking to the people Professor Gold has connected me with, as well as Professor Gold himself\! As a first\-generation university student, it’s been daunting to find mentorship and an academic support system within my circle\. Networking has always felt very alien and hollow to me, so this program has been a more personal and enjoyable way to meet people who have both the expertise and kindness to support me and my goals\.

**What kinds of connections have you made through this program?** 

Aside from Professor Gold, I’ve chatted with Dr\. Henry Lu, Isobel Mouat, and Kevin Ng\. They’ve all given me different perspectives on potential futures in science, and I’ve learned so much\. These connections have helped me take important steps in pursuing my future goals, and I feel really grateful for their kindness, knowledge, and support\.

**How has the program benefitted you, especially during this year of learning from home?**

I learned a lot about the different things I can do with a microbiology and immunology degree\. With the pandemic, it’s been really hard to connect with people in the department, especially since this is my first year really “in” the major\. Speaking with new people has helped me discover a lot about myself and my own interests\. Although I'm only in my third year right now, Professor Gold and his colleagues have really inspired me to pursue graduate school and follow in their footsteps\. I’m really excited about immunology and I’m so happy that Professor Gold’s program has provided this opportunity for me\!

## **Nemat Haroon**

**[Nemat Haroon](https://mbim.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-images/First-Gen%20Nemat%202.jpg)What are some of your favourite experiences from this mentorship program?** 

The greatest part for me was knowing we had a great mentor, Professor Gold, who was always open to discussing ideas and connecting us to others to further pursue what we were interested in\. As first\-generation students, it is much harder for us to connect to resources and people for career advancement due to our socioeconomic background\. The fact that Professor Gold, who is a very busy faculty member in the Department of Microbiology and Immunology, made time for us and communicated with us throughout the year was truly amazing\. It was this aspect of the program that made it so enjoyable, especially during a global pandemic\. Another great aspect of the program is that going forward we will help mentor the next cohort of students in this program\. The program has truly been worth it, and I'm looking forward to helping Professor Gold mentor even more amazing students\. 

**What kinds of connections have you made through this program?** 

One of the most valuable aspects of this mentorship program were the diverse connections that Professor Gold introduced me to\. I had the opportunity to meet his former students and a diverse selection of faculty members in a variety of different topics I was interested in\. Some of the people I had the opportunity to meet include Amee Manges, a professor in the School of Population and Public Health\. This helped enhance my view of epidemiology and the type of research associated with the field\. I also was informed about the application process for applying to graduate programs in public health and epidemiology\. Another notable faculty that I’ve been acquainted with is Kelly Brown, who is a rheumatic disease researcher for children at BC Children’s Hospital\. With her guidance and help, I’ve solidified my choice of pursuing bioinformatics as a career option\. Of all my interactions, the one with Professor Brown has stood out as the most helpful and I am very grateful for her advice and guidance, which is ongoing\. It is due to Professor Gold’s mentorship program that these connections were possible, so I am very thankful for that\. 

**How has the program benefitted you, especially during this year of learning from home?**

The program has allowed me to gain some understanding about what fields interest me and what I want to pursue down the line\. I started off by wanting to explore Epidemiology and Public Health, but after my Co\-op experience at the BC Cancer Research Centre, I became interested in bioinformatics, especially in the field of medical research\. Through the cumulative advice given through this mentorship program, I’ve been able to solidify my choice in pursuing bioinformatics research as a potential career choice\. I am really grateful for all the opportunities and advice that I’ve received through this mentorship program, and I especially would like to thank Professor Gold for all the effort and time he’s put into this program\. 

## **Farhang Ahadzadeh**

**[Farhang Ahadzadeh](https://mbim.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-images/First-Gen%20-%20Farhang.jpg)What are some of your favourite experiences from this mentorship program?** 

Being first in your family to go to university is unique in itself\. You are going to make history and be the first one that has had a chance to get higher education in your family, and my goal is to do my best to make sure I will not be the last\. Future generations will look back and reflect at some point, so it is important to be a leader in my life and in society\. Coming to UBC, I never felt alone\. The environment is friendly yet competitive, and this lets you grow day by day\. I am really glad that UBC and Professor Gold in particular provided me an opportunity to be a member of First\-Generation Mentorship Program, which has allowed me to become a better version of myself\. 

**What kinds of connections have you made through this program?** 

Another important experience in this mentorship program was connecting and speaking with a current medical student as my mentor, whose academic goals have resembled mine but who has taken different paths to achieve those goals\. Conversations with this mentor allowed me to gain a much more comprehensive perspective about the medical school journey\. In addition, communicating with Professor Gold and sharing my experiences as a first\-generation student further allowed me to appreciate the help and support that is present at UBC for students that are the first in their families to attend the university\. In short, being exposed to many different ideas from other individuals allows your perspective to grow, and this is indeed amazing\. 

**How has the program benefitted you, especially during this year of learning from home?**

When it comes to writing, having someone who can critically read, analyze, and provide genuine feedback not only improves the quality of your writing, but also allows you to think from another angle that was not previously considered\. Not being able to have in\-person interactions with others during this time of pandemic imposes many restrictions and limitations in our activities\. However, I am extremely thankful that my mentor and I have had clear communication via online means throughout this program, which has allowed me to further explore ways that I can become a better and more effective writer\. Words cannot express my appreciation towards Professor Gold and my mentor for all their help and support throughout this program\. Indeed, one of my goals moving forward is to become a mentor for future first\-generation students to share the help and support that I personally received throughout this program\.
