**Breaking down \(intestinal\) barriers: Interrogating the role of an aged gut in a model of multiple sclerosis**

The gut–brain axis influences neurological diseases such as Parkinson’s, Alzheimer’s, and multiple sclerosis \(MS\), with aging linked to worsened outcomes\. Aging alters microbiome composition and weakens intestinal barrier integrity, thereby increasing systemic exposure to gut\-derived mediators\. To model this, mice were chronically pre\-treated with low\-dose 1\.5% DSS to induce a “leaky gut” prior to induction with the EAE murine model of MS\. DSS\-pretreated EAE mice showed altered immune trafficking to the brain, including reduced frequencies of anti\-inflammatory Foxp3\+ Tregs\. Independent of DSS, Tregs and pro\-inflammatory Th17 cells exhibited reciprocal shifts between the CNS and intestines over disease progression, indicating strong tissue crosstalk\. Transient increases in small and large intestinal permeability correlated with EAE severity and decreased gut Foxp3\+ Tregs\. These findings suggest intestinal barrier integrity regulates inflammatory cell migration to the CNS and represents a potential therapeutic target for MS, particularly in aging\.
