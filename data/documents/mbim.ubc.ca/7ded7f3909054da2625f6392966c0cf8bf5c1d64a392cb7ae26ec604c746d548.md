**The role of vanishing taxa in protection against neuroinflammatory disease**

Rising rates of inflammatory and autoimmune disease in industrialized countries have been correlated with the loss of key gut microbes, known as vanishing taxa\. However, mechanisms that mediate dialogue between specific microbes within the multibiome and the host remain poorly understood, particularly those that allow helminths and commensal bacteria to alter host immune states in the gut and central nervous system \(CNS\)\. Using model organisms largely absent in industrialized countries, I found that chronic helminth infection with *Trichinella spiralis* remodels the neuroimmune landscape in a sex\-dependent manner, establishing a robust population of CNS\-resident T helper 2 cells that limit CNS inflammation and disease severity in a mouse model of multiple sclerosis\. Separately, I characterized the immune response to Muribaculaceae \(Mb\), a rare bacterial taxa and found it exacerbates disease following neuroinflammatory challenge\. These findings suggest that immune education and the presence of these taxa prime inflammatory pathways, influencing susceptibility to autoimmune neuroinflammation and highlighting their potential to modify disease outcomes\.
