To treat COVID\-19, many patients take the antiviral drug nirmatrelvir\-ritonavir \(PaxlovidÔ\), which inhibits SARS\-CoV\-2 chymotrypsin\-like protease \(M\[pro\]/3CL\[pro\]\), an enzyme involved in virus replication\. In this study, the authors analyzed 1,528 samples from patients treated with nirmatrelvir\-ritonavir and identified antiviral resistance mutations in M\[pro\] that occurred far from the catalytic site, leaving the protease activity intact while still conferring resistance to the antiviral drug\. As these mutations were not identified in cell culture\-based studies, this work highlights the importance of integrating clinical data into investigations of antiviral resistance\.
