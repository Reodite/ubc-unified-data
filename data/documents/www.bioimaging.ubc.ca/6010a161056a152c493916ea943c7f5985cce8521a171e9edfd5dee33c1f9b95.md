The BIF has equipment to cut thick samples using a vibratome or ultra\-thin sections with ultramicrotomes\. Ultramicrotomy is a method for slicing specimens into extremely thin slices, called ultra\-thin sections \(usually &gt;100nm thick\), that can be studied and documented in a transmission electron microscope \(TEM\)\. It is commonly used for biological specimens, but sections of plastics and soft metals can also be prepared\.

- **Row 1**

  - **Cell 1 (colspan 2):**
  
    **Leica VT1000S Vibratome**  
      
    Capabilities:  
    \- Vibrating blade microtome  
    \- Produces thick sections \(&gt; 5 um\) of fixed or fresh tissue without freezing or embedding in resin for light microscopy  
      
    Cost:  
    Academic users: $13\.20/hr  
      
    Specifications:  
    The frequency can be adjusted from 0 – 100 Hz  
    The amplitude can be adjusted from 0\.2 – 1 mm in 5 mm increments  
    Knife advance speed is adjustable from 0\.025 to 2\.5 mm/sec  
    The section window and specimen retraction mechanisms are programmable  
    Samples are usually embedded in agar or gelatin for support
