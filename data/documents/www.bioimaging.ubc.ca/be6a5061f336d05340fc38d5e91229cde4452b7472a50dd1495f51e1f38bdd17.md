#### **April 24, 2026 4pm to 6pm at the BioSci Atrium and Rm 1001**

[Image](https://bioimaging-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/Asset-16BIF_landscape_no_location.png)

Get excited for an evening under the lens\! Weave your way through artful microscopy hosted by BGSA, ZGSA \(Botany and Zoology Graduate Student Associations\), Characterization@UBC, and BioImaging Facility\. This event is supported by TESCAN, SFR, Hitachi and Evident\. *Event location will be updated soon\!*

This event will feature a microscopy images contest \(details can be found below\) and an exposition by local artists who use various types of microscopy for their work\. Invited artists are Ruby Singh and John Desnoyers\-Stewart, and Tania Marie Hennessy\. Food and drinks will be provided\.

For contestant only, please ensure you have read all the information below before submitting your application\. Deadline to submit your application to be considered for the contest is **April 17, 2026 \(Friday\)**\.

#### Microscopy Images Contest Information

1.  **Eligibility:**
    -   Current UBC \(Vancouver or Okanagan\) undergraduate students, graduate students, staff, and/or researchers;
    -   A complete application includes your micrograph, title, and a brief written description\.
2.  **Contest category:** Each contestant can choose to participate in either the Visual Arts \(V\) **or** Scientific Micrograph \(S\) category –
    
    1.  **Visual Arts \(V\):** This category accepts art image submissions that were *in part* generated through the use of scientific instrumentation at UBC \(e\.g\., a micrograph from the BioImaging Facility\)\. Micrograph images can be altered, added to or used in *any artistic manner* the contestant would like\.
        
    2.  **Scientific Micrograph \(S\):** This category accepts micrograph submissions generated through scientific research at UBC\. Micrographs must be captured using scientific instrumentation and represent real research observations or data\. While colorization and graphic processing are permitted \(and encouraged\) to enhance clarity and communicate the underlying science, the micrograph itself must remain authentic\. When significant post\-processing is applied, contestants are encouraged to submit both the original \(raw\) and altered versions of the micrograph\.
        
3.  **Submission details:**
    
    1.  Any modality allowed, but images must have at least 1024 x 1024 pixels\.
        
    2.  BIF will print your image \(11″x17″ portrait or landscape\), title and description to ensure consistency among submissions\.
        
    3.  Submissions cannot exceed 1GB\.
        
    4.  Images must have been created in association with UBC or UBCO\.
        
    5.  Images may be submitted in one of three file formats \(e\.g\., \.png, \.tiff, or \.pdf\)
        
    6.  Upload your file in the following naming format: Category \(V or S\)\_Lastname\_Technique\_2026 \(e\.g\., V\_ Doe\_SEM\_2026\)\.
        
4.  **Selection and evaluation:** Images and micrographs will be evaluated based on the submission’s visual impact, scientific/ artistic illustration, and description clarity\. Full set of criteria will be posted soon\.
    
5.  **Awards:** Winners for both categories will compete for People’s Choice and Sponsor’s Choice – the exact prizes will be determined closer to the event\.
