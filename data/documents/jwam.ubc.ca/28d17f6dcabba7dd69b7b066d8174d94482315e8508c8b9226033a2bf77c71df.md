[Tsuk Ekanam: Water Stories](http://indigenousreporting.com/2013/) is the 2013 Reporting in Indigenous Communities class’s final project\. For a full term, 12 first year students immersed themselves in several First Nations communities in B\.C\.’s Lower Mainland to explore water\-related issues\. The final project consisted of six multimedia stories\.

The stories explored Squamish Nation’s [canoeing traditions](http://indigenousreporting.com/2013/a-canoe-family-called-billy/), the [fight for compensation over a drained lake](http://indigenousreporting.com/2013/the-lost-lake/) in Sto:lo Nation, the [relationship between environmental groups and the Tseil\-Waututh Nation](http://indigenousreporting.com/2013/eco-partners-fighting-together-for-the-health-of-an-inlet/), [water pollution in sacred sites](http://indigenousreporting.com/2013/criminal-dumping-puts-aboriginal-spiritual-practices-at-risk/) in Sto:lo Tribal Council, the [healing power of sweat lodges](http://indigenousreporting.com/2013/urban-sweats/) in the urban Aboriginal community and [cross\-border fishing rights](http://indigenousreporting.com/2013/a-fishermans-fight-for-aboriginal-rights/) at Tsawwassen First Nation\.

The students’ reporting was not only featured on the website, but in a [week\-long CBC Radio series](http://www.cbc.ca/bc/community/blog/2013/04/tsuk-ekanam---water-stories-in-first-nations-communities.html) that aired across the province and in a [one\-hour episode of the national CBC Radio program, *In The Field*](http://www.cbc.ca/player/Radio/In+The+Field/ID/2391037515/)\.

Adjunct professor and award\-winning CBC\-TV reporter [Duncan McCue teaches the course](http://j-source.ca/article/five-questions-duncan-mccue)\. Professor McCue is Anishinaabe, from the Chippewas of Georgina Island First Nation\.
