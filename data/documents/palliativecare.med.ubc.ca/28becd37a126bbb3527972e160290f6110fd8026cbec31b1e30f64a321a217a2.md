**🌟** Resident Spotlight: Dr\. Qing Ning \(Randi\) Mao 🌟  
We’re excited to introduce Dr\. Qing Ning \(Randi\) Mao, one of our palliative care residents\. Passionate about holistic, patient\-centered care, Randi brings warmth, curiosity, and a deep commitment to making people’s lives better\. From memorable moments with patients to her love of basketball, travel, and food, her journey reminds us of the heart and humanity at the core of medicine\.

**1\. What inspired you to choose palliative care?  
**– I chose to pursue palliative care because it always reminds me of why I went into medicine in the first place – to make people’s lives better\. I love the holistic focus we have on patients, the problem\-solving required to manage complex symptoms, and of course the wonderful people and culture within palliative care\!

[Image](https://med-fom-fpit.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/Photo-2-225x300.jpg)

Randi on top of a mountain during a motorbike tour in Sapa, Vietnam\.

**2\. What’s been the most memorable or meaningful moment in residency so far?  
**– The most meaningful moment in residency so far was being told by a patient’s wife that I “practice medicine from the heart\.” It was a simple comment, but left a huge impact on me\.

**3\. What part of your training has surprised you the most?  
**– Moving from Ontario to BC for fellowship, I’ve been pleasantly surprised by how differently palliative medicine is practiced here\. The medications and interventions available in BC are so different and novel \(I’ve never heard of interventions such as cordotomies or tunneled epidural catheters until I came here\!\), and really speaks to how UBC is truly a leader in innovation within palliative care\.

**4\. If you weren’t in medicine, what career might you have chosen?  
**– I love asking my friends in medicine about this, and I’ve given a lot of thought to my own answer\. I’d either be a color commentator for the NBA or a food critic\! Both jobs that fuse hobbies of mine with lots of opportunities for travel 🙂

[Image](https://med-fom-fpit.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/Photo-3-225x300.jpg)

Randi walking with her friends in a forest in Ucluelet, BC\.

**5\. What’s something outside of medicine that brings you joy?  
**– Spending time with family and friends, travelling, going to music festivals, trying new restaurants, working out, skiing, watching basketball, improving \(slowly\) at golf\.

**6\. A podcast, show, or movie you’d recommend right now:  
**– I absolutely love the science fiction horror show, ‘From\.’ Fantastic plot, just the right amount of jump scares, and interesting characters\. A movie from this year that I’d highly recommend is ’Ne Zha 2\!’ Such a comical, new take on a timeless Chinese myth and filled with really deep messaging about the meaning of good vs\. evil, the difficulties of challenging fate, and the depth within family bonds and friendships\.

**7\. One thing you’ve learned recently \(inside or outside medicine\):  
**– I’ve learned that driving across Canada is extremely tiring, and you need to be particularly careful of the patrolling police officers near Dryden, Ontario\!

****8\. What do you hope patients, families, or colleagues remember most about you?****
