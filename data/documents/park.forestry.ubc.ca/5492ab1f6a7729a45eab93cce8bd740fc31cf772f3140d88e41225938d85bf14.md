**Park, K\.,** Devisscher, T\., **Chen, J\., Zhang, D\., Chen, M\., &amp; Khanal, A\.** \(2026\)\. Identifying Social Experience Typologies across Urban Parks from User\-Generated Reviews\. *Urban Forestry &amp; Urban Greening,* 125, 129678\. [https://doi\.org/10\.1016/j\.ufug\.2026\.129678](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ufug.2026.129678) \[[download](https://kpark.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Park-et-al.2026Identifying-social-experience-typologies-across-urban-parks-from-user-generated-reviews.pdf)\]

[Image](https://kpark.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/figure-2-Map-of-park-typology-distribution_new3-1024x721.png)

This study uses nearly 50,000 Google Reviews from 154 Vancouver parks and fine\-tuned RoBERTa models to examine reported social experiences across the city\. We identify four park typologies and find that high social\-review volume does not necessarily correspond to positive experiences\. Park size and amenity diversity were more consistent predictors of typology than neighbourhood demographics\. The study demonstrates how user\-generated content and transformer models can support scalable, citywide assessment of park social experience\.

**Jazi, E\.**, Dietsch, A\. M\., van den Bosch, M\., Triguero\-Mas, M\., Meitner, M\. J\., Atkisson, C\., **Thomson, A\.**, &amp; **Park, K\.** \(2026\)\. Perceived naturalness and wellbeing: Differential benefits for marginalized and privileged groups\. *Urban Forestry &amp; Urban Greening*, *120*, 129448\. [https://doi\.org/10\.1016/j\.ufug\.2026\.129448](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ufug.2026.129448)

[Image](https://kpark.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/Screen-Shot-2026-04-21-at-12.33.18-PM-1024x337.png)

This quasi\-experimental study examined whether walking in environments perceived as more natural improves psychological well\-being, and whether benefits differ across socioeconomic and racial/ethnic groups\. Among 475 U\.S\. university students, highly natural walks were associated with greater post\-walk wellbeing\. For Pell Grant recipients, wellbeing disparities narrowed as perceived naturalness increased, suggesting some equigenic potential, but this pattern was not consistent across other measures of socioeconomic status or race/ethnicity\. The findings show that naturalness can support wellbeing, but its benefits are not necessarily distributed equally\.
