### Welcome to the PASS—the Precedents Archive for Scholarly Speaking\! This site has been created to help students gain confidence and competence in academic speaking situations\. At the heart of the site are speaking precedents—video recordings of student presentations—that you can watch to become familiar with what it looks like to speak in university settings\. You will also find guides that focus on the specific moves scholars make when they speak, and resources specifically for instructors\.

### Different speaking situations invite different forms or styles of speaking\. How do we speak to each other as scholars? How do students new to university learn to speak confidently and successfully here? One of the best ways to learn about the conventions and practices of scholarly speaking is by seeing how others have done it before you\. This site is designed to offer easy access to what scholarly speaking—by students, to students—looks like\.

## Who should use this site – and how?

- **Row 1**

  - **Header cell 1:**
  
    Students
  
  - **Header cell 2:**
  
    Faculty

- **Row 2**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    -   [Browse](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/scholarly-speaking-archive/) the scholarly speaking archive\. Watch a first\-year student give a 10\-minute presentation on, for example, [healthcare systems in Canada and the US](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/comparing-healthcare-in-canada-and-the-u-s/) or the [criminalization of protest](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/mass-media-and-criminalized-protest/)\. There are many videos to choose from and while they all feature student speakers, they vary in length, format, discipline, and topic\.
    -   [Learn](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/learn-with-the-archive/) about the features of scholarly speaking\. We have developed guides that specifically highlight how speakers [cite their sources](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/speaking-with-citation/), [make their arguments](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/joining-the-conversation/), [anticipate their audience’s needs](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/anticipating-the-audiences-needs/), and [participate in discussions](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/discussion/) in oral settings\.
    -   Reflect on the importance of [equity and accessibility](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/equity-and-accessibility/) in the context of scholarly speaking\. Learn more about [Linguistic Justice](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/linguistic-justice/) as a form of social justice, and about how to make your presentations [accessible](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/accessibility/) to your audience\.
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    -   Use our archives in your teaching\. Share [precedent videos](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/scholarly-speaking-archive/) with your classes\. You may also want to explore or assign our guides that break down the [specific features of scholarly speaking](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/learn-with-the-archive/)\.
    -   Explore our instructor resources\. In our [Teaching with Precedents](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/teach-with-the-archive/) section, we provide some [scholarly context](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/scholarly-context/) for this project as well as a selection of [suggested activities and assessments](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/assessments-activities-post/) for teaching with the PASS\. We have also included a page with guidance \(including a selected bibliography\) to help you [foster equitable classrooms](https://speaking.arts.ubc.ca/fostering-equitable-classrooms/) that are safe spaces for all student speakers\.
