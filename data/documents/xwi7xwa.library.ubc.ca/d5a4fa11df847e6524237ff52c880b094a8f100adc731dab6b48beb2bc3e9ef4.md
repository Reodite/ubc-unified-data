Xwi7x̱wa Archives is excited to announce that we have added our first archival collection to Explore Archives, the University of British Columbia’s archival database\! For the full story [see here](https://about.library.ubc.ca/2026/07/13/xwi7xwa-archives-adds-the-verna-j-kirkness-fonds-to-ubcs-explore-archives/)\!

Our Verna J\. Kirkness fonds has been arranged and described, and a finding aid detailing its contents can now be browsed: [https://rbscarchives\.library\.ubc\.ca/verna\-j\-kirkness\-fonds](https://rbscarchives.library.ubc.ca/verna-j-kirkness-fonds)     

**What is a** ***fonds*****?** 

While X̱wi7x̱wa Library collects published books, the Archives focuses on unpublished materials like letters, photographs, and other one\-of\-a\-kind records\. Archives are also different in that our records are collected as groups of materials, rather than discrete objects like library books\. These groups of records \(called *fonds*\) are organized according to the individuals or organizations that created them, so they can be used by researchers to learn about what those individuals/organizations got up to\.  

Because we collect records in groups, we also organize and describe them differently than libraries\. While X̱wi7x̱wa Library describes each book individually as an entry in the library catalog, the Archives describes *fonds* collectively, focusing on the context of the records instead of the contents of each file or folder\. You can see the difference between these approaches if you compare the Library catalog record for Verna Kirkness’ autobiography, *Creating Space*, to the finding aid for the Verna J\. Kirkness fonds \[links\]\. 

**Who is Verna Kirkness?** 

Verna Jane Kirkness is a foundational figure in the history of Indigenous education in Canada, as well as a driving force behind the creation of the First Nations House of Learning at UBC\. Before she came to UBC she worked for many years as a teacher and advocate, working to change how Indigenous peoples were represented in textbooks and classrooms, and calling for Indigenous peoples to control the education of their children\. At the municipal, provincial, and federal levels she conducted research, consulted with communities, and wrote reports, papers, and policies that forwarded the goal of reclaiming Indigenous control over education, most notably the 1972 policy paper *Indian Control of Indian Education* \(available in X̱wi7x̱wa Library’s special collections \([https://webcat\.library\.ubc\.ca/vwebv/holdingsInfo?bibId\=2833929](https://webcat.library.ubc.ca/vwebv/holdingsInfo?bibId=2833929)\)\. 

Verna came to UBC in the early 1980s, serving as the first Indigenous supervisor of the Indigenous Teachers Education Program \(NITEP\)\. In her leadership role she worked to increase Indigenous representation in the program, hiring Indigenous scholars like Q’um Q’um Xiiem \(Dr\. Jo\-Ann Archibald\) of Sto:lo First Nation\. In her time at UBC Verna also led the establishment of the First Nations House of Learning \(FNHL\) and pushed to build the UBC First Nations Longhouse and X̱wi7x̱wa Library\. In this time Verna also hired Wet’suwet’en – Nadleh Whut’en librarian Dr\. Gene Joseph to serve as X̱wi7x̱wa’s founding librarian\. 

Since leaving UBC, Verna has continued in her advocacy through writing, editing, and speaking, most recently speaking at the 2025 World Indigenous Peoples’ Conference on Education in New Zealand \(a conference she helped to found in 1987\)\. A more detailed account of Verna’s work can be found in UBC’s Explore Archives database here: [https://rbscarchives\.library\.ubc\.ca/kirkness\-verna\-jane](https://rbscarchives.library.ubc.ca/kirkness-verna-jane)   

**What records does the Archives have?** 

Our Verna J\. Kirkness fonds includes documents, photographs, cassettes, and VHS tapes from across Verna’s career, going as far back as the mid\-1960s\. The fonds includes handwritten and annotated copies of her many speeches and publications \(as well as some of her speech cards\), letters sent to and by Verna to colleagues, and hundreds of photographs showcasing her travels across Canada as a tireless advocate for Indigenous education\. The many materials that come from her time at UBC are important evidence of the rich history of Indigenous education and activism at UBC\.

If you are interested in viewing any materials in the X̱wi7x̱wa Archives, please contact Jesse Carson, X̱wi7x̱wa Archivist, at [jesse\.carson@ubc\.ca](mailto:jesse.carson@ubc.ca)\. In addition to our Verna Kirkness fonds, we have a wide variety of record collections relating to the history of Indigenous education at UBC\. As we continue to process records, more of our finding aids will become available through Explore Archives\. Stay tuned\!
