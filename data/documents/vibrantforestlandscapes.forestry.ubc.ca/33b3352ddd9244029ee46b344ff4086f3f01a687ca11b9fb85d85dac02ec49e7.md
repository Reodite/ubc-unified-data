North and East Kalimantan Provinces host one of Indonesia’s largest and most ecologically important mangrove landscapes, centered on the Kayan–Sembakung Delta and its surrounding coastal systems, including coral reefs and seagrass beds\. These ecosystems support fisheries and aquaculture livelihoods, protect coastlines, store carbon, and sustain high biodiversity\. However, as a rapidly developing frontier province, North Kalimantan faces increasing pressure from fisheries, aquaculture, infrastructure expansion, and upstream mining, creating challenges for the long\-term sustainability of its mangrove ecosystems\.

The Nature\-based Solutions for Climate\-smart Livelihoods in Mangrove Landscapes \(NASCLIM\) project, implemented by the Global Green Growth Institute and Wetlands International, promotes practical and policy\-oriented approaches to mangrove restoration\. Research by the Vibrant Forest Landscapes Lab at the University of British Columbia supports these efforts by analyzing mangrove landscape dynamics and emerging development pressures to inform sustainable restoration strategies in North and East Kalimantan\.
