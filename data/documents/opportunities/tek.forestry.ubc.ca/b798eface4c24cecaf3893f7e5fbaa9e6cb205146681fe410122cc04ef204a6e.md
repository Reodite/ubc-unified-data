## The future of global trade flows in forest products

Are we running out of wood? Never\! But sort of, as in the short term as industry and markets recover from the global financial crisis in 2009, the global supply chain is expected to become heavily taxed with regards to its capacity limits\. Most other forest products market cycles have had too much supply chasing demand\. When prices go up, supply quickly emerges, and when demand falls, prices plunge from over\-supply and excess capacity and the cycle starts all over again\.

This cycle, some major differences have emerged that have become game changers:

-   China is now in play in terms of growing imports;
-   Russia is much less in play in terms of exports;
-   Timber supply shortages in Canada will result in huge reductions in timber harvesting and lumber production, with huge implications to the U\.S\. market\.
-   There are huge supply chain dislocations occurring throughout North America that will take time to fix completely\.

It is very possible that in the short\-term to even medium that supply shortages could exist\. The prospects of record higher log and lumber prices is forecast so as to attract new timber and lumber supplies to feed demand in the U\.S\. and China\.  
The presentation will provide a perspective on where all the trees and sawmill processing capacity will come from over the next five years to allow lumber supply and demand to balance\. This means that global trade flows should be quite different in this decade as compared to the last decade\.

## Biography

Russell Taylor conducts wood products marketing analysis and business assessment/investment projects for numerous global industry clients and across North America\. He travels extensively and conducts research and analysis in many dynamic global regions, including Russia, China, Asia, and throughout the Southern Hemisphere and Europe\. Mr\. Taylor has published over 200 articles, reports and presentations and leads the *International WOOD MARKETS Group* in its various strategic market intelligence multi\-client reports, newsletters and forecasts for the wood products industry\. *WOOD MARKETS* has maintained an office in China since 2005\. He has a forestry degree \(BScF\) and an MBA, both from the University of B\.C\., and is also a B\.C\. registered professional forester \(RPF\)\.
