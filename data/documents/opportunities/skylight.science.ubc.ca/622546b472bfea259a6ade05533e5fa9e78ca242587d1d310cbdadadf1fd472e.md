A speaker series featuring a variety of events including workshops, seminars, and panel discussions designed to strengthen community engagement, raise awareness of accessibility barriers in labs, and highlight the lived experiences of people with disabilities in STEM\. 

**This series is part of the** [**Lab Accessibility in Science**](https://skylight.science.ubc.ca/projects-initiatives/enhancing-lab-accessibility-in-science) **project\.**

**Description:**

This workshop offers a fresh perspective on how we understand and engage with accessibility and accommodations\. Through an intersectional lens, it will explore these concepts and connect them to everyday experiences\. 

Ash is a biomedical laboratory technician with more than 10 years of experience working in shared\-resource laboratories and training learners at every stage, from high school and undergraduate students to graduate students, postdoctoral fellows, and clinical professionals\.

Attendees will explore how experiences vary across individuals and communities, cultivate greater empathy, and identify small, meaningful actions that can create lasting change\. Ultimately, the workshop aims to foster accessibility as a shared, communal practice—one that involves and benefits every member of a community\.

### August 2026

#### Designing for Accessible Lab Experiences: Concerns, Questions, and Strategies

**Date:** Tuesday, August 25, 2026   
**Time:** 12:00 p\.m\. \- 2:00 p\.m\. \(lunch at noon &amp; session at 12:30 p\.m\.\)   
**Format:** Hybrid \(in person &amp; online\)

**Presenter:** 

-   AC Deger, OER Support Pilot Strategist \(UBC Centre for Accessibility\)

**Description:**

This workshop will offer a space to think through questions and concerns shared by science educators regarding both implementing individual student accommodations in laboratory settings and creating more accessible laboratory spaces for all Science students\.  
  
Building on comments shared by instructors, we’ll begin by examining specific questions about translating “what the accommodation letter says” into laboratory\-specific contexts\. Next, we’ll discuss accessible\-design\-informed strategies for providing accommodations while meeting the demands of learning outcomes and while respecting the constraints instructors face regarding time, space, overall workload, and their own personal commitments and needs – including their own needs for accommodation, when applicable\.  
  
Accessible design goes beyond the individually\-determined, legal\-and\-medical framework of the accommodations letter, and acknowledges that all individuals can benefit from flexibility, supportive environments, and open communication\. All of us – instructors, TAs, staff, and students – may face barriers that impact our ability to fully engage to the best of our abilities on any given day\. Accommodations address disability\-related barriers experienced by specific individuals; a philosophy of accessible design recognizes that this legal\-and\-medical model can present its own barriers, and may not address barriers that fall outside those narrow definitions\.  
  
Since accessible course design can help reduce pressure on instructors and TAs by anticipating and addressing those barriers in advance, we’ll discuss opportunities to “design around” some of the most common student accommodations and accessibility needs, potentially reducing the need to redesign activities after the term has begun or to offer deferrals and “make\-ups\.” We’ll share strategies, resources, and connections to help support instructors and TAs both when implementing student\-specific accommodations and when planning, designing, or revamping coursework and laboratory experiences to improve accessibility\. Accessible design cannot completely eliminate the need for individual accommodations – certain individuals will still require specific supports that are beyond the ability of instructional teams to provide – but by reducing those barriers, we can provide a smoother, more equitable, and hopefully less stressful laboratory experience for all\. This session is open to faculty, staff, and teaching assistants\. Please bring your questions with you\!

### **April 2026**

#### **Understanding Disability Justice to Promote a Culture of Accessibility in Science Labs**

**Date:** Monday, April 13, 2026  
**Time:** 1:00pm\-2:30pm  
**Location:** Online via Zoom

**Registration:** [https://ubc\.ca1\.qualtrics\.com/jfe/form/SV\_0IGWETNKW0x1L5s](https://ubc.ca1.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_0IGWETNKW0x1L5s)

**Speaker:** Jennifer Gagnon, President, UBC Disability Affinity Group \(DAG\) &amp; Lecturer, School of Journalism, Writing, and Media

**Description:**

Together we will explore how Disability Justice can be a catalyst for transformational change, radically critiquing our ideas of “normal” and “productive," while creating spaces of belonging for Disabled folks who are often made to feel that their bodies and minds do not “fit” in academia, science, and our society\. This talk will use the lens of Disability Justice to explore the intersections of ableism, accessibility v\. accommodation, models of disability \(the medical v\. the social model\), and Disability as an equity denied identity\. Participants will be guided through reflection on how these concepts intersect with Science as fields of expertise and with the lab as a site of belonging and knowledge making\. Our discussion will also center how these concepts are already present in our own work, lives, and contexts\. Lastly, participants will be able to engage in critical reflection through learning activities that focus on holding spaces that center access intimacy, empathy, and inclusion, and will leave the workshop with resources for further unlearning of ableism using Disability Justice as a radical politics for change and transformation\. As such, this workshop invites action and reflection on the following questions: What supports do you need to embody disability justice and accessibility? What could action look like in your context and roles? What piece of learning will you take with you?
