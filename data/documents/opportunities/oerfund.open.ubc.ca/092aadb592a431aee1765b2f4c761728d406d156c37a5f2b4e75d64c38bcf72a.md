    **Project Summary**
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    This project will create an open\-educational resource on Indigenous and Aboriginal Law in Canada\. In the fall of 2025, the Allard School of Law approved a proposal to merge its existing first\-year courses on Indigenous Settler Legal Relations and Aboriginal and Treaty Rights into a new course titled Indigenous Peoples and Law\. The new course includes content that meets the Truth and Reconciliation Commission’s Call to Action for legal education and the Federation of the Law Societies of Canada’s National Requirement\. This content includes Indigenous legal theories and methodologies, the history and legacy of residential schools, Aboriginal and treaty rights, Crown\- Indigenous relations, and the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples\. The proposed scholarly textbook offers an overview of this content related to Indigenous legal orders and Aboriginal law in social, political and historical context\. The OER will be used for the new course, which is delivered across both terms in the first\-year curriculum at Allard to all of Allard’s 200 first\-year students\. For the purposes of this project, Aboriginal law means Canadian law related to Indigenous peoples, in particular their constitutional rights, while Indigenous law refers to the legal traditions, practice and legal orders of Indigenous Peoples\.
