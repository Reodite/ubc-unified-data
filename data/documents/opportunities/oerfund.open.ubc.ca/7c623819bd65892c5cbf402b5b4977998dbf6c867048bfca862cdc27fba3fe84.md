  - **Cell 2:**
  
    UBC’s Spanish Program has recently undergone a substantial curriculum renewal, launching in 2026W1\. This change prioritizes multi\-format \(in\-person, hybrid, online\), communicative, and experiential language study, and thematic and interdisciplinary approaches to Global Hispanic Studies, placing greater emphasis on topics like migration, gender, \(de\)colonization, and cultural and linguistic diversity\. This project aims to begin the process of aligning our Intermediate Spanish language courses—the only ones required for all majors and minors—with our new curricular vision by designing and piloting in all sections of SPAN 301: Intermediate Spanish I \(2025W1\), a sample multimedia module for a larger 10\-chapter OER Textbook\.  
      
    The prototype will feature Module 5: “Roots in Transit: Afro\-Cuban Identity in Vancouver,” and leverage H5P to deliver an interactive, learner\-centered experience that blends authentic storytelling with scaffolded learning activities for autonomous and collaborative engagement and pedagogical strategies and assessments for teaching and evaluating students across multiple course formats \(in\-person, online\)\. The core of the module will be a video\- recorded interview with UBC alumnus Julio Montero, founder of Dance Umbrella and internationally respected ambassador of Afro\-Cuban culture in Vancouver\. The interview, to be delivered in three 3\-4 minute interactive H5P video segments, will situate the module’s central cultural and linguistic themes within the context of Afro\-Cuban experiences in Vancouver and serve as a thematic gateway for related curated texts that put the local context and issues into dialogue with global ones\.
