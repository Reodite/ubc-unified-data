## Speaker Bio

[James Andrew \- Líl̓wat Nation Associate Director, Indigenous Initiatives, Office of the Vice Dean, Education, UBC Faculty of Medicine](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/08/UBC_20210521_4237-edited-1-scaled.jpg)

James Andrew, a member of Lil’wat Nation, has been with the University of British Columbia \(UBC\) just over 30 years\. Twenty\-26 of those years has been with the Faculty of Medicine as the Indigenous Student Initiatives Manager where his role was to recruit and support the Indigenous medical students thru the Indigenous Pathway\. The pathway has graduated a over 160 Indigenous physicians\. In 2023, he received the inauguration the Indigenous Health Advocacy Award from the Association of Faculties of Medicine of Canada, Honorary Medical Alumni Award from UBC’s Medical Alumni Association, and the 2023 LIMElight Award: Excellence in Indigenous Student Recruitment, Support and Graduation from the Leaders in Indigenous Medical Education \(LIME\) Network\. James is currently the Associate Director of Indigenous Initiatives in the Office of the Vice Dean, Education, Faculty of Medicine where he works with the other health profession programs on Indigenous health initiatives\. 

[Meghan MacGillivray \- Métis Nation Indigenous Student Initiatives Manager, MD program](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Meghan-Headshot-2-edited-scaled.jpg)

Meghan MacGillivray is a member of the Métis Nation of British Columbia and the Manitoba Métis Federation, with Métis roots in the Red River Settlement\. She has worked with the UBC Faculty of Medicine since 2019, and has been in her current role as the Indigenous Student Initiatives Manager for the MD Program since 2022\. In her role, she manages the Indigenous MD Admissions pathway, overseeing outreach to prospective Indigenous applicants and providing applicant support for Indigenous students who are ready to apply to the MD Program\. Once admitted to the program, Meghan also provides support for Indigenous medical students so that they feel connected to culture and community throughout their medical school journey\. She is grateful to work with a provincial team of Indigenous Initiatives Coordinators to ensure these supports are available for current and prospective Indigenous students around BC\.

[Nadine Duke, Indigenous Strategic Initiatives Manager, Faculty of Pharmaceutical Sciences](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/mw2-edited-2.jpg)

Nadine Duke is the Indigenous strategic initiatives manager in the Faculty of Pharmaceutical Sciences\. Nadine is Dehcho Dene from Fort Simpson, NWT\. Nadine completed a BA in psychology at the University of Alberta\. She joined Pharmaceutical Sciences in May 2023 and is honoured to have the opportunity to help Indigenous pharmacy students with their journey\. Nadine loves to run and hike in North Vancouver\. If she is not out running, she is at home with her dog Freya\.

[Shannon Field](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/2023-Shannon-Field-Photo-2-edited.png)

Shannon Field \(MSc\.\) is Red River Métis and mixed European\. Shannon grew up on the beautiful ancestral, unceded lands of the hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓speaking Musqueam people in Richmond, BC\. Professionally, she has worked as a kinesiologist and worked in various government and health authority offices in the area of Indigenous health\. She currently works as the Indigenous Initiatives Manager for the UBC Department of Physical Therapy, where she supports current and prospective Indigenous students, promotes education on anti\-racism and Indigenous cultural safety, and advocates for the program to be more inclusive of Indigenous knowledges, histories, and experiences\.

[Rachel De Cicco\- Métis Nation Doctor of Dental Medicine \(DMD\) student, Faculty of Dentistry](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/headshot-edited-1.jpeg)

Rachel DeCicco is a proud citizen of Métis Nation British Columbia\. She is a Doctor of Dental Medicine \(DMD\) student at UBC Faculty of Dentistry, and the DMD class of 2029 Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion Representative\. Rachel is committed to advancing Indigenous equity and representation in research and healthcare\. During her Bachelor of Science, Rachel completed a thesis project that aimed to establish conservation and sustainability research practices that were guided by Indigenous Ways of Knowing, and grounded in Traditional Knowledge\. Now, during her DMD education, Rachel is a research student working to bring the voices of Indigenous Peoples into the oral healthcare model to improve the accessibility, cultural humility, and safety of dental care for Indigenous patients\. In the past, Rachel participated as a member of the Port Coquitlam Anti\-Racism and Discrimination Roundtable, and contributed to the development of Port Coquitlam’s first Anti\-Racism Policy to be in effect in all municipal workplaces\. When not in school, Rachel enjoys doing watersports like swimming and surfing, and spending time with her family and dog, Rosie\.

[Ainsley Light](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/IMG_9326-edited-scaled.jpeg)

Ainsley Light is a Cree and Métis woman currently living in Musqueam territory\. Ainsley grew up in Kiʔláwnaʔ \(Kelowna\) and completed her Bachelor of Science \(BSc\) in Biochemistry at UBC Okanagan\. Ainsley is currently working towards her Doctor of Dental Medicine \(DMD\) degree at UBC Vancouver\. Ainsley is seeing the lack of Indigenous representation in healthcare, especially dentistry, and is aiming to create more opportunities and awareness throughout the faculty and profession\. Ainsley enjoys beading, playing volleyball, and being with friends and family during her free time\.

## **Description** 

*Written by Derek K Thompson – Čaabať Bookwilla \| Suhiltun , Director, Indigenous Engagement*

For generations, First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples have faced barriers to entering, participating in and thriving within health professional education\. Today, universities have an important responsibility not simply to recruit more Indigenous students, but to examine the environments into which Indigenous learners are being invited\. Creating meaningful pathways into the health professions requires attention to the entire journey — from early outreach, recruitment and admissions to mentorship, financial and cultural supports, belonging, academic success, graduation and ultimately leadership within the health system\.

This Indigenous Speakers Series panel brings together James Andrew, Meghan MacGillivray, Shannon Field, Vicki Koulouris, Nadine Duke, Ainsley Light and Rachel DeCicco for a broad conversation about Indigenous student pathways across health\-related academic and professional programs within the UBC Faculty of Medicine\. Together, they will explore the work happening across programs and disciplines to create opportunities for Indigenous learners, strengthen relationships with First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities, and support students throughout their educational journeys\.

The conversation will ask us to think beyond recruitment numbers alone\. What does it mean to create an academic environment in which Indigenous students genuinely feel that they belong? How do we ensure that Indigenous learners are not expected to leave their identities, cultures, families or communities at the door when they enter university? And how can health professional education recognize Indigenous students not simply as learners entering existing systems, but as future practitioners, scholars, educators and leaders whose knowledge, experiences and relationships can help transform those systems?

At the heart of this conversation is a recognition that Indigenous student success is relational\. It begins long before an application is submitted and continues long after a degree is conferred\. It is shaped by relationships with families and communities, Indigenous faculty and staff, mentors, peers and the institutions themselves\. Supporting Indigenous learners therefore requires more than individual programs or initiatives; it requires sustained institutional commitment, accountability and environments where Indigenous identity, knowledge and ways of knowing are respected as strengths\.

Through the experiences and perspectives of our panelists, this session will consider what has been learned, where meaningful progress is occurring, where barriers remain, and what more is possible\. It invites faculty, staff, students and health professionals to consider their own roles in widening pathways into health education and creating the conditions in which Indigenous learners can flourish\. Ultimately, this is not only about increasing Indigenous representation within health professions\. It is about supporting generations of Indigenous people who will become practitioners, researchers, educators, advocates and leaders — and whose presence and leadership will contribute to healthier Indigenous peoples, families and communities and to the transformation of health care more broadly\.

### Join Us Again Next Year — In Person

 In 2027, the UBC Faculty of Medicine will mark an extraordinary milestone: 25 years of the Indigenous MD Pathway\. The Indigenous Speakers Series is proud to announce that the **UBC Faculty of Medicine Indigenous MD Pathway 25th Anniversary Gathering** will take place in **September 2027 as the fourth in\-person, all\-day event of the Indigenous Speakers Series**\. We will bring together the generations of the Indigenous MD Pathway — those who helped create it, Elders and community leaders who guided it, Indigenous physicians who walked it, current medical students who are continuing the journey, and young Indigenous people who may one day follow\. Together, their voices will create a living history of Indigenous medical education at UBC, told not simply through institutional milestones, but through the stories and experiences of the people who made those milestones possible\.
