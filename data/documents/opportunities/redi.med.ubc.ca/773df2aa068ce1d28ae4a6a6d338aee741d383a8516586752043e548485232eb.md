Please join us for this full\-day event on Monday, September 21, 2026, from 9:00 am to 4:00 pm at Jack Poole Hall on the UBC Vancouver campus\. The event will be offered both in person and via livestream\. A recording will be available to view after the event\.

This [Indigenous Speakers Series](https://redi.med.ubc.ca/indigenous-initiatives-speakers-series/) session features **Dr\. Evan *Tlesla* Adams and Mr\. Joe Gallagher**, members of the *Tla’amin* Nation\. Dr\. Adams is the Deputy Chief Medical Officer, Public Health at the First Nations Health Authority and host of the docuseries *[The Conversation](https://watch.telusoriginals.com/view/the-conversation)*\. Mr\. Gallagher is the Vice President, Indigenous Health and Cultural Safety at the Provincial Health Services Authority\.

Learn about their journeys in storytelling, medicine, and health\-system transformation, and how these experiences have shaped their vision for thriving Indigenous futures\.

The program is intended to show and convey the value and goodness of doing this important work with the use of First Nations culture, ceremony and spirituality\. We will be using our traditional songs, prayer chants and cultural processes to welcome our Ancestors, and to anchor ourselves in a spirit of purpose, dignity and healing\. We have been intensely mindful and respectful of the speakers that have agreed to talk about and share their experiences and perspectives\.

The program outlines the day’s sequence of proceedings, and is generally customary in many BC First Nation communities when doing important cultural and ceremonial work\. The program is not set to a standard western academic schedule\. The event will begin at 9:00 am and aim to conclude at 4:00 pm, and lunch will be provided at about 12:00 pm with refreshments provided throughout the day for all of those in attendance\. There is no cost for this event, and the food will be provided for everyone because it is also important to nourish the body while nurturing our minds and hearts\.

It is important to be aware of how you are feeling throughout the event and to be aware of the need to be mindful, respectful and dignified with the speakers and with each other\. Listening and participating in these important conversations about the context of truth and reconciliation is both difficult and unfamiliar for many of us, and particularly for those in the audience who are Indigenous – First Nations, Inuit, Métis\. Similar to the virtual Indigenous Speakers Series sessions, this event will not include a Q&amp;A or open comment period for the audience\.

## Speaker Bio

[Dr\. Evan Tlesla Adams](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/11/20240930_cmwf-277-Edit-1-edited-scaled.jpg)

Evan Tlesla Adams is a Coast Salish physician and actor from the Tla’amin First Nation near Powell River, B\.C\. He is perhaps best known for his role as Thomas Builds\-the\-Fire in the Miramax film *Smoke Signals*\.

Dr\. Adams completed his Doctor of Medicine at the University of Calgary and a residency in the Aboriginal Family Practice program at UBC in Vancouver\. He holds a Master of Public Health from Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore, Maryland\. Dr\. Adams served as the Deputy Provincial Health Officer for B\.C\. \(2012–2014\), the Chief Medical Officer of the First Nations Health Authority \(2014–2020\), and the Deputy Chief Medical Officer of the First Nations and Inuit Health Branch, Indigenous Services Canada \(2020–2023\)\.

He was a Harkness Fellow \(2024–25\) at the John A\. Burns School of Medicine in Honolulu, Hawai‘i, and is currently the Assessment Lead for the National Circle for Indigenous Medical Education \(NCIME\) in Canada\. On a personal note, Evan is married to Allan, and they have six children\.

[Joe Gallagher \- k’ʷunəmɛn,](https://med-fom-cpp-2022.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/11/Joe-Gallagher-edited-1.jpg)

Joe Gallagher guides the further development and evolution of PHSA’s Indigenous Health and Cultural Safety Strategy, leads the delivery of cultural safety and humility education, and supports PHSA’s Indigenous Health and Human Resources Plan\.

Joe is Coast Salish from the Tla’amin First Nation and has made extraordinary contributions to B\.C\.’s health\-care system over the course of his career\. He was the founding Chief Executive Officer of the First Nations Health Authority—the first and only health authority of its kind in Canada—and served as CEO from 2009 to 2019\. Prior to this role, Joe held senior leadership positions in several governmental and First Nations organizations, working in areas such as health care, community development, economic development, and intergovernmental affairs\.

A recipient of the King Charles III Coronation Medal, Joe holds an Honorary Doctor of Laws \(LLD\) from the University of Victoria and has provided executive\-level consulting support to B\.C\. health regulatory colleges and other institutions as they work to address Indigenous\-specific racism and advance cultural safety and humility in health\-care settings\. He also co\-authored *Now You Know Me: Seeing the Unhidden Truth in Settler Colonialism*, received a Leadership in Quality Award from Health Quality BC, and most recently was honoured with the Trail Blazer Award from Native Education College\.

## **Description** 

*Written by Derek K Thompson – Čaabať Bookwilla \| Suhiltun , Director, Indigenous Engagement*

[Dr\. Evan Adams](https://drevanadams.ca/) is a household name in Indian country, and his alias [Thomas Builds\-The\-Fire](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120321/) captured our collective attention and imagination with his catchphrase, “Hey Victor\!” This simple phrase reignited deeper conversations in our communities about who we are and where we come from, of storytelling, memory, reconciliation, and the irony and sincerity of our relationships to one another as Indigenous peoples\. Evan and his extraordinary determination are an equal measure of inspiration and admiration for First Nations, Inuit, Métis, and Indigenous peoples to take up the call in coming to terms with each other, to heal with each other, to hold each other up, and to anchor our identities to a meaningful purpose\. 

Dr\. Adams has been instrumental in creating and leading processes of transformative change in health systems and related services for Indigenous peoples\. He has been key in advising leadership in BC, Canada and around the world about how to prioritize and advance Indigenous health and related services\. Evan is instrumental in leading [The Conversation](https://realworldmedia.tv/project/recent-releases/theconversation/), an empowering docuseries where substance use survivors, their families and friends, engage in a raw and emotive conversation about their life, addiction, and how they found a pathway to healing\. 

Joe Gallagher was instrumental in leading the development and implementation of the BC First Nations Health Authority, and has inspired other health systems to transform health and related services to better serve the needs of BC First Nations and Indigenous peoples\. He is co\-author of the successful publication, *Now You Know Me*, a personal and reflective insight into reconciliation between himself and longtime friend, John Matterson\. Joe is the Vice President, Indigenous Health and Cultural Safety at the Provincial Health Services Authority and he continues to inspire transformative change across the province for Indigenous and non\-Indigenous peoples\. 

We are honored and thrilled to welcome Dr\. Evan Adams and Joe Gallagher to the Indigenous Speakers Series for this inspiring and insightful conversation\. We’ll talk about their lives and the pride they have for their families and community of *ɬaʔəmen* – Tla’amin Nation, and how their respective careers have emboldened us as Indigenous people to define the significance of empowerment, pride and resilience within the context of health and reconciliation\. We will talk about what they’re currently doing and what they have planned for the future, and what the past has meant for them in an era of telling our many truths and reconciling the many relationships in our lives, as Indigenous and non\-Indigenous Canadians\.

### **Description of *********Arlene’s Frybread is Magic************\!* **Artwork**

This artwork was created by **west coast artist, Ray Sim \| *Aa\-Aats iḱ\-nu***k, and its rich symbolism tells the story of Arlene Joseph and her magic frybread\. Acclaimed actor, Tantoo Cardinal, portrays the resilient and complex character, Arlene Joseph, in the movie [Smoke Signals](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0120321/)\. Arlene is caught between the tension and anger of her son, Victor, and her estranged husband, Arnold, and with Thomas Builds\-the\-Fire braiding all of these stories together\. Arlene’s character provides a deeply authentic look at the everyday strength, perseverance and healing found in Indigenous women\. This beautiful design symbolizes the beauty and strength of family, belonging and healing amidst great adversity, and is underpinned by twin wolves – symbols of loyalty, the strength of family ties, intelligence, and working together\.

This artwork was commissioned by **Derek K Thompson – *Čaabať Bookwilla \| Suhiltun* , Director, Indigenous Engagement** to honor the speakers, Dr\. Evan Adams and Joe Gallagher, for the session *Frybread Power:  The Significance of Indigenous Empowerment, Pride &amp; Resilience for Health* as part of the Indigenous Speakers Series\.
