## About

### The Research Agenda for Promoting Women’s Health and Well\-being 

Presenters: [Dr\. Saima Hirani](https://nursing.ubc.ca/our-people/saima-hirani) and [Dr\. Rubee Dev](https://nursing.ubc.ca/our-people/rubee-dev)

Women’s health and well\-being are multidimensional constructs shaped by biological, psychosocial, and cultural determinants\. Investing in women’s health and well\-being can improve health outcomes, not only for women but for their families and communities\. Current evidence suggests there are various tools and interventions for improving women’s health; however, there is a gap in the provision of screening tools and interventions that are women\-driven\. This session will present two research studies highlighting the examples of women\-driven tools/interventions\.  
 

### **Dr\. Rubee Dev**: Overcoming barriers to cardiovascular disease risk assessment in postpartum women with reproductive and pregnancy\-related risk factors

Globally, the burden of cardiovascular disease \(CVD\) related morbidity and mortality in postpartum women has increased\. Women who experience adverse pregnancy outcomes have an increased lifelong risk of developing CVD\. Yet, CVD amongst postpartum women remains under\-studied, under\-screened, and under\-diagnosed\. Early diagnostic testing and risk\-based recommendations are required to combat the increasing trend\.

**Bio:** [Dr\. Rubee Dev](https://nursing.ubc.ca/our-people/rubee-dev) is a Global Women's Health researcher\. Her program of research sits in the intersection of reproductive and cardiovascular health of postpartum women\.  Her work entails extensive research to identify the data gaps and opportunities for action in meeting the needs of global women’s health and improving care delivery in primary health care settings, mainly through collaboration, partnerships, and knowledge translation\.

### **Dr\. Saima Hirani**: Co\-design of an empowerment tool kit: A collaborative approach to promote maternal mental health and well\-being

Despite existing interventions, many mothers living in socioeconomically disadvantaged conditions experience mental health challenges due to individual and systemic barriers\. Through a collaborative project, our team has co\-developed a contextually relevant, women\-driven empowerment tool kit to support maternal mental health and well\-being that is a unique combination of women’s recommendations and evidence\-based resources\.

Bio: [Dr\. Saima Hirani](https://nursing.ubc.ca/our-people/saima-hirani) is a mental health researcher whose work centers on mental health and mental health promotion, with a particular focus on populations living in vulnerable and high\-risk conditions\. Her research focuses on empowering people by identifying the factors and interventions that strengthen the capacity of individuals, families, and communities to take control over their own mental health and support the well\-being of others\.
