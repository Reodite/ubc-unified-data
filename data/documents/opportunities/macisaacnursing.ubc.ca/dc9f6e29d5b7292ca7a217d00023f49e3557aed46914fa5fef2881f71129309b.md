## **Who is Healthcare Designed for?**   
Closing Gaps in Health Inequity Through Nursing Leadership

**Keynote Speaker:** Dr\. Oluwabukola \(Bukola\) Salami is a nurse and Professor in the Department of Community Health Sciences at the Cumming School of Medicine, University of Calgary\. She holds a Tier 1 Canada Research Chair in Black and Racialized Peoples' Health\.

*The Mr\. and Mrs\. P\.A\. Woodward Foundation has generously supported the annual Marion Woodward Lecture since 1969\.* 

Health inequities in Canada persist along intersecting lines of racism, gender, income, and immigration status\. Black and racialized people in Canada experience persistent health inequities with measurable consequences: poorer access to care, delayed diagnoses, and worse health outcomes\. Racism – structural, institutional, and interpersonal – is a fundamental determinant of health, and its costs are borne not only by racialized communities but by overall population health\. These inequities are not accidental; they reflect a healthcare system that was not designed with all Canadians in mind\.

The question, then, is not only how inequity is produced, but who has the responsibility – and the capacity – to redesign the systems that sustain them\. As Canada’s largest health profession, with a unique reach across clinical care, education, research, and policy, nursing is uniquely positioned to lead this redesign\. This lecture argues that closing gaps in health equity is not a peripheral concern for nursing but a defining test of its leadership\. 

Drawing on her work as the Tier 1 Canada Research Chair in Black and Racialized Peoples’ Health, **Dr\. Bukola Salami** will explore nursing leadership across four interconnected levels: clinical leadership that embeds culturally safe, anti\-racist practice at the point of care; organizational leadership that diversifies the workforce and dismantles barriers facing internationally educated and racialized nurses; academic leadership that transforms curricula and mentors the next generation of equity\-focused scholars; and policy leadership that brings nursing voices to boards, government advisory bodies, and national strategy\. Together, these four domains position nursing to move equity from aspiration to infrastructure\.

The lecture concludes with a call to action: advancing health equity is among the defining leadership challenges for nursing\. As clinicians, educators, researchers, advocates, and policy leaders, nurses have both the opportunity and the responsibility to help redesign health systems so they work for everyone\.

**KEYNOTE BIO:**

### Professor Bukola Salami, RN, BScN, MN, PhD, FCAN, FAAN

**Dr\. Bukola Salami** is a Registered Nurse and Full Professor and Tier 1 Canada Research Chair in Black and Racialized Peoples’ Health in the Department of Community Health Sciences, Cumming School of Medicine, University of Calgary, with a cross\-appointment in the Faculty of Nursing\. Her research program examines the policies and practices that shape the health and well\-being of migrants, Black communities, and other racialized populations\.  
Dr\. Salami has led or collaborated on more than 100 funded studies totaling over $250 million, and has published approximately 200 scholarly papers\. She is the Founder and Director of the African Child and Youth Migration Network, an international network of 42 scholars across four continents, and co\-led the establishment of the Institute for Intersectional Studies at the University of Alberta\.

In 2020, she founded the Black Youth Mentorship and Leadership Program, which has supported and mentored over 350 Black undergraduate and high school students\. Her research on Black youth mental health directly informed the creation of Western Canada’s first mental health clinic for Black Canadians\.  
A recognized leader in health equity, Dr\. Salami has presented her work to policymakers, including the House of Commons Standing Committee on Health, and has contributed to several policy changes advancing the well\-being of Black and racialized communities\.

Nationally, she serves as Vice President of the Canadian Nurses Association, Co\-Chair of the Public Health Agency of Canada’s Advisory Committee on Science, and Member of the Governing Council of the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada \(SSHRC\)\. She also serves on advisory boards for the CIHR Institute for Human Development, Child and Youth Health, the Public Health Agency of Canada’s Mental Health of Black Canadians Working Group, and the Government of Canada’s Scientific Advisory Committee on Global Health\.

Through her research, mentorship, and leadership, Dr\. Salami continues to shape national and global conversations on health equity and the structural determinants of health for Black and racialized peoples\.
