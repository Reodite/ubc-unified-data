**Reflections from Mastercard Foundation Scholars  
  
– *Oluwaseun Makanjuola and Kudirat Abdulkareem***

This winter, two Mastercard Foundation scholars from the University of British Columbia, **Oluwaseun Makanjuola** and **Kudirat Abdulkareem**, had the opportunity to participate in a two\-week academic program at **Sciences Po, Paris**, focused on international relations and global catastrophic risk\.

Set in one of the world’s leading institutions for political and social sciences, the experience offered more than classroom learning\. It became a transformative space for reflection, interdisciplinary thinking, and global engagement\.

**Rethinking Global Challenges Through Interdisciplinary Lenses**

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/IMG_4642-2.jpg)

For **Oluwaseun,** the program provided a more profound understanding of how global risks are shaped not only by scientific realities but also by shared values, cultural systems, and structures of global governance\. One of the most powerful insights was the need to move beyond theoretical models and examine the underlying stories and systems that define global challenges\.

This perspective has strengthened his approach as an emerging environmental specialist, prompting more critical questions around representation in decision\-making, global responsibility, and the intersection of science and political ideologies in shaping a sustainable future\.

Similarly, **Kudirat** found herself challenged and inspired by stepping outside her scientific background into the world of international relations\. Engaging with different schools of thought revealed that science alone cannot solve complex global problems; solutions must also be grounded in social, political, and cultural contexts\.

Together, their experiences highlight a shared realization: addressing global challenges requires integrated, multidisciplinary approaches that connect science, policy, and society\.

**Learning Beyond the Classroom**  

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/IMG_3848-2.jpg)

Beyond lectures and discussions, the program offered experiential learning opportunities that deepened their global perspectives\.

For Kudirat, visiting the UNESCO headquarters was a particularly meaningful moment\. Engaging directly with global education initiatives and contributing perspectives on issues affecting the African continent created a sense of inclusion and empowerment\.

Cultural experiences, including visits to the Louvre, Musée d’Orsay, and Mont Saint\-Michel, further enriched the program, reinforcing the importance of cultural heritage, diversity, and global interconnectedness\.

**From Knowledge to Impact**

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/IMG_4635-2.jpg)

Both scholars left the program with a renewed sense of purpose and a strengthened commitment to applying their learning in meaningful ways\.

**Oluwaseun** reflected on the importance of engaging more critically with global systems and contributing to conversations on equity, sustainability, and environmental governance\.

For **Kudirat**, the experience built confidence in navigating global discussions and sharpened her ability to critically assess information, an essential skill in today’s complex information landscape\. It also reinforced her understanding that policy and governance play a critical role in shaping environmental and societal outcomes\.

As she continues her journey in environmental conservation and planetary health, she is now more intentional about bridging the gap between scientific knowledge and policy implementation\.

**Gratitude and Looking Ahead**

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/IMG_3836-2.jpg)

Both scholars expressed deep gratitude to the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at UBC and Sciences Po, whose support made this opportunity possible\. They also acknowledged the contributions of lecturers, mentors, and peers whose diverse perspectives enriched the learning experience\.

As they return to their academic and professional paths, **Oluwaseun** and **Kudirat** carry forward not just knowledge but also a renewed commitment to lead, engage, and contribute to global solutions\.

Their experiences serve as a reminder that when young leaders are equipped with the right opportunities, perspectives, and tools, they are empowered to shape a more equitable, sustainable, and interconnected world\.
