**Mastercard Foundation Alumna, Class of 2018**

**Debbie Oyebanji** often describes herself as someone with a deeply curious mind\. It is a simple statement, but one that carries the weight of a journey shaped not just by ambition but by observation, reflection, and a quiet insistence on understanding how the world works\.

Her story begins in **rural Southwestern Nigeria,** where she grew up surrounded by communities whose livelihoods depended largely on agriculture\. There, she witnessed a reality that would stay with her long after she left: hardworking individuals doing everything they were supposed to do yet still struggling with income instability\. It was a contradiction she could not ignore\. Over time, it became a set of persistent questions that guided her thinking: ***Why does this happen? What could be done differently? How can systems and policies better support these communities?***

Those questions eventually led her to agricultural economics\. At the time, she did not have a perfectly defined career plan\. What she had instead was a sense of direction, a desire to use economic theory and quantitative tools to solve real\-world problems\.

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/IMG-3-940x705.jpeg)

Becoming a Mastercard Foundation Scholar marked a pivotal moment in that journey\. It expanded her access to education, but more importantly, it broadened her perspective, strengthened her confidence, and connected her to a network that would shape her path in profound ways\.

Like many international students, Debbie’s transition from school into the professional world was far from linear\. She began her career in **finance**, building strong analytical skills and gaining valuable technical experience\. Yet, over time, she found herself drawn to work that extended beyond numbers, work that could be felt in people’s lives and communities\. That realization required her to pause, reflect, and ultimately pivot\.

She began to intentionally explore opportunities at the intersection of policy, economics, and community impact\. It was not an immediate shift, but a gradual, strategic transition\. While still working in finance, she took on government contract roles to gain relevant experience, managing projects from data collection to analysis and report writing\. These experiences allowed her to build both the technical and practical skills needed for the direction she wanted to pursue\. Along the way, she remained deliberate about building relationships, maintaining connections with colleagues, stakeholders, and professors from the University of British Columbia, who offered guidance, honest feedback, and access to opportunities aligned with her strengths\.

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/20251003_131215-edited-940x529.jpg)

One of the defining choices she made during this period was a decision not to hold back from opportunities, even when she felt unprepared\. She consistently put herself forward, applying for roles, stepping into leadership positions, and volunteering on boards of organizations she cared about\. These spaces exposed her to experienced leaders, expanded her perspective, and strengthened her ability to navigate complex environments\. Many of those early experiences felt uncomfortable at first, but over time, they became the very moments that defined her growth\.

> ***“The more I stepped forward, the more I built both my skills and my reputation,” she reflects\. “Clarity comes from action\.”***

Today, Debbie works as an economist in government, focusing on rural economic development in British Columbia\. Her work centers on improving how data informs policy and decision\-making, particularly for rural and underserved communities\. She currently leads the development of the BC Rural Data Hub, a framework designed to make data more accessible, actionable, and useful for the communities that need it most\.

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/FFCF-2-edited.jpeg)

Yet her impact does not stop within the boundaries of her professional role\. She has continued to contribute through leadership and service in multiple spaces, serving on the **UBC Alumni Advisory Council,** where she supports strategic initiatives that engage a global network of over **370,000 alumni,** and acting as a **board director** with the **Land to Table Network Society,** advancing efforts to build sustainable and resilient food systems in British Columbia\. Earlier in her journey, she also served as one of the youngest board members of FarmFolk CityFolk, contributing to governance and financial oversight for programs supporting more than 500 farms and food businesses\.

Beyond these roles, Debbie has remained committed to creating pathways for others\. Via **“The Debbie Oyebanji Advisory”** and her social media platforms, she mentors and coaches students and young professionals, particularly those navigating career transitions or seeking global opportunities\. For her, this work is deeply personal, rooted in a belief that support, guidance, and access can change the trajectory of someone’s life\.

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/Panel-Photo-edited-940x528.jpeg)

Mentorship has, in many ways, come full circle in her story\. Early in her career, her **internship supervisor became her first mentor**, helping her understand workplace dynamics, think more strategically, and aim higher than she had imagined for herself\. That experience not only shaped her growth but also introduced her to the idea of serving on boards aligned with her passions\. It’s an idea that would go on to influence her leadership journey significantly\. Today, she carries that forward, investing in others and finding fulfillment in their growth\.

At the same time, Debbie has remained intentional about maintaining her personal relationships, especially with family\. Living and working across different time zones has not always been easy, but she has made consistent efforts to stay connected through calls, messages, and visits whenever possible\. Over time, she has also found ways to bring her family closer, reinforcing the importance of staying grounded in what matters most\.

Reflecting on her journey so far, Debbie emphasizes that there is no single, perfect path\. What matters is intentionality, openness, and the willingness to evolve\. She encourages current scholars to embrace exploration, to remain open to opportunities, and to recognize the power of relationships, even those formed in the most unexpected places\. In her case, a chance encounter with a software engineer at a swimming class led to guidance that helped her navigate the government recruitment process, shaping the next phase of her career\.

She also stresses the importance of staying connected to purpose, especially in moments when external rewards may no longer be enough\. Passion, values, and a sense of impact, she believes, are what sustain long\-term growth and fulfillment\.

[Image](https://isd-mcfscholars.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/IMG-987UY7-edited-940x529.jpeg)

For those preparing for life after graduation, her advice is both practical and grounded\. Learn how to communicate your story clearly\. Build experiences that demonstrate your capabilities beyond academics\. Seek mentorship, ask questions, and take initiative even before you feel ready\. And above all, remain consistent, because small, intentional actions, repeated over time, lead to meaningful outcomes\.

Looking back, one thing she wishes she had done differently stands out with clarity\.

> “I wish I had been more intentional about documenting my achievements and reflecting on my experiences as they happened,” she says\. The ability to track progress, articulate impact, and tell one’s story effectively, she notes, is just as important as doing the work itself\.

For all the depth and intentionality that defines her journey, Debbie remains refreshingly relatable\. She describes herself as a homebody who dislikes leaving the house yet loves to travel, a contradiction she now shares with her two children, who are beginning to enjoy planning trips of their own\.

Her story is not one of a straight path but of a thoughtful one shaped by curiosity, strengthened through action, and guided by a commitment to creating impact wherever she has the capacity to do so\.

And in many ways, it all comes back to the questions she first asked, questions that continue to guide not just her career, but the difference she is making in the world\.
