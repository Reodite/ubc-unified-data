We invite Indigenous students to participate in co\-op as a way to gain practical work experience, finance their degree, access career resources, and [more](https://coop.ubc.ca/students/why-co-op)\.

If you’re an Indigenous student who receives post\-secondary funding from your nation, please discuss your plans with your funding coordinator, as soon as you know you’d like to do co\-op\.

#### Common Questions

### How might co\-op affect my funding status?

Co\-op students are considered full\-time students while on work terms\. Under federal Post\-Secondary Student Support Program \(PSSSP\) guidelines, full\-time students remain full\-time students as long as they are considered so by their institution\.

Any individual nation may set their own policy on co\-op\. It is important to discuss your desire to participate in co\-op with your funding coordinator, as early as you can\.

You may bring possible options to your funding coordinator for them \(and any possible deciding boards\) to consider, such as:

-   Full funding during work terms including living allowance
-   Tuition/co\-op fees only during work terms, without living allowance\. \(This is the most common\)\.
-   Temporarily pausing your funding during work terms\.

You may also provide this resource to your funding coordinator to help with any uncertainty: [Co\-op &amp; Indigenous Third Party Sponsorship Funding](https://arco.cms.arts.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/41/2023/08/Co-op-Indigenous-Third-Party-Sponsorship-Funding.pdf)\.

### Will co\-op delay my graduation?

Yes\. Depending on the program, co\-op adds an additional year, or half\-year to your program length\. Students complete a minimum of 3, sometimes 4, 4\-month work terms, depending on the program\. Joining co\-op earlier in your degree will shorten the length of your overall program\.

Some nations may require your degree to be completed during a 4\-year period\. Refer to your nation's post\-secondary funding policy\. If this is the case with your nation, connect with your nation's funding coordinator, and discuss your desire to do co\-op early\. Many nations will provide exceptions for students who wish to do co\-op\.

If your nation requires you to complete your degree within a 4\-year period, we still welcome you to apply and participate in UBC co\-op programs during those 4 years\.

### What if I find my own opportunity outside of co\-op?

Jobs found outside of UBC co\-op job boards can be counted as work terms if they meet co\-op program criteria\. Please share the information about your job with your co\-op office to determine if it could count towards your co\-op requirements\.

Some examples of outside opportunities that Indigenous students have access to that can fulfill co\-op requirements are:

-   First Nations Health Authority Indigenous Student Program \(any area, including IT, public health engineering, policy, HR, communications, finance, project/admin support, planning, environmental health, health benefits administration\)
-   BC Housing Indigenous Student Program \- various positions
-   BC Indigenous Youth Internship Program \- Students spend 9 months in a BC Government Ministry that aligns with their interests or area of study, then 3 months in an Indigenous community or organization\.
