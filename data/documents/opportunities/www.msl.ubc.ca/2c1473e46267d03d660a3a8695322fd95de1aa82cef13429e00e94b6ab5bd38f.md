## September 25 @ 3:00 pm \- 4:00 pm

[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Faculty-Invited-Speaker-Seminar-Dr.-Kathy-Gough.png)

Dr\. Kathy Gough, a Professor in the Department of Chemistry at the University of Manitboa, will be delivering a talk on Friday, September 25 at 3:00 pm in the MSL seminar room \(MSL 102\)\. This visit has been organized by Dr\. Jamie Piret\.

**Talk title:** Multi\-modal and correlative vibrational spectroscopic studies of biomaterials

**Abstract:**  
Vibrational spectroscopic imaging allows us to identify and locate individual components in complex materials based on their molecular signatures\. Thanks to recent developments in multi\-modal and correlative imaging, we have moved from wavelength\-dependent, diffraction\-limited \(5 to 10 mm\) FTIR spectrochemical imaging to wavelength\-independent, sub\-micron imaging, with super\-resolution fluorescence\-detected photothermal IR \(FL\-PTIR\), correlative \[Optical\-PTIR \+ Raman\] imaging and spectroscopy, and near\-field \(sSNOM\) IR\. In this talk, I will present some of our recent results employing such techniques\. With buccal cells, we seek information that can facilitate the identification of spectroscopically relevant clinical biomarkers at different stages of Alzheimer Disease \(Mathur et al\. 2014; Garcia et al\. 2017\)\.  With Arctic sea\-ice diatoms, we have applied FTIR and widefield fluorescence\-detected photothermal infrared \(FL\-PTIR\) imaging to analyze seasonal changes in autofluorescent biocomponents of Arctic sea ice diatoms\. FL\-PTIR takes advantage of temperature\-dependent autofluorescence emission modulations on the order 1%/K to enable super\-resolution IR spectroscopy and chemical imaging \(Prater et al, 2024\)\. Other examples include identification of disordered collagen in scar tissue sections \(Wiens et  al\. Farad\. Disc\. 2016\), in parchment, and as sub\-micron fibrils, as well as IR and two\-dimensional correlation spectroscopy \(2D\-COS\) of poly\-L\-lactic acid polymer films, where the goal is to analyze the efficacy of approaches that may  enhance the rate of PLA film biodegradation\.
