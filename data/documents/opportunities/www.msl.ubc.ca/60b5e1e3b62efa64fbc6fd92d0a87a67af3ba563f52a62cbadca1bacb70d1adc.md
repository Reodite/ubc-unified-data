## August 11 @ 3:00 pm \- 4:00 pm

[Image](https://www.msl.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Faculty-Invited-Speaker-Dr.-Alaksh-Choudhury.png)

Dr\. Alaksh Choudhury, a Researcher with the French Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission \(CEA\) Genoscope team, will be delivering a talk on August 11, 2026 at 3:00 pm in the MSL seminar room \(MSL 102\)\. This visit has been organized by Dr\. Nobuhiko Tokuriki\.

**Talk title:** High\-throughput navigation of adaptive sequence space across gene and genome scales

**Abstract:**  
Understanding how bacteria adapt to new environments is fundamental to both basic biology and applications in medicine and biotechnology\. In this talk, I will present our efforts to systematically explore the adaptive sequence landscape by combining high\-throughput genome engineering with quantitative lineage tracking\.

To uncover the molecular basis of adaptation, I developed CRISPR/Cas9\-mediated error\-prone editing \(CREPE\), a genome engineering platform that enables efficient mutagenesis of essential genes\. Using CREPE, we generated thousands of mutations in RNA polymerase and identified modular functional domains that independently regulate growth and maintenance, providing new mechanistic insights into bacterial adaptation and a framework for the rational engineering of microbial strains\.

To move beyond the characterization of individual genes, I also developed a DNA barcoding platform capable of tracking over 100,000 bacterial lineages simultaneously at ultra\-low frequencies\. This technology enables quantitative measurements of evolutionary dynamics while dramatically increasing the throughput for identifying beneficial mutations during adaptive evolution\.

I will highlight applications of this platform for two applications\. Firstly, the Long\-Term Evolution Experiment, where we uncover the change in evolutionary dynamics over 20,000 generations and identify approximately 250 fitness\-enhancing mutations across \~30 genes\. Secondly, to understand evolution in bacterial biofilms, where combining lineage tracking with spatial sampling revealed migration as a key determinant of resilience and evolutionary potential\.

Together, these approaches demonstrate how high\-throughput genome engineering and lineage tracking enable systematic exploration of adaptive sequence space, providing new opportunities to understand and ultimately engineer bacterial physiology and evolution\.
