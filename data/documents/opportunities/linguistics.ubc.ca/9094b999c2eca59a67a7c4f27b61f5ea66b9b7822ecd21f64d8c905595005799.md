**Presenter**

Péter Rácz, Cognitive Science Department of the Budapest University of Technology and Economy

**Title**

The natural history of Hungarian vowel harmony

**Abstract**

Natural phonological patterns, like reduction and assimilation, can be tied to mechanisms of articulation, perception, or transmission\. Unnatural patterns, like the \[k\]\~\[s\] alternation in “electric\~electricity”, have no such grounding, yet speakers learn them and extend them to new words, to some extent — unnatural patterns are typologically rarer and learners acquire and generalise them less readily\. Is this a bias in the learner, or an asymmetry in the input? I take up variable vowel harmony in Hungarian, a natural pattern with an unnatural twist, and show that its lexical conditioning tracks two records of the words’ history at once: their shapes, and the company they keep\. Together these account for the pattern’s synchronic conditioning, and for the apparent learner bias — a new word arrives with a shape, but without company\.
