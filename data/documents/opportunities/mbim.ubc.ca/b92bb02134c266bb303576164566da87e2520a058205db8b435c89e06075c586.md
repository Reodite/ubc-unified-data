**Measuring, moving, and evolving interactions between bacteria and their viruses**

Nearly every aspect of bacteria has been shaped by their viruses, bacteriophages, making quantifying and understanding phage\-bacterial interactions central to our understanding of the microbial world\. I will first show that new nonparametric methods can more accurately extract bacterial traits from high\-throughput growth data, and that this method can be extended to infer the effects of phages on bacteria\. Next, I will describe tests of whether bacteria can evolve to avoid phages with flagellar motility in spatially complex environments\. Finally, I will show how phage\-bacteria interactions in multispecies communities can be better understood with medium\-complexity experiments to bridge top\-down and bottom\-up approaches\. Indeed, we find that diversity can favor the coexistence of phages and bacteria in a microbial community despite consistent patterns of evolution\. Together, these studies demonstrate how novel methods and experiments can broaden our understanding of phages and bacteria\.
