### **What is a Teaching Philosophy Statement?**

A teaching philosophy statement is a **purposeful and reflective** essay about the author’s **teaching beliefs and practices** [\[1\]](http://ctlt.ubc.ca/resources/teaching/portfolios/)\. It is an individual narrative that offers **concrete examples** of the ways in which he or she **enacts these beliefs in the classroom**\.

**What a teaching philosophy statement IS**

-   1\-2 pages single space document
-   written in first\-person, \(mostly\) present tense
-   intended to demonstrate your **teaching approach** and **concrete examples** of specific course topics, assignments, assessments, and strategies
-   drawn from courses that you have taught or are prepared to teach

**What a teaching philosophy statement IS NOT**

-   a summary of the experiences on your CV
-   an article on pedagogy

### **What is a Teaching Portfolio?**

A Teaching Portfolio is a document that contains carefully selected and assembled materials and representative artifacts of one’s achievements in teaching\.

**A teaching portfolio is**:

-   A collection of materials that document teaching performance \(Seldin, 2003\)\.
-   **Not** a container for everything that you’ve done as a teacher; it presents the materials that best illustrate your teaching approach and methods\.
-   A cumulative document \(updated at least **yearly**\)\.
-   A map or template helping you to organize, articulate, and support and document your teaching contributions\.

### **Elements of a Teaching Portfolio**

#### 1\. Teaching Philosophy

*Purpose*: To introduce the reader to your views about teaching, learning, and students\.

*Tips*:

-   Statement should be reflective and personal
-   Brief \(few paragraphs to 1\-2 pages\)
-   Use first person \(narrative style\)
-   Avoid technical language – not everyone reading it will be an expert in your field\!

#### 2\. Activities

a\) Teaching Responsibilities: include course types, class sizes, times, course goals \(i\.e\. are you providing information, coaching, encouraging self\-direction, etc\.\)

Evidence:

-   Teaching methods used in the classroom
-   Titles and numbers of courses taught, \# of students, etc\.
-   Sample Syllabi
-   Courses Planned \(tailor for specific applications\)
-   Sample Assignments and Assessments
-   Video of a class

b\) Supervising and Advising Students

c\) Activities Engaged\-In to Improve Teaching and Learning

-   summarizes your attendance in teaching\-related seminars, workshops, conferences, and how you used this new information in your teaching
-   List of activities: Description of efforts made to improve the classroom or your teaching methods\.

d\) Committee Service \(Teaching and Learning Issues\)

-   List of committee service that pertains to teaching and learning issues\. Include details \(names of committees, dates, and the nature of your contribution\)\.
-   Discuss and provide supportive documentation about any involvement in developing and teaching seminars or workshops; publications; funding received, development of curriculum materials, etc\.

e\) Publications and Professional Contributions

#### 3\. Evidence of Student Learning

Evidence of student learning can be both objective and subjective\. Here are some examples:

-   Changes in student grades/progress in a course or series of courses
-   Comments on student papers
-   Student evaluations
-   Evaluations by faculty member or other observer
-   Teaching awards
-   Feedback from supervisors or employers of graduates

#### 4\. Reflections &amp; Assessments of Teaching

The ways that you monitor and evaluate your own teaching

Ways to assess and reflect may include:

-   Departmental teaching evaluations
-   Peer evaluations or reviews
-   Teaching awards received or nominated for
-   Student\-initiated feedback
-   Personal teaching development journal \(you wouldn’t include the whole thing in a portfolio, but might include sections/statements\)\.

This section often ends by tying together philosophy, approaches, evidence, and evaluative sections, and detailing a plan for future actions, including your motivation and challenges and short and long\-term teaching goals\.
