### Registration

The registration for the Fall 2026 FoP session is now open\! Fall 2026 FoP will be held **online** from September 24 – end of November\. You can register [here](https://events.ctlt.ubc.ca/events/foundations-of-pedagogy-september-november-2026/)\.

### Goal

At the end of the one\-term course, you will be able to use concepts in evidence\-based teaching to effectively teach a lesson in an undergraduate classroom, with emphasis on alignment of learning objectives, activities and assessment\. The context is interdisciplinary and concepts are transferable to your home discipline\. UBC graduate students and postdoctoral fellows from all disciplines are invited to participate\.

### Instructors

We teach FoP with different instructors each term\. The Fall 2026 FoP session is taught by:

-   Jens Vent\-Schmidt, Educational Consultant &amp; Lecturer
-   Natalie Westwood,  CIRTL Program Developer &amp; Sessional Instructor, Faculty of Science

### Requirements

Successful completion involves participation in 7 out of 8 classes including pre\-work, teaching one 10 minute mini\-lesson and writing a first draft of your teaching portfolio\. Upon completion, you will be awarded CIRTL Associate status\.

There are no pre\-requisites to participate in this course\. The concepts are similar to what is covered in an [ISW](http://ctlt.ubc.ca/programs/all-our-programs/instructional-skills-workshops/?login) with more emphasis on theory\. Previous students who had taken an ISW prior to FoP said that both courses complement each other well\.

### Course Schedule

### Exemplary course schedule from September 2018\. Final schedule subject to changes\.

**Week 1**: Introduction to undergraduate teaching

Learning Objectives:

-   Formulate your individual teaching development goals
-   Reflect on your teaching teaching philosophy

**Week 2**: How People Learn \(Theories of Learning\)

Learning Objectives:

-   Relate theories of learning to your own experience\.
-   Contrast novice and experts approaches to learning
-   Describe how students’ prior knowledge and metacognition influence learning

Pre\-Reading:

-   Bransford \(2000\) [Learning: From Speculation to Science](https://www.nap.edu/read/9853/chapter/3)
-   Ross \(2006\) [The Expert Mind](http://ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/login?url=http://www.nature.com/scientificamerican/journal/v295/n2/full/scientificamerican0806-64.html)
-   Dweck \(2015\) [The Remarkable Reach of Growth Mindsets](http://ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/login?url=http://www.nature.com/scientificamericanmind/journal/v27/n1/full/scientificamericanmind0116-36.html)

**Week 3**: Designing Well Aligned Lessons

Learning Objectives:

-   Formulate a learning objective for your STEM mini lesson
-   Develop well aligned active learning and assessment techniques
-   Give and receive meaningful feedback on any lesson plan

**Week 4**: Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives:

-   Design realistic, achievable, measurable and student\-centered learning objectives
-   Analyze any learning objective with respect to Bloom's Taxonomy\.

Pre\-Reading:

-   Ambrose \(2010\) How Learning Works Appendix D
-   O’Neill \(2010\) [Biology Implementation of Blooming Tool](http://ir.lib.uwo.ca/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1010&amp;context=cjsotl_rcacea)
-   Dunham \(2015\) [Statistics Implementation of Blooming Tool](http://www.cwsei.ubc.ca/SEI_research/files/Stat/Dunham-etal_StatBlooming_JSE2015.pdf)
-   Simon &amp; Taylor \(2009\) [What is the Value of Course\-specific Goals?](http://web.b.ebscohost.com.ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/ehost/detail/detail?vid=0&amp;sid=d2965592-4b29-4a3b-a699-f3823aa677a0%40sessionmgr103&amp;bdata=JnNpdGU9ZWhvc3QtbGl2ZSZzY29wZT1zaXRl#AN=508105550&amp;db=eue)
-   Krathwohl \(2002\) [A Revision of Bloom’s Taxonomy](http://ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/login?url=http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15430421tip4104_2?journalCode=htip20)

**Week 5**: Active Learning

Learning Objectives:

-   Describe several common STEM active learning strategies
-   Evaluate active learning strategies in light of theories of learning

Pre\-Reading:

-   Freeman \(2014\) [Active Learning Increases Student Performance in Science, Engineering, and Mathematics](http://ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/login?url=http://www.pnas.org/content/111/23/8410.full)
-   Jones \(2007\) [Reconsidering the Lecture](http://ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/login?url=http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03098770701656816)
-   Wieman \(2014\) [Large Scale Comparison of Science Teaching Methods Sends Clear Message](http://www.pnas.org/content/111/23/8319.full.pdf)
-   Hake \(1998\) [Interactive engagement vs traditional lecture \- a six\-thousand student survey](http://ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/login?url=http://aapt.scitation.org/doi/10.1119/1.18809)

**Week 6**: Assessment that Support Student Learning

Learning Objectives:

-   Describe several assessment techniques and recognize their alignment with particular types of learning goals\.
-   Evaluate assessment techniques as learning/feedback opportunities

Pre\-Reading:

-   Key points from Gibbs and Simpson \(CWSEI, 2015\) [Assessments that Support Student Learning](http://www.cwsei.ubc.ca/resources/files/Assessment_That_Support_Learning.pdf)
-   Gilley and Clarkston \(2014\) [Collaborative Testing: Evidence of Learning in a Controlled In\-Class Study of Undergraduate Students](http://www.cwsei.ubc.ca/SEI_research/files/Gilley-Clarkston_2-Stage_Exam_Learning_JCST2014.pdf)
-   WNCP \(2006\) [Rethinking Classroom Assessment with Purpose in Mind](https://www.wncp.ca/media/40539/rethink.pdf); Section II

**Week 7**: Learning Through Diversity

Learning Objectives:

-   Define diversity, equity and inclusion, and their implications for the classroom
-   Implement strategies for creating equitable and inclusive classrooms

Pre\-Reading:

-   Kivel \(2004\) [*The Culture of Power*](https://tomprof.stanford.edu/posting/544)
-   Tanner \(2013\) [21 Teaching Strategies to Promote Inclusivity](http://ezproxy.library.ubc.ca/login?url=http://www.lifescied.org/content/12/3/322.full)
-   Winkelmes \(2016\) [A Teaching Intervention that Increases Underserved College Student’s Success](https://www.aacu.org/peerreview/2016/winter-spring/Winkelmes)

**Week 8**: Teaching Portfolio

Learning Objectives:

-   Refine your statement of teaching philosophy
-   Identify at least one idea for each component of your teaching portfolio
-   Define at least 1 next\-step to preparing your teaching portfolio0

Pre\-Reading:

-   Grundman \(2006\) [Writing a Teaching Philosophy Statement](http://www.ams.org/notices/200611/comm-grundman.pdf)
-   Vanderbilt University [Online Guide on Teaching Philosophy Statements](https://cft.vanderbilt.edu/guides-sub-pages/teaching-statements/)
-   White &amp; Conrod \(2016\) [pIs nothing sacred? Our personal teaching philosophies have been plagiarized](http://www.universityaffairs.ca/opinion/in-my-opinion/is-nothing-sacred-our-personal-teaching-philosophies-have-been-plagiarized/)
