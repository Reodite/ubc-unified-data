Xwi7xwa carries many titles written by professors here at UBC\. This list is dedicated to the publications of 8 UBC professors but there are many more in the library’s collection\. Stop by the branch to check out these listed books and find more by varying profs\!

**Dr\. Billy Ray Belcourt**

[Image](https://indigenous.ubc.ca/files/2020/01/2019_BRBelcourt288.jpg)

Dr\. Belcourt is from the Driftpile Cree Nation and holds a Ph\. D in English from the University of Alberta\. He has been both a Rhodes Scholar and a PE Trudeau Foundation Scholar as well\. This academic year \(2026/27\), he will be teaching *CRWR 220: Introduction to Creative Writing with an Indigenous Focus, CRWR 420B: Indigenous Writing, CRWR 451B: Writing Poetry II, and CRWR 521B Indigenous Writing*\. **Dr\. Candis Callison**

[Image](https://aboriginal-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/ccallison-288.jpg)

Dr\. Callison is the Canada Research Chair in Indigenous journalism, media, and public discourse, and a Professor jointly appointed in the [**School of Public Policy and Global Affairs**](https://sppga.ubc.ca/), and the **Institute for Critical Indigenous Studies**\. Candis is [**Tahltan**](http://tahltan.ca/) \(Tałtan\), an Indigenous people located in what is now Northwestern British Columbia and founding board member of [**Tu’dese’cho Wholistic Indigenous Leadership Development Society**](https://www.twild.org/) \(T\-WILD\), the first Tałtan non\-government organization, created to provide Tałtan people with land\-based learning, leadership development, and cultural programming\. **Dana Claxton**

[Image](https://aboriginal-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/dclaxton-288.jpg)

Dana Claxton of Lakota First Nations, works in film, video, photography, single\- and multi\-channel video installation, and performance art\. She investigates beauty, the body, the socio\-political, and the spiritual\. Her work has been shown internationally at the Museum of Modern Art, Walker Art Centre, Sundance Film Festival, Eiteljorg Museum, and the Museum of Contemporary Art \(Sydney\) and held in public collections including the Vancouver Art Gallery, National Gallery of Canada, Art Bank of Canada, and the Winnipeg Art Gallery\.

**Dr\. Glen Coulthard**

[Image](https://aboriginal-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/gcoulthard-288.jpg)

Dr\. Coulthard is Yellowknives Dene and an associate professor in the First Nations and Indigenous Studies Program and the Departments of Political Science at the University of British Columbia\. His book, *Red Skin, White Masks: Rejecting the Colonial Politics of Recognition* \(Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 2014\), was the winner of the 2016 Caribbean Philosophical Association’s Frantz Fanon Award for Outstanding Book, the Canadian Political Science Association’s CB Macpherson Award for Best Book in Political Theory, published in English or French, in 2014/2015, and the Rik Davidson Studies in Political Economy Award for Best Book in 2016\. Find it at Xwi7xwa Library\!

**Larry Grant**

[Image](https://aboriginal-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/lgrant-288.jpg)

Musqueam Elder, Larry Grant, was born and raised in Musqueam traditional territory by a traditional hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓ speaking Musqueam family\. After 4 decades as a tradesman, Larry enrolled in the First Nations Languages Program, which awoke his memory of the embedded value that the hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓ language has to self\-identity, kinship, culture, territory, and history prior to European contact\. He works in revitalizing hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓ in the Musqueam Language and Culture Department and co\-teaching the introductory hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓ course through UBC\.

**Dr\. Dallas Hunt**

[Image](https://aboriginal-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/dhunt-288.jpg)

Hunt is Cree and a member of Wapsewsipi \(Swan River First Nation\) in Treaty 8 territory in Northern Alberta\. He is an Assistant Professor in the Department of English Language and Literatures at the University of British Columbia where he also holds an affiliation with the UBC Centre for Climate Justice\. Hunt’s work centers on Indigenous literatures, decolonial narratives, and the politics of settler colonialism\. His research and teaching challenge colonial historiographies while amplifying the resilience and complexity of Indigenous voices across what is currently called North America\. Dallas Hunt will be teaching *ENGL 100: Reading and Writing about Language and Literatures, ENGL 373: Indigenous Literature, ENGL 376B: Topics in Place, Power, Poetic, and Narrative* this academic year\!

**Dr\. Daniel Health Justice**

[Image](https://aboriginal-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/djustice-288.jpg)

Daniel was born in Colorado and is a member of the Cherokee Nation\. He works as a Full Professor and Distinguished University Scholar in the Institute for Critical Indigenous Studies and Department of English Languages and Literatures at UBC\. His work in Indigenous cultural and literary studies takes up questions and issues of nationhood, kinship, and belonging, with increasing attention to the intersections between Indigenous literatures, speculative fiction, and other\-than\-human peoples\. Daniel will be teaching *FNIS 100A: Indigenous Foundations* this coming school year\!

**Dr\. Alice Te Punga Somerville**

[Image](https://aboriginal-2018.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/asomerville-288.jpg)

Previously teaching in New Zealand, Australia and Hawai’i\., Dr\. Alice Te Punga Somerville joined UBC in 2022\. She holds a joint appointment with the [UBC Institute for Critical Indigenous Studies](https://cis.arts.ubc.ca/)\. At its heart, her research and teaching engage texts that centre Indigenous expansiveness and de\-centre colonialism\. Her MA \(Auckland\) and PhD \(Cornell\) focused on Māori written literatures; as she sought broader contexts for thinking about the writing of her own community\. She developed a twin interest and expertise in Indigenous studies and Pacific studies\. Dr\. Somerville taught *ENGL 110: Approaches to Literature and Culture* this past summer\!
