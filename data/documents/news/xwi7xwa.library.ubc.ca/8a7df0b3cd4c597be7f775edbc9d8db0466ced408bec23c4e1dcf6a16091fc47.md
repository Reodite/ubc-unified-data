#### 4\. Sts’iyóye smestíyexw slhá:li

[NRTF \| First Nations Students Scholarships \|](https://www.nrtf.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/saylesh_wesley.jpg)

###### Image retrieved from [https://www\.nrtf\.ca/impact/2012\-recipient\-stories/](https://www.nrtf.ca/impact/2012-recipient-stories/)

Sts’iyóye smestíyexw slhá:li \(Twin\-Spirited Woman or Saylesh Wesley\) is both Stó:lõ  and Tsimshian\. In her article “[Twin\-Spirited Woman:](https://read.dukeupress.edu/tsq/article/1/3/338-351/24726) Sts’iyóye smestíyexw slhá:li” Wesley shares her stories and process of coming into community with the assistance of her grandmother\. Wesley, “wishes to revitalize the cultural roles of transgendered/two\-spirit people within the Coast Salish territory and ways in which they historically contributed to their societies prior to colonization\.” 

In 2017 as a result of [transphobia/homophobia in Chilliwack’s community](https://www.straight.com/life/1058791/human-rights-complaints-filed-against-chilliwack-school-trustee-barry-neufeld), Peggy Janicki wove a shawl for Sts’iyóye smestíyexw slhá:li and held a ceremony to bring “order and grace” out of “chaos and hurt”; [see page 20 of *Teacher Magazine*](https://www.bctf.ca/uploadedFiles/Public/Publications/TeacherNewsmag/archive/2018-2019/2019-05/Teacher%20Magazine%20May%202019%20web.pdf)\.

#### 5\. [Shawnee](http://www.shawneemusic.com/Shawnee/Shawnee.html)

[Image result for shawnee](http://www.shawneemusic.com/Shawnee/Shawnee_files/Shawnee5.jpg)

Shawnee is a soul, R&amp;B, pop, and alternative Mohawk Two\-Spirit artist\. Her music aims to “support, heal, and empower\.” Her work has been featured on Disney TV, at NYC Pride, and to support Canada’s suicide crisis\.

Although her album has [yet to be released](http://www.shawneemusic.com/Shawnee/Store.html), you can still stream it [online](https://soundcloud.com/shawneemusic)\.

**6\. [Cris Derksen](http://crisderksen.virb.com/bio)**

[Image](http://media.virbcdn.com/cdn_images/resize_1024x1365/c7/fdfaf77c9b846dae-CrisandVelosmall.jpg) [Orchestral Powwow](http://media.virbcdn.com/cdn_images/crop_300x300/86/a56ed42d1808c82f-OrchestralPowwowSinging.jpg)

#### 7\. [Continuous](https://www.jennyirenemiller.com/continuous) by [Wiagañmiu \(Jenny Irene Miller\)](https://www.jennyirenemiller.com/contact)

[64274457\_10220268330965435\_1756190244476026880\_n\.jpg](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/59bdc6cae3df28bd2da4095f/1560793360228-V0NZL74BN0NTXXTDH3D2/ke17ZwdGBToddI8pDm48kCuXkeTgOBEQp-zwqMsuty9Zw-zPPgdn4jUwVcJE1ZvWQUxwkmyExglNqGp0IvTJZamWLI2zvYWH8K3-s_4yszcp2ryTI0HqTOaaUohrI8PIZ_8CgSJQ1upoJNHfNttxvS2l5f5M1hVtcI1CvEzB0sMKMshLAGzx4R3EDFOm1kBS/64274457_10220268330965435_1756190244476026880_n.jpg?format=300w)

Wiagañmiu is Inupiaq from Nome, with roots in Kiqigin \(Wales, Alaska\)\. Miller identifies as both gay and Two\-Spirit and her photography supports the lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer and Indigenous LGBTQ\+ and Two\-Spirit communities\.

*“Continuous* is my small answer to the large question: how do we as Indigenous people decolonize our sexualities, genders, and the way we treat individuals who identify outside of the standard binary of male or female? I have replied to that question with this ongoing portrait series featuring members of the Indigenous lesbian, gay, bisexual, queer, and Two\-Spirit \(LGBTQ2\) community\.”

Miller on instagram: [@jennyirenemiller](https://www.instagram.com/jennyirenemiller/)

Read more about Miller in her article [The Many Ways We Love](https://search.proquest.com/docview/2210314618?accountid=14656&amp;pq-origsite=summon) from [Canadian Art\.](https://search.proquest.com/publication/publications_34309?accountid=14656)

#### 8\. Gender

[Image](https://www.mcnallyrobinson.com/event-img/138923101812937.jpg) [Image result for masculindians conversations about indigenous manhood](https://uofmpress.ca/images/made/images/books/_resized/Masculindians_cover_SM_300_318_90.jpg)

***Masculindians: Conversations about Indigenous Manhood***

Between October 2010 and May 2013, Sam McKegney conducted interviews with leading Indigenous artists, critics, activists, and elders on the subject of Indigenous manhood\. In offices, kitchens, and coffee shops, and once in a car driving down the 401, McKegney and his participants tackled crucial questions about masculine self\-worth and how to foster balanced and empowered gender relations\.

Find me at [UBC Library\!](http://webcat2.library.ubc.ca/vwebv/search?searchArg=masculindians&amp;searchCode=TALL&amp;setLimit=1&amp;recCount=100&amp;searchType=1&amp;page.search.search.button=Search)

[Image result for "kim anderson" author](https://www.canadianscholars.ca/media/W1siZiIsIjIwMTQvMDEvMTYvMjMvMDEvNTIvODI5L0tpbV9BbmRlcnNvbi5qcGciXSxbInAiLCJ0aHVtYiIsIjMwMHgzMDBcdTAwM2UiXV0/4fe2e7ba4c9d15d1/Kim-Anderson) [Image](https://i.cbc.ca/1.5119795.1556805730!/fileImage/httpImage/image.jpg_gen/derivatives/16x9_780/rob-innes.jpg)

***Indigenous Men and Masculinities: Legacies, Identities, Regeneration***

Innes and Anderson bring together prominent thinkers to explore the meaning of masculinities and being a man within such traditions, further examining the colonial disruption and imposition of patriarchy on Indigenous men\. Building on Indigenous knowledge systems, Indigenous feminism, and queer theory, the sixteen essays by scholars and activists from Canada, the U\.S\., and New Zealand open pathways for the nascent field of Indigenous masculinities\. The authors explore subjects of representation through art and literature, as well as Indigenous masculinities in sport, prisons, and gangs\.

For CBC Interview with Rob Innes \(co\-editor\) see [here](https://www.cbc.ca/radio/unreserved/indigenous-masculinity-and-the-lasting-impacts-of-colonization-1.5109762/for-indigenous-men-masculinity-can-be-a-glass-ceiling-of-sorts-professor-says-1.5109769)\.
