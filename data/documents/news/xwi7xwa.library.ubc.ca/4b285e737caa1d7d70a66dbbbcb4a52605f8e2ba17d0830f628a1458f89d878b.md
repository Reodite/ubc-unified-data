To honour and commemorate National Indigenous People’s Day, Xwi7xwa Library staff have curated a list of books that discuss Indigenous histories, poetry, survival, social conditions, youth, and identity\.

***Memoirs***

[From the Ashes \| Book by Jesse Thistle \| Official Publisher Page \| Simon &amp; Schuster Canada](https://d28hgpri8am2if.cloudfront.net/book_images/onix/cvr9781982101213/from-the-ashes-9781982101213_hr.jpg)

[**From the Ashes**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=10054986) by Jesse Thistle

Call Number: YS T45 F76 2019

This debut memoir by Jesse Thistle, a Métis Cree scholar, explores issues Thistle faced in his early life in Saskatchewan and Ontario\. Including struggles with addiction, and experiences of homelessness, it also touches on themes of resilience, love, and reconnecting with one’s identity\. Winning the Kobo Emerging Writer Prize for Nonfiction, the Indigenous Voices Award, High Plains Book Award, and being a finalist for CBC Canada Reads, *From the Ashes* has remained a Canadian bestseller for its contributions to Indigenous literature and memoir writing\.

[They Called Me Number One \| CBC Books](https://i.cbc.ca/1.3995532.1487859199!/fileImage/httpImage/image.jpg_gen/derivatives/original_620/book-cover-they-called-me-number-one-by-bev-sellars.jpg)

[**They Called Me Number One**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=6549711) by Bev Sellars

Call Number: ER S45 T44 2013

Writer and Chief of Xat’sull First Nation, Bev Sellars shares her harrowing childhood experiences from attending the St\. Joseph’s Mission church residential school in British Columbia\. Sellars explores themes of resilience, trauma, identity, as well as touching on her advocacy work with Indigenous rights and environmental issues\. *They Called Me Number One* has received critical acclaim, winning the 2014 George Ryga Award for Social Awareness and is widely recognized for its candid portrayal of the residential school system, call for acknowledgement, and healing journey in Indigenous communities\.

***Fiction***

[10 essential reads for National Indigenous Peoples Day \| Lifestyle](https://i.cbc.ca/1.5451972.1580854307!/fileImage/httpImage/image.jpg_gen/derivatives/original_780/book-cover-five-little-indians-by-michelle-good.jpg)

[**Five Little Indians**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=11469605) by Michelle Good

Call Number: YF G66 F58 2020

*Five Little Indians* explores the lives of Kenny, Lucy, Maisie, Clara, and Howie, five children who are forcibly taken from their families and sent to a church\-run residential school in Canada\. The novel portrays the abuse they endure as well as the long\-term effects this trauma has on them as they navigate their later adult lives in Vancouver\. While touching on heavy themes, *Five Little Indians* is also a story of resilience, healing, friendship, growth, and love\.

[Son of a Trickster: Robinson, Eden: 9780345810786: Books \- Amazon\.ca](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/51fXta61+YL._SY445_SX342_.jpg)

[**Son of a Trickster**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=8593676) by Eden Robinson

Call Number: YF R63 S66 2017

The first book in Robinson’s Trickster trilogy, *Son of a Trickster* follows 16\-year\-old Jared Martin, a teenager from Kitimat, BC\. Throughout the story, Jared navigates life coming from a broken family, substance abuse, and social pressures all whilst discovering his connection to the Haisla trickster, Wee’jit\. Drawing heavily on Haisla and Heiltsuk oral storytelling traditions, Robinson creates a riveting coming of age story that touches on family dysfunction, identity, Indigenous culture, myths, humour, and heartbreak\.

[Indian Horse \| Milkweed Editions](https://milkweed.org/sites/default/files/books/front/IndianHorse_300dpi_RGB.jpg)

[**Indian Horse**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=9098900) by Richard Wagamese

Call Number: YF W34 N36 2018

\(Also, a movie adaptation from 2018\!\! The DVD can be taken out [on loan](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=9170830) and the stream is available [here](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=11822421)\.\)

Originally released in 2012, *Indian Horse* follows Saul, an Ojibway boy, who survives the residential school system and grows up to become a talented ice hockey player, only for his past childhood traumas to resurface in adulthood\. The novel explores themes of survival, healing, identity, and racism in sports\. In 2013, the novel won the Burt Award for First Nations, Métis, and Inuit Literature, and a film adaptation of the same name was released in 2018\.

***Graphic Novels***

[This Place: 150 Years Retold : Akiwenzie\-Damm, Kateri, Assu, Sonny, Mitchell, Brandon, Qitsualik \.\.\.](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/51yQMzqLtQL._SY445_SX342_ML2_.jpg)

[**This Place: 150 Years Retold**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=9739173) by varying authors

Call Number: YUC T45 2019

Explore the last 150 years of Canadian history through the eyes of Indigenous creators in this graphic novel anthology\. Emotional and enlightening stories revolve around magical realism, serial killings, psychic battles, time travel, and what a post\-apocalyptic world could look like since settler contact\. After reading the graphic novel, you can also enjoy listening to the 10\-episode podcast hosted by Rosanna Deerchild on CBC books which explores the stories through audio, music, and more\!

[A Girl Called Echo: Omnibus by Katherena Vermette, illustrated by Scott B\. Henderson \| CBC Books](https://i.cbc.ca/1.6946980.1692912281!/fileImage/httpImage/image.jpg_gen/derivatives/original_1180/a-girl-called-echo-omnibus-by-katherena-vermette-illustrated-by-scott-b-henderson.jpg?im=)

[**A Girl Called Echo**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=12963149) by Katherena Vermette

Call Number: YUC V47 G57 2023

The first in a graphic novel series, *A Girl Called Echo* follows a 13\-year\-old Métis girl named Echo Desjardins as she navigates foster care and the separation from her mother while discovering her ability to time travel\. She slips back in time to pivotal moments from Métis history like the Pemmican Wars of 1814 and Red River Resistance in 1869\. Echo discovers more about her identity, resilience, and what it means to belong\. The series has been praised for its ability to portray Métis history in an engaging and accessible way for young readers\.

***Non\-Fiction***

[As We Have Always Done: Indigenous Freedom through Radical Resistance: Simpson, Leanne \.\.\.](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/71cS6qsneWL._SL1200_.jpg)

[**As We Have Always Done**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=8918648) by Leanne Betasamosake Simpson

Call Number: KZ S56 W4 2017

Simpson’s *As We Have Always Done* is an exploration of Indigenous resistance and political insurgence\. The importance of Indigenous theorizing, writing, and organizing is emphasized throughout the book with the author discussing Indigenous resistance as a radical rejection of contemporary colonialism\. She calls for unapologetic, place\-based Indigenous alternatives to the destructive logics of the settler colonial state, including the heteropatriarchy, white supremacy, and capitalist exploitation\.

[Truth Telling by Michelle Good \| CBC Books](https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th/id/OIP.kAZXcOpUNVvFGnbWQtj0-gHaLH?r=0&amp;rs=1&amp;pid=ImgDetMain&amp;o=7&amp;rm=3)

[**Truth Telling**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=12832180) by Michelle Good

Call Number: YR G66 T78 2023

*Truth Telling* is a collection of seven personal essays that explore a range of issues affecting Indigenous peoples in Canada today, including reconciliation, the rise of Indigenous literature in the 1970’s and the impact it has today, the ongoing issue of “pretendians,” and so much more\. The collection reveals facts about Indigenous life in Canada that are both devastating and enlightening\. Good affirms that meaningful and substantive reconciliation relies on the recognition of Indigenous self\-determination, the return of lands, and a just redistribution of the wealth that has been taken from such lands without any regard for Indigenous peoples\.

***Poetry***

[Pemmican Eaters, The eBook : Marilyn, Dumont: Amazon\.ca: Books](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/51C0BJSCKlL._SY445_SX342_QL70_ML2_.jpg)

[**The Pemmican Eaters**](https://resolve.library.ubc.ca/cgi-bin/catsearch?bid=8095967) by Marilyn Dumont

Call Number: YP D861 P46 2015

Combining free verse and metered poems, *The Pemmican Eaters* aims to recreate a sense of the Riel Resistance period and evoke the geographical, linguistic/cultural, and political situation of Batoche during this time through the eyes of those who experienced the battles\. There are poems about the bison, seed beadwork, and the Red River Cart with some poems exploring the Michif language, as well as French and Cree, all languages spoken by Dumont’s ancestors\.
