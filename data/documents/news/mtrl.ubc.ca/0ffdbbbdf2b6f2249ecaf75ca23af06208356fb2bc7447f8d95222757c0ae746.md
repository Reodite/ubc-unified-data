## Navigating the Semiconductor Frontier at Intel

[Image](https://apsc-mtrl.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/09/Alumni-Guangnan-Zhou.png)

  
I’m currently working as a Yield Development Engineer at Intel, where I lead post\-silicon characterization and debugging efforts to support product validation and yield improvement\. I coordinate cross\-functional teams for electrical and physical failure analysis, driving root\-cause investigations and defect resolution\. I also develop Python\-based tools to automate statistical data analysis, enabling faster decision\-making and improved collaboration\. I really enjoy working across the entire cycle of semiconductor development – from transistor\-level design to full\-chip validation\. This allows me to engage with design, fabrication, testing, and post\-silicon analysis, and continually expand my knowledge and skills\.

**Always learning: the more you know, the more you realize you don’t know\.**

The semiconductor industry is well known for its fast pace\. As devices become more complex and process nodes shrink, debugging and validation require increasingly sophisticated tools and approaches\. Staying informed with the latest knowledge and methodologies, while solving intricate issues under tight timelines, demands both adaptability and deep technical insight\.

****How did your graduate studies in Materials Engineering prepare you for your current career?****

I have always been fascinated by semiconductor devices and wanted to deepen my understanding through hands\-on research since my undergraduate degree\. My graduate studies at UBC provided a solid foundation in semiconductor device physics, fabrication processes, and advanced characterization techniques\.

During my PhD, I worked extensively on Gallium Nitride High Electron Mobility Transistors \(GaN HEMTs\), which sharpened my skills and deepened my knowledge in semiconductor device physics, device fabrication, reliability analysis, and failure mechanisms\. Those skills have translated well to my current job\.

I was incredibly fortunate to have Dr\. Guangrui \(Maggie\) Xia as my PhD supervisor\. Her support and mentorship played a pivotal role in shaping my academic and professional journey, and I’m deeply grateful for the opportunity she gave me to pursue advanced research in this field\.

****What did you enjoy the most about your time as a graduate student in Materials Engineering?****

What I enjoyed most was the opportunity to dive deep into hands\-on research while being part of a supportive and intellectually stimulating environment\. Performing a wide range of materials characterization techniques helped me develop an engineering problem\-solving mindset and technical depth – both critical in the semiconductor industry\.

I also appreciated the collaborative culture within the department\. I had the chance to discuss ideas with peers and learn from others at the same time\. Graduate studies are challenging but rewarding – they truly laid the groundwork for my professional career\.

****What is your best piece of advice for current graduate students preparing for their future careers?****

Seize the days, gather the flowers while you may\.
