---

### What first motivated you to become an educator?

I always enjoyed teaching and learning, and worked as a tutor, volunteer teacher, and teaching assistant in high school and my BSc\. During my graduate studies here at UBC, I was fortunate to have many wonderful teaching opportunities, which really helped clarify for me that I enjoyed teaching, was good at it, and cared about improving students' learning experience\.

---

### Tell us more about your work\.

I teach core and upper\-year computer science courses, with a focus on active learning and scalable teaching strategies\. My work extends beyond the classroom: I mentor new instructors and explore innovations like flipped classrooms and AI\-permissive assessments\. I aim to create learning environments that are rigorous yet supportive, and to build capacity for excellent teaching, especially in large, multi\-section courses where consistency and engagement are critical\.

---

### What inspired your particular approach to teaching?

My approach was shaped by early experiences with students who feared failure and hesitated to take on challenges\. I realized that learning is often uncomfortable \(and that this is okay\!\)\. This inspired me to create a classroom culture that values effort, creativity, and peer collaboration\. I use active learning, open\-ended problems, and structured support to help students build confidence and independence—skills that are essential in a fast\-evolving field like computer science\.

---

### What have you learned while teaching that has surprised you the most?

I've been surprised by the importance of structure\. On the teaching side, a well\-structured question or exercise can scaffold student learning and encourage students to engage where they might otherwise have not known how to start\. And on the administrative side, I've found that clear expectations and consistent policies help reduce anxiety and foster trust\. Even if you're being slightly strict, students can appreciate that you're being fair and transparent\.

---

### What impact do you hope to have on your students?

I hope to help students become confident, independent learners who embrace challenges and think critically\. I want them to leave my courses not just with technical skills, but with the ability to learn on their own, collaborate effectively, and persist through difficulty\. I also aim to model balance and inclusivity, so students see that success in computing—and in life—can take many forms and doesn’t require sacrificing well\-being\.

---

### Are there any colleagues or mentors you’d like to acknowledge and why?

My PhD advisor, Chen Greif, has been a big source of mentorship and support both during my PhD and in the time since\. Steve Wolfman also helped guide me through my first teaching experience and shape my instructional approach\. More broadly, I’ve learned a great deal from many generous and thoughtful colleagues—through collaboration, conversation, and example—and I continue to be inspired by the teaching community around me\.
