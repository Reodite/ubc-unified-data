### Provide an overview of your research in 75 words or less:

I am a community researcher leading a qualitative research project exploring intimate partner violence \(IPV\) experienced by Canadian physicians\. We are particularly interested in how the experience of physicians may differ from other Canadians experiencing domestic abuse \(DA\), including which barriers are accessed and how\. Also, we are exploring differences in the experiences of rural and urban physicians with IPV/DA\. This study is funded by the Rural Co\-ordination Centre of BC\.

---

### What first motivated you \(or motivates you\) to conduct your research?

I want to help save the lives of my colleagues\. Despite the many resources available to physicians, doctors experience intimate partner violence \(IPV\) at higher rates than the general public\. This is shocking, and I was motivated to find out more about why\. Doctors are trained to recognize signs of abuse in our patients, but we fail to recognize our colleagues experiencing IPV\. Under\-recognition is deadly\. Silence on IPV is deadly\.

---

### What do you hope will change as a result of this research?

Our hope for this initial qualitative study is that it will increase our knowledge about physician experiences with IPV and the barriers that physicians, especially rural physicians, may face when addressing those relationships, so that we can begin to develop effective interventions for physicians\. The findings of this study will be of interest to medical education and physician wellness, but also to other "helping professions" who also experience higher than average rates of IPV\.

---

### Are there any research collaborators you'd like to acknowledge and why?

The [Rural Scholar Program at UBC](https://crhr.med.ubc.ca/rural-scholars-program/) has been essential to the success of this research project\! The Rural Scholar program facilitates research by rural clinical medical faculty and builds capacity for medical/clinical research in rural BC\. Dr\. Denise Jaworsky, Director, and Dr\. Jenny Phillips, fellow Rural Scholar, have been amazingly supportive in this work\! The Rural Co\-ordination Centre of BC's Physician Research Grant, which encourages novice rural researchers, has made this study possible\.

---

### What have you learned during your research that has surprised you the most?

Stigma surrounds the topic of intimate partner violence\. That stigma breeds silence, and that silence is deadly\. Naysayers warned there would be little uptake for a qualitative project tackling IPV as directly as we are\. But the support of our medical and academic communities has been overwhelming\! Our participants have been vulnerable and brave, courageously telling their stories, hoping for change\. Sharing the learnings from our brave participants, this study combats stigma and ultimately will save lives\.

---
