ImageWe’re excited to share the [latest episode](https://blogs.ubc.ca/swkx/episodes/episode-12/) of our podcast on **Eloping: Autism and Community\-Driven Research\.**

In this episode, **Nicole Corrado**, **Sandra Nakata**, **[Tina Wilson](https://socialwork.ubc.ca/profile/tina-wilson/)** and **[Meg Gibson](https://uwaterloo.ca/social-development-studies/profiles/margaret-f-meg-gibson)** discuss their community\-led research project that explores autistic people’s perspectives and experiences of elopement – when autistic individuals depart suddenly from supervised settings and relationships without permission or notice\.

Rooted in lived experience and informed by a Participatory Action Research approach, the project explores the potential that partnership offers in transforming current practices of policing autistic people’s behaviours and encouraging belonging through understanding\. Together, the team shares how the project emerged from community needs, reflects on the harms of epistemic injustice, and explores how rethinking social norms can better support autistic individuals’ safety and autonomy\.

The conversation invites listeners insights into the experiences of elopement and the process of co\-creation and co\-production of research\.
