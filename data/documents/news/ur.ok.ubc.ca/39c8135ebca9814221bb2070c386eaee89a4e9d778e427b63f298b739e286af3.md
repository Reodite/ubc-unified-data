In the event of heavy snow or an extreme weather event, the UBC Okanagan homepage \([ok\.ubc\.ca](https://ok.ubc.ca/)\) is the official source for campus updates, including information about class cancellations and service disruptions

For public transit service updates, visit [Kelowna Regional Transit](https://www.bctransit.com/kelowna/home) and [Vernon Regional Transit](https://bctransit.com/vernon/home)\.

## Winter weather protocol

Heavy snowfall or extreme weather may result in the cancellation or rescheduling of classes or the curtailment of non\-essential services\. The university remains open unless otherwise communicated\.

**UBC’s Board of Governors policy states:**

*“The University will remain open during snow storms but may cancel or reschedule classes on a university\-wide basis and/or curtail non\-essential services in response to conditions\.”* — [UBC Policy \#68: Disruption Of Classes/Services By Snow](https://universitycounsel.ubc.ca/policies/snow-policy/)

### Frequently asked questions

### 

Who decides to cancel classes and call a snow day?

University officials monitor weather and travel conditions during extreme snowfall and consult with BC Transit, School District 23 and local public safety authorities to assess conditions\.

The Deputy Vice\-Chancellor and Principal may cancel classes when weather conditions warrant\. During a snow day, all credit and non\-credit classes are cancelled and rescheduled where possible\. Necessary services remain active\.

**UBC Okanagan will generally maintain scheduled classes when BC Transit is operating regular service, public schools remain open and no local travel safety notices have been issued by the City of Kelowna or RCMP\.**

### 

How is a snow day communicated?

A decision is first communicated to deans and senior administrators\. Following overnight snowfall, the objective is to make and begin communicating a decision by 6am\.

**If classes are cancelled, a notice will be posted on the UBC Okanagan homepage at** **[ok\.ubc\.ca](http://ok.ubc.ca/)** and shared through UBC Okanagan’s primary social media channels\. Local news media will also be notified as early as possible\.

The [UBC Alert](https://operations.ok.ubc.ca/security-and-emergencies/ubc-safeapp-and-ubc-alert/) messaging system may also be used to share information through text messages and voice calls\.

**Department heads and administrators** should have procedures in place to notify students, faculty and staff when classes are cancelled or non\-necessary services are curtailed\.

Faculty and staff should be familiar with these procedures and know where to find information about winter weather disruptions\. Additional communications may include signage in high\-traffic areas, faculty or department website updates, emails, social media posts that share official notices and telephone trees\.

### 

What are the necessary services?

Necessary services include food services for students in residence, heating plant operations and campus security\.

Employees designated by their supervisor to provide necessary services are expected to work during snow days\.

### 

Do I have to go to work during a snow day?

When classes are cancelled due to snow and non\-necessary services are curtailed, faculty and staff scheduled to work will receive their regular salary or wages if they choose to stay home\.

Employees travelling to campus should prioritize a safe commute\. Those unable to report to work due to weather conditions should contact their supervisor as early as possible\.

### 

Can I leave work early on a snow day?

If weather conditions deteriorate during the workday, administrators may permit staff who are not designated to provide necessary services to leave early without loss of pay, following direction from the Deputy Vice\-Chancellor and Principal’s Office\.

### Campus safety

Facilities Management manages snow and ice on the UBC Okanagan campus during extreme weather\.

Information about snow and ice management and reporting slippery conditions is available on the [UBCO Facilities Management website](https://operations.ok.ubc.ca/grounds-and-waste-management/snow-and-ice-removal/)\.

Appropriate footwear is recommended when travelling around campus during winter conditions\. Building main entrances should be used whenever possible\.
