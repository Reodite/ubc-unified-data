Dr\. S\. Larry Goldenberg has been named the recipient of the 2026 UBC Medicine Bill and Marilyn Webber Lifetime Achievement Award, recognizing more than four decades of leadership that have transformed prostate cancer care, advanced urologic sciences at UBC, and strengthened men’s health across Canada\.

[Dr\. S\. Larry Goldenberg](https://med-fom-urlgsci.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/Larry-Goldenberg-703x960.png)

The award recognizes extraordinary members of the Faculty of Medicine who have had sustained distinguished careers at UBC in research, teaching, or service\.

Twenty years after becoming the founding head of UBC’s Department of Urologic Sciences, Dr\. Goldenberg is being honoured for a career combining scientific discovery with compassionate care, mentorship, and institution\-building\. His work has improved the lives of patients and families while helping establish UBC and British Columbia as internationally recognized centres of prostate cancer research and urologic care\.

> “Receiving the Bill and Marilyn Webber Lifetime Achievement Award from the UBC Faculty of Medicine is a profound honour, one I never dreamt of receiving, especially when I look at the list of previous awardees, but this milestone does not belong to me alone\. It belongs to our entire urology department, clinicians, nurses, researchers, administrative staff, and the extraordinary network of colleagues across our affiliated institutions\.”
> 
> – Dr\. S\. Larry Goldenberg, Professor at UBC Department of Urologic Sciences

Dr\. Goldenberg will be formally recognized at the UBC Faculty of Medicine Awards Reception this fall\.

## Advancing prostate cancer research and care

When Dr\. Goldenberg began his career as a surgeon\-scientist in 1984, prostate cancer treatment options were limited, and many fundamental questions remained unanswered\.

His early research examined prostate cancer biology and how planned breaks from androgen deprivation could affect treatment response\. He later led influential early clinical trials of this intermittent approach, which pauses and restarts treatment that reduces the hormones prostate cancer relies on to grow\. Conducted through the Canadian Urologic Oncology Group, a clinical trials consortium that he co\-founded in 1989, the studies helped shape subsequent research and clinical care\.

Dr\. Goldenberg also advanced research on treatment before radical prostatectomy and became an early champion of active surveillance for people with low\-risk prostate cancer, supporting careful monitoring more than a decade before the approach became widely accepted\.

As care evolved, he led the early adoption of robotic\-assisted prostatectomy at Vancouver General Hospital\. He also supported the integration of advanced imaging into prostate cancer diagnosis and surveillance\. More recently, his research has explored AI\-assisted histopathology, multiparametric ultrasound, advanced imaging, and targeted radioligands\.

[Dr\. Goldenberg shows the da Vinci surgical robot to Mr\. Jim Pattison\.](https://med-fom-urlgsci.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/LarryGoldenberg_daVinciRobot-940x520.jpg)

Dr\. Goldenberg shows the da Vinci surgical robot to Mr\. Jim Pattison\.

“At the core of Dr\. Goldenberg’s work has always been an unwavering commitment to improving the lives of patients,” said Dr\. Martin Gleave, Distinguished Professor at UBC Department of Urologic Sciences and Chief Scientific Officer at the M\.H\. Mohseni Institute of Urologic Sciences\. “Through his ability to combine compassionate patient care with scientific innovation and academic leadership, Dr\. Goldenberg has helped transform the landscape of prostate cancer care and men’s health\.”

## Building a collaborative home for urologic sciences

Dr\. Goldenberg believed progress would accelerate by bringing clinicians, scientists, engineers, learners, and health\-system partners together around the needs of patients\.

He became executive director of the UBC Prostate Clinic in 1993 and the Prostate Centre at Vancouver General Hospital in 1998, helping create a multidisciplinary environment that brought discovery, treatment, education, and supportive care together\.

This collaborative model helped lay the groundwork for a major transformation in urologic sciences at UBC\. After becoming head of the Division of Urology within the Department of Surgery in 2000, Dr\. Goldenberg accelerated its growth in clinical care, education, and research\.

By 2006, the division had become a major contributor to the Department of Surgery’s research funding and developed the critical mass needed to become an independent department\. Dr\. Goldenberg was instrumental in the transition and served as founding head of the Department of Urologic Sciences from July 2006 to June 2014\.

The new department placed UBC among the few Canadian universities with a standalone academic urology department, increasing its capacity to recruit faculty, educate future specialists, expand research, and build interdisciplinary partnerships\.

In 2015, the Goldenberg Family Chair in Urologic Sciences was established to provide enduring support for the Department Head\.

[Mr\. Mohammad Mohseni and Dr\. Larry Goldenberg \(2024\)](https://med-fom-urlgsci.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/LarryGoldenberg_Mohammad-Mohseni-940x940.jpg)

Mr\. Mohammad Mohseni and Dr\. Larry Goldenberg \(2024\)

Over his career, Dr\. Goldenberg helped lead teams and initiatives that attracted more than $550 million in research funding and philanthropic investment, strengthening programs and creating opportunities for colleagues and emerging investigators\.

His vision for bringing the diverse fields of urologic sciences together reached a new milestone in 2024\. In collaboration with Dr\. Martin Gleave and a broader team of clinical, research, and philanthropic partners, Dr\. Goldenberg helped make that vision a reality through the creation of the [M\.H\. Mohseni Institute of Urologic Sciences](https://mius.ca/about/)\. Building on the success of the Vancouver Prostate Centre, a longstanding leader in prostate and bladder cancer research and care, the Institute brings six centres together to advance research and care across the broader field of urologic sciences\.

As a campaign co\-chair, Dr\. Goldenberg also helped bring together partners and donors supporting VGH &amp; UBC Hospital Foundation’s $65 million Urologic Sciences campaign\. Today, researchers, clinicians, and trainees continue to build on the collaborative foundation he helped establish\.

“Few clinician\-scientists have left a legacy as far\-reaching as Dr\. Goldenberg\. Through his vision and leadership, he helped build the Vancouver Prostate Centre, championed the growth of the Mohseni Institute for Urologic Sciences, and established the Department of Urologic Sciences at UBC\. These institutions continue to drive innovation, train future leaders, and improve patient outcomes around the world,” reflected Dr\. Peter Black, Professor and Head of the UBC Department of Urologic Sciences and Director of the Vancouver Prostate Centre\.

## Supporting patients and advancing men’s health

Dr\. Goldenberg understood that advancing treatment was only part of the challenge\. Because prostate cancer can affect every aspect of life, he believed excellent care must support patients and families throughout treatment and beyond\.

That conviction led him to establish the Prostate Cancer Supportive Care Program at the Vancouver Prostate Centre, a comprehensive survivorship program addressing quality of life, informed decision\-making, and the physical and psychological effects of prostate cancer and its treatment\.

He also recognized the importance of accessible information\. In 1991, he first published *Prostate Cancer: All You Need to Know to Take an Active Part in Your Treatment*\. Its fourth edition, released in 2014, reflects advances in care and his enduring commitment to patient education\. Together, these efforts broadened prostate cancer care to emphasize not only disease outcomes but also how well people are supported during and after treatment\.

Dr\. Goldenberg also saw a broader challenge: too many men were not seeking care early or accessing the information and support they needed to stay healthy\.

In 2009, he founded the Men’s Health Initiative of British Columbia, which laid the groundwork for the Canadian Men’s Health Foundation, established in 2014 with Dr\. Goldenberg as chair\. Through education, prevention, advocacy, and accessible health information, these initiatives encouraged men to become more active participants in their health and helped establish men’s health as a field of academic inquiry and advocacy in Canada\.

## Creating opportunities for others

Over more than four decades, Dr\. Goldenberg taught and mentored medical students, residents, fellows, graduate students, and junior faculty, many of whom went on to careers in clinical care, research, and academic leadership in Canada and internationally\.

For more than two decades, he co\-chaired the Issues and Controversies in Prostate Care conference\. He also served the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada for more than a decade as an examiner, chief examiner, and specialty chair, helping educate generations of urologists and shape professional standards\.

His scholarly record includes more than 425 peer\-reviewed articles, as well as book chapters, educational videos, and conference presentations\. Colleagues also remember the personal ways he fostered talent, from holding informal “fireside chats” with residents to championing emerging researchers and creating opportunities for others\.

“What distinguishes him is not any single achievement but the coherence and integrity of a career that has always been oriented toward building something larger than himself,” said Michael A\.S\. Jewett, Professor Emeritus at the University of Toronto\.

Dr\. Goldenberg’s honours include appointments to the Order of British Columbia and the Order of Canada, along with lifetime and distinguished career awards from leading Canadian and international urological associations\.

The Bill and Marilyn Webber Lifetime Achievement Award brings that recognition home to UBC, where he trained as a resident, built his academic career, and helped transform urologic sciences\.

Dr\. Goldenberg continues to advance research, mentorship, fundraising, and men’s health, supporting the people and ideas that will move the field forward\. More than four decades after his career began, the full measure of his achievement lies not only in what he has accomplished but also in what he has made possible for others\.

> “The true metric of this legacy is the future potential of the Department and Mohseni Institute\. Those following in my footsteps possess the talent, curiosity, and drive to far exceed what I and my generation accomplished\. The future of health innovation at UBC Urologic Sciences is in their hands, and I have absolute confidence that they will lead our institution into a brilliant new era\.”
> 
> – Dr\. S\. Larry Goldenberg, Professor at UBC Department of Urologic Sciences
