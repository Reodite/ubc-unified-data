**Timeline:** Jan 2024 – December 2025

**Committee:** Melissa McHale, PhD; Matthew Mitchell, PhD

Urban trees and their canopies provide crucial regulating ecosystem services to the urban population, in the face of climate change and urbanization\. As cities grow both in size, population, and infrastructure climate change related extreme weather events\. The two weather extremes most applicable to Vancouver are Urban Heat Islands \(UHI\) and Atmospheric rivers\.

Biophysical processes such as tree canopy interception of solar radiation and rainfall, as well as evapotranspiration altering moisture of soil and air in the immediate surrounding of trees, have effects on the microclimate\. Understanding how different canopy characteristics play a role in providing heat mitigation and rainfall interception services is necessary for understanding the potential of urban forests in supporting climate adaptation\. Additionally, the spatial distribution of these two services is also of interest for future urban forest design and management\.
