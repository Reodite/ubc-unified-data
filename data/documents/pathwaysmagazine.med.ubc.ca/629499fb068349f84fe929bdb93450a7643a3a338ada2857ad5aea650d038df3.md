Dean’s Message

We live in extraordinary times\. Medicine and medical education must adapt\. The UBC Faculty of Medicine is leading the way, here at home and on the global stage

[Image](https://med-fom-pathways-2025.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/02/Sharmila-Anandasabapathy_940x625_v2.jpg)

Dr\. Sharmila Anandasabapathy, Dean, Faculty of Medicine and Vice\-President, Health, UBC

Necessity is the mother of invention, and innovation\. As doctors, scientists and health professionals, we’re often at our very best when faced with problems that require a transformative solution — whether it’s finding a better, lower\-cost treatment for a chronic disease, adapting a surgical technique to a new context, or delivering care that is effective and culturally safe to remote and underserved communities\.

I have learned this first\-hand in my career as a clinician\-scientist with a focus on translational research and global health equity\. Working in countries like Malawi, India and Brazil, where resources can be scarce, I’ve seen how innovative approaches to treatment, care and training — often developed under difficult circumstances in response to an urgent local need — can go on to become powerful, game\-changing health solutions for the world\.

The other lesson I’ve taken to heart is that better health starts with training and education\. We need to evolve the way we train doctors, researchers and health professionals so they have not only the skills and experience to deliver the best possible care and treatment, but also the creative, interdisciplinary mindset to find new and even better ways of doing things\.

> ## The old boundaries between disciplines, between research and practice, between local and global, are dissolving\. Medicine and medical education must adapt\.

In my new role as Dean of the UBC Faculty of Medicine and Vice\-President, Health, UBC, I see this conviction woven through everything the Faculty does\. It’s what animates the Faculty’s province\-wide education and training programs, which, in their scale and ambition, are unlike any other I’ve seen in North America\.

They include Canada’s largest family medicine residency program, immersive training in rural medicine, and interdisciplinary graduate programs that bring researchers and clinicians together in a highly dynamic biotech ecosystem\.

From urban centres to rural, remote and Indigenous communities, UBC students and resident doctors learn and train in every imaginable setting where care is delivered, gaining the diverse skills required to meet the changing health needs of people in British Columbia and beyond\.

That’s more than 5,000 learners training under the guidance of over 13,000 academic and clinical faculty at more than 145 sites across a region the size of Western Europe\. And 93 per cent of UBC MD grads who complete postgraduate medical training at UBC stay and practice in B\.C\.

In the new issue of Pathways, you will take a journey across the province and beyond to meet some of the many people who are bringing this extraordinary network to life\.

You’ll meet dedicated clinical faculty [mentoring the next generation of doctors and health professionals](https://pathwaysmagazine.med.ubc.ca/feature-stories/a-day-in-the-life-of-ubc-clinical-faculty/) in busy hospitals and community clinics\. You’ll follow MD students [immersed in rural and Indigenous communities](https://pathwaysmagazine.med.ubc.ca/snapshots/northern-immersion/) where culturally sensitive, relationship\-based care shapes both practice and purpose\. You’ll see how these experiences inspire so many UBC graduates to build their careers where they train, strengthening care across the province\.

You’ll also discover how resident doctors across more than 75 specialty and sub\-specialty programs are [delivering complex care while they hone their skills](https://pathwaysmagazine.med.ubc.ca/snapshots/resident-doctors-on-the-front-lines/); how clinician\-scientists and graduate students are [advancing clinical trials and community\-based research](https://pathwaysmagazine.med.ubc.ca/snapshots/training-locally-building-research-capacity-for-bc-and-the-world/) in the North and Interior; and how clinical faculty and biomedical engineering students are together [creating innovative tools](https://pathwaysmagazine.med.ubc.ca/snapshots/how-an-innovative-low-cost-training-tool-is-delivering-better-surgical-care-for-women-around-the-world/) like STITCH, a made\-at\-UBC surgical training simulator for better post\-partum care here at home and around the world\.

Together, these stories show what’s possible when medical education is integrated, interdisciplinary and grounded in community\.

We live in extraordinary times, both in terms of the growing challenges to human health but also the rapidly expanding capabilities of modern technology\. Health systems are under strain\. The old boundaries between disciplines, between research and practice, between local and global, are dissolving\. Medicine and medical education must adapt\.

It’s a challenge and an opportunity\.

The UBC Faculty of Medicine is ready to lead the way, here at home and on the global stage\. Together with our government, health authority, academic and community partners, we can build the healthcare and life sciences workforce of the future — and create better health for **everyone, everywhere, every day**\.

Sincerely,

**Sharmila Anandasabapathy**, MD, AGAF, FASGE  
Dean, Faculty of Medicine  
Vice\-President, Health  
The University of British Columbia
