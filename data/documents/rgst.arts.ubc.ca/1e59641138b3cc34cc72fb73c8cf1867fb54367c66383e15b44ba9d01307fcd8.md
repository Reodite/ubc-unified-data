# Emergent Christianity as Neighbourhood Urban Religion

A neglected topic in the social historical analysis of the origins of Christ religion has tended to overlook the neighbourhood as central to its development\. Drawing on urban studies, this talk explores the faceblock neighbourhood and urban density as constituent components in the development and practices of some forms of Christianity\. The term Faceblock neighbourhood drawn from contemporary urban studies refers to neighbourhood life centred around and socially oriented to street facing residences\. The Roman insula created such faceblock neighbourhoods and high levels of urban density magnified the importance of social relations within and between insula\. The talk considers some of the ways that Christ religion promoted certain forms of neighbourhood social relations and socio\-economic connection to account for one of the ways that it represented a new form of neighbourhood religion\. In doing so it examines ways in which emergent Christ religion was at once affected by the organization and living quarters of cities and ways in which it also helped to shape social relations within them\. It thus discusses the reciprocal influence of city and religion in the case of emergent Christianity\.
