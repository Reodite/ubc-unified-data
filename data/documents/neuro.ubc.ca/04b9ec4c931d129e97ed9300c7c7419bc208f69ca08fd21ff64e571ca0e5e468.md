## About the EDI\-D Committee

The Equity, Diversity, Inclusivity and Decolonization \(EDI\-D\) Committee of the UBC Neuroscience Undergraduate Program is a student\-led body dedicated to fostering a more inclusive and equitable academic community\. We work collaboratively to support cultural awareness, accessibility, and fair opportunities for all members of the Neuroscience program — including undergraduate and graduate students, postdoctoral fellows, and faculty\.

While the committee does not intervene in individual cases, we serve as a supportive and advisory group by raising awareness of available resources, referring community members to appropriate services, and advocating for structural improvements in policies, programming, and campus culture\. Our work is grounded in transparency, student leadership, and meaningful collaboration with other trainee\-focused groups across UBC\.

## Our Mission

Our mission is to advocate for equity, diversity, inclusion and decolonization within the UBC Neuroscience community by identifying and addressing systemic barriers, supporting student\- and trainee\-led initiatives, and promoting mental health, accessibility, and representation\. We are committed to creating a learning and research environment where all individuals feel respected, supported, and empowered — regardless of background or identity\.

Through events, strategic planning, advocacy, and partnerships, we aim to embed EDI\-D principles into various aspects of the Neuroscience program\.

## Our Goals

As part of our ongoing commitment to equity, diversity, and inclusion, the EDI\-D Committee is currently focused on deepening our engagement with UBC’s Indigenous Strategic Plan \(ISP\) by identifying how we can further support its implementation within the Neuroscience program\.

Our key goals include:

• **Conducting a thorough review of the ISP** to highlight specific goals and commitments most relevant to neuroscience education and research\.  
• **Mapping existing efforts** within the UBC Neuroscience community that support Indigenous engagement, representation, and inclusion — including events, teaching practices, research, and student support initiatives\.  
• **Identifying gaps and opportunities** where the EDI\-D Committee can actively support or enhance current efforts, in collaboration with students, faculty, and partner organizations\.  
• **Developing actionable goals** and initiatives for the EDI\-D Committee that directly advance the ISP’s objectives — such as increasing Indigenous representation, creating culturally safe spaces, and promoting Indigenous\-led knowledge systems in neuroscience\.  

Through this work, we aim to support meaningful and lasting change by centering Indigenous perspectives and strengthening relationships between the Neuroscience program and Indigenous communities\.

## General Resources

**Campus Lightbox**

Campus Lightbox centralizes information about mental health and wellbeing resources on campus, and how students can best access them\. [More information](https://campuslightbox.com/)\.

**Health and Wellness**

The Faculty of Arts has complied a list of health and wellness support options available to students\. [More information](https://www.arts.ubc.ca/student-support/health-wellness/)\.

**UBC’s Student Health and Dental Plan** **\(Studentcare\)**

Studentcare covers 100% of the cost of a registered psychologist or clinical counselor up to $300 per year\. Eligible prescription drugs are covered up to 80%\. All students who pay AMS fees are automatically covered\. [More information](https://studentcare.ca/rte/en/UniversityofBritishColumbiaAMSGSS_Health_HealthCoverage_HealthPractitioners_Psychologists)\.

**Early Alert**

Our faculty participate in the **Early Alert** program, which helps us support students who are struggling in life and/or school\. [More information](https://facultystaff.students.ubc.ca/systems-tools/early-alert/information-students/students-frequently-asked-questions)\.
