## Reflections on the Life and Work of Jürgen Habermas: Intellectual Journeys Across Disciplines 

[Image](https://isgp2.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/06/Call-for-Submissions-Mantle-June-2026.png)

**This special issue invites scholars from all disciplines to reflect on how Jürgen Habermas’s life and work have shaped their own intellectual trajectories—whether in alignment with, or in critical response to, his ideas\. Submissions should not exceed 3,000 words \(including footnotes but excluding bibliography\) and follow APA citation style\.**

-   Manuscript Length: Maximum 3,000 words \(including footnotes, excluding bibliography\)

Eligibility: We welcome contributions from established and early\-career scholars, as well as those working at disciplinary intersections; we encourage collaborative works \(esp\. from and with students\); creative formats and styles of writing are likewise welcome\.

-   Publication Date \[15 Dec 2026\]

### **Call for Submission: Special Issue on the life and work of Jürgen Habermas \(1929\-2026\)** 

Few thinkers have left as profound and wide\-ranging a mark on contemporary thought as Jürgen Habermas \(1929\-2026\)\. Habermas’s intellectual journey has been inseparable from the major social, political, and philosophical currents of the twentieth and twenty\-first centuries\. His pioneering analyses of the public sphere, communicative action, discourse ethics, and deliberative democracy have transformed debates in philosophy, sociology, political science, law, communication studies, education, international relations, and theology\. 

This special issue seeks to honor and critically engage with the legacy of Habermas by inviting scholars to reflect on how his work has influenced their own intellectual development, their ideas regarding the responsibility of academia, interdisciplinary courage and/or public scholarship\. Whether working with or against his ideas, contributors are encouraged to explore the personal, disciplinary, and societal impact of Habermas’s thought\. 

We welcome submissions from across all the faculties that offer reflective, critical, or creative engagements with Habermas’s oeuvre\. Contributions may address, but are not limited to: 

-   Encounters with key Habermasian concepts such as the public sphere, communicative rationality, lifeworld vs\. system, discourse ethics, or deliberative democracy\. 
-   Intellectual journeys shaped by working alongside, extending, or contesting Habermas’s theories in your field\. 
-   The relevance of Habermas’s work to contemporary challenges: digital public spheres, multiculturalism, globalization, legitimacy crises, or the role of religion in secular societies\. 
-   Dialogues and debates with Habermas’s interlocutors \(e\.g\., Gadamer, Foucault, Rawls, Luhmann, Derrida\) and their impact on your thinking\. 
-   Reflections on the interdisciplinary reach of Habermas’s work in philosophy, sociology, political science, law, communication studies, education, international relations, or theology\. 
-   Settler colonialism and Habermas’s abandonment of democratic principles in support of Israel\. 
-   Should feminists and scholars of race, gender &amp; sexuality read Habermas? 
-   Do we need grand theorists anymore? 

Possible themes include, but are not limited to: 

-   Personal encounters with key texts, such as, *The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere* or *The Theory of Communicative Action* or *Between Facts &amp; Norms* or *The* *Philosophical Discourse of Modernity* 
-   Habermas and the rise and fall of Critical Theory 
-   Applying or critiquing discourse ethics in research or practice 
-   Habermas and colonialism 
-   Habermas and the unfinished project of modernity 
-   Lifeworld, system, and the challenges of contemporary society 
-   Deliberative democracy in theory and practice 
-   Habermas’s influence on legal, educational, or media studies 
-   The role of communicative rationality in interdisciplinary research 
-   Habermas and the postsecular turn: faith, reason, and pluralism 
-   The Language Turn and Habermas 

### About **Mantle**: The Annual Review of Interdisciplinary Research 

**Mantle** is the student\-run and peer\-reviewed online journal of the Interdisciplinary Studies Graduate Program \(ISGP\) at the University of British Columbia\. 

**Mantle** is an open\-access online journal dedicated to publishing thoughtfully provocative and rigorously reviewed work from across all disciplines\. We solicit manuscripts that open new fields of inquiry; create new intellectual methods, models or paradigms; and demonstrate how interdisciplinarity can work within and beyond disciplines as well as open new vistas\. The journal is a forum in which to argue, synthesize, report, and critique all issues in interdisciplinary theory and practice\. 

We seek a wide range of submissions on an ongoing basis, including research articles, literature reviews, essays, creative writing, graphical contributions, and more\.  

We are grateful to the Dean of Graduate and Postdoctoral Studies and the Teaching and Learning Enhancement Fund \(Office of the Provost and Vice\-President Academic\) for their ongoing support\. **Mantle** also owes its existence and success to the unswerving commitment and support of ISGP Administration\. 

Learn more: [https://isgp\.ubc\.ca/mantle/](https://isgp.ubc.ca/mantle/) 

We look forward to your submissions and to collectively reflecting on the ongoing impact of Jürgen Habermas’s life and thought\.
