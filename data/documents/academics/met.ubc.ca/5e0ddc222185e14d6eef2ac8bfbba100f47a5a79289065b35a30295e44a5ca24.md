## Five Lessons from Building Goosechase

Insights from a founder on building a company, shaping a product, and staying curious in a rapidly changing technological landscape\.

1.  **Don’t fall in love with your rigid vision too much\.**  
    A core part of our journey has been customers using the platform in ways we wouldn’t have expected, and us adapting to better support them\. It’s important to have a strong, compelling vision, but you need to be humble enough to take in new information and adjust\.
2.  **You can’t be everything to everyone\.**  
    One of the hardest things for a very adaptable product like ours is that we are constantly getting requests or ideas for how it could be improved\. If we were somehow able to do everything, it would actually make the product worse since it would become a Frankenstein’s monster situation\. So having the courage to say “no, that’s not a core part of where we’re trying to go” is very hard, especially early on when you’re figuring everything out, but so crucial\.
3.  **When it comes to building a team, aim for fewer, better people, and treat them amazingly well\.**  
    That’s been a core part of our company strategy since the very beginning\. Because we aren’t a venture\-backed company, we were never going to win the pure monetary compensation game, but we *could* make Goosechase the best place to work, which we doubled down on\. That first led us to being fully remote well before COVID, as well as being one of the first companies to adopt a four\-day week, which has been a game\-changer\.
4.  **Fight the inner curmudgeon and stay curious and optimistic\.**  
    I’ve been doing this 15 years now, which means several waves of technology have come and gone during that time, the latest being AI\. It’s really easy, the older you get, to get comfortable doing things the way you’ve always done them and stop exploring\. It’s not laziness; it’s often just a matter of bandwidth\. But we all have to fight that\.
5.  **Don’t just do what you love, do what you believe has meaning\.**  
    The classic refrain is that if you do what you love, you’ll never work a day in your life\. And while that’s true to some degree, I think that’s a shallow view\. With Goosechase, I still love what we’re working on, but what’s more motivating is the fact that there’s extra meaning and purpose\. So when things get inevitably tough, having the backbone of the “why” makes a massive difference\.
