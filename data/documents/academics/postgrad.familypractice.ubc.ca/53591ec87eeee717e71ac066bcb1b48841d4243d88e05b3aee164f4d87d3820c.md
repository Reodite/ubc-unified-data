##### **Purpose**

The purpose of this policy is to provide support for Residents in our program to optimize physical and mental health during pregnancy\. This policy is for FP Program Resident pregnancy and is intended to be implemented with respect for and in collaboration with the PARBC Collective Agreement, with references to relevant provisions\.

##### **Background**

A review of published literature on pregnancy during Residency revealed that while many medical training programs have maternity leave policies, few offer guidance to programs for support during pregnancy\. More research in this area is encouraged\.  
Trainees will have different needs during pregnancy and should feel supported in their requests for the necessary individualized accommodations\. Residents should feel welcome and encouraged to discuss their pregnancy with their Site Directors and Coordinators\. A Resident’s ability to work during pregnancy should not be compared to that of other Residents who have worked during pregnancy\. A Resident should be reassured that any accommodations will not result in increased service duties for their Resident colleagues; literature suggests that this is often a source of guilt for pregnant medical trainees\.  
Pregnancy during medical training may be planned or unplanned\. Regardless, psychological and non\-psychological support is necessary\. The literature lists the following reasons that a pregnancy is planned during Residency: they are often in their prime childbearing years, they are guaranteed paid maternity leave, they don’t want to take time off when they are starting to build their practice, or they are worried about finding someone to cover their practice if they take maternity leave after they are established\. The right to decide when to have children is protected by the PARBC contract and is supported by our Program\. It is important that those with attitudes against pregnancy in training are informed\.  
Common pregnancy symptoms such as poor sleep, edema, nausea, syncopal episodes and fatigue can affect the function and wellbeing of pregnant trainees\. Although more studies are necessary, possible risks identified are standing more than 3 hours per day, lifting more than 10kg, shift work more than 40 hours per week\. These may be associated with preterm labour, hypertension of pregnancy, and IUGR\. Other obvious risks are exposures \(e\.g\. to infectious disease, radiation\)\. Some formal statements have suggested leave at 38 weeks in normal gestation, and at 30\-32 weeks in complicated or multiple pregnancies\.

##### **Principles**

1.  The Department of Family Practice will do what we can to support residents who become pregnant during training, to ensure a healthy outcome for the resident and her child\. Any discrimination towards a resident because of their pregnancy needs to be reported to the Site or
2.  Program director and will be addressed\.
3.  Program and Site Directors will make Residents aware of this policy early in their training\.
4.  Residents are encouraged to discuss their pregnancy with their Site Director early, so that there is adequate time to arrange any accommodations or changes to schedules\. \(E\.g\. possibly move the more demanding clinical experiences to earlier on in the pregnancy if appropriate\)\.
5.  Based on the best available evidence, the program supports Residents who choose to start maternity leave 2 weeks or more prior to their estimated due date\.
6.  Residents will be given the opportunity to work reduced hours to a minimum of 50%, if requested, with the appropriate lengthening of their overall program\.
7.  Residents will be allowed to reduce, alter, or eliminate call if this is deemed medically necessary at any time during their pregnancy\. Even if not supported with a medical note, Residents will be given the opportunity to modify their call schedules to enable them to meet call requirements in an alternative manner if requested\. \(E\.g\. split weekend call, work 2 evenings to midnight rather than one overnight shift\.\)\. Residents on Family Medicine rotations will not be required to do call after 34 weeks\. It is suggested that Residents on other rotations be relieved of call duties after 34 weeks \(in line with the UBC Obstetric &amp; Gynecology post\-graduate pregnancy policy\)\. While Residents should try to negotiate alternatives to minimally disrupt a schedule, the onus is not on them to make a schedule work\. On return from maternity leave, the resident may be required to make up for the experiences missed due to these call accommodations\.
8.  Residents may be able to meet some of the curriculum requirements through personal study, research, or online courses if they are unable to maintain clinical work due to pregnancy related illness\. This would need to be pre\-approved by the Program Director and documented appropriately\.
9.  Residents will be required to make up time missed to ensure the equivalent of 24 months of training is completed\.
10.  Residents can choose alternative ways to demonstrate knowledge and skills if appropriate\. \(E\.g\. in situations where pregnant trainees must be protected from exposures such as radiation and infectious disease\)\.
11.  The program should not put extra demands on remaining Residents to cover the pregnant learners’ duties\.
12.  Pregnant Residents must be allowed to maintain nutrition and adequate sleep\. They must also be allowed to attend scheduled prenatal visits and procedures\.
13.  Pregnant Residents should be made aware of possible complications of long work hours \(E\.g\. hypertension, IUGR, preterm labour\) and discuss it with their caregivers\.

##### **TIPS FOR THE PREGNANT MEDICAL TRAINEE – RESIDENT TO RESIDENT**

-   Prioritize duties in the morning – e\.g\. Do the more strenuous ones early in the day, alternate duties between those requiring standing &amp; sitting
-   Use the elevator when you can
-   Stay hydrated
-   Pack snacks
-   Book your appointments well in advance, at the end of the day
-   Talk to your program director early, so that you have lots of time to alter your schedule\. Try to put your heavier rotations at the best time of your pregnancy\.
-   Ask if there are options for shorter shifts\.
