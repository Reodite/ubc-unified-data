Under the guidance of a clinician, Clinical Teaching Associates train students how to perform sensitive examinations, including:

-   chest/breast and pelvic exams, and
-   urogenital exams—involving the penis, scrotum, rectum, prostate, and inguinal region\.

Clinical Teaching Associates use their own bodies to teach a small group of students how to develop a comfortable, sensitive communication style and clinical techniques to ensure a safe and effective exam\.

### Who can become a Clinical Teaching Associate?

Clinical Teaching Associates must have strong communication skills and have a high degree of comfort with their bodies, since these examinations are intimate and may at times involve mild physical discomfort\. Clinical Teaching Associates need to learn related anatomy, medical terminology, exam technique, interpersonal communication skills, and how to provide effective feedback\.

Clinical Teaching Associates must be able to participate in a supportive and highly professional manner to help medical students meet their learning objectives\. This includes verbal and non\-verbal communication, as well as clinical and medical techniques, to ensure patient comfort and optimal student learning\.

### What kind of training will I receive?

Clinical Teaching Associates will become confident and certified through an intensive training session, which covers anatomy, medical terminology, clinician\-patient communication, and physical exam skills\. It will include team\-building exercises, large\- and small\-group workshops, and hands\-on curriculum practice using task trainers\.

### How often are Clinical Teaching Associates needed?

Clinical Teaching Associates exams occur in the fall term\. There are typically four sessions between September and November\.

### What if I become uncomfortable?

Please let us know immediately\. Your well\-being is a top priority\. SMP faculty and students are licensed with the [College of Physicians and Surgeons of British Columbia](https://www.cpsbc.ca/) and bound by the [Professional Standards for the UBC Faculties of Medicine and Dentistry](https://med-fom-faculty.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2012/02/Professional-Standards-for-the-Faculties-of-Medicine-and-Dentistry.pdf)\.

### Where are the sessions held?

All sessions take place at the Clinical Academic Campus at Kelowna General Hospital\. Our office is located on the second floor at [2312 Pandosy Street, Kelowna, BC](https://www.google.ca/maps/place/2312+Pandosy+St,+Kelowna,+BC+V1Y+1T3/@49.8725608,-119.4938464,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m5!3m4!1s0x537d8b527468deeb:0x28ae16818790dc34!8m2!3d49.8725608!4d-119.4916577)\. Public parking is available in the parkade directly attached to the Clinical Academic Campus \(on Rose Avenue\)\.

### Will I be paid?

Clinical Teaching Associates will receive remuneration for participating in the training and exam sessions\.
