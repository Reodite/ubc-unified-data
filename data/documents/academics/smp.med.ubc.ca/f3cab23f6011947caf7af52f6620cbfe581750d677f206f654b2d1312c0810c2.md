The Southern Medical Program is excited to welcome our new and returning students for the new academic year at UBC Okanagan and our clinical education sites across the region\. Meet some of our students\.

**[Image](https://smp.med.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/93/2026/09/Richard-Mageto.jpg) Richard Mageto  
**Hometown: SurreyYear in Program: 4

**What inspired you to pursue your program?  
**I came to medicine later than most\. Basketball defined much of my childhood and adolescence and was the focus of nearly all my time and energy\. It was during my undergraduate studies that I began to seriously consider a career in medicine\. The more I thought about it, the more I realized that medicine offered many of the same things I loved about sports: team collaboration for a common goal and the challenge of trying to be a little better every day\.

**Why did you choose UBC?  
**B\.C\. is home for me, however, I didn’t have the fortune of exploring its beauty prior to medical school\. I was excited that training at UBC, through both its distributed sites model and elective opportunities, would allow me to travel throughout the province and experience its diversity while learning how to best serve the people who call it home\.

**Tell me about your research\.  
**One of the aspects I value most about UBC’s MD program is the dedicated curricular time for research endeavors\. I’m currently involved in studies evaluating exercise modalities for the management of bothersome upper limb symptoms in Parkinson’s Disease, investigating pharmacological management of interstitial cystitis \(bladder pain syndrome\), and exploring ways to reduce wait\-times for specialist consultation\.

**What are you most looking forward to this year?  
**My medical training has given me the opportunity to meet some really wonderful people\. As I start my final year, I’m most looking forward to travelling around the country for electives and meeting more of my future colleagues\.

**[Image](https://smp.med.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/93/2026/09/Taryn-Murray.jpg)Taryn Murray**  
Hometown: Kelowna  
Year in Program: 3

**What inspired you to pursue your program?**  
What ultimately drew me to medicine was the opportunity to use knowledge in a way that directly and meaningfully impacts people’s lives\. The opportunity to support individuals and families through some of their most important and vulnerable moments is a privilege\. I was also drawn to the variety within medicine and the opportunity to challenge myself and grow throughout my career continually\. Through my own family experiences, I became specifically interested in how geography shapes access to healthcare, particularly in rural and remote communities, and I hope to help improve health equity for these populations\.

**Why did you choose UBC?**   
I was drawn to UBC’s distributed model of medical education and the opportunity to learn from patients and physicians in communities throughout British Columbia\. Having grown up in Kelowna, BC, the Okanagan always felt like home, and I knew I wanted to complete my medical training in the province and ultimately give back to the communities that shaped me\. I also completed my undergraduate degree at UBC Okanagan, so UBC has always played an important role in my education and continuing at UBC felt like a natural fit\.

**What are you most looking forward to this year?**   
I am most looking forward to spending my clerkship year in the Comox Valley through UBC’s Integrated Community Clerkship program\. After two years of classroom\-based learning, I am excited to learn directly from patients, build longitudinal relationships, and see how their care evolves across different clinical settings\. Beyond medicine, I am excited to make the Comox Valley home and become part of the community\. I hope to explore the region’s trails, mountains, and coastline, and to enjoy hiking, biking, skiing, and ocean adventures\.

[Image](https://smp.med.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/93/2026/06/Roadshow-2026.jpg)

From May 4 to 8, 2026, the Healthcare Travelling Roadshow rolled into the communities of Logan Lake, Salmon Arm, and Vernon to showcase health professions to rural elementary and high school students\.

Over 400 students took part in hands\-on, interactive experiences and connected with health profession students in medicine, nursing, midwifery, respiratory therapy, paramedicine, social work, and audiology\.

As part of the week\-long tour through the region, the health profession presenters were hosted for a tour of the Nicola Valley Hospital and Health Centre in Merritt\. Students learned about the various scopes of practice and gained a deeper understanding of the unique challenges and opportunities of rural healthcare delivery\.

The Roadshow is a provincial initiative that has connected with more than 16,500 high school students during 102 community visits throughout northern and rural BC since 2010\. This year’s edition of the Roadshow also included two northern BC trips and one on Vancouver Island\.

The Roadshow is delivered in partnership with the University of Northern British Columbia, UBC Faculty of Medicine, Northern Medical Programs Trust, Interior Health, Island Health, and Rural Education Action Plan\. To learn more about the provincial program, [visit the Roadshow homepage](https://www2.unbc.ca/healthcare-travelling-roadshow)\.

Dr\. Matthew Petrie has been appointed Regional Associate Dean pro tem, Interior, effective May 25, 2026 to May 24, 2027\. Dr\. Petrie is a clinical assistant professor in the Department of Emergency Medicine, and an emergency physician based at Kelowna General Hospital\.

Dr\. Petrie previously served as the inaugural Co\-Director of Clinical Skills for the Southern Medical Program \(SMP\), helping to build the clinical skills program from the ground up\. Over the years, he has served as a dedicated mentor to medical students and residents training in Kelowna, and has contributed extensively to curriculum development and implementation, as well as the renewal of the MD Undergraduate Program’s curriculum\.

Dr\. Petrie also leads multi\-disciplinary medical education through numerous simulation and course education roles\. As a co\-lead with Real\-Time Virtual Support at the Rural Coordination Centre of British Columbia, he is at the forefront of multidisciplinary innovation, connection, and virtual medical support and education of health care providers in rural, remote and Indigenous communities across B\.C\.

During his term as Regional Associate Dean pro tem, Interior, Dr\. Petrie will provide continuity and sustained strategic oversight of the SMP and training programs throughout B\.C\.’s Interior\. Working in partnership with Interior Health, and in collaboration with faculty, staff and learners in the region, Dr\. Petrie will provide support and leadership to maintain excellence in the delivery and growth of medical education and innovation across the Interior\.

Dr\. Petrie’s appointment follows the leadership of Dr\. Sarah McCorquodale, who will be stepping into a new role as interim Provincial Program Director with the Family Practice Residency Program\. She will also serve as Special Advisor to the Regional Associate Dean pro tem, Interior\. She will begin both of these roles on May 25, 2026, for a one\-year term\.

During her tenure as Regional Associate Dean, Interior, Dr\. McCorquodale was instrumental in expanding the SMP and supported the expansion of residency training programs across the Interior\. Her efforts strengthened the integration of distributed medical education and research throughout the region, particularly in rural, remote and Indigenous communities\.

A passionate advocate for rural health equity, Dr\. Tkach is dedicated to expanding clinical trials beyond urban centers and involving patients and rural health partners in that process\.
