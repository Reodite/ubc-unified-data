## **Backed by Science**

The evidence is clear and it’s growing: spending time learning outdoors has a huge impact on your child’s brain development and problem\-solving skills as well as their physical wellbeing\. Nature is like a super\-stimulator for their senses, engaging their brains in ways that a typical classroom just can’t\.

Think about it: have you noticed how your child’s attention seems to spark when they’re outside? That’s not just a coincidence\! [Research shows](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19121124/) that being in nature can actually improve attention spans\. It’s called the “Attention Restoration Theory,” and it suggests that nature’s gentle allure helps your child’s brain recharge, reducing mental fatigue and making it easier for them to focus\. This is especially helpful for kids who might struggle to stay attentive in more structured indoor settings\.

Beyond just attention, outdoor learning, especially when it’s hands\-on, strengthens your child’s thinking skills, critical thinking, and ability to solve problems\. Nature throws ever\-changing, unpredictable challenges their way, encouraging your child to think creatively and adapt\. Whether they’re building a fort, figuring out a path through the woods, or observing a busy ant colony, these experiences push them to analyze situations, form ideas, and test solutions\. This active, “learn\-by\-doing” approach helps them [truly grasp concepts and become more flexible thinkers](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2167/irgee225.0)\.

And it’s not just about academics\. When kids are outside, they often engage in collaborative problem\-solving, which is fantastic for their social and emotional development\. Learning to work together, communicate, and navigate group dynamics are vital life skills\.

These benefits even extend to stress reduction\. Studies have consistently shown that [spending time in nature lowers stress levels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5981243/)\. When your child is less stressed, they’re simply better able to focus, process information, and engage in higher\-level thinking\. So, when you combine improved attention, opportunities to solve real\-world problems, and stress reduction, you get a powerful recipe for your child’s cognitive growth\.

## **Ways to Play and Learn**

[a group leader and child at a Williams Lake outdoor learning program, creating a structure with sticks in the Alex Fraser Research Forest](https://wildandimmersive.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/outdoor-education-williams-lake.jpg)

Understanding the difference between structured outdoor learning and free play is crucial for recognizing how each contributes to your kid’s development\. 

### **Structured Outdoor Learning**

When we talk about structured outdoor learning, we’re referencing activities that adults, like teachers or caregivers, plan and guide\. These experiences usually have specific goals in mind, often connected to what your child is learning in school\.

**Goal\-Oriented**: These activities have a clear purpose\. For example, your child might go on a nature walk with a specific task to identify different trees, play an organized outdoor game with a set of rules, or work on a gardening project\.

**Focused Learning**: The main aim here is for your child to gain particular knowledge or skills\. This often involves some instruction, demonstrations, and guided exploration to help them learn effectively\.

### **Free Play**

Free play is quite different\. This is all about giving your child the freedom to choose their own activities and play however they like, with very little or no adult interference or rules\.

**Open\-Ended**: This kind of play is spontaneous and driven by imagination, without any pre\-planned outcomes\. Think about your child building a fort, exploring a natural space, or inventing their own imaginative games\.

**Exploration and Creativity**: The focus here is on sparking creativity, nurturing imagination, and developing social skills\. Children learn through their own discoveries and by experimenting with the world around them\. 

Ultimately, both structured outdoor learning and free play are incredibly important for your child’s development\. Structured learning offers those targeted opportunities for acquiring knowledge and building specific skills\. Meanwhile, free play is vital for boosting creativity, enhancing social skills, and supporting their emotional well\-being\. It’s important to make sure your kid gets a healthy balance of both types of outdoor activity\.
