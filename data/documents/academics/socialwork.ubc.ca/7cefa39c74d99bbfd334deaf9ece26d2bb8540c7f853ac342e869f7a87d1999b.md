### *MSW Mission Statement*

*The mission of UBC\-Vancouver’s advanced standing MSW program is to educate our graduates to be ethical, reflexive, and skilled professionals who champion social justice in all areas of practice\. As students pursue a micro or macro program concentration, they continue to develop values, knowledge, and skills that prepare them to advance social work practice, policy, and research, and contribute to a more just, compassionate, and equitable society\. A critical analytic lens anchors a curriculum that is theoretically sound and empirically supported, and focused on practice with individuals, families, groups, communities, and broader systems\.*

> ---

UBC\-Vancouver offers a 12\-month MSW program intended for practitioners with a Bachelor of Social Work \(BSW\) degree and prior professional practice experience\. The program is designed to prepare graduates for advanced roles across service sectors and practice settings\. While our MSW requires learners to complete core courses that address all levels of intervention, students are able to choose electives that allow them to enhance their capacity for either micro\- or macro\-level social work practice\.

Given the scheduling of courses, the program is designed to take exactly 12 months to complete\. Students are typically in courses from September through the end of August, and in practicum two days a week from September through April\. Most courses are offered on\-campus and during the day, but the program offers some courses online and in the evening to help with accessibility\.

While a thesis option is available for some students, contingent on faculty availability, corresponding research interests, and funding, this option typically adds one year to the student’s program\. Most students elect a one\-year course\-based program integrated with an advanced practicum experience\.

---

## Practice concentrations

Students choose electives that reflect their interest in either micro\- or macro\-level social work practice\.

Pursuing a concentration in **micro\-level social work practice** involves choosing electives that primarily involve developing advanced knowledge and skills for direct practice with individuals, families, and/or groups\. On the other hand, students completing a concentration in **macro\-level social work practice** take electives that allow them to deepen their understanding and capacity for practicing at the level of organizations and communities, and in functions such as programming and policy development\.

### Core courses \(12 credits\)

All students are required to complete the following four courses, which together make up 12 credits:

-   **SOWK\_V 526** \(3 credits\): Assessment and Intervention in Advanced Social Work Practice
-   **SOWK\_V 575** \(3 credits\): Weaving Indigenous Knowledge into Social Work Practice
-   **SOWK\_V 550** \(3 credits\): Social Work and Social Justice
-   3 credits of research methods, which can be satisfied by taking one of the following:
    -   **SOWK\_V 553** \(3 credits\): Research Methods for Social Work Inquiry
    -   **SOWK\_V 554** \(6 credits\): Program Evaluation****\*\*****

**Important:** SOWK\_V 526 and SOWK\_V 575 are offered in Winter Term 1 \(September through December\), while SOWK\_V 550 and SOWK\_V 553 are held in the summer term \(May through August\)\.

***\*\*** SOWK\_V 554 is an 8\-month 6\-credit course that normally runs from September through April\. If a student uses this course to satisfy the research methods requirement of their degree, 3 of the credits from the course are used for this purpose, and the remaining 3 credits can be used by the student to meet elective requirements for a macro concentration\.*

### Field requirements \(6 credits\)

Students must complete a 450\-hour practicum, which normally runs two days a week from September through April\. For more information on the practicum, [click here](https://socialwork.ubc.ca/field-education/graduate-practicum/)\.

While in practicum, students are enrolled in the following two courses, which amount to 6 credits:

-   **SOWK\_V 560** \(3 credits\): Directed Field Studies in Social Work
-   **SOWK\_V 559** \(3 credits\): Advanced Integrative Seminar

While SOWK\_V 560 represents the practicum placement and does not involve a classroom component, SOWK\_V 559 is a regularly scheduled seminar that provides students with opportunities to make connections between their coursework and practicum\-based learning\. SOWK\_V 559 normally runs once every two weeks between September and April\.

### Elective requirements \(12 credits\)

Students are required to choose 12 credits of electives reflecting a concentration of studies in either **micro\-** or **macro\-level social work practice**\.

The following courses can be used to achieve a concentration of studies in **micro\-level** social work practice:

-   **SOWK\_V 521** \(3 credits\): Social Work Practice in Addictions
-   **SOWK\_V 525** \(3 credits\): Advanced Social Work Practice: Mental Health
-   **SOWK\_V 528** \(3 credits\): Social Work Practice with Groups
-   **SOWK\_V 532** \(3 credits\): Social Work Practice with the Family

The following courses can be used to achieve a concentration of studies in **macro\-level** social work practice:

-   **SOWK\_V 524** \(3 credits\): Social Services Management
-   **SOWK\_V 529** \(3 credits\): Communities and Social Development: Debates, Approaches, and Fields of Practice
-   **SOWK\_V 554** \(6 credits\)***\*\****: Program Evaluation

***\*\***only 3 credits from SOWK 554 can be applied toward fulfillment of elective requirements, as the other 3 credits are normally used to satisfy the research requirement*

The courses below can be used toward fulfillment of either a concentration of studies in **micro\- or macro\-level** social work practice:

-   **SOWK\_V 531** \(3 credits\): Social Work Practice in the Field of Aging
-   **SOWK\_V 551** \(3 credits\): Health and Social Care Praxis
-   **SOWK\_V 571** \(3 credits\): Global Health

## Thesis Option

Though not required, students may elect to complete a thesis\. If they choose this option, the thesis is completed **in addition** to the 30 credits of coursework, above\. Students pursuing a thesis are responsible for securing a suitable supervisor from within the UBC School of Social Work\. Once a supervisor is secured, students enrol in SOWK 549, and normally complete an additional course in research methods, which they select in consultation with their supervisor\. Thesis students normally take an additional year to complete the MSW\.
