ImageI joined the UBC School of Social Work as an Associate Professor in July 2024, and over time it has come to feel like a meaningful academic home – one grounded in collaboration, care, and mutual respect\. What drew me here, and what continues to shape my experience, is the School’s commitment to an “Ethics of Care\.” This is not just a statement of values, but something I see enacted in everyday interactions – in how people support one another, engage in difficult conversations, and remain accountable to the communities we serve\. I have found myself continually learning from colleagues and students whose perspectives, experiences, and questions challenge me to think more deeply and differently\. The intellectual openness across the School and the wider university has expanded my work in ways I hadn’t anticipated, especially through opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration and creative approaches to teaching and research\.

As a South Asian diasporic scholar, my work is closely tied to my own questions about belonging, justice, and responsibility\. I am particularly committed to addressing systemic inequities and to working with communities whose voices are marginalized – immigrants, refugees, migrant workers, and LGBTQ\+ individuals in Canada and beyond\. These endeavours reflect my ongoing commitment to advancing knowledge in the field of social work, fostering cross\-cultural academic partnerships, and contributing to the broader social work community\. Over the past five years, I have led or co\-investigated more than 18 research projects, securing over $3\.07 million in grant funding\. These projects investigate pressing issues such as immigration and refugee health, HIV, precarious employment, gender\-based violence, human trafficking, and anti\-racist, anti\-casteist, and anti\-colonial learning in Canada and internationally\.

Image

My publication record includes 50 peer\-reviewed works: 42 refereed journal articles \(17 as sole or first author\), six peer\-reviewed book chapters \(five as first author\), and one sole\-authored book published with Springer\. I have also presented my work extensively, with 55 peer\-reviewed conference presentations \(including seven as lead author at international conferences\), as well as nine invited talks and eleven workshops, webinars, and panel discussions\.

Below, I highlight a few examples of the community\-engaged and collaborative projects I have been involved in, along with key areas of my research and scholarship\. These endeavours reflect my ongoing commitment to advancing knowledge in the field of social work, fostering cross\-cultural academic partnerships, and contributing to the broader social work community\.

[**Emotional** **Well\-Being Institute Canada \(EWBI Canada\)**](https://www.ewbi-canada.org/team/)

I am one of the co\-founding Directors of Emotional Well\-Being Institute Canada \(EWBI Canada\), a non\-profit multicultural organization dedicated to promoting emotional well\-being and fostering human flourishing within communities\. Its global centre is based in Geneva, Switzerland\. I currently serve as Vice\-President and Director of Higher Education &amp; Professional Development \(EWBI Canada\) in a voluntary capacity\. In this role, I contribute through grant writing, partnership building, and collaboration with local and global partners to support historically marginalized communities and advance inclusive, sustainable change\.  EWBI Canada works closely with community stakeholders and international partners to build global professional networks centered on emotional well\-being\. Our initiatives align with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, particularly Goal 3 \(Good Health and Well\-Being\) and Goal 17 \(Global Partnerships\)\. In collaboration with EWBI Geneva, we contribute to advancing these shared priorities and to promoting more accessible and inclusive approaches to emotional well\-being\.

[**Emotional** **Well\-Being Institute Geneva \(EWBI Geneva\)**](https://www.ewb.institute/team)

I also serve as Co\-Director of Education and Professional Development at EWBI Geneva\. We are working toward the UN ECOSOC membership while building partnerships with UN\-affiliated agencies, governments, NGOs, and professional bodies\. Through this work, I have been involved in strengthening EWBI’s global engagement and contributing to its growing international presence\.

Image

Book Launch at the Kerala Conference

EWBI has convened two international conferences to date—the first in Mauritius in 2023 and the second in Kerala, India in 2024—with a third planned in Nepal in April 2027\. I have been actively involved in both the conference planning and scientific committees, including serving as Chair of the Abstract Review Committee for the first two conferences\. My contributions have also extended to knowledge mobilization and scholarly collaboration\. I co\-edited a book titled:  *Emotional Well\-being: Contemporary Approaches and Practices*\. DiST Publication\. The book was launched at the Second International Emotional Well\-being Conference 2024 in Kerala, India\. I am currently working on a second edited volume, to be launched at the upcoming conference in Nepal\.

[**Pathways to Prosperity Canada \(P2P** **Canada****\)**](https://p2pcanada.ca/members-of-the-standing-committee-on-student-and-junior-scholar-engagement/)

I have been involved with [Pathways to Prosperity: Canada](http://p2pcanada.ca/) \(P2P\) since 2015, and over time it has become an important part of my academic and community\-engaged work\. I remain connected to P2P because of its commitment to bringing together researchers, practitioners, and policymakers in ways that feel genuinely collaborative and grounded in real\-world challenges related to immigration and integration\. My involvement has evolved over the years – from serving on the Student Committee, to taking on the role of Co\-Chair, and now as a member of the Advisory Council, while continuing to contribute to the Student and Junior Scholar Engagement Committee\. Through these roles, I have supported initiatives aimed at improving newcomer settlement experiences and have participated in ongoing conversations about building more inclusive communities\.

Image

Standing Committee on Student and Junior Scholar Engagement, Pathways to Prosperity Canada

Conference work has been a particularly meaningful part of this engagement\. As a member of the Advisory Committee, I am actively involved in planning the annual national conference\. The 13th Annual National Conference was held in Halifax in November 2025\. It is one of the largest gatherings in Canada focused on immigration and settlement, bringing together policymakers, practitioners, academics, and community organizations to address key issues in immigration and integration\.

[**Dr\. Ambedkar Symposium on Emancipation \(ASE2026\)**](https://www.chetna.ca/)

In March 2027, I co\-organized a two\-day symposium, *“Overcoming Caste Barriers for Social and Economic Equality,”* in collaboration with the Chetna Association\. This work felt both academically and personally significant, particularly in creating space to engage with caste in the Canadian context, where it is often overlooked or insufficiently addressed\. One of our projects, “Caste in Canada” is funded by SSHRC Partnership Grant\.
