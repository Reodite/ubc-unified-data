I write this from our renewed and recently reopened Jack Bell Building\. We moved back into our new space in December to allow teaching to resume in Social Work’s home in January of this year\. All went smoothish—missing boxes, confusion about where things should go as movers brought in seemingly endless boxes, rooms overcrowded with misplaced furniture—however, now four months in, we have begun to feel a semblance of calm and an appreciation of the delights of a new space\. For those reading this who have not been into the new Jack Bell, it is, in addition to its seismic upgrading, dramatically renewed\. One of the pleasures of being back in our own building is having students around—and they are appreciative of their lovely new student lounge\.

## **Faculty updates**

We had two new promotions in the past year: Antoine Coulombe and Kelly Allison joined our increasing ranks of Associate Professors\. Congratulations to both of you in your new roles as Associate Professor, Educational Leadership\. Your contributions to the School have been significant and it is wonderful to see this very appropriate and timely acknowledgement\.

Kelly plays an additional and important role as Director of Education for UBC Health\. Combining these roles, she has been actively involved in the educational programming for the new Gateway Clinic, which is an interprofessional primary care teaching clinic opening soon in the new Gateway Building\. Social Work joins most of UBC’s health professions as a partner in the new clinic which will provide primary care provision and student training opportunities in team\-based care\. This initiative is supported by the province and is a promising new model for training health professional students in interprofessional care\.

Pascale de Kerckhove, a long\-time and highly valued adjunct, now joins the School in a new full\-time role as a Lecturer/Clinical Instructor primarily focused at Gateway\.

Dr\. Deb O’Connor will be retiring from the School of Social Work on June 30 this year\. While we certainly wish Deb all the best in her retirement \(likely she will be found on the water, sailing\), she will be much missed for her enormous contributions to the School over many years\. This has included her role as a Director of the Centre for Research on Personhood in Dementia\. We will, of course be planning a suitable celebration for Deb in the upcoming months\.

## **Staff updates**

Christine Graham, our Program Advisor of many years and a UBC employee for more than 35 years, has also retired\. Christine took up retirement as of April 30 with some planned vacation and she too will be appropriately celebrated later this term\. Christine has been such a stalwart part of the School, saving all of us in leadership positions from errors, reminding us of due dates and due process and in every way imaginable keeping the School and all of the School community well\-managed\. Her roles with students, particularly those who have needed extra supports is especially important to recognize\.

While a real replacement for Christine is impossible, with excitement for her new vision and energy, we welcome Jaynus O’Donnell into the Program Advisor role\. Jaynus comes to the School from the School of Architecture and Landscape Architecture with deep knowledge of UBC and its sometimes\-complicated processes\! Jaynus, we are happy to have you join us\!

On other staffing notes, we welcome Josh Tan, Field Education and Admissions Assistant, who has stepped in to take up the role previously held so admirably by Amit Dhawan, who left the School at the end of December to move to Edmonton with his family and take up new opportunities\. Josh has already proved himself to be a huge asset, with new ideas, fresh energy, and the role flexibility we are really needing these days\!

Many of you know of—and have experienced—our staffing crisis over the past while\. A note of thanks is due to Rachel Wong, who has filled in on a half time basis from her home in the School of Creative Writing\.

Michelle Kaczmarek joins Jack York as staff for our growing Continuing Education program, albeit in a slightly different role\. Michelle too has UBC experience, including a PhD from the iSchool\. Michelle’s work will have some overlap with Jack’s continued and amazing contributions, but will focus more on our developing Post Baccalaureate Program\.

For the first time—perhaps ever—we have our own dedicated communications staff\. That *The Bridge* was last sent out more than a year ago speaks to our need to be in more regular contact with our School community\. This is important for Social Work, perhaps more than might be the case in other units at UBC\. Our growing roster of Continuing Education programs are of potential interest to our alumni, many of whom also supervise placement students\. Adjunct instructors and potential adjunct instructors are critically important to the School\. Because we are a practice\-based discipline, people working directly in the filed add real value to our teaching; it is important that we keep you informed\. Aline Bouwman has recently joined us on a PT basis to help with our overall communications—beginning with developing a strategic plan\! Welcome Aline\!

A not\-so\-new but so valuable a contributor to the School is our Administrator, Alissa Kinnis\. Thank you to [Rita Amisano](https://ires.ubc.ca/rita-amisano/), our previous Administrator since 2022, who has moved on to a Director role at the Institute for Resources, Environment and Sustainability in the Faculty of Sciences\. Alissa has now been with us for a full year, so her contributions at this point are too numerous to mention in full\. However, Alissa has been with UBC and the Faculty of Arts in many significant roles and since joining us in Social Work, has contributed hugely to the School in attending to and updating our systems—in just about every area\. In addition, Alissa is a pleasure to work with and brings to her role much more than administrative skills but also creativity and vision, helping to shape new ideas and initiatives\.
