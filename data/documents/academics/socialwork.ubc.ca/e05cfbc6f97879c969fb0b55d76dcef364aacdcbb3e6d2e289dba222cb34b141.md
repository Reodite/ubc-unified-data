The Continuing Education and Certificate Program \(CECP\) has continued to grow over the past year, delivering a total of nine synchronous courses since the start of the program, and launching the new [UBC Mental Health and Substance Use Micro\-Credential Certificate program](https://extendedlearning.ubc.ca/programs-credentials/mental-health-substance-use-level-1-microcertificate), which provides foundational education on mental health and substance use work\.  We have also begun work on a new DSM course, with funding support from the Faculty of Arts Non\-Credit fund\. In the coming year, we have many more exciting synchronous sessions planned, on a variety of subjects related to clinical practice, including courses centering Indigenous knowledge, and facilitated by Indigenous instructors\.  We are also developing other certificate programs for future launch, which will center more advanced clinical skill development\.  In addition, we are taking steps to expand our audience and build new partnerships to inform our program and expand our reach\.  To learn more about our current and past offerings, you can visit our [website](https://socialwork.ubc.ca/continuing-education-and-certificate-programs/)\.

Over the past year, The Continuing Education and Certificate Program \(CECP\) has continued to grow in scope and in the breadth of professional development that we offer to practitioners\.

In November, we launched the new [UBC Mental Health and Substance Use Micro\-Credential Certificate program](https://extendedlearning.ubc.ca/programs-credentials/mental-health-substance-use-level-1-microcertificate), developed in partnership with the Continuous Learning Advancement Fund \(CLAF\), and being delivered in partnership with UBC Extended Learning\.  This program is an online, self\-paced asynchronous program, consisting of two courses and 60 hours of learning activities, including multi\-media content featuring frontline practitioners, assessments of learning, and optional monthly synchronous sessions\.  Initial feedback has been very positive, and enrollment has exceeded expectations\.  This program provides foundational knowledge and skills for effective work with individuals experiencing mental health and substance use challenges in a broad range of work contexts\.  The program is designed to be highly accessible to learners, regardless of their existing educational attainment and professional credentials\.  The program also obtained the support of the StrongerBC Future Skills Grant, which can cover the registration costs for a significant number of prospective students, and will likely qualify for another round of this funding in the coming year\.

Since the start of our Continuing Education and Certificate Programs, we have delivered courses on the following synchronous \(live\) courses to our audience:

-   Single Session Family Therapy
-   Releasing Trauma: An Integrative Model of Trauma Practice
-   Narrative Therapy: Foundational Skills for an Anti\-Oppressive Approach to Clinical Practice
-   Satir Therapy: An Introduction to Virginia Satir’s Systemic, Family\-Centered Therapy
-   Neurodiversity\-Affirming Care
-   Beyond De\-Escalation: Conflict Management Skills
-   Narrative Therapy II: Advanced Skills for Reauthoring Conversations in Practice\.
-   Systemic Family Therapy: Core Skills
-   Narrative Therapy III: Experiential Skill Building for Advanced Practice

The anonymous surveys conducted with learners after each of these courses expressed overwhelmingly positive feedback\.

We also have the following courses coming up this Spring:

-   Neurodivergence – Informed Care Experience \(NICE\) – May 28\-29
-   Counselling Skills for Supporting Individual Considering Medical Assistance in Dying \(MAiD\) – June 19

As well as two in\-person courses that centre Indigenous knowledge, and facilitated by Indigenous clinician educators:

-   Journey to Wholeness: An Indigenous and Virginia Satir Approach – June 11\-12
-   Decolonizing Therapeutic Practice on Stolen Land: Tensions and Tending – September 25

In the coming year, we have courses planned on the following topics:

-   Neurodivergence Informed Care \(subsequent courses in series\)
-   Narrative Therapy \(subsequent courses in series\)
-   Systemic Family Therapy \(subsequent courses in series\)
-   Grief and Loss
-   Moral distress

In addition, we have been approved for the new Faculty of Arts Non Credit Fund for continuing education, which we will use to develop a hybrid synchronous/asynchronous DSM course intended to support social workers with meeting RCSW registration requirements\.  We are also developing other certificate programs for future launch, based on the course series that we have already begun delivering\.

In the past year, we have also partnered with the Faculty of Arts to develop and launch a promotional strategy to extend our audience reach\.  We are excited to further expand our program over the coming year, including through new partnerships, new facilitators, and continuing to grow our audience by implementing our promotional strategy, all while continuing to deliver quality continuing education to our learners\.

To learn more about our program as it evolves, be sure to visit our [website](https://socialwork.ubc.ca/continuing-education-and-certificate-programs/) regularly\.

For any questions or inquiries about the Continuing Education and Certificate Programs, contact our Program Coordinator, [Jack York](https://socialwork.ubc.ca/profile/jack-york-2/)\.
