**Q1\) ‘*1 in 151,091’ should be simplified to******:***

a\) 1 in 151,091 *\(this is quite difficult to interpret/visualize\)  
*b\) 1 in 151,100 *\(this is basically as difficult to interpret/visualize as the original version\)  
*c\) 1 in 151,000 *\(this is the second best option but still needlessly specific, and quite difficult to interpret/visualize\)  
***d\) 1 in 150,000 \(this is an easier number to interpret/visualize, and is very similar to 1 in 151,091 so it is still accurate enough\)**

**Q2\) ‘*1 in 106’ should be simplified to:***

a\) 1 in 106 *\(this is quite difficult to interpret/visualize\)  
*b\) 1 in 110 *\(this is basically as difficult to interpret/visualize as the original version\)  
*c\) 1 in 105 *\(this is basically as difficult to interpret/visualize as the original version\)  
***d\) 1 in 100 \(this is an easier number to interpret/visualize, and is just similar enough to 1 in 106 so it is still accurate enough\)**

**Q3\) *‘1 in 12’ should be simplified to:***

**a\) 1 in 12 \(this is easy enough to visualize, and simplifying it to anything else would make the comparison too inaccurate when relating to the real chance\)  
**b\) 1 in 10 *\(this is too inaccurate\)  
*c\) 2 in 25 *\(this is not completely accurate and is difficult to interpret/visualize\)* 

**Q4\)** A:Realistically, there is no chance that the volcano will erupt in the next week \= **4 \[WORST: This is wrong; there is a chance\]\.**

**Q5\)** B:It is a bit more likely that the volcano will erupt by 2065 than by 2025 \= **2 \[SECOND BEST: It is more likely, but more than a bit\]\.**

**Q6\)** C: It is very unlikely that the volcano will erupt in the next 50 years \= **3** \[**SECOND WORST: This is potentially misleading – what does ‘very unlikely’ mean? 1 in 12 is not ‘very unlikely’ to most people; perhaps ‘unlikely’ would acceptable**\]

**Q7\)** D: There is an absolutely tiny chance that the volcano will erupt in the next week \= **1** \[**BEST**: **This is accurate – there is a chance, but is it incredibly small**\]

**Q8****\)** What is the main problem with the following statement in terms of affecting a reader’s understanding: **“**If the pest outbreak spreads to the city, there is a 1\.07% chance that all the maple trees will die\. Scientists think there is approximately a 34% chance that it will reach the city\.”

a\) The two percentages \(1\.07 and 34\) are not reported to the same precision \(one is a whole number, and one is not\)\. *This is not really an issue; it is also possible that 1\.07 and 34 represent the finest precision that could be attained for these assessments\.  
*b\) The two percentages \(1\.07 and 34\) should be simplified to make them easier to interpret\. *This is true, but the numbers are small enough that a reader would be able to picture the basic message being delivered\.  
*c\) **The two percentages \(1\.07 and 34\) should be presented as one \(the percentage that the outbreak spreads to the city *and* all the trees die\. E\.g\. 0\.0107\*0\.34 \= 0\.0003638, or about 0\.4%\)\.**

 **Q9\)** How could the following statement be improved most so as to improve a reader’s understanding: **“**Statistics show thatcyclists involved in traffic accidents have a 45% chance of missing at least two weeks of work to recover from injuries\. Car drivers have only a 15% chance, but on average they are involved in six times as many accidents\.”

a\) Instead of giving percentages \(cyclists 45 and car drivers 15\) the author should have given comparisons \(cyclists are 3 times more likely\)\. *This is incorrect, as the 45:15 comparison tells you more than a 3:1 comparison – ‘three times more likely’ could be 3% compared to 1%, which is nowhere near the true chances for each traveller\.  
*b\) **Instead of wrongly implying that cyclists are more likely to miss work due to having a greater risk of being injured in traffic accidents, the author should have begun by stating that car drivers are much more likely to be in these accidents in the first place\.** **This would remove the framing bias, because, in reality, car drivers are twice as likely to miss work for two weeks or more \(15% is three times less likely than 45%, but they are six times as likely to be involved in accidents\)  
**c\) Instead of giving percentages \(cyclists 45 and car drivers 15\), the author should have used descriptive language to explain the risks\. *The author could introduce further confusion by using subjective language – the figures themselves tell an absolute, objective story, whereas words would not\.*

**Q10\)** What is the main problem with the following statement: **“**98 of our 100 test subjects improved their performance in biology exams following our instructional course, so there is only a 2% chance that you would pay for it and not see an improved result at the end of term\.”

a\) **It is misleading because other people might perform differently\. If you tested 100 different people, there is no guarantee that 98 would improve; it depends entirely on the sample used\.  
**b\) It does not say how much the average person improved by\. *This is true, but is less important than the best answer \(a is incorrect, this is just inaccurate\)\.  
*c\) It duplicates the same statistical information \(98 out of 100 and 2%\)\. *This is not really an issue, and is far less important than the best answer\.*
