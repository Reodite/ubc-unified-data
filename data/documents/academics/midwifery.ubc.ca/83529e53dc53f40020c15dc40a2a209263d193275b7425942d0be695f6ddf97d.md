Jade shares what inspired her to pursue midwifery and the importance of culturally competent care, advocacy, and education within Black communities\.

## Jade

\(She/Her\)

## About my path

## What drew you to midwifery?

  
There is a shortage of black midwives and birth workers that needs to be addressed\. Black birthing people deserve to have access to culturally competent care and a care provider that can better understand their personal context\. I’d like to be an advocate and support for my future clients and the cornerstones of midwifery care align with my desire to do so\.

## What motivates you on challenging days?

The women in my family that have sacrificed so much for me to be able to pursue this career\. My grandmother was a registered nurse and midwife in Jamaica, and I love the idea of continuing on her legacy\.

## What values guide how you want to practice?

Respect, empowerment, and love for clients, myself, and the childbearing process guide the way I’d like to practice\.

## What does success look like for you right now?

As I am still in my first year, success for me looks like stepping outside my comfort zone, making connections with my peers, and learning\!

## In my own words

## Is there something you’d like people to understand about you, your journey, or your hopes for the  
profession?

  
I grew up in a predominantly Black neighbourhood in Toronto, Ontario, and I’ve never had a Black healthcare provider\. I would love to see more and more representation in the healthcare field, and especially midwifery\.

## Looking forward

## As a future midwife I hope to…

Bring the things I learn back to my community\. I believe that empowerment begins with education, and as a midwife, it will be my duty to disperse this knowledge\!
