On **International Day of the Midwife 2026**, the global call for *One Million More Midwives* highlights a growing need for care—one that is also felt here in British Columbia\. We spoke with midwives and student midwives from across BC about what this need looks like in practice, and how it is shaping care for families and communities\.

## Jane Wines, educator

### The world needs One Million More Midwives—what does that mean for care here in BC?

Almost all areas need more midwives\. More so, the rural and remote areas, and for culturally diverse clients\. There is also a need for more midwives doing full scope care – especially births and homebirths\. Midwives are so stretched that they are taking larger caseloads to try and serve more clients, but finding they have less and less work\-life balance\.  

### How does midwifery education in BC help strengthen the future workforce?

Without new midwives coming through, we cannot grow our profession\. The two programs at UBC are crucial in strengthening midwifery and increasing the diversity of our workforce\. 

### How do midwives support families across BC, especially in rural and remote communities?

Where possible, midwives can help keep families together in their own communities\. However, this is rare due to the lack of midwives, especially those serving Indigenous communities\. Midwives such as those in BC Women’s Hospital are able to support people with breech presenting babies to have midwifery care when they have to travel to Vancouver\. This support is important when a parent has to travel away from their home community and family, and enables the choice of birthplace and continued midwifery support\. 

### What keeps you hopeful about the future of midwifery in BC? 

Midwives are the way forward in addressing the maternity care crisis\. They provide safe, individualised care as supported by countless studies, with better outcomes and lower interventions\. Even if based on economics alone, increasing and supporting midwifery is essential for our system and for the people in our care\. The enthusiasm of student midwives keeps me hopeful that our profession will grow and thrive\.

## Cecillia Jevitt, educator

### The world needs One Million More Midwives—what does that mean for care here in BC?

When I came to UBC 8 years ago and the program planned to graduate 20 midwives each year, I was worried about market place saturation, that there wouldn’t be enough midwifery positions for new graduates\. The Dean said to me, “Primary care is a leaky bucket\. You could graduate 20 new midwives for the rest of your life and we’d still need more\.” There’s a lot of truth in that advice\.  

### How does midwifery education in BC help strengthen the future workforce?

Midwifery education MAKES the future workforce\. If we want to see accessible reproductive health care for all and birth that welcomes new lives to the light, we must educate ourselves into that future\. We must never stop learning to be the best midwives ever and to spread that energy to the midwifery and health care community around us\.  

### How do midwives support families across BC, especially in rural and remote communities?

Midwifery education MAKES the future workforce\. If we want to see accessible reproductive health care for all and birth that welcomes new lives to the light, we must educate ourselves into that future\. We must never stop learning to be the best midwives ever and to spread that energy to the midwifery and health care community around us\.  

### What keeps you hopeful about the future of midwifery in BC? 

BC midwives are involved in the largest percentage of births in North America\. Tipping over 25% is a social science marker for inevitable change\. With the province so directly responsible for the health of its citizens, midwifery with its proven safe, satisfying, and cost\-effective outcomes is in a position for continued growth\.
