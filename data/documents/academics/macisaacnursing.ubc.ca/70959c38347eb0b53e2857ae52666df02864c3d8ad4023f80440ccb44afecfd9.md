The BSN program is an advanced standing, 20\-month accelerated nursing program that requires you to engage in full\-time study over five \(5\) consecutive terms from September to May \(with a break in August\)\. Curricula include lecture/theory, skills laboratory, simulation and practice experiences in a variety of healthcare settings\. As nursing is a practice discipline, a large portion of the BSN nursing program will be at practicum sites, where students will be working with real patients of all ages, across a variety of healthcare settings including hospitals, clinics, patients’ homes in the community, or with virtual patients in simulated practice environments\.

# Student Practice Education Core Orientation \(SPECO\) Overview

Student Practice Education Core Orientation \(or SPECO\) are provincial guidelines that were developed to support nursing students' practice experiences in health care settings across the province\. 

### SPECO Checklist

To record your progress/completion in this mandatory education, you will need to complete the SPECO checklist\. This checklist tracks the completion of the following in three \(3\) parts:

1.  BSN program &amp; practice pre\-requisites \(reflects Section 2 of SPECO Checklist\)
2.  Completion of mandatory core training modules \(reflects Section 3 of SPECO Checklist\)
3.  Health Authority\-specific Requirements \(reflects Section 4 of SPECO Checklist\)

Each of the 3 parts \(section 2, 3, and 4\) will consist of required education that all students must take prior to their practicum placements in BC Health Authorities \(and they must be current to be valid\!\)\. 

The other sections, Section 1 and Section 5 will, respectively, consist of your personal details and your signature of acknowledgement that your requirements are met and accurate\.

Section 2 contains some of the time\-sensitive action items that you will need to complete, as mentioned above, such as SHS Immunization Review Appointment Booking, Criminal Record Check \(CRC Review\), N95 Mask Fit\-Test Certification, and Basic Life Support for Health &amp; Life Sciences Students \(BLS\-HLS\) Certification\.

Section 3 is courses on Learning Hub that you will need to complete before you can begin your clinical practice \(which can be online or in\-person training classes\)\. 

### General Guidelines

As a BSN student,

-   It is your responsibility to keep prerequisites up to date \(including completing renewal modules and requirements, and re\-submitting updated checklists and certificates as required\)
-   Your SPECO checklist should be completed prior to starting practice education \(the completion of your requirements will be tracked by the UBC School of Nursing; however, you are advised to retain a copy of your completion for your personal records\)\.
-   Health Authorities may request proof of completion \(for any component of SPECO\) at any time and you must be able to provide said proof when requested\.

# Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to buy my own uniforms and supplies?

As per the UBC School of Nursing \(SON\) policy, [BSN Professional Appearance for Practice Learning Experiences](https://nursing.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/documents/policies/Professional%20Appearance%20in%20the%20BSN%20Program%20Oct_2024.pdf), students are required to wear the UBC SON approved and branded uniform during their practice placements and the uniforms must be obtained from our approved supplier\. You will also need to acquire certain equipment before classes begin for lab and practicum\. 

You will be given a list of items you need to purchase/assemble before classes start \(usually as part of your *Getting Ready for Clinical Practice* booklet\)\. 

### Can I work as a student nurse or health care aide during the BSN program?

It is important to remember that the UBC BSN program is an accelerated 20\-month program that includes theory, skills lab/simulation, and practice experiences\. We also encourage students to consider their well\-being among all the responsibilities which come with your commitment to the program\. We understand that some students may consider working as Employed Student Nurse \(ESN\) or Health Care Aides \(HCA\) during the duration of this program; however, this opportunity is only available after successful completion of NURS 361 in Term 3\.

Before deciding to take on an ESN/HCA role, please take time to review our policy on being a ESN or HCA as a BSN student, [Employed Students Nurses \(ESN\) and Health Care Assistant/Aide \(HCA\) Policy for the BSN Program](https://nursing.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/documents/policies/ESN_HCA_Policy_Approved_Nov%202023.pdf), to understand the requirements and the process\. More information can also be found on our [FAQ page](https://nursing.ubc.ca/undergraduate/faqs-undergraduate?combine=esn) – search for “ESN” or “HCA” if the results do not show up automatically\.

### Am I expected to travel during the program?

As a condition of accepting entry into this program, you are responsible for and expected to travel to your assigned practicum sites across the Lower Mainland\. Specifically, it is your responsibility as a student under the BSN program to make your own transportation and scheduling arrangements to ensure that you can attend and meet the site\-specific practice placement requirements\.

### Should I expect to be going to class or to my practice placement site Mondays to Fridays?

As per UBC Academic regulations, the practice component of the BSN program involves attending healthcare settings across the entire Lower Mainland \(which may include North Vancouver, Abbottsford, Coquitlam, Delta, Langley, South Surrey, etc\.\)\. Clinical shifts may be scheduled on weekdays and weekends, and on both daytime and evening hours\. Students generally have two \(2\) days off per week, but these may not always be consecutive days\.
