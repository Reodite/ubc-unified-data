### Profile

## Bio

Dr\. Farinaz Havaei is an Associate Professor in the School of Nursing at the University of British Columbia, a former Scholar with Michael Smith Health Research BC \(2021\-2026\), and a Tier 2 Canada Research Chair in Strengthening the Health System \(2026\-2031\)\. She is the founder and director of the HOPE Lab \(Healthy Outcomes and Patient and Provider Experiences\), where her research focuses on strengthening the nursing workforce and creating healthier, safer, and more sustainable healthcare systems\.

Her program of research examines how healthcare work environments—including staffing and workload, workplace violence, team\-based models of care, and other organizational factors—shape the health and well\-being of nurses and other healthcare workers, workforce retention, and the quality and safety of patient care\. Her work aims to generate actionable evidence that informs workforce policy and health system improvement\.

Dr\. Havaei works closely with health system and workforce partners to address pressing healthcare challenges and support the implementation and evaluation of evidence\-informed solutions\. Her research is grounded in strong partnerships with healthcare workers, leaders, policymakers, and professional and regulatory organizations, with an emphasis on translating research into meaningful improvements in policy and practice\.

### Research

## Area of Research

Dr\. Havaei’s program of research focuses on strengthening the health workforce and building healthier, safer, and more sustainable healthcare systems\. As a Tier 2 Canada Research Chair in Strengthening the Health System and founder and director of the UBC HOPE Lab, she leads a multidisciplinary program of research that addresses some of Canada’s most pressing health workforce and health system challenges\.

A central focus of Dr\. Havaei’s research is understanding how organizational and system\-level factors—including staffing and workload, workplace violence, models of care, and health workforce policies and strategies, such as the integration of internationally educated nurses—shape nurses’ health, well\-being, and retention, as well as the quality and safety of patient care\. Her research has generated important evidence on the consequences of challenging working conditions for nurses and patients and has contributed to policy and practice changes, including provincial legislation related to workplace psychological injury\. By identifying the conditions that enable nurses to thrive and remain in the workforce and evaluating strategies to strengthen workforce capacity, her research contributes to addressing nursing shortages and building a more resilient and sustainable health workforce\.

Using quantitative, qualitative, and mixed\-methods approaches, Dr\. Havaei works collaboratively with researchers, trainees, healthcare workers, patients and families, health system leaders, policymakers, and professional and regulatory organizations\. Her research is grounded in integrated knowledge translation and the co\-development of actionable solutions to real\-world health system challenges\. Through this work, she aims to transform evidence into policy and practice improvements that strengthen the health workforce, improve healthcare workers’ experiences and well\-being, and enhance the quality, safety, and sustainability of healthcare delivery\.
