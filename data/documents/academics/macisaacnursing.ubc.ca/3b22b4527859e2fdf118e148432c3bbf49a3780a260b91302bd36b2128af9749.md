## About

[Agnes Eatock, 1939](https://macisaacnursing.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-images/agnes-1939.jpg)

Agnes in 1939

Agnes Eatock was born in 1877 in Liverpool England, to John and Jane Capstick\.

John came from a long line of farmers whose ancestry can be traced back to Miles Capstack in 1525\. The family came from a remote area of northern England around the small town of Dent\. John was born in1841 and was the first of his family to leave the area, making his home in Liverpool\. Here he set up as a "cowkeeper", milking a few cows in a back yard and selling the milk, butter, cheese in the surrounding streets, before the age of refrigeration\. By the time he and Jane had produced 9 children, his business was successful enough for him to move out of the city and rent a farm\.

In a neighbouring farm lived the Eatock family, including their son Frederick, born in 1872\.

In 1900 Fred immigrated to Canada and is recorded on the Homestead Grant Register of Saskatchewan\.

Back in England, in 1905 Agnes qualified as a midwife and in 1910 Frederick returned to marry her\.

Their wedding photo shows the couple flanked by Agnes's sisters as bridesmaids\. Their father John Capstick, with the white beard is just visible behind and mother Jane is 3rd from the right\. \(My grandparents, Hugh and Alice Capstick are 2nd and 3rd from the left\.\)

[Eatock Capstick Wedding Party](https://macisaacnursing.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-images/agneswedding2.jpg)

The author's grandparents, Hugh and Alice Capstick, are 2nd and 3rd from the left\.\)

After the wedding, Fred and Agnes sailed for Canada, farming in Saskatchewan until he retired in 1921\. Although Agnes gave her occupation as nurse on some census forms, nothing is known of what this involved or what connection she had with the University of British Columbia\. The couple had no children\.

After retiring from farming they moved to Vancouver and travelled extensively over the next 25 years, including several trips back home to Liverpool\. Frederick died in1946 but Agnes lived another 20 years\. They are buried together in Capilano View Cemetery in W Vancouver\.

The Frederick and Agnes Eatock Memorial Fellowship continues to be awarded to nursing students\.
