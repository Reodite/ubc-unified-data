## **Guiding Principles on Prompt Designing and Prompt Design Activities**

### **Guiding Principles**

1.  **Adopt a Persona/Role for AI**  
    Define the role or perspective you want the AI to assume\. For example: 

-   -   *“You are the Assistant Dean in a university setting\.”* 
    -   *“You are an expert in drafting professional emails with a formal yet approachable tone\.”* 

2.  **Clearly Specify the Task**  
    Outline the specific goal or action for the AI to achieve\. For instance: 

-   -   *“Your task is to draft an email introducing a new course to faculty members\.”* 

3.  **Provide Context**  
    Include details to help the AI generate relevant and accurate outputs, such as:

-   -   *“The email is targeted at tenured faculty members\.”* 
    -   *“The tone should be engaging and professional, and the message should not exceed 250 words\.”* 

4.  **Facilitate Step\-by\-Step Interaction**  
    Encourage the AI to clarify its understanding by asking questions one at a time\. This ensures the output aligns closely with your expectations\.

-   -   *Example: “Ask clarifying questions one by one to refine your understanding of the task\.”* 

5.  **Use Examples for Guidance**  
    Upload documents or share examples that resemble the desired output\. This helps the AI better understand the format, tone, or style you need\.  
    
6.  **Evaluate the Output Critically**  
    Assess the AI’s response using these criteria:

-   -   Accuracy: Does the information align with the task requirements? 
    -   Relevance: Is the content appropriate for the audience and purpose? 
    -   Structure and Organization: Is the output well\-structured and easy to follow? 
    -   Language and Style: Does the tone and style suit the context? 
    -   Depth of Analysis: Does it demonstrate insight and thoroughness? 
    -   Originality: Is the content unique and thoughtfully crafted? 
    -   Biases or Errors: Are there any inaccuracies or unintended biases? 

7.  **Prioritize Information Security**  
    Share only “Low Risk” information as defined by [UBC’s Information Security Standard U1](https://cio.ubc.ca/information-security-standards/U1)\. This includes data that can be freely disclosed or would cause minimal harm if exposed\. Also visit [UBC’s Interim PIA Guidelines on Generative AI Tools](https://privacymatters.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/2024-09/PIA_Guidelines_GenAI.pdf)\.

## **Generating Visual Content with GenAI Software Tools**

**What AI\-powered image generation software is available at UBC?**

Microsoft Copilot is the only GenAI chatbot tool that is available at UBC that has undergone a privacy impact assessment and can be used securely\. As staff, you have access to unlimited text and image generation that leverages current technologies through UBC’s enterprise subscription\. 

Adobe Creative Cloud \(Adobe CC\), including Adobe Firefly, a GenAI image generation software, is also available at UBC\. Adobe CC also offers Adobe Express, a user\-friendly design app similar to Canva\. However, Adobe is available to staff only by paid subscription, approved on a case\-by\-case basis at the department level\.

Canva is a popular, user\-friendly alternative to Adobe CC\. Some staff and departments at UBC may choose to use it in their work, but note that it is not UBC\-supported\. Canva offers a free plan; however, Magic Canva, their GenAI tool, is available only to paid subscribers\. 

**What are some implications of using AI tools to generate images?** 

**Copyright:** Most AI\-generated images lack clear copyright protection, and legal frameworks are still evolving to address AI image generation\. 

**Creator compensation:** AI training often uses existing artwork without directly compensating the original artist\(s\)\. Many artists argue this constitutes unauthorized use of their works and violates their copyright privileges\. 

**Environmental impact:** AI image generation is energy intensive\. The data centers powering AI image generation consume significant amounts of electricity and potable water, and it’s estimated that producing a single AI image is equivalent to the energy needed to charge a smartphone\. 

### EXPLORE

**Generate an image using Copilot or Adobe Firefly:**  

Select either Copilot or Firefly and use it to generate an image\. You can generate an image you might use in your workplace \(such as an image for a presentation or an event flyer\) or something else that appeals to you\. If you can’t think of anything, you can use one of the following sample prompts: 

-   *A promotional image for a bake sale that includes cookies, cupcakes, tarts and other desserts\.* 
-   *A laptop with an image of an AI chatbot on the screen\.*
-   *A nature scene including water, trees and a field with a herd of deer\.*
-   *A university staff member delivering a presentation to a room full of other staff\.*

Prompt the AI engine to adjust the image to include additional specifications such as style, colour palette, composition and lighting\.

Example prompt: *Please adjust the image to the following specifications:* 

-   *Style: Modern, clean* 
-   *Colour palette: Navy blue, magenta, and white* 
-   *Composition: A laptop computer on a dark wood table, with an image of an AI chatbot on the screen* 
-   *Lighting: Natural daylight from upper left* 

Review the image for problems such as bias, distorted text, unrealistic imagery, or failure to accurately reflect the prompt\. 

**Adjust an image with Adobe Photoshop’s Generative Fill**

Adobe Photoshop is an industry tool for creating, editing and altering images\. Its GenAI feature, Generative Fill, allows users to easily alter or generate elements for existing images\.

1.  Use Generative Fill to add something to the image on the screen, or to extend the image to make it larger\.  

1.  Review the generated output\. How well did the Generative Fill / Extend feature work for you? Are there any problems with the image it generated \(e\.g\., bias, distortion, stylistic mismatch, unrealistic imagery, failure to accurately reflect the prompt\)? 

### REFLECT 

-   How did you feel about the image\(s\) generated? Do you notice any distortions or other problems? What does that say about the capabilities of AI image generation tools?

-   Are the images you created suitable for their purpose? If not, do you think they could be improved to make them more suitable?

-   On balance, given the potential benefits but also the harms and drawbacks of using AI for image generation, would you feel comfortable using these tools in your work at UBC? 

### EXTEND

-   Do you think the companies that create these GenAI tools have a responsibility to fairly compensate artists for the images that their tools are trained on? 

-   Are initiatives like Adobe’s Content Credentials program robust enough to help solve the problems with copyright and AI for creators? 

-   What approaches can we take to reduce the carbon footprint of Generative AI?

## **Misinformation: Navigating AI\-Generated Images** 

**Why Copilot and Firefly?**  

-   Microsoft Copilot is the only GenAI chatbot tool that has undergone a privacy impact assessment and can be used securely with your UBC credentials\. As staff, you have access to unlimited text and image generation through UBC’s enterprise subscription\.  

-   Adobe Creative Cloud \(Adobe CC\), including Adobe Firefly, a GenAI image generation software, is also available at UBC\. Adobe CC offers Adobe Express, a user\-friendly design app similar to Canva\. However, Adobe is available to staff only by paid subscription, approved on a case\-by\-case basis at the department level\. 

**Learning Intentions** 

-   Explain how to determine if an image is AI\-generated or taken by a human\. 

-   Reflect on how AI tools have changed how we interact with visual media and visual evidence\. 

-   Understand how to find the source and context for images

### EXPLORE  

1.  Complete the AI Misinformation Quiz \(link coming soon\), which features real bird photographs alongside AI\-generated images of birds\. Can you tell which images are AI generated, and which are taken by a photographer? 
2.  Was it harder or easier than you expected?  
3.  Were there any visual clues that supported you to separate the human art from the AI generated art?   
    
4.  Four visual cues to help spot an AI generated image: 
5.  AI image generators sometimes struggle with fine details\. 
6.  AI image generators can be bad at backgrounds and text\. 
7.  AI image generators have often struggled at rendering fingers and hands – though they are rapidly improving\. 
8.  AI images can have a strange visual texture and look filtered or abnormally smooth\. 

The rise of more AI generated images means that finding the source and context for images is often more important than looking for visual clues of authenticity\. There are four steps to find the source and context for visuals to identify misinformation: 

1.  Fact\-Checking Tool: A professional fact\-checker like [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/) has already done the work for you\. 
2.  Reverse Image Search: [Google Images](https://images.google.com/) and [TinEye](https://tineye.com/) allow you to upload an image and find visually similar images online to find the original image or identify if it has been manipulated\. [How\-to: Reverse Image Search mini tutorial\.](https://www.pbslearningmedia.org/resource/nmlit17-ela-howtoreverse/is-it-real-using-reverse-image-search-common-sense-education/) 
3.  Verify the Source: Do they really exist? Are they who they say they are? Are they trustworthy? \([MediaSmarts, 2019](https://mediasmarts.ca/sites/default/files/tip-sheet/tipsheet_break_the_fake3.pdf)\) 
4.  Specialized Tools: [FotoForensics](https://fotoforensics.com/) 

Don’t let AI technology undermine your willingness to trust what you see\. Instead learn to use critical literacy skills to identify the truth\. 

### REFLECT  

-   What problems can arise when people mistake AI content as authentic?  

-   What problems can arise when people dismiss authentic visuals as AI\-generated?  
    

### EXTEND  

-   How might your department or institution need to adapt its academic integrity or visual media policies in response to AI\-generated content?
