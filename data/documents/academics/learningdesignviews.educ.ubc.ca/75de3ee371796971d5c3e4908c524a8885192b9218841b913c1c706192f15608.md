## UBC Guidance[https://learningdesignviews\.educ\.ubc\.ca/ai\-corner/\#intro](https://learningdesignviews.educ.ubc.ca/ai-corner/#intro)[Image](https://educ-learningdesignviews-2021.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2024/05/a0-150x150-1.jpg)

Generative AI is a powerful technology that has the potential to transform the way we teach, learn, and work\. However, we must carefully consider issues such as data privacy, bias in algorithms, and the potential impact on education\. Striking the right balance between AI and human interaction is crucial\. UBC encourages experimentation and use within the boundaries of ethical and responsible use\.

## Activities in the Classroom[https://learningdesignviews\.educ\.ubc\.ca/ai\-corner/\#intro](https://learningdesignviews.educ.ubc.ca/ai-corner/#intro)[Image](https://educ-learningdesignviews-2021.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2023/11/ARROW2.png)

Are you thinking of discussing the use of AI tools with your students? Are you concerned about the ethics, risks and challenges posed by these tools? We have developed a range of resources to assist you in guiding these important conversations and ensuring that students understand the significance of responsible AI usage\.

### Co\-Constructing AI Guidelines with Students

Students engage in exploring and discussing the capabilities, limitations, and ethical considerations of AI tools, specifically focusing on their use in coursework by examining the course syllabus\.

[View Activity](https://learningdesignviews.educ.ubc.ca/co-constructing-ai-guidelines/)

### Pen &amp; Bot: Decoding the Power of Writing and AI

This discussion activity is designed to encourage students to think critically about the relationship between writing and thinking\. It aims to explore how writing aids in clarifying thoughts, expanding ideas, and the potential role of AI in this process\.

[View Activity](https://learningdesignviews.educ.ubc.ca/pen-and-bot/)

### Evaluating AI Text

Students are tasked with using an AI tool of their choice to generate a 5\-paragraph essay on a topic of their interest\. They are then asked to evaluate the output using the provided rubric, assessing the AI text for potential ethical issues and risks\.

[View Activity](https://learningdesignviews.educ.ubc.ca/evaluating-ai-text/)

### The Deep Fake Detection Challenge

Based on the MIT Labs “Detect Fakes” experiment, students view transcripts, audio, and videos of speeches by politicians\. They try to identify whether the media is fabricated and how confident they are in this judgment\. They are then asked to reflect on the experience\.

[View Activity](https://learningdesignviews.educ.ubc.ca/the-deep-fake-detection-challenge/)

## External Resources[https://learningdesignviews\.educ\.ubc\.ca/ai\-corner/\#intro](https://learningdesignviews.educ.ubc.ca/ai-corner/#intro)[Image](https://educ-learningdesignviews-2021.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2024/03/a4.jpg)

How is our wider educational community responding to the challenges of AI? What insights are scholarly research providing us regarding the new generative AI tools? What approaches are being considered? This section houses emerging resources and research\.

### Teaching Materials &amp; Resources

-   **Towards AI Literacy: 101\+ Creative and Critical Practices, Perspectives and Purposes**  
    [Towards AI Literacy](https://zenodo.org/records/11613520) is an open crowdsourced collection by \#creativeHE which presents creative and critical practices, perspective and purposes from educators, researchers and students between September 2023 and January 2024\.
-   **AI Pedagogy Project**  
    A set of curated resources created by educators to make informed, intentional decisions about the role of AI in the classroom, the [AI Pedagogy Project](https://aipedagogy.org/) features two main sections: the [AI Guide](https://aipedagogy.org/guide/), which offers an introduction to current AI tools in educational settings with the aim of dispelling common misconceptions, and the [Assignments](https://aipedagogy.org/assignments/) section, an evolving collection of assignments and materials for educators curious about how AI affects their students and syllabi\.
-   **Exploring AI Pedagogy: A Community Collection of Teaching Reflections**  
    [Exploring AI Pedagogy](https://exploringaipedagogy.hcommons.org/) is a crowdsourced space that facilitates reflective discussions on AI experiments in the classroom with an ethos of open, collaborative inquiry\. Here, you can explore the pedagogical experiences shared by community members, including reflections on successes, failures, and results that often present conundrums\.
-   **More Useful Things: AI Resources**  
    This site is a collection of resources from Ethan Mollick, an Associate Professor at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania and the author of the book '[Co\-Intelligence \- Living and Working with AI](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/741805/co-intelligence-by-ethan-mollick/?ref=PRH410E2C567AF)\.' Mollick has been actively exploring and experimenting with how to effectively engage with AI in education\. If you are looking for guidance on incorporating AI into classroom preparation, teaching, and student exercises, the [Prompt Library](https://www.moreusefulthings.com/prompts) is a valuable resource to explore\. The site also features [Crash Course video series on AI](https://www.moreusefulthings.com/resources) for educators and students designed to provide an introduction to the world of Large Language Models\.
-   **TeachAI: AI Guidance for Schools Toolkit**  
    The [AI Guidance for Schools Toolkit](https://www.teachai.org/toolkit), licensed under Creative Commons, aims at equipping school leaders and teachers to incorporate artificial intelligence \(AI\) into primary and secondary education while understanding and mitigating the potential risks\. It articulates [Seven Principles of AI in Education](https://www.teachai.org/toolkit-principles) for developing effective guidance\.
-   **AI &amp; Ethics Presentation by [Dr\. Torry Trust](https://www.torreytrust.com/) \(March 2024\)**  
    These [open access slides about AI &amp; Ethics](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1x3G2zHUNz0_luCP8WghoPWbXBRWLaRv0Nern2DSAuIc/edit#slide=id.g1ffbc01e9c3_0_300), covering privacy and student data, bias, misinformation, hallucinations, human labor, digital divide, intellectual property rights, and environmental costs of AI, serve as a useful and well rounded resource for discussing the ethical dimensions and societal impacts of artificial intelligence\.
-   **A Framework for AI Literacy  
    **This [AI literacy framework](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2024/6/a-framework-for-ai-literacy?utm_source=Selligent&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=er_content_alert_newsletter&amp;utm_content=6-05-24&amp;utm_term=_&amp;m_i=lq4lhrErtge3%2Bmqn88DbHurlXlALgsWeHieC%2B3bRgWsceaELO6%2BsvUYIJp1ZCVM9wuXZF8MOTSF7QPvMRhjD52LO7LDt%2BNlll4&amp;M_BT=102146802322) from Barnard College offers a conceptual foundation for AI education and programming eﬀorts in higher education institutional contexts\.
-   **The AI Assessment Scale  
    **Leon Furze's [AI Assessment Scale](https://leonfurze.com/2023/12/18/the-ai-assessment-scale-version-2/) is a tool with five options for whether and how to allow AI use for an assignment in the course\.
-   **The Fundamentals of Teaching for Integrity in the Age of AI  
    **Developed by the UC San Diego Academic Integrity Office, this [GenAI and Academic Integrity Guide](https://sites.google.com/ucsd.edu/crafting-a-genai-and-ai-policy/home) covers the fundamentals for navigating Generative AI for instructors and provides guidance on creating AI policy for courses\.
-   **Teaching AI Ethics  
    **The [Teaching AI Ethics](https://leonfurze.com/2023/01/26/teaching-ai-ethics/) collection by Leon Furze is designed to help educators navigate key areas of ethical concern with their students\.
-   **Student Guide to Artificial Intelligence  
    **An initiative of Elon University‘s Imagining the Digital Future Center, in partnership with the American Association of Colleges and Universities \(AAC&amp;U\), [Student Guide to Artificial Intelligence](https://studentguidetoai.org/) is designed as an AI How\-to manual for college students \.
-   **Online Course on Indigenous perspectives in AI  
    **CIFAR's free self\-paced course on [Indigenous perspectives in AI](https://learning-s-site-12cb.thinkific.com/collections/products) teaches about Indigenous knowledge\-keeping and worldviews\.

### Articles, Videos &amp; Blog Posts

-   **Surfshark\. \(2025, May 22\)\. [*Meta AI just beat Gemini as the most data\-hungry chatbot*\.](https://surfshark.com/research/chart/ai-chatbots-privacy)**  
    Have you wondered what personal information AI chatbots are tracking? Surfshark’s research explores the data collected by popular AI chatbots, highlighting privacy concerns and the extent to which these platforms gather user information\.
-   **Sutherland, G\. \(2025\)\. [Understanding GenAI through Indigenous ways of knowing](https://www.teachermag.ca/post/understanding-genai-through-indigenous-ways-of-knowing)\. *Teacher Magazine*\.**  
    In this post, Dr\. Gregory Sutherland, lecturer in Indigenous pedagogies and practice at Simon Fraser University highlights how the tenets of Indigenous education can help us to reframe the discourse around GenAI from one of surveillance, academic dishonesty, and appropriation, to one that is about relationships, ethicality, and respect
-   **Burrell, J\., &amp; Metcalf, J\. \(2024\)\. [Introduction for the special issue of “Ideologies of AI and the consolidation of power”: Naming power\.](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/issue/view/749) *First Monday, 29\(4*\)\.**  
    The articles in this special issue employ a constellation of terms to capture dimensions of power, such as censorship, domination, eugenics, racism, and colonialism\. In using these terms, they explore how power in the field of artificial intelligence, power held by those in possession of a singular status \(i\.e\., the ‘godfathers’ of the field\) steers us away from much broader truth\-seeking and richer arguments\.
-   **Elrod, A\. G\. \(2024, March 19\)\. [The Machine\-Translated Web: Signs of a New Educational Reality\.](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2024/3/the-machine-translated-web-signs-of-a-new-educational-reality?utm_source=Selligent&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_campaign=er_content_alert_newsletter&amp;utm_content=3-27-24&amp;utm_term=_&amp;m_i=04qshvJOLWzISiN5roeVR_29y1eD9IbnJ45LQu2LeaLu_abtKL8pgJd0pgnjrq6jXg_3O4ZakDd2DuvJ%2BQf8oOmv0YHAFY800r&amp;M_BT=102146802322) *EDUCAUSE***  
    With AI models producing counterfeit knowledge and AI translation creating homogenized version of the languages, Elrod calls for the need to maintain educational standards and sustain epistemological foundations upon which education and scholarly discourse rests\.
-   **Bali, M\. \(2024, February 26\)\. [Where are the crescents in AI?](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/highereducation/2024/02/26/where-are-the-crescents-in-ai/)*LSE Higher Education Blog***  
    In this [LSE Higher Education](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/highereducation/) blog post, Maha Bali, advocates the need for cultivating critical AI literacy in students, and shares her current teaching practices, ideas and exercises for educators\.
-   **Bozkurt, A\., Junhong, X\., Lambert, S\., Pazurek, A\., Crompton, H\., Koseoglu, S\., \.\.\. &amp; Romero\-Hall, E\. \(2023\)\. [Speculative futures on ChatGPT and generative artificial intelligence \(AI\): A collective reflection from the educational landscape\.](https://www.asianjde.com/ojs/index.php/AsianJDE/article/view/709)** *Asian Journal of Distance Education, 18\(1*\), 53\-130\.  
    Through fictional storytelling, the narratives in this paper demonstrate that the integration of AIEd in education is nuanced, complex, and context\-dependent\. The researchers conclude that the central argument in all debates is the question of what humans will do and what it means to be human if AI can do things that humans have been doing all the time\. It is high time to decide how to position the value of human vs\. AI\-generated content\.

### Academic Innovation

-   **[STORM](https://storm.genie.stanford.edu/) from Stanford University** is  a free AI research tool trained to minimize hallucinations\.
-   **[SMARTIE](https://www.smartie.dev/)** \(Strategic Module Assistant for Rubrics, Tasks and Inclusive Education\) is an AI tool designed by [University of Calgary professor Soroush Sabbaghen](https://rabble.ca/education/how-ai-can-be-used-to-create-post-secondary-curriculum/?utm_content=buffera9978&amp;utm_medium=social&amp;utm_source=twitter+buffer&amp;utm_campaign=buffer) that helps educators design curriculum\. Like an intelligent assistant, this web\-based application assists educators with designing EDIA\-aware course content such as descriptions, reading lists, learning activities, and rubric designs, creating learning environments which are accessible and adaptable for students\.
-   [**Assessment Partner**](https://assessment-partner.com/), developed at McMaster University \- MacPherson Institute, is an open\-access  generative AI tool that creates customized assessments for educators in post\-secondary settings\.
-   [**OpenScholar**](https://openscilm.allen.ai/), developed by the Allen Institute for AI and the University of Washington, is an open\-source AI research assistant that analyzes over 45 million academic papers to generate citation\-backed insights\. You can use it without signing in, offering easy access to accurate and reliable scientific information\.
