## Gathering Reflections on Technology

We were delighted to see so many insightful reflections shared on our community board last month \(November 2025\)\. Thank you to everyone who posted a note with their unique perspective\! Analyzing your comments helps us develop intentional support practices that cater to community needs\.

[Technology for Betterment Community Board](https://educ-learningdesignviews-2021.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/12/Technology-for-Betterment-Community-Board-360x262.jpg)

The Community Board on display during [TEC Expo](https://lddi.educ.ubc.ca/tec-expo/)

Students passing through the Neville Scarfe foyer, as well as participants at **[TEC Expo](https://lddi.educ.ubc.ca/tec-expo/),** were invited to post their answers to the following questions:

-   How can we use technology more mindfully to promote both learning and well\-being?
-   Has technology helped you connect, learn or find balance — or sometimes caused stress or distraction?
-   In what ways has technology supported your personal growth or academic success?

Through thematic analysis of fifty\-one responses, we identified two major takeaways\.

## Balanced Approaches to Ed Tech

Stances on technology were mixed across comments, with near equal mentions of positive and negative impacts\. Moreover, even within individual responses, technology was described as both helpful and harmful — it supports learning, access, connection and creativity, yet also contributes to distraction, stress, inequity and overwhelm\. Therefore, a key takeaway is that we must balance the powerful functions of technology against the burdens that it imposes\.

### The paradox of online connectedness

Social technology well illustrates how tech can create a paradox: in this case, connection and disconnection at once\. Across the board, students described technology as something that both expands their world and fragments it\. It strengthens relationships across distance while weakening presence in daily life, supports learning while pulling attention away from it\. The “double\-edged sword” of social media appears frequently — connection intertwined with toxicity, inspiration bound up with exhaustion\. Their responses reveal a generation that already grasps the tension between connection and isolation\.

### Burdens and boons

Respondents often mentioned stress, overwhelm and the mental\-health burden of the digital world\. Many expressed frustration with distraction, over\-stimulation, unreliable systems and the fatigue of constant connection, and they framed these not as isolated incidents but as part of the emotional landscape of learning today\. Yet, above all else, learning with technology was described as increasingly productive to the point of being necessary for success\. Balancing these potent issues should be a high priority in any implementations of educational technology\.

## Intentional Principles for the Use of Technology

We identified a recurring theme of valuing mindful, intentional use of technology and recognizing that the impact of tech depends on how it is used\. Comments repeatedly emphasized that “how” matters more than “what\.” Participants understood that technology becomes beneficial or harmful depending on the intentionality behind its use\. Our community members recognize that they have agency within digital ecosystems\. As such, our second takeaway is the importance of intentional principles that can guide the practices of these learners and educators\. For them, mindful engagement is not optional — it is the condition for balance\. Below are two examples of how our community already shows an inclination toward intentional tech\-use principles\.

### Access framed through an equity lens

Students resist simplistic ideas of “access \= devices\.” They articulate that meaningful benefit depends on environmental, social, and cultural capital\. This speaks to inequities often overlooked in tech\-forward educational narratives\. Their awareness signals readiness for conversations about justice, accessibility, and the infrastructures that enable \(or constrain\) learning\.

### Desire for balance and healthier relationships with technology

Students mentioned limiting screen time, reconnecting with analogue tools, and stepping away to preserve mental health\. This is not nostalgia; it is a desire for recalibration\. Their reflections suggest a readiness for learning experiences that foreground balance, well\-being, and intentional digital engagement\.
