Global health practicums provide unique opportunities for students to practice health are in immersive cultural contexts\. UBC Okanagan School of Nursing \(SON\) offers Global Health practicums in rural and remote communities both in Canada and internationally\. Members of the SON Global Health Committee \(GHC\), the School of Nursing Indigenous Liaison, and course instructors will be available as a resource to students interested in pursuing a Global Health Practicum\.

### International Global Health Practicum

In the final semester of the BSN program, students can apply for a practicum experience in the international setting of either Ghana or Zambia\. In preparation for this experience, students participate in an intensive week of pre\-departure training that focuses on global health and healthcare in the destination country, including the impact of colonization, its culture, health care system, and current health challenges\.

### About the program

You have the opportunity to work with communities in either Ghana or Zambia\. Clinical practicums take place in March/April\.

While this practicum fits best with the Cohort 3 schedule, if you are interested, please see the Assistant Director to explore your options\.

**UBCO\-OKNR Info** [**nursing\.info@ubc\.ca**](mailto:nursing.info@ubc.ca) 

Our intention is to enter into these practicums with a sense of cultural humility and practice with cultural safety\. We come with the attitude to build on strengths in the community, and collaborate with and learn from our partners\. Student learning is experiential and transformative in the application of global health theory and the lived experience of community members\. The student experience is one of self\-growth and critical reflection, which often leads to transformative growth and learning\.

### Costs

Cost of the program and travel will be between $7,000 to $8,000\. The final cost is dependent on the number of students in the global health practicum and the current costs of accommodations, flights, etc\. You will be provided with a more detailed budget in September in the academic year you are going\.

Please be aware that you will also being paying the full Semester VIII tuition and student fees\.

**Note:** The costs will be offset by $1,000 for UBC students qualifying for the Go Global Award\.

### Timeline &amp; Coursework

The approximate timeline for these five\-week practice placement is early\-March to mid\-April for Ghana and early\-November to mid\-December for Zambia\.

#### Week one:

Preparatory seminars and labs,  then travel to destination \(approximately three days\)\.

#### Week two to five:

Practicum begins\. You will rotate through various settings throughout the four weeks in country and then debrief before coming home\. You may choose to continue traveling once you’ve completed your practicum\.

### Canadian Rural and Remote Practicum

In the final semester of the BSN program, students can apply for a practicum experience in a rural or remote community within Canada\. A range of different types of clinical placements may be available \(hospital, health centre, community\-based, or a combination\)\. Previous placements include, but are not limited to: Haida Gwaii, Revelstoke, Golden, Williams Lake, Tofino, Osoyoos, Dawson City, and Whitehorse\.

This 240\-hour practicum can be completed by students in any cohort of the BSN program during semester 8\.

### About the program

Through this practicum you have the opportunity to apply nursing concepts and skills in a rural and/or remote setting in Canada\.

You will further your knowledge and skills in the areas of community engagement, health promotion, community identified Indigenous health priorities, cultural safety, and acute care/emergency services\.

You will have a faculty supervisor; however, you will be primarily working with a nurse within the community in a 1:1 preceptorship model\.

This rich immersion opportunity helps you to understand the complex health challenges that impact people with limited healthcare resources\. You will live in the community where you are learning\.

### Eligibility &amp; Timeline

Commonly recommended electives for rural and remote placements are NRSG\_O 428 Advanced Community Health and HINT\_O 429 Advanced Global Health\. Nursing elective pre\-requisite course recommendations may vary based on the practice site\. Please consult with the practice placement coordinator or rural and remote instructor for individual advising about electives\.

View the [Out of Region Practice Placement Standard](https://nursing.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2025/10/Out-of-Region-Placements-October-2025-Final.pdf)\. You should meet the out\-of\-region requirements to be considered for a Rural and Remote placement\.

### Costs

You are responsible for travel and accommodation costs during your placement\. Costs may be offset by a rural and remote nursing award, or by practice site contributions to travel and accommodation\.

Please be aware that you will also be paying the full Semester VIII tuition and student fees\.

Please review the [Out of Region Placement Standard](https://nursing.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2025/10/Out-of-Region-Placements-October-2025-Final.pdf) for more information about travel costs and returning to the Okanagan if not meeting practice competencies\.

### Rural and Remote Nursing Practicum—Whitehorse, YT

I am beyond grateful for this experience, as it brought opportunities I would have never imagined possible\. The nurses here utilize their entire scope of practice and care for critically ill patients\.

I gained confidence and skills utilizing my full student scope of practice every shift and was able to practice with nurses in a variety of hospital units such as the ED, ICU, Med/Surg, Maternity, Mental Health, Daycare Surgery, Endoscopy, PACU, and shadow in the OR and Chemotherapy room\.

I already know that I would like to return to a remote location such as Whitehorse, as I enjoyed the challenge of constantly critically thinking and working with talented healthcare providers\.

I developed a completely new attitude towards Nursing and was able to develop my relational practice skills as well\.

**—Chloe Harrold, BSN 2022**
