### MDIA 450 — Advanced Media Projects 

Bachelor of Media Studies, University of British Columbia  
2026–2027 Academic Year

### **Are you a non\-profit, community organization, or UBC\-affiliated program with a media challenge you need help solving? We want to hear from you\.** 

MDIA 450: Advanced Media Projects is a 6\-credit capstone course in UBC’s Bachelor of Media Studies \(BMS\) program, running September 2026 through April 2027\. In its inaugural year, the course partners with a select group of community and institutional organizations to develop ambitious, public\-facing media projects\. We are currently inviting applications from prospective community partners for next year’s cohort\.

### **What Is MDIA 450?** 

MDIA 450 is a practice\-based capstone course that brings together fourth\-year BMS students to develop complex, interactive media projects for real community partners\. The course is grounded in community engaged learning \(CEL\) principles: relationships between students and partners are built to be meaningful, respectful, and reciprocal\. 

Rather than working in isolation, student groups are intentionally assembled to be multidisciplinary — drawing on expertise across the three BMS modes of practice: 

- **Row 1**

  - **Cell 1:**

    Data visualization, infographics, interactive data, computational methods
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    **🎨** **Visual**

  - **Cell 3:**
  
    **✍️** **Narrative** 
    
    Journalism, podcasting, storytelling, written campaigns, editorial strategy, and public writing

### **A Team\-Taught Course: Expert Guidance Across Disciplines** 

One of the defining features of MDIA 450 is its team\-taught structure\. Five faculty members with distinct disciplinary specializations co\-instruct the course, each contributing their research expertise to guide student projects: 

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Christine D’Onofrio \(T1 lead\)** **Visual Arts** | Design research, artistic research and production, materials, visual interactions |
| **Dr\. Patrick** **Pennefather \(T2 lead\)** **Theatre &amp; Sound** | Media praxis, interdisciplinary research frameworks, prototyping, interactive environments |
| **Dr\. Keren Zaiontz** **Cinema Studies** | Theoretical inquiry, cultural &amp; audience analysis, media theory, critical media studies |
| **Kathryn Gretsinger** **Journalism** | Narrative structures, interviews, podcasts, editorial strategy, public communication |
| **Dr\. Fatemeh Sia** **Information Studies** | Data collection and visualization, coding, information design, data ethics |

Faculty guide student through appropriate elements of interdisciplinary projects and are part of in\-class visits to each project group throughout the year, meaning your project benefits from expert oversight at every stage of development\. 

### **What Media Student Teams Can Build** 

Projects are public\-facing and grounded in rigorous research\. Project ideas may include: 

-   Social media campaigns with accompanying websites or interactive landing pages 

-   Podcast or interview series with editorial design packages 

-   Physical or digital installations with interactive elements 

-   Print and digital outreach campaigns \(posters, brochures, motion graphics\) 

-   Interactive data stories and infographic editorial features 

-   Sound walks, story maps, and location\-based digital experiences 

-   Event design, promotion and facilitation \(symposia, screenings, community gatherings\) 

-   Online archives, databases, or multimedia publication platforms 

### **Examples of Projects** 

A few illustrative examples: 

-   A multimedia storytelling and sound walk campaign supporting UNESCO World Heritage status for Vancouver’s Chinatown 

-   An interactive data story translating UBC climate science research for public audiences 

-   A social media and installation campaign for caribou protection and animal rights advocacy 

-   A campus\-wide student wellness activation using app technology, murals, and mindfulness environments 

Projects are always adapted to your specific needs — the student team will work in close dialogue with your organization to identify the right media forms and research methods\. 

### **What We Look For in a Community Partner** 

Community partners in MDIA 450 function as clients in a professional, collaborative relationship\. We value genuine partnership built on mutual exchange\. Strong partner projects tend to share a few key characteristics: 

-   Connected to a non\-profit, activist, social cause, educational or critical academic endeavour 

-   Require a media output that is public facing and interacts with users &amp; viewers 

-   Involve a real, meaningful critical media problem or communication challenge where media can make a difference 

-   Allow students to conduct research, develop strategy, and exercise creative judgment, as well as learn and experiment 

-   Are open to interdisciplinary perspectives and willing to be challenged and surprised 

Projects do not need to be fully formed before you apply\. We are open to hearing inklings of an idea\. Part of the collaboration is developing the scope of the project together\. Applications will be followed up in July for further development with the team\.  

### **Partnership Expectations** 

At various phases of the process, partners will be invited to check in and provide feedback in person and/or online\. This includes a; \(1\) project development \(2\) partnership orientation, \(3\) information gathering interview, \(4\) all partner “green light” pitch session, \(5\) development check\-in, and \(6\) final showcase\.
