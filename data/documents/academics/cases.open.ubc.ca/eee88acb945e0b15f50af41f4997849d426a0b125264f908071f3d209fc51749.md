### Morphology

Grizzly bears \(*Ursus arctos*\) are a major representative of the wild\. \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\] Grizzly bears usually weigh 136\-680 kilograms and the males are usually larger in size \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\]\[[\[2\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-rs-2)\]\. They belong to the same family as polar bears and black bears \[[\[3\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Mattson-3)\]\. The correct scientific name is brown bear, however, they are referred as grizzlies due to distinct lighter colour hair on their back which gives them “grizzled” appearance \[[\[4\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Ng-4)\]\. Grizzly bears have a hump on their shoulders\. This is due to their mass of muscle which is specifically designed for digging \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\]\. It is easy to distinguish black bears and grizzlies by looking at their faces: black bears have straight\-faced profile while grizzlies have a dished\-face profile\. \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\] \[[\[3\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Mattson-3)\] In the colour range, Grizzlies are usually dark brown, but can vary from light cream colour to black which is mainly affected by their genetics \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\] \[[\[4\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Ng-4)\]\. In addition, grizzlies have relatively longer claws than other species of bears, designed for digging\[[\[3\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Mattson-3)\]\. Grizzlies can adapt to cold environment with the help of their thick fur\. Also, their long claws and powerful shoulders help them create winter dens and scavenge for food \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\] \.

### Habitat /Distribution/ population

Grizzlies can be found throughout Western Canada and Alaska\. Alaska has the healthiest population\. \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\] They usually prefer to settle down around streams, lakes and rivers where salmon is plentiful\. Grizzly bears can be observed in a lot locations in BC near salmon habitats, \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\] in both southern and northern BC\. Grizzlies can also be found in various ecosystems, from dense montane forests to subalpine meadows, open plains and arctic tundra\. Historically, there were around 50,000 grizzly bears in North America\. Less than half of them now remain\. Canada has a relatively stable population, especially in Northern Canada\. However, grizzly bear population are historically at risk in the southwest\[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\] \[[\[3\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Mattson-3)\]

There are around 15,000 Grizzly bear in British Columbia, around a quarter of the entire North American population\. Their habitat quality and population density varies across the province\[[\[5\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-5)\]\.

### Diet

Grizzlies are omnivores and their diet is mostly plant based \(plants, fungi\) but they eat insects and some small mammals if available \[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\]\[[\[3\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Mattson-3)\]\. Grizzlies are “hibernators”, they will drop their body temperature and breathing rate to maintain their functions\. They enter hyperphagia in the late summer and fall\. They will intensify their calorie intake to gain weight and store energy for winter denning in 2\-4 months\[[\[1\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-DOW-1)\]\.\)

They are a symbol of ecological integrity and represents part of the natural beauty\. They are slow\-reproducing mammals that are sensitive to changes in the environment and have high mortality risks associated with human activities\[[\[6\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Gailus-6)\]\. \(They have strong cultural and social power\. Most people feel enriched and connected with nature when seeing bears, or knowing they are there\. Canadians recognize grizzlies as a national treasure\. ### Role as a Keystone Species

### Prey Regulation

Grizzly bears play an incredibly important ecological role, and are considered a keystone species because of the necessity of their presence for healthy ecosystem functioning\. These bears are noticeably important food chain regulators, as they have strong top\-down effects on their direct prey and associated species \[[\[7\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Apps16-7)\]\. Their removal can cause trophic cascades \[[\[8\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Berger-8)\] to the extent that was seen with wolves in Yellowstone National Park\. Bears and wolves are the main large predators in the north\-west ecosystem, and strongly regulate moose populations \[[\[8\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Berger-8)\]\. Without the regulation of bears and wolves, moose populations would boom and decimate the local vegetation, rapidly decreasing biodiversity and food sources for other herbivores, affecting the entire ecosystem\. Besides from herbivore booms, loss of large predators can cause changes in prey behaviour and changes in the diets/behaviour of smaller meso\-carnivores in the region \[[\[8\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Berger-8)\]\. Grizzly bears consume mammals, fish, invertebrates, and plants, thus their ecological effects extend to all areas of the British Columbian ecosystem, including both terrestrial and aquatic realms\.

### Seed Dispersal

Grizzly bears are omnivores and consume both meat and vegetation\. Bears will hunt animals, which provide a higher energy pay\-off, when available or populations are high enough that they are energetically efficient to hunt, mainly in the summer\. In the fall they typically dig for roots \[[\[7\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Apps16-7)\] and in the spring they scavenge for ripening fruits and berries \[[\[9\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Martinka-9)\]\. Bears are part of a symbiotic relationship with the plants they eat in the spring, as they gain energy and nutrients from eating the fruit and then disperse seeds for the plants \[[\[7\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Apps16-7)\]\. Specifically, grizzly bears have been recorded to be important seed dispersers for blueberries and buffalo berries, although they perform this important function for a wide variety of plant species\. When bears disperse seeds after ingestion they release them in their feces, which provides a nutrient rich environment from which they grow\. Thus grizzly bears play an important role not only in seed dispersal but also in creating an optimal environment for seed germination\. This encourages plant growth and dispersal, which increases biodiversity and vegetation biomass for other herbivore’s consumption\.

### Nitrogen transfer and soil Disturbance

In British Columbia, grizzly bears feed on adult salmon when they return to their original rivers and streams to reproduce\. Pacific salmon play an important role in the transfer of nutrients and energy from the marine to terrestrial environment \[[\[10\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-10)\]\[[\[11\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-11)\]\. Grizzly bears facilitate this by dragging salmon carcasses up from the river onto the land where they are consumed by the bears themselves and other terrestrial predators inserting an energy source into the terrestrial food web\. As well the salmon carcasses decompose on the land, releasing nutrients \(specifically high levels nitrogen\) into the surrounding soils\. These nutrients are necessary for productive soils that encourage plant growth, in turn facilitating high biodiversity\. As well, as grizzly bears scavenge for roots and tubers in the fall they dig and turn up the soil\. Homogenized soil is a major factor in unproductive land, thus turning up the soil increases productivity of the land, in the same manner that farmers will regularly till their soil\. Increasing the productivity of the soil thus again increases vegetative biomass and facilitates plants biodiversity, which supports further animal biodiversity of both herbivores and predators\.

### Current Threats

#### Habitat loss and fragmentation

Habitat loss \[[\[2\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-rs-2)\]

\(1\)Lack of secure habitat for foraging and denning

\(2\)Natural sources of food \(e\.g\., wild bison\) for grizzly bears less available than historically

If there is one symbol of Canada’s remaining wilderness, it is the grizzly bear \(Ursus arctos\)\. As human population has expanded, the Grizzly Bear’s habitat has shrunk\.\[[\[12\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-12)\] Once found across most of western and northern north america, grizzly bears now can only survive in the western sliver of alberta, and most of British Columbia, alaska and the Canadian north \[[\[13\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-13)\]\.

Today there are a minimum of 13,800 Grizzly Bears in British Columbia\. These 13,800 animals must co\-exist with four million humans\. As one of the most widespread huge mammals in the worldwide, the former surviving environment of the Grizzly Bear included Europe, North Africa, northern and central Asia, and North America\. The species persists in small isolated populations in western Europe, the Middle East, China, and Japan, but it occurs more continuously across eastern Europe and northern Asia\. That is the different part among the world\. In North America, Grizzly Bears once occurred as far south as northern Mexico and as far east as the Great Plains and Hudson Bay\. However, the habitats are decreasing rapidly in recent years \[[\[14\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-14)\]

Fragmentation \(\=Greater potential for human\-bear interactions and conflicts\) As a large carnivores, grizzly bears in particular require special consideration to occupy the top of the biotic pyramid for their thrive\. However, from the BC government statistics, we can learn that the habitats of grizzly bears are separated into three main groups: Extirpated, Threatened, and Viable\. Current habitat quality and population density are affected by human\-caused disturbance, the most negative one is road construction\. This is the main factor to isolate grizzly bears into different groups further affect their movement in nature\. \[[\[15\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-15)\]\. Additionally, resource\-based and extractive land use activities \(e\.g\. logging, gas, oil and mineral exploration and extraction, hydro projects, recreational and backcountry uses\) which contribute to the extremely bad fragmentation, loss or significant alteration of critical habitat features and food sources\. \[[\[16\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-16)\]\. What’s worse, the long\-term negative effect is happening based on the fragmentation, long period scale makes genetic fragmentation spread over in the southern parts of its range, where some populations are increasingly isolated and subject to demographic stochasticity \[[\[17\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-COSEWIC-17)\]\.

#### Prey depletion

Though grizzly bears are descended from carnivore ancestors, Grizzly Bear are basically giant omnivores\. Key food sources are those rich in both animal and plant protein\. In the spring, after emergence from denning, this species targets meadows and vegetated shorelines where it can graze on abundant supplies of high protein sedge and grass species\. Throughout the summer foraging includes berries, insects, small mammals and some ungulates \(e\.g\. elk, caribou and moose fawns\)\. In the fall, returning salmon form a key part of the diet which continues to be supplemented with berries and vegetation\. Bears spend most of their time perusing a patchwork of habitats throughout the year, feeding on vegetation, insects and other more reliable, though lower calorie food sources, mostly in spring and summer\. Plant foods make up the majority of a bear’s diet – sometimes as much as 90 per cent\. More importantly, grizzly bears will chase after fish and meat to survive in cold seasons\. However, the food supply in decreasing in BC\. it is an optimization process and should be targeted especially at those populations that have identifiable conservation concerns\. As the food supply diminished, the reproductive rate halved \(to the lowest known in North America\), and the population declined precipitously \(2–5%/year\)\.

#### Trophy Hunt

Since 1977, the earliest date that accurate records were kept, until the end of 2009, resident and non\-resident trophy hunters have killed at least 9,484 grizzly bears\. Since 2004 alone \(when revisions were made to provincial population estimates\), hunters killed 1,773 grizzly bears \(1,174 males and 593 females, as well as six unidenti ed individuals\) in B\.C\. \[[\[3\]](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/grizzly-bear-conservation-in-bc/#cite_note-Mattson-3)\] Many British Columbia people are concerned about the ethics of trophy hunting of apex species such as grizzly, and others are concerned that overhunting threatens grizzly bears’ continued viability in the province\. But, the most important thing is to protect the grizzly bear in a sustainable way to thrive them in nature without or with lower threats and disturbance\. That is the main theme and religious faith of trophy hunt\.
