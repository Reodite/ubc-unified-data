## **Transition to Discipline EPAs**

### **1\. Applying the palliative medicine approach to patient assessment**

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on the application of a holistic approach to information gathering, which considers the patient’s medical condition, symptoms, level of function, social circumstances and support, values, beliefs, and cultural considerations\.
-   This may include the application of common assessment tools such as PPS or ESAS\-r\.
-   This EPA may be observed with any new consult, admission, or follow\-up visit\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation or case review with supervisor, Core or TTP palliative medicine resident, or clinical nurse specialist/nurse practitioner
-   Type of Observation: Direct; Case Review
-   Required Numbers: 1 Direct Observation of Achievement; 1 Observation by a Supervisor

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  COM 1\.1 Communicate using a patient\-centred approach
2.  COM 1\.2 Optimize the environment for patient and family comfort, dignity, privacy, engagement, and safety
3.  COM 2\.3 Seek and synthesize relevant information from other sources \(e\.g\., family, medical record\)
4.  ME 2\.2 Elicit an accurate, relevant history
5.  ME 2\.2 Elicit information about the patient’s and/or family’s understanding of the illness
6.  ME 2\.2 Gather information about the patient’s social circumstances and supports
7.  ME 2\.2 Use validated tools to assess functional status and symptoms
8.  ME 2\.2 Perform a physical examination relevant to the presentation
9.  ME 2\.2 Synthesize and organize clinical information for presentation to a supervisor

### **2\. Sharing information with patients and families**

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on the application of communication skills, to include demonstrating empathy and compassion, attending to expressions of emotion, and using plain language\.
-   It includes identifying and managing barriers to giving and receiving information e\.g\., need for translator\.
-   An important aspect of this EPA is creating safety and room for open discussion in conveying relevant information regarding the patient’s case, including conversations about disease trajectory, prognosis, and death\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation by supervisor or other health care professional
-   Required Numbers: 2 Observations of Achievement; At least one observation by a supervisor

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  COM 1\.2 Optimize the environment for patient and family comfort, dignity, privacy, engagement, and safety
2.  COM 1\.1 Demonstrate empathy, respect, and compassion
3.  COM 3\.1 Provide accurate information
4.  COM 3\.1 Use plain language and avoid medical jargon
5.  COM 1\.5 Recognize when strong emotions \(such as anger, fear, anxiety, or sadness\) are affecting an interaction and respond appropriately
6.  COM 3\.1 Verify and validate the patient’s and/or family’s understanding
7.  COM 4\.3 Answer questions from the patient and/or family
8.  COM 4\.1 Communicate in a manner that is respectful, non\-judgmental, and culturally aware
9.  COM 5\.1 Document the encounter to convey the discussion and its outcome

### **3\. Managing the on\-call duties of Palliative Medicine**

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA includes responding to calls from patients and families as well as physicians, surgeons, and other health care professionals and may be observed during daytime as well as overnight/weekend activities\.
-   It includes being available when unexpected/unscheduled events arise, gathering information, recognizing the urgency of a request, triaging, and providing appropriate advice about initial management and/or patient disposition as well as recognizing issues that can be managed remotely \(by telephone\) versus those that require in\-person assessment\.
-   It also requires documentation of clinical encounters and safe and effective handover\.
-   An important aspect of this EPA is recognizing one’s own limits and seeking assistance\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation and/or review of cases and documentation
-   Required Numbers: 2 Observations of Achievement; At least two different assessors

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  P 1\.1 Respond punctually to requests or calls from patients or other health care professionals
2.  ME 2\.2 Gather information to establish the clinical question
3.  ME 2\.1 Determine the urgency of the situation
4.  ME 2\.4 Provide recommendations for management
5.  ME 2\.4 Determine if an in\-person assessment is required
6.  L 2\.1 Apply knowledge of local protocols and resources
7.  P 1\.1 Work within one’s own limits, asking for assistance as needed
8.  COM 5\.1 Prepare documentation that accurately conveys the encounter\(s\) and outcome
9.  COL 3\.2 Summarize and prioritize patient issues for handover to an accepting physician

### **4\. Supporting patients and families near the time of death**

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on the last hour\(s\) and days of life as well as the immediate period after a patient’s death\.
-   This includes communicating with the patient and/or family, acknowledging their concerns about and describing the dying process, providing support for anticipatory grief and bereavement, and recognizing risk factors for complicated grief and bereavement\.
-   This includes recognizing when to consult with other members of the interprofessional team and seeking assistance as necessary\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation by supervisor or other health care professional \(e\.g\., Core or TTP palliative medicine resident, nurse practitioner, palliative medicine nurse, clinical nurse specialist, or counsellor\)
-   Required Numbers: 2 Observations of Achievement; At least one observation in the last  hours of life

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  COM 1\.1 Demonstrate empathy, respect, and compassion
2.  COM 4\.1 Validate the patient’s and/or family’s experiences and/or concerns
3.  COM 3\.1 Provide information about the process of dying and signs of impending death
4.  COM 4\.1 Ask about and integrate rituals and end\-of\-life practices in keeping with the patient’s and family’s cultural, spiritual and religious beliefs
5.  ME 2\.2 Recognize risk factors for complicated grief and bereavement
6.  ME 2\.4 Provide supportive counselling and resources for coping with loss
7.  P 1\.1 Work within one’s own limits, asking for assistance as needed

### **5\. Completing medico\-legal responsibilities at time of death**

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on the medico\-legal responsibilities of the palliative medicine physician after a patient’s death\.
-   This includes death pronouncement and managing medico\-legal responsibilities at the time of death, such as preparing a death certificate and following local/institutional procedures for notification of death, including considerations regarding notification of the medical examiner/coroner, autopsy and organ donation\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Case discussion and/or documentation review by supervisor or Core or TTP resident
-   Task completed \(select all that apply\): death pronouncement; death certificate; discussion re\. medical examiner/coroner notification; organ donation; request for autopsy; other
-   Required Numbers: 1 Observations of Achievement; At least one death certificate

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  ME 2\.2 Determine the time and nature of death
2.  P 3\.1 Prepare the death certificate in a complete and timely manner
3.  P 3\.1 Adhere to requirements for notification of death, including considerations for mandatory reporting to the medical examiner/coroner
4.  P 3\.1 Apply knowledge of procedures for autopsy and/or organ donation
