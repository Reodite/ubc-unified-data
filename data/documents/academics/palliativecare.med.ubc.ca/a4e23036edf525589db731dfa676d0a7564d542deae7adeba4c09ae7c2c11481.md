## **Foundations EPAs**

### **1\. Recognizing and managing patients with an emergent/urgent presentation**

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on timely assessment and management, including consulting with/referring to other services for definitive management of the problem\.
-   Examples of emergent/urgent presentations include pain or dyspnea crisis, airway obstruction, acute bowel obstruction, severe agitated delirium, suicidality, bleeding, spinal cord compression, superior vena cava syndrome, seizures, or other acute changes in the patient’s status\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation or case review by palliative medicine, medical oncology or radiation oncology supervisor
-   Required Numbers: 5 observations of achievement
    -   At least 1 patient with pain crisis
    -   At least 1 patient with dyspnea crisis
    -   At least 1 patient with severe agitated delirium
    -   At least 1 observation in the inpatient setting
    -   At least 1 observation in the community setting

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  ME 2\.1 Determine the urgency of the situation
2.  ME 2\.2 Perform a history and physical exam relevant to the patient presentation, in a time\-effective manner
3.  ME 2\.3 Review goals of care and realign if needed
4.  ME 2\.2 Select and/or interpret investigations
5.  ME 2\.2 Develop a differential diagnosis
6.  ME 2\.4 Develop and implement an initial management plan
7.  ME 4\.1 Determine the need for and urgency of referral for definitive management
8.  ME 4\.1 Determine appropriate disposition and/or setting for ongoing care
9.  COM 3\.1 Share information about the patient’s condition and plans for management clearly and compassionately
10.  COM 5\.1 Prepare documentation that accurately conveys the encounter\(s\) and outcome
11.  P 1\.1 Work within one’s own limits, asking for assistance as needed

### 2\. Providing consultation for patients with less complex needs

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA includes all aspects of a palliative medicine consultation: applying a biopsychosocial\-spiritual approach to the assessment, gathering information from the history, physical exam, and other sources, working effectively with other members of the palliative medicine team as well as with the referring service, assessing the resources required by the patient and family, and providing advice regarding management\.
-   This EPA may be observed in any care setting \(e\.g\., clinic, community, hospice, acute care, palliative care unit\)\.
-   This EPA includes patients with a serious illness that is potentially life\-limiting but who are not currently in crisis and whose condition is not further complicated by multiple medical/mental health comorbidities or management challenges including those due to social determinants of health, geographic and/or cultural complexities\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation or case review with verification of key findings by supervisor
-   Required Numbers: 5 observations of achievement
    -   At least 3 direct observations
    -   At least 3 different presentations
    -   At least 2 observations in inpatient setting
    -   At least 2 observations in outpatient setting

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  ME 1\.4 Apply a biopsychosocial approach to the clinical assessment
2.  COM 2\.3 Seek and synthesize relevant information from other sources \(e\.g\., family, medical record\)
3.  ME 2\.2 Elicit a history and perform a physical exam
4.  ME 2\.2 Use validated tools to assess functional status and symptoms
5.  ME 2\.2 Assess prognosis using disease specific indicators, including clinical signs, symptoms, and investigations
6.  COL 1\.2 Integrate the assessment and perspectives of other members of the interprofessional team
7.  ME 2\.4 Develop a management plan
8.  COM 3\.1 Explain the palliative care philosophy to the patient and family
9.  HA 1\.1 Facilitate patient and family access to needed services and resources
10.  COL 1\.3 Work effectively with the referring physician and/or service
11.  COM 5\.1 Document the encounter to convey clinical reasoning and the rationale for recommendations

### 3\. Managing patients with common symptoms

Key Features:

-   This EPA focuses on the management of symptoms that are common in palliative medicine practice such as delirium, agitation, depression, anxiety, demoralization, fatigue, nausea, constipation, dyspnea, and insomnia\.
-   It includes assessing the cause of the problem from a biopsychosocial\-spiritual perspective, educating the patient and family about the symptoms and their management, and providing non\-pharmacologic and/or pharmacologic treatment as relevant\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation or case review by supervisor, Core or TTP palliative medicine resident, or clinical nurse specialist/nurse practitioner
-   Required Numbers: 5 observations of achievement
    -   A variety of symptoms
    -   At least 1 psychological symptom \(i\.e\., anxiety, demoralization, depression\)
    -   At least 1 observation in the community setting
    -   At least 2 observations by supervisor

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  ME 1\.4 Apply a biopsychosocial approach to the clinical assessment
2.  ME 2\.2 Elicit a history and perform a physical exam
3.  ME 2\.2 Use validated tools to assess functional status and symptoms
4.  COM 1\.6 Integrate the patient’s beliefs, concerns, expectations and experiences relevant to the presenting problem
5.  ME 2\.2 Assess response to previously attempted treatments, including side effects, toxicity and efficacy
6.  ME 2\.4 Develop a management plan, which may include non\-pharmacologic, pharmacologic and/or interventional treatment\(s\) as relevant
7.  ME 2\.4 Select and prescribe appropriate monitoring strategies
8.  HA 1\.2 Work with the patient and/or family to increase their understanding of their symptoms and health care needs
9.  COM 5\.1 Document the encounter to convey clinical reasoning and the rationale for recommendations

### 4\. Managing patients with pain

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA includes assessing the cause of the pain from a biopsychosocial\-spiritual perspective, educating the patient and family about pain and its management, and providing non\-pharmacologic and/or pharmacologic treatment as relevant\.
-   An important aspect of this EPA is the demonstration of an evidence\-based approach to the selection of one pain management strategy over another\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation or case review by supervisor, Core or TTP palliative medicine resident, or clinical nurse specialist/nurse practitioner
-   Required Numbers: 5 observations of achievement
    
    -   A variety of pain management strategies
    -   At least 1 observation in the community setting
    -   At least 2 observations by supervisor

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  ME 1\.4 Apply a biopsychosocial approach to the clinical assessment
2.  ME 2\.2 Elicit a history and perform a physical exam
3.  ME 2\.2 Use validated tools to assess functional status and symptoms
4.  COM 1\.6 Integrate the patient’s beliefs, concerns, expectations and experiences relevant to the presenting problem
5.  ME 2\.2 Assess response to previously attempted treatments, including side effects, toxicity and efficacy
6.  ME 2\.4 Develop a management plan, which may include non\-pharmacologic, pharmacologic and/or interventional treatment\(s\) as relevant
7.  ME 2\.4 Select and prescribe the appropriate dose and regimen
8.  ME 2\.4 Select and prescribe appropriate monitoring strategies
9.  HA 1\.2 Work with the patient and/or family to increase their understanding of their symptoms and health care needs
10.  COM 5\.1 Document the encounter to convey clinical reasoning and the rationale for recommendations

### 5\. Exploring patient and family suffering

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on the application of open and empathic communication to explore the physical, psychological, socio\-cultural, and/or spiritual/existential aspects of suffering\.
-   The observation of this EPA must be based on an encounter in which at least two components \(i\.e\., physical, psychological, socio\-cultural and/or spiritual/existential\) are explored\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation by supervisor or another member of the interprofessional team, or case discussion with supervisor with input from another team member who was present at the encounter
-   Required Numbers: 2 observations of achievement
    -   At least 1 direct observation by supervisor
    -   At least 1 observation with patient as interlocutor

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  COM 1\.1 Demonstrate empathy, respect and compassion
2.  COM 2\.1 Set up the conversation and ask permission to proceed
3.  COM 2\.1 Invite the patient and/or family member to share what they are experiencing
4.  ME 2\.2 Assess the presence, nature, and sources of an individual’s suffering, exploring underlying depression or anxiety
5.  COM 2\.1 Use empathic listening and appropriate non\-verbal communication to demonstrate attentiveness, interest, and responsiveness
6.  COM 1\.4 Identify, verify and validate non\-verbal cues
7.  COM 4\.1 Validate the patient’s and/or family member’s experience of illness and suffering
8.  COM 4\.1 Identify the individual’s strengths and resources in dealing with suffering
9.  COM 2\.2 Summarize and close the encounter effectively

### 6\. Discussing and documenting goals of care

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA includes exploring the patient’s and/or family’s values and wishes in order to prioritize and set goals of care\.
-   This includes assessing their understanding of the patient’s current status and anticipating the disease trajectory and possible complications of treatment\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation by supervisor or Core or TTP palliative medicine resident
-   Required Numbers: 2 observations of achievement

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  COM 1\.1 Demonstrate empathy, respect and compassion
2.  COM 1\.1 Seek permission from patient and/or their family to talk about their values and wishes for their care
3.  ME 2\.1 Ascertain the patient’s and/or family’s understanding of the patient’s condition
4.  COM 3\.1 Provide information about the disease trajectory and possible complications
5.  COM 2\.1 Explore the patient’s and/or family’s values and wishes for their care
6.  COM 3\.1 Share information and explanations that are clear and accurate
7.  COM 3\.1 Use plain language and avoid medical jargon
8.  COM 4\.1 Communicate in a manner that is respectful, non\-judgmental, and culturally aware
9.  COM 1\.3 Demonstrate an understanding of the disease experience from the patient’s and family’s perspective
10.  COM 3\.1 Recommend goals of care based on the patient’s and/or family’s values and wishes for their care
11.  COM 2\.2 Summarize and close the encounter effectively
12.  COM 5\.1 Document the encounter to convey the discussion and its outcome

### 7\. Facilitating family meetings

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on leading meetings for the purposes of discussing complex issues related to clinical status, patient and family expectations, illness trajectory, and/or possible complications\.
-   This includes creating a safe space and room for free discussion, eliciting patient and family concerns, providing information, encouraging participation from all members, attending to meeting flow and organization, facilitating shared decision\-making, and summarizing and documenting decisions reached during the meeting\.
-   This EPA includes complex communication scenarios such as those requiring the use of an interpreter\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct observation and documentation review by supervisor, which may include input from other health care professionals
-   Required Numbers: 2 observations of achievement
    -   At least 2 different focus of meetings
    -   At least 1 where patient is present

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  COL 1\.2 Determine who is part of the meeting and their roles, both from the patient side and the health care team side
2.  P 1\.2 Prepare for a meeting, reviewing its purpose and background information
3.  ME 2\.1 Ascertain participants’ understanding of the situation and the reason for the encounter
4.  COM 1\.4 Respond to non\-verbal communication and use appropriate non\-verbal behaviours to enhance communication
5.  L 3\.1 Encourage free discussion and participation from attendees
6.  L 3\.1 Attend to meeting flow, organization, and time management
7.  COM 3\.1 Share information and explanations that are clear and accurate
8.  COM 4\.3 Use communication skills and strategies that help the patient and family make an informed decision
9.  L 3\.1 Summarize and clarify key points and discussions
10.  COM 1\.5 Recognize when strong emotions \(such as anger, fear, anxiety, or sadness\) are affecting an interaction and respond appropriately
11.  COM 5\.1 Document the encounter to convey the discussion and its outcome

### 8\. Working effectively within an interprofessional team

**Key Features:**

-   This EPA focuses on respectful and effective collaboration with the care team\.
-   This includes understanding and valuing the role and scope of practice of interprofessional team members \(e\.g\., nurses, pharmacists, psychologists, spiritual care providers\)\.
-   The observation of this EPA is not based on a single patient encounter, but rather on the resident performance over a period of at least one week\.

**Assessment Plan:**

-   Direct and indirect observation by supervisor with input from members of the interprofessional team
-   Required Numbers: 3 observations of achievement

**CanMEDS Milestones:**

1.  COL 1\.2 Demonstrate an understanding of the scope and expertise of other health care professionals within the specialist palliative care team
2.  COL 1\.3 Communicate effectively with other physicians and health care professionals
3.  P 1\.1 Respond punctually to requests or calls from patients or other health care professionals
4.  COL 1\.1 Respond appropriately to input from other health care professionals
5.  HA 1\.1 Facilitate patient and family access to needed services and resources
6.  P 1\.1 Behave in a professional manner
