From January 17–21, the CTLT Teaching Assistant \(TA\) Institute includes a collection of workshops designed to support TAs as their roles change along with the move to online courses\. The week of professional development workshops are open to graduate students and undergraduate TAs\. This year, workshops will be organized into five themes, and if you **complete four workshops** within a theme, and complete the related **[pre &amp; post\-session](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/) online modules**, you are eligible for a letter of completion that you can include in your Teaching Portfolio and CV\. [Session materials and relevant resources](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Documentation:CTLT_programs/CTLT_Institute/2022-TA-Institutes/January) will be curated on the UBC Wiki to support your ongoing learning\.

[Schedule of Events \(PDF\)](https://institute2-ctlt.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2021/12/TA-INS-Schedule_20211201_RevA.pdf)

> TA Institute participants who meet the eligibility criteria for a Completion Letter will be receiving their letters by the end of March \(see below for the eligibility criteria\)\. If you meet the eligibility criteria and have any questions about the status of your letter *after* that date, please contact [CTLT\.TAInstitute@ubc\.ca](mailto:CTLT.TAInstitute@ubc.ca)\.
> 
> Eligibility criteria:
> 
> -   *Fully* attended 4 sessions under a theme
> -   Completed the [pre and post modules](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/) per theme\(s\)
> -   Submitted a [completion form](https://ubc.ca1.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_ehacHtr8Tal8SUu)
> 
> The pre\-session modules must be completed by January 17, 2022 by 9 am, and the post\-session modules must be completed by January 31, 2022 at noon\.

### [Cultivating Connection and Community: In\-Person vs\. Online](https://events.ctlt.ubc.ca/events/cultivating-connection-and-community-in-person-online/)

**Monday, Jan\. 17 \| 10:00 am – 11:30 am \| Zoom**

THEME: [FACE\-TO\-FACE, ONLINE, AND HYBRID TEACHING TRANSFERABLE SKILLS](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/); [INTERDISCIPLINARY AND EXPERIENTIAL LEARNING](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/#experiential)

The social context in which our teaching and learning occurs is a crucial factor in how we achieve our learning outcomes\. Building connections and community in our classrooms is essential to create the environment needed for our learners to be able to contribute their insight, question assumptions \(their own &amp; others’\), offer different perspectives, and support each other in their learning\. Classrooms, like most social spaces, have complex and intertwining power dynamics\. Some like the instructor\-learner relationship may be more apparent while others\- especially those stemming from facets of our identities such as race may be more “coded”\. In this session, we will reflect on our own positionality in terms of race and culture and how this informs our role as TAs\. We will discuss how we can identify and address barriers that learners may face in a classroom and we will design strategies to help make the classroom a safe space for learning\.

### [In a Room or on Zoom: How to Balance Participatory Learning with Didactic Lecturing](https://events.ctlt.ubc.ca/events/in-a-room-or-on-zoom-how-to-balance-participatory-learning-with-didactic-lecturing/)

**Friday, Jan\. 21 \| 10:00 am – 11:30 am \| Zoom**

THEMES: [FACE\-TO\-FACE, ONLINE, AND HYBRID TEACHING TRANSFERABLE SKILLS](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/); [TEACHING FUNDAMENTALS](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/#fundamentals)

Student engagement in an online classroom has been reported to be challenging for both the students and the instructors\. However, given that some forms of online learning have shown to be effective, it looks like they are here to stay\. So how do we design our teaching to maximize student engagement and balance the participatory elements of a lesson with the traditional style of lecturing? In this workshop, participants will be able to identify the engagement practices they have implemented or observed over the past months of online teaching and learning\. Participants will also have the chance to compare and contrast the active learning offered by those engaging elements with the traditional didactic style of teaching\. Lastly, they will get the opportunity to practice designing and modifying their own active learning strategies in the context of both online and in\-person classrooms\.

Facilitators:

### [Making the Grade: Marking, Feedback and Assessment for Learning](https://events.ctlt.ubc.ca/events/making-the-grade-marking-feedback-and-assessment-for-learning/)

**Friday, Jan\. 21 \| 3:00 pm – 5:00 pm \| Zoom**

THEMES: [FACE\-TO\-FACE, ONLINE, AND HYBRID TEACHING TRANSFERABLE SKILLS](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/); [TASHIP 101 \(ESSENTIALS TO PREP FOR TASHIP\)](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/#taship); [TEACHING FUNDAMENTALS](https://institute.ctlt.ubc.ca/ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute/january-2022-ta-institute-themes/#fundamentals)

In this session we will explore all aspects of assessment, from the basics required for you to excel in your position to deeper considerations of the role of assessment in student learning\. As a Teaching Assistant, one of the most important parts of your role will include assessing students’ work fairly and supporting their learning with constructive feedback\. These aspects of TAing also have a reputation for being some of the most time consuming, especially at first\. To help flatten the learning curve, we’ll explore evidence\-based techniques for marking to make the most of your limited hours, both online and in\-person\. You’ll leave with easy\-to\-implement strategies and tools to help you grade assignments and tests, give feedback, and design your own assessments\.
