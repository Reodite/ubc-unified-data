## Beyond One\-Size\-Fits\-All: Intersectional Approaches to Teaching

## December 7 and 8th, 2026

Our students bring a wide range of social, cultural, and political identities and experiences to our learning spaces, including race, ability/disability, gender and sexual identity and orientation, class, migration, age, language, and more\. Intersectional pedagogy asks us to recognize how these different aspects of students’ identities and experiences can overlap and shape how they experience teaching, learning, and belonging\. It encourages educators to think about whose experiences and perspectives are represented, who may face barriers to participation, and how we can create learning environments where all students feel seen, respected, and able to succeed\. There has never been a more pressing time for educators to come together to share practical tools, strategies, and inclusive practices that bring intersectional approaches into our teaching and learning spaces\. 

We invite UBC educators, researchers, and scholars to propose interactive workshops that explore what it means to **put intersectional thinking into action**—and how intersectional perspectives can shape what we teach, how we teach, how we assess, and how we create learning environments\. 

Proposals might explore questions such as: How can we challenge compulsory able\-bodied\-ness and narrow expectations of how students’ bodies and minds should perform? How might creative practices such as visual art, drama, music, and storytelling offer different ways for students to explore and demonstrate their learning? How can we challenge settler\-colonial assumptions about ownership and reinhabitation by restoring Indigenous relationships to, and responsibilities for, the land? These are just a few examples—we welcome approaches that invite us to rethink established practices and imagine other possibilities\. 

Bring us your ideas, practices, questions, and experiments\. What have you tried? What have you learned? What are you rethinking? What can educators do differently, and what can participants take into their own teaching practice? 

We are especially interested in workshops that: 

-   share practical tools, strategies, activities, or approaches that educators can adapt to their own teaching; 

-   explore how intersecting identities, experiences, and ways of knowing shape students’ experiences of teaching and learning; 

-   invite participants to reflect on and examine their own pedagogical practices; 

-   create opportunities for participants to learn from one another and engage in meaningful dialogue; and 

-   leave participants with new ideas, resources, or practices they can put into action\.
