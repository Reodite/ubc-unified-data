Abstract: 

The *chromatic* and *Tutte symmetric functions* were introduced by Richard Stanley in the 1990s as generalizations of the well\-known graph polynomials\. As with the polynomials, some of the ongoing research of these functions focuses on the extent to which they distinguish non\-isomorphic graphs\. In particular, a major open question asks if these functions distinguish trees\.

In this talk, I present two results in this direction\. First, I give a construction for graph pairs with arbitrarily high girth but equal chromatic and Tutte symmetric function\. Second, I give a complete characterization of all local modular relations that preserve these functions\.

This talk is based on work with José Aliste\-Prieto, Sophie Spirkl, and José Zamora\.
