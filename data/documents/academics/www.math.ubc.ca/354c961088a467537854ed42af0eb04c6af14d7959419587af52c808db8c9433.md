Abstract: 

The main objects of study in algebraic geometry are varieties, which are spaces that locally look like solution sets of multivariate polynomial equation systems\. One of the most important sub branches of algebraic geometry is intersection theory, which may be regarded as the study of Chow rings of varieties, which are rings, the elements of which are sub varieties of the variety of interest, and whose multiplication corresponds to the intersection of sub varieties that meet transversally\. Chow rings of smooth varieties were rigorously defined by Fulton and  
MacPherson in the 70s, but there is no generally accepted theory of Chow rings of singular varieties\.

In this talk, I will introduce derived algebraic geometry, and explain how to use it to construct a vast generalization of the algebraic cobordism of Levine\-\-Morel, constructed in the early 2000s\. Algebraic cobordism is a much stronger invariant than the Chow ring, and the latter can be recovered from the former\. In particular, we obtain a candidate for Chow rings of singular varieties\. We also explain some of the structural results known for our algebraic cobordism rings, in particular the projective bundle formula, which can be used to establish a strong relationship between algebraic cobordism and algebraic K\-theory\. Hopefully, we will end by classifying all varieties, "up to cobordism", in the algebraic cobordism ring with rational coefficients\.
