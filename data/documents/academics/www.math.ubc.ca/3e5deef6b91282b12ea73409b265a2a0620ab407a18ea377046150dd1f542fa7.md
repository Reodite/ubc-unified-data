Abstract: 

In mesenchymal cell motility, three key migration patterns are typically detected: directional, exploratory and immotile\. Two key members of the Rho\-family of GTPases, Rac and Rho, along with paxillin, have been particularly implicated in the formation of such migration patterns\. Together, they form a regulatory network that is sufficient for generating wave\-pinning which underscores cellular polarization\. However, it remains unclear how they interact collectively to induce other modes of migration detected in Chinese hamster Ovary \(CHO\-K1\) cells\. We have recently reduced a six\-variable \(6V\) reaction–diffusion model of this system into an excitable 4V spatiotemporal model possessing three different timescales to produce not only wave\-pinning in the presence of diffusion, but also mixed\-mode oscillations \(MMOs\) and relaxation oscillations \(ROs\) in the absence of diffusion\. We showed that delayed Hopf and canard explosions are responsible for generating MMOs\. Implementing the model using the Cellular Potts Model \(CPM\) produced outcomes in which protrusions in the cell membrane changed Rac–Rho localization, resulting in membrane oscillations and fast directionality variations similar to those observed experimentally\. The latter was assessed by comparing the migration patterns of CHO\-K1 with CPM cells using four independent metrics\. Variations in migration patterns induced by mutating paxillin’s serine 273 residue were also captured by the model and detected by a machine classifier, revealing that this mutation alters the dynamics of the system from MMOs to ROs or nonoscillatory behaviour\. In this talk, I will present an overview of these results\.
