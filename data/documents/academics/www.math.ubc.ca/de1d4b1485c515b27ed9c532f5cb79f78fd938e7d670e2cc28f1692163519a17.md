Abstract: 

Around 2008, Schramm conjectured that the critical probability \\\(p\_c\\\) of a transitive graph is entirely determined by the local geometry of the graph, subject to the global constraint that \\\(p\_c &lt; 1\\\)\. In other words, if \\\(G\_n\\\) is a sequence of transitive graphs with \\\(p\_c\(G\_n\) &lt; 1\\\) for all \\\(n\\\) converging locally to a transitive graph \\\(G\\\) then \\\(p\_c\(G\_n\)\\\) converges to \\\(p\_c\(G\)\\\)\. Previous works had verified the conjecture in various special cases, including nonamenable graphs of high girth \(Benjamini, Nachmias and Peres 2012\); Cayley graphs of abelian groups \(Martineau and Tassion 2013\); nonunimodular graphs \(Hutchcroft 2017 and 2018\); graphs of uniform exponential growth \(Hutchcroft 2018\); and graphs of \(automatically uniform\) polynomial growth \(Contreras, Martineau and Tassion 2022\)\. In this talk I will describe our complete resolution of the conjecture in forthcoming joint work with Hutchcroft\.
