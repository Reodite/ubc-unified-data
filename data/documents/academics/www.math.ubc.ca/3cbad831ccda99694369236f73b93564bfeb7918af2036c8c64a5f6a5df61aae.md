Abstract: 

Probability distributions produced by quantum measurements are more general than the ones produced by classical measurements\. This is due to the fact that only commuting observables, for example represented by a set of commuting unitaries, can be simultaneously measured\. Nonclassical properties of these distributions can be studied using classifying spaces assembled from commuting unitary operators\. In this talk I will describe recent applications of these classifying spaces to the study of certain problems in quantum foundations which have also important applications in quantum computing\.
