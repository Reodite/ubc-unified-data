Speaker: 

Yuveshen Mooroogen

Speaker Affiliation: 

UBC

October 11, 2022

PIMS 4133 \(library\)

Vancouver, BC

Canada

Earlier this year, Joe Repka \(Univ\. of Toronto, Maths\) asked me the following question: "Fix p in \[0,1\), and consider a subset E \= E\(p\) of the real line with the property that for each interval Q of length 1, the intersection E n Q has Lebesgue measure at least p\. Must E contain an infinite arithmetic progression?" Informally, this question can be interpreted as follows: if the length of a set E takes up at least 100% of the length of the real line, must there necessarily be infinitely many equally spaced points inside E? In a recent preprint, joint with Laurestine Bradford \(McGill Univ\., Linguistics\) and Hannah Kohut \(Univ\. of British Columbia, Maths\), we prove that the answer is 'no' by constructing an explicit counterexample\. In this talk, I will explain \(and illustrate\!\) the geometric features of our set that allow it to avoid arithmetic progressions\. I will also discuss \(without proof\) a recent pattern\-avoidance result of Mihail Kolountzakis \(Univ\. of Crete, Maths\) and Effie Papageorgiou \(Univ\. of Crete, Maths\) that was inspired by our work\.
