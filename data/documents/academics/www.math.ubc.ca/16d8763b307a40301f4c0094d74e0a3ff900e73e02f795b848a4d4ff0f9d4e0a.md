Abstract: 

Agents \(human and non\-human animals, for instance\) tend to prefer large rewards to small rewards\. Similarly, they prefer rewards which come sooner rather than later\. Of particular interest is when one must decide between a smaller/sooner reward and a larger/later reward; an agent's decision is determined by its time preferences\. Two models relating to time preferences are put forward\. In the first, we construct an adaptive dynamics framework to quantitatively evaluate the benefit of rewards received at different ages over a lifespan\. The second model, Squirrel Banking, explores the pressures faced when resources can either be stored as a safeguard against future starvation or consumed immediately for reproduction\.
