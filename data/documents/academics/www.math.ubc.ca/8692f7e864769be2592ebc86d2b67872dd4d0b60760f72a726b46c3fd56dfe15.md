Abstract: 

Causal inference seeks to quantify how strongly variables influence one another\. Whilst interventions — experiments that deliberately change one variable to observe its effect on another — can reveal causal effects, such experiments are often unethical or impossible \(for example, forcing people to smoke to see if they develop cancer\)\. In such cases, we must instead infer causal effects from observational data alone, without intervening\.

This talk introduces the graphical and algebraic foundations of causal inference, explains how to formalize interventions and manipulate them algebraically using do\-calculus, and presents the intuition behind ongoing work on generalizing a central theorem in this field\.
