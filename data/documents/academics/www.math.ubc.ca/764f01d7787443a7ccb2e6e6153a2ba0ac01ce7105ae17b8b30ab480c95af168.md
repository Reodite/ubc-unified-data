Abstract: 

Abstract: Suppose that labelled particles occupy the vertices of a graph\. Whenever an edge rings, the particles at its endpoints exchange places\. The resulting process, known as the interchange process, can equivalently be viewed as a random walk on the symmetric group\.

How does this interaction affect the time it takes the system to approach equilibrium? I will describe new results comparing the interchange process with independent random walks, resolving a conjecture of Oliveira and establishing cutoff—an abrupt transition to equilibrium, for both systems around the same time\.

A theme of the talk is that this enormous many\-particle system can sometimes be understood through the motion and collisions of just a few particles, leading to connections between probability, analysis and the representation theory of the symmetric group\.
