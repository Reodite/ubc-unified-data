Abstract: 

In this talk I will give an overview of how methods from dimension theory can be used to study embeddability of the continuum random tree and the Brownian map\. While these two important objects in random geometry are fairly well\-behaved on a global level, a study of their local extremal behaviour in terms of weak tangents \(or microsets\) reveals high local irregularity\. Both spaces turn out to be far away from being embeddable using quasisymmetric mappings into "nice" classical spaces\. Given time, I will go full circle and explain how the probabilistic methods used in the construction of these spaces also give rise to a "dual" notion of continuous functions in terms of \(deterministic\) continuum trees\. The dimension theory of these trees is correlated to analytical properties of their associated functions \(e\.g\. Hölder continuity\) and allow new methods to be used to study e\.g\. Weierstraß\-like functions\.
