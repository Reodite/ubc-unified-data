Abstract: 

Residually stressed structures are common in nature across many scales, and they have become increasingly relevant in man\-made materials\. Recently, geometrical formulations of elasticity have elucidated that the rich physics of stressed structures — which results in shape transitions, symmetry breaking, and anomalous mechanics — stems from geometrical frustration via the violation of Gauss’s Theorema Egregium\. An open question remains as to whether this is the only source of geometrical frustration\. In this talk, I will present the first experimental realization of a new type of geometrical frustration that is as general as Gauss frustration, and I will demonstrate that its characteristics are inherently different\. Then, I will introduce an asymptotic geometrical model for elastic ribbons with an arbitrary geometrical frustration\. A careful and simultaneous expansion of the elastic energy and the geometrical compatibility conditions reveals a hierarchical structure of geometrical frustrations\. Identifying the leading frustrated energy terms associates a given ribbon with its corresponding geometrical frustration, revealing whether it will undergo a shape transition and, if so, what its scaling will be\. This model completes our understanding of frustrated ribbons, providing a full analysis of their shape and mechanics that fosters the development of new shape\-morphing platforms\.
