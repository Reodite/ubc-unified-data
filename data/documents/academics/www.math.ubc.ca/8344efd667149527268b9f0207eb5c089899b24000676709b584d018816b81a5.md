Abstract: 

 Living organisms stochastically move through high\-dimensional "health\-space" as they age, and before they die\. Developing useful quantitative models for aging phenomenology is challenging because there are hundreds of distinct physiological attributes that are often measured\.  We pursue three modelling strategies to simplify aging while embracing its complexity\. First, we develop minimal models of networked health in a bottom\-up strategy\. These "generic network models" allow us to model how damage propagates through the organism and leads to death\. We have extended \(and simplified\) these models to study the effects of disease during aging\.  Second, we develop statistical and machine learning models in a top\-down data\-driven strategy to either characterize or predict aging health\. Finally, we develop simplified dynamical models to assess system stability\. While our broad focus is on organismal aging, the best data available is for human aging – so that has been our focus\.
