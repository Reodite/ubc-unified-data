Abstract: 

The boxicity of a graph $G \= \(V, E\)$ is the minimum dimension $d$ such that $G$ is the intersection graph of a family $\(B\_v\)\_\{v\\in V\}$ of $d$\-dimensional boxes in $R^d$\. In this talk, we present two approaches for providing new results and proofs on the boxicity of relevant graphs\. 

The first approach is based on the analysis of the interval order subgraphs of the complement of the graph under the assumption that the complement is a line graph\.  
Initially motivated by the study of the Petersen graph \(i\.e\. the complement of the line graph of $K\_5$\), our approach can be generalized to deal with the complement of the line graph of any graph $G$\. We settle a conjecture proposed by Caoduro and Lichev \[Discrete Mathematics, Vol\. 346, 5, 2023\] concerning the boxicity of the Kneser graph $K\(n, 2\)$ \(i\.e\. the complement of the line graph of $K\_n$\)\. In addition, we show that the problem of recognizing if a graph has boxicity $d$, in general $NP$\-hard, can be solved in polynomial time for the class of complement of line graphs\. 

The second approach relies on “chordless” paths to study the interval order subgraphs of the complement of the graph\.  We work out two examples of innovative applications leading to the boxicity of the Grötzsch graph and the Ramsey graph $G\(3, 5\)$ on thirteen vertices \(i\.e\. the largest graph without triangles or stable sets of size five\)\. The attention to graphs of small orders is inspired by the necessity of finding lower bounds to a long\-standing conjecture of Wegner \(1965\)\.

This is joint work with András Sebő\.
