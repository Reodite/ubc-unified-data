Prospective students

# Thinking about the *BMLSc Program?*

Everything you need to work out whether the program fits — what you’ll need to get in, what you’ll study, how to apply, and what it costs\.

Who the program is for

## Four types of people who tend to find their way here

Future health professional

You’re planning a career as a health professional\.

Graduates finish having met most prerequisites for medical, dental and graduate school — with two years of hands\-on laboratory work behind them\.

Future researcher

You want the practical laboratory skills that get you hired\.

Bacterial identification, tissue culture, PCR, electrophoresis, chromatography, immunohistochemistry — performed, not just read about\.

Already working in a lab

You work in a clinical laboratory and want a degree\.

Certified MLTs have their own entry route into the program, and a degree that opens advancement and career change\.

Still deciding

You’re drawn to health science but unsure where it leads\.

The degree keeps several doors open at once — medicine, research, biotechnology, public health, laboratory management\.

If any of those sound like you, the BMLSc may be the perfect choice\.

What the two years are like

As a student in the program, you’ll gain practical lab experience and knowledge across a broad range of health science subjects, while developing crucial transferable competencies\.

With only 24 students accepted each year, our students develop instrumental collegial relationships with each other and with their instructors — learning from nationally and internationally recognized researchers and award\-winning teachers\.

You enter at

Year 3

Length

Class size

Taught by

Practising researchers

The BMLSc is not an MLT training program

Despite the similar name, the Bachelor of Medical Laboratory Science is a completely different program from Medical Laboratory Technologist \(MLT\) training\. **You cannot become a certified MLT through the BMLSc Program\.** If you’re looking for MLT certification, or want to know how the two differ, see the [FAQs](https://bmlscpathology.med.ubc.ca/home-page/faqs/)\.
