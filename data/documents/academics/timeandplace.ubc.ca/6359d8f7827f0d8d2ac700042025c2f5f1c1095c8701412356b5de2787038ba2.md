The timeline locates the history of Aboriginal students in the UBC context so that we can see the history in a way that is intimate and relevant to our present life at UBC\. To make the history even closer to you, we encourage you to insert yourself into the timeline\. Below are some suggestions you may want to try:

-   You may want to plug your historical knowledge in the timeline to enhance the timeline with what you think relevant\.
-   You may also bring in a different point of reference depending on your social position or interest\. For example, Hanae drew on the history of Asian Canadians and Canadian Multiculturalism as a point of reference so that she could put the history of Aboriginal peoples in perspective\.
-   You may track and add your family history or the history of the group or department, to which you belong at UBC\.
-   You may compare the timeline that you have developed with your peers or colleagues who have also worked on the timeline\.
-   You may discuss similarities and differences between your timelines and share each other’s reflections on the learning process\.

The timeline will have accomplished a great part of its goal when you create a reflective dialogue with others and gain a renewed or greater awareness of the history that we share in the unceded Musqueam territory today\.
