October 24, 2014

**Session 1: 10:30 – 12:00**

**A1       Deconstructing Myths –** **Fostering Reconciliation Through Understanding**

**Carol Arnold &amp; Charlene Bearhead**

For as long as Canadian ‘history’ has been taught from a western lens, fiction more than fact has informed non\-Aboriginal people’s understanding of Aboriginal history and culture\.   Long standing, fabricated stories of the helpless, hopeless and dependent Aboriginal people of this land and the benevolent governments and religious orders who came to save them have formed the basis of History and Social Studies curricula in this country\. The Truth and Reconciliation Commission process in Canada has provided a catalyst for Aboriginal and non\-Aboriginal peoples of this land to work together, in innovative and engaging ways of knowing and learning, to seek truth, teach reality and build respectful relationships upon which reconciliation can be founded\.

In this workshop participants are submerged into the depths of the mistruths that have made up the belief systems of mainstream Canada for far too long, and will be challenged to dig deeper to seek truth through research\. Research methodologies must include historical documentation and oral testimony: action research as opposed to acceptance of archaic misinformation and hidden fabricated narratives\. In a commitment to the continuation of the work that teachers started with the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada, BCTF invites all teachers to join in the effort to seek truth through the deconstruction of myths in curriculum\.

**A2       Hidden Legacies**

**Bertha Lansdowne, Jacinda Mack &amp; Bev Sellars**

[Hidden Legacies](http://www.movingimages.ca/store/products.php?hidden_legacies) is a documentary in which Anishinaabe filmmaker, Lisa Jackson, examines the legacy of the Canadian Indian Residential School system on intergenerational survivors whose parents/grandparents attended the Residential Schools\. After viewing Hidden Legacies, participants will engage in a deeper discussion around the intergenerational effects of Residential Schools\. A new version of a teaching guide \(grades 8\-12\) developed for this film will be introduced and participants will have an opportunity to try out some of the activities in the guide\.

**A3       BC Blanket Exercise**

**Meredith Rusk**

This powerful hands\-on workshop, developed by Kairos Canada, is designed to give participants a unique and interactive experience to develop a deeper understanding of the history of Aboriginal Peoples of Canada since the time of the arrival of Europeans\. The Blanket Exercise is versatile and has been presented to a variety of age groups \(9 and up\) for over a decade\. The BC Blanket Exercise has been developed to allow participants to experience the exercise through a BC historical perspective\. Participants can take this exercise back to the classroom\. This exercise often raises deep feelings/emotions and will be followed by a talking circle\. See the [Kairos Canada](http://www.kairoscanada.org/dignity-rights/indigenous-rights/blanket-exercise/) website for more information on The Blanket Exercise\.

**A4       The** **Residential Schools System– an Experiential History Lesson**

**Perry Smith,** District Principal &amp; **Peggy Janicki,** District Teacher, Aboriginal Education Department, Abbotsford School District \#34\.

This presentation, as offered by Abbotsford School District’s Aboriginal Department, is an interactive lesson that allows students to experience the residential school system from the perspective of Aboriginal people\. Participants will be asked an inquiry question that they will be expected to respond to at the end of the lesson\.

**A5**       **FNESC Residential School Curriculum – Elementary**

**Starleigh Grass –** Senior Policy Analyst, FNESC

The First Nations Education Steering Committee \(FNESC\) and the First Nations Schools Association \(FNSA\) created this curriculum in order to contribute to the reconciliation process\. The project has been inspired by the recommendations of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada to develop age\-appropriate residential school curriculum\. The draft curriculum, which is for grades 5,10, and 11/12, is BC\-specific and care has been taken to ensure that the literature, primary sources, and video interviewees are largely from BC\. It is founded upon the First Peoples Principles of Learning\. In this workshop participants will have an opportunity to review the draft materials and hear about the pilot activities taking place in school districts and First Nations schools\. Participants will receive a copy of the Residential School Curriculum Guide\.

**A6**       **Button Blankets \(max 30\)**

**Una\-Ann Moyer,** **Tahltan/Tlingit Artist**

Learn about traditional robes and their significance to Coastal First Nation cultures\. Una\-Ann will share how the use of traditional robes faded with the introduction of wool blankets and became known as “Button Blankets\.” Make your own button blanket wall hanging using traditional coastal designs\.

**A7       Seven Sacred Laws through Graffiti**

**Jeska Slater**

The theme of this workshop is *Honouring our Inner Wisdom*, and exploring how the Seven Sacred Laws relate to our lives, and the lives of the youth we work with\. Note that no artistic ability is required\. This workshop uses pre\-designed graffiti images\. This workshop brings together 3 main concepts**:**

-   Intergenerational Resilience and the passing down of sacred gifts \(resources, protective factors and capacities\) for well\-being through traditional knowledge\.
-   The Seven Sacred Laws and how they are relevant in our lives today\.
-   Youth and the Seven Sacred Laws: How we can make traditional knowledge relevant to youth today\.

**An Education Kit will be available for purchase, and will include:**

-   Medicine Wheel Wellness for Teens Handout
-   Materials list for a multitude of projects that could be completed with kit
-   Booklet that includes the 7 Sacred Teachings, and how to apply them to each quadrant of our medicine wheel
-   Handout on Colour Teachings
-   Graffiti drawings of the Seven Sacred Laws that can be copied for classroom art project

**B2       Project of Heart British Columbia**

**BCTF &amp; Charlene Bearhead**

At a National level **Project of Heart** won the Governor General’s Award for Teaching History and the program is now being transferred to organizations at regional levels to ensure the continued development of meaningful and relevant resources, social justice actions and survivor testimony for provinces and territories\.   Developed by Sylvia Smith, Project of Heart is a student directed, inquiry\-based project to develop an understanding of the true history and legacy of the Canadian Indian Residential School System\.   The BC Teachers’ Federation has been awarded responsibility for Project of Heart in British Columbia\.  Through ongoing dialogue with provincial education partner groups BCTF will guide this unique opportunity to build upon this engaging, experiential program\.

Project of Heart has had 7 years of unprecedented results in promoting truth in education regarding the history and legacy of Indian Residential Schools\.  Regional and national commemoration innovations have garnered national and international interest creating levels of awareness like never before\.  The far reaching social justice actions, including those hosted by Dr\. Cindy Blackstock and the First Nations Child and Family Caring Society, have engaged Aboriginal and non\-Aboriginal students and teachers in creating social change in their schools and communities across Canada\.

This workshop brings the opportunity for teachers to engage, first hand, in the development of Project of Heart BC to ensure that it meets the needs of BC teachers and students, Aboriginal and non\-Aboriginal, on and off\-reserve\.  Join the workshop and make your input count in the evolution of this important teaching program at the ground level as it launches in British Columbia\.  Participants will leave with ideas and materials ready to implement Project of Heart in their classrooms\.

**B3       First Nations Child &amp; Family Caring Society\- Shannen’s Dream**

**Denise Hendry**

Shannen Koostachin, youth leader from the Attawapiskat First Nation, had a dream: safe and comfy schools and culturally based education for First Nations children\. First Nations schools receive less funding than Provincial/Territorial schools, and many are plagued by serious health concerns\. Shannen worked tirelessly to try to convince the Federal government to give First Nations children a proper education before tragically passing away at the age of 15 years in 2010\.  Named in her memory, the campaign engages students to explore contemporary issues, develop critical thinking skills and foster social engagement\. Teachers who have used Shannen’s Dream report that students show an improved sense of respectful citizenship, agency, and academic success\.

Participants will learn about **Shannen’s Dream**, the annual **“Our Dreams Matter Too”** walk and other initiatives sponsored by the **First Nations Child &amp; Family Caring Society**\. Clips from **Hi\-Ho\-Mistahey**, a recently released documentary about Shannen’s story, will also be shown\. Denise will share her experience with using Shannen’s Dream and participating in the “Our Dreams Matter Too” walk\. Those participants who sign up to participate in the 2015 “Our Dreams Matter Too” walk will receive a special incentive\.

**B4       Weaving a Quarter Bag \(max 30\)**

**Anjeanette Dawson**

Learn to weave a small wool quarter bag with **Anjeanette Dawson\.** Anjeanette has been weaving for five years, and is most proud of her wall hanging currently exhibited at the Squamish\-Lil’wat  Cultural Centre in Whistler, BC\. Beyond her work with preschool and elementary schools, she has taught workshops such as the 31st Annual Elders Gathering/Workshop, and the 1st Annual Weaving Gathering\. Many of her pieces have been for private use as well as for ceremonial purposes including: children’s tunics, sitting blankets, headbands, bags, men’s memorial sashes and regalia\. Two of her newest pieces, “*The First Salmon*” and “*The Four Seasons*,” are included in the series of blankets displayed in Simon Fraser University’s Saywell Hall atrium illustrating the story of Burnaby Mountain\.

**B5       UBC Botanical Gardens Canopy Walkway – Guided Tour \(max 20\)**

**Additional fee: $15**

Take a guided tour of the UBC Botanical Gardens from a unique perspective, the Greenheart Canopy Walkway\. Reaching as high as 20 metres in places, this 310 metre long canopy walkway will give you a bird’s eye view of these amazing gardens\.   Learn how First Nations use trees for traditional medicines\. You may even see some of the residents of the beautiful cedar and fir trees that support the canopy\. Please note that no open toe shoes \(sandals, flip\-flops, etc\.\) are allowed on the walkway and both hands must be free to hold onto the canopy guide ropes\. An **additional $15 fee** applies to this workshop\.

**B6       Museum Of Anthropology\(MOA\) – Guided Tour \(max 20\)**

Take part in a guided tour of UBC’s Museum of Anthropology, known for its stunning architecture and world\-renowned cultural and arts collections\. You will be taken on a tour of both indoor and outdoor exhibits, including the spectacular Great Hall, Bill Reid Rotunda, and more\. After the tour you may want to stop by the current exhibit *Claiming Space: Voices of Urban Aboriginal Youth\.* Housed in the O’Brian Gallery, this contemporary exhibit features multi\-media works of over 20 young Aboriginal artists that illustrate the impacts of colonization and challenge stereotypes\. After visiting this exhibit you may decide to book a school group tour \(available for grades 4\-12\)\. This extraordinary exhibit runs until January 4, 2015\.
