**Vancouver** \(pronounced [/væn\.ˈkuːvər/](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:IPA_for_English "Wikipedia:IPA for English")\) is a coastal city located in the [Lower Mainland](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Mainland "Lower Mainland") of [British Columbia](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia "British Columbia"), [Canada](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada")\. It is named for [British](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain "Kingdom of Great Britain") Captain [George Vancouver](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Vancouver "George Vancouver"), who explored the area in the 1790s\. The name *Vancouver* itself originates from the [Dutch](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language "Dutch language") “van Coevorden”, denoting somebody from [Coevorden](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coevorden "Coevorden"), a city in the [Netherlands](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands")\.\[[\[2\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver#cite_note-1)\]

The largest metropolitan area in [Western Canada](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada "Western Canada"), Vancouver ranks [third largest](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_100_largest_metropolitan_areas_in_Canada "List of the 100 largest metropolitan areas in Canada") in the country and the city proper ranks eighth\.\[[\[3\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver#cite_note-2)\]\[[\[4\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver#cite_note-3)\] According to the 2006 census Vancouver had a population of 578,041\[[\[1\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver#cite_note-pop2006-0)\] and its [Census Metropolitan Area](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_Metropolitan_Area "Census Metropolitan Area") exceeded 2\.1 million people\.\[[\[1\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver#cite_note-pop2006-0)\] Its residents are [ethnically](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Canada "Ethnic groups in Canada") and linguistically diverse; 52% do not speak [English](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") as their [first language](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language "First language")\.\[[\[5\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver#cite_note-4)\]\[[\[6\]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver#cite_note-5)\]

**Environmental studies** is the [academic field](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_field "Academic field") which systematically studies [human interaction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_behavior "Human behavior") with the [environment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment")\. It is a broad [interdisciplinary](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary "Interdisciplinary") field of study that includes the [natural environment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment"), [built environment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built_environment "Built environment"), and the sets of relationships between them\. While distinct from [ecology](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology "Ecology") and [environmental science](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_science "Environmental science"), the discipline encompasses study in the basic principles of those two fields of learning as well as the associated subjects, such as: [policy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy "Environmental policy"), [politics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_politics "Environmental politics"), [law](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_law "Environmental law"), [economics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_economics "Environmental economics"), [sociology](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_sociology "Environmental sociology") and other [social aspects](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_justice "Environmental justice"), [planning](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_planning "Environmental planning"), [pollution control](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution_control "Pollution control"), [natural resources](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource_management "Natural resource management"), and the interactions of human beings and nature\.

As part of the [College for Interdisciplinary Studies](http://www.cfis.ubc.ca/), the Neuroscience graduate program aims for flexibility so that the individual needs and background of each student can, as quickly as possible, be accommodated\. The core program of courses offered to entering students consists of Neuroanatomy, Neurophysiology, Neurochemistry, Psychobiology, Molecular Neurobiology, and Neuropharmacology\. Additional related courses are available for selection by the student and his/her Supervisory Committee\. The Program is research oriented: students are expected to engage in research from the start of their studies\. Research is undertaken in individual departments, over a wide range of basic and clinical Neuroscience topics\.

Please note Program Application Deadlines: The Admissions Committee meets to consider applications from the beginning of December\. It is in your interest to have your application in to this office as soon as possible\. Deadlines for submission of applications is November 30 for International applicants and January 31 for Canadian and U\.S\. applicants\.

The Canadian Society for Unconventional Gas is offering three scholarships valued at $2500 each, for graduate students undertaking research in topics related to unconventional gas\. This year the student scholarship winners will be selected from applicants enrolled in a post\-graduate degree program related to the petroleum industry, including engineering, geology, geophysics, environmental science and business\.
