**Environmental studies** is the [academic field](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_field "Academic field") which systematically studies [human interaction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_behavior "Human behavior") with the [environment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment")\. It is a broad [interdisciplinary](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary "Interdisciplinary") field of study that includes the [natural environment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment "Natural environment"), [built environment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built_environment "Built environment"), and the sets of relationships between them\. While distinct from [ecology](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology "Ecology") and [environmental science](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_science "Environmental science"), the discipline encompasses study in the basic principles of those two fields of learning as well as the associated subjects, such as: [policy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy "Environmental policy"), [politics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_politics "Environmental politics"), [law](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_law "Environmental law"), [economics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_economics "Environmental economics"), [sociology](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_sociology "Environmental sociology") and other [social aspects](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_justice "Environmental justice"), [planning](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_planning "Environmental planning"), [pollution control](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution_control "Pollution control"), [natural resources](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource_management "Natural resource management"), and the interactions of human beings and nature\.
