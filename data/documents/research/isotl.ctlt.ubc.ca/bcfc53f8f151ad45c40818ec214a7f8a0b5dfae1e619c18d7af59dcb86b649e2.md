Project Lead: Oluwakemi Ola \(Associate Professor of Teaching, Computer Science\)

## Project Summary

Teaching the technical aspects of creating visualizations is relatively standardized\. However, design thinking, the ability to understand what makes a visualization effective, remains more challenging and less clearly understood\. Students enter data visualization courses with varied, often unexamined beliefs about design\. This SoTL project will track how students’ beliefs evolve throughout a project\-based course, using pre\-, mid\-, and post\-surveys and course artifacts\. Some of the learning practices I will explore include low\-fidelity sketching and design critiques\. I hope the findings will lay the groundwork for a framework to assess and scaffold expert design thinking in data science education\.

## Research Questions

1.  How do students’ beliefs about what constitutes good visualization design change over the course of a project\-based data visualization course?
2.  What kinds of reasoning do students use to justify their visualization and interaction design decisions in course artifacts and oral reflections?
3.  How do students’ self\-reported beliefs about visualization design align with the reasoning evident in their submitted artifacts?
