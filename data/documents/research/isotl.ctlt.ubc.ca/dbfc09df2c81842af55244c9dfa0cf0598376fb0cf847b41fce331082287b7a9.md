Project Lead: Casey Keulen \(Associate Professor of Teaching, Materials Engineering\)

## Project Summary

This project investigates women’s participation in manufacturing engineering through a phenomenological lens, focusing on embodied experiences of confidence, belonging, and sense of purpose\. This will be examined through interviews with upper\-year and graduate students, and surveys of early\-stage students following the interviews, in order to understand how expectations differ from lived experience\. By identifying how key factors are formed through academic and social environments, the study aims to inform potential program\-level interventions\. Findings will support the design of more inclusive and effective curricula and learning environments to improve recruitment, retention, and student success in manufacturing engineering\.

## Research Questions

1.  How do women in manufacturing engineering describe their lived and embodied experiences of confidence, belonging, and purpose within the program and industry?
2.  What factors most influence women’s decisions to enter, persist in, or leave manufacturing engineering?
3.  How do perceptions of these factors differ between incoming students and upper\-year or graduate students, and what implications do these differences have for program design?
