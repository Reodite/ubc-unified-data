### Community Bioenergy Systems Training

For many remote communities in Canada, using bioenergy systems rather than diesel generation can help reduce greenhouse gas emissions and increase energy security\. The motivation behind building this training program stems from the desire of communities to move away from fossil fuel consumption, increase wood fibre utilization in remote locations, provide jobs in the community, and improve the local economy\.

This five\-day experiential training combines classroom instruction, demonstrations, site visits, and hands\-on learning to provide participants with practical knowledge of forestry processes, biomass supply chains, and bioenergy principles\. The course introduces participants to the planning, implementation, operation, and community applications of Combined Heat and Power \(CHP\) and other bioenergy systems for both grid\-connected and remote communities\.

Participants gain practical knowledge of community bioenergy systems through a combination of classroom instruction, demonstrations, site visits, and selected hands\-on learning activities\. Using the biomass Combined Heat and Power \(CHP\) system at the Alex Fraser Research Forest as a real\-world demonstration platform, participants explore the technical, environmental, economic, and operational aspects of community bioenergy projects\.

**Throughout the program, participants will learn about:**

-   Community bioenergy systems and Combined Heat and Power \(CHP\) technologies
-   Forest biomass resources, biomass supply chains, and fuel quality
-   Biomass feedstock preparation and fuel management
-   CHP system components, operating principles, and maintenance considerations
-   Bioenergy applications for grid\-connected and off\-grid
-   Technical, environmental, and economic considerations for bioenergy projects
-   Community energy planning and project development
-   Forest management, including wildfire risk reduction
-   Pre\-feasibility assessments and project planning
-   Funding programs and grant opportunities for community bioenergy initiatives
-   Site visits to operational bioenergy facilities and practical demonstrations

Participants also gain insight into how community bioenergy systems can support renewable energy, sustainable forest management, energy security, community resilience, and local economic development\.

We are committed to making this course practical and relevant to participants\. Participants learn how to assess the feasibility of a bioenergy system for their own community\.

This in\-person training takes place at the University of British Columbia’s Alex Fraser Research Forest \(AFRF\) in Williams Lake, British Columbia\. The AFRF’s biomass Combined Heat and Power \(CHP\) system serves as the primary demonstration platform, where participants engage in classroom instruction, practical demonstrations, and selected hands\-on learning activities\.

To provide a broader understanding of community bioenergy systems and forest\-based renewable energy, the program also includes educational site visits to operational bioenergy facilities and forestry organizations, such as Atlantic Power Plant, local community forests, pellet heating installations, and other partner organizations\. These visits allow participants to explore a variety of biomass supply chains, energy technologies, and real\-world community applications\.

This training was originally developed to support capacity building in remote and Indigenous communities; however, the CHP Academy is now open to anyone interested in community bioenergy, sustainable forestry, and renewable energy\. Indigenous and non\-Indigenous participants from remote, rural, and urban communities are all welcome\.

**This course is a great fit for you and your community:**

-   Is on\-grid or not connected to the provincial/territorial electrical grid, making fuel supply and energy security a significant concern
-   Relies on diesel generators and is seeking cleaner, more reliable, and cost\-effective energy solutions
-   Is exploring or committed to developing a Combined Heat and Power \(CHP\) or other bioenergy system to improve community energy resilience
-   Is seeking innovative uses for wood residues, slash piles, or other by\-products from forest management or wildfire fuel reduction projects
-   Wants to reduce wildfire risk while creating value from forest biomass resources
-   Represent an Indigenous community, municipality, utility, community forest, or organization interested in community bioenergy systems
-   Work in forestry, wildfire mitigation, biomass utilization, renewable energy, or community infrastructure planning
-   Are involved in forest or land management and would like to better understand how biomass resources can support clean energy production
-   Lead or support greenhouse, food security, or community development initiatives that could benefit from biomass energy
-   Are a student, researcher, consultant, engineer, technician, educator, or industry professional interested in community bioenergy technologies and applications

The CHP Academy is offered as a fee\-based professional training program\.

-   Five days of professional instruction
-   Access to the CHP Academy online learning platform and course materials

-   Access to the University of British Columbia’s Alex Fraser Research Forest training facilities and demonstration equipment
-   Daily lunches and refreshments during the training
-   Transportation for local site visits and airport transportation provided by UBC AFRF
-   Certificate of Completion
