A documentary film produced through the UBC Comics Studies Cluster’s Turtle Island Research Cluster has been selected for the inaugural [Miccosukee Indigenous Film Festival](https://www.miccofilmfest.com/), taking place October 9–12, 2026, in Miami, Florida\.

*Kokum Dorothy’s Story: We Can Still Sing and Dance*, directed by Andy Rutter, documents the development of a graphic novel created by Cree residential school survivor Dorothy Visser and Métis illustrator Natasha Donovan\. The film follows their conversations as Donovan transforms Visser’s memories and experiences into comics\. UBC Comics Studies Cluster director Biz Nijdam and Turtle Island Cluster co\-lead Shannon Leddy also participated in the documentary\.

[Embedded content: YouTube video player](https://www.youtube.com/embed/dHWM7McXaGE?si=OCq1LMIsNrDuT3pR)

The film is part of [**Survivor\-Centred Visual Narratives \(SCVN\)**](https://visualnarratives.org/), an international SSHRC Partnership Grant project led by the University of Victoria\. SCVN brings together researchers, artists, survivors and community partners to explore arts\-based approaches to testimony and commemoration\. Its Turtle Island Cluster focuses specifically on Indigenous experiences of residential schools and the ways graphic narratives can contribute to conversations about memory, survivance and the legacy of colonial violence\.

Documentary filmmaking is an important part of this work\. Rather than documenting only the finished graphic novel, the film records the process through which testimony becomes visual narrative\. As the UBC Pop Culture Cluster explains, filming the process makes the research itself visible and creates another way for audiences to encounter the project\.

The Miccosukee Indigenous Film Festival’s Documentary Short category features films under 35 minutes with Indigenous or Native content as their subject matter\. Screenings will take place at the Bill Cosford Cinema on the University of Miami Coral Gables campus\.

The selection comes as the Turtle Island Cluster continues toward the completion of the graphic novel\. The documentary is expected to be released in early 2027\.
