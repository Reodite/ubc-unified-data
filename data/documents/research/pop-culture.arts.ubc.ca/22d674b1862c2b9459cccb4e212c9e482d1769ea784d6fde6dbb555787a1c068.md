**Comics and Cultural Continuity** is a community\-engaged research\-creation project that works with Indigenous partners and Indigenous cartoonists to support cultural continuity, language revitalization, and Indigenous storytelling through comics\. Building on the success of *Xwémalhkwu Hero Stories: A Graphic Novel* \(2024\), the project centers Indigenous leadership, priorities, and methodologies in the co\-creation of new graphic narratives\.

Through sustained partnerships with First Nations, Métis, and Inuit collaborators, the project commissions original comics that draw on oral histories, land\-based knowledge, and contemporary Indigenous experiences\. These works embed Indigenous languages directly into visual narratives and are accompanied by open\-access educational materials designed for use in community programs, K–12 classrooms, and postsecondary teaching\.

Comics and Cultural Continuity positions comics as a powerful medium for arts\-based research and knowledge mobilization—one that honours Indigenous storytelling traditions while creating accessible, engaging resources for learners of all ages\. Alongside publication and exhibition, the project emphasizes relationship\-building, mentorship of Indigenous and student collaborators, and the development of ethical, community\-led models for collaborative research\.

*This project is co\-led by **Shannon Leddy** \(Métis\), **Strang Burton**, **Tim Frandy** \(Sámi\), and **Biz Nijdam** and is supported by the **Indigenous Strategic Initiatives** Fund and administered through the **Popular Media for Social Change Research Excellence Cluster** in collaboration with the **UBC Comics Studies Cluster***
