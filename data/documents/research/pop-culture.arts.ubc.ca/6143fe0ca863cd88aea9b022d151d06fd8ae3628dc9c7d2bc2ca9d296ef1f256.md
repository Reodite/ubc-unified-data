The following guide offers resources on teaching tabletop games to students\. We hope the guide offers an opportunity t o consider how analog game s reflect cultural practices and ideas\. T h e guide includes lesson plans developed by the team, links to archives, academic journals on board games, and other useful material\.  

### Archives

#### **The Strong: The National Museum of Play** 

The Museum of Play was founded in 1968 as the Margaret Woodbury Strong Museum of Fascination\. Strong was a wealthy, prolific collector of dolls and toys, and when she passed away in 1969, she left her collection and financial resources for the construction of a museum\. The Museum’s website features a searchable catalogue of board games as well as articles written about the collection’s holdings\. The website largely reflects the legacy of a wealthy, white collector’s interest in everyday objects\. 

[https://www\.museumofplay\.org/blog/category/board\-games/](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/category/board-games/) 

#### **The National Archives** \(UK\)

The National Archives is the official archive and publisher of the United Kingdom\. Their collections include an extensive selection of board games from the Victorian Era, when board games flourished as a form of entertainment\. The following link offers several lesson plans using games from their collection that underlines the colonial project that the games reflect\.  

[https://www\.nationalarchives\.gov\.uk/education/resources/all\-on\-board/](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/all-on-board/) 

#### **The Canadian Museum of History** 

In the early 2000s, the Canadian Museum of History acquired a collection of board games from the Elliott Avedon Museum and Archive of Games, which was previously housed at the University of Waterloo\. Much of the collection is digitized and can be searched through the catalogue page\. Many games in the collection, such as the 1925 game Coast to Coast \(2004\.18\.778\.2\) r eflect Canada’s colonization projects in 19 th and 20 th century\.  

[https://www\.historymuseum\.ca/collections/](https://www.historymuseum.ca/collections/)\.

### Academic Journals

#### **The Board Game Studies Journal \(BGSJ\) \(Open Access\)** 

The Board Game Studies Journal has operated since 2014 as a peer\-reviewed international academic journal\. The open access journal aims to “further our understanding of the nature, development, and distribution of board games within an interdisciplinary context\.” BGSJ features articles on board games from around the world, both ancient and modern\.  

[https://sciendo\.com/journal/BGS](https://sciendo.com/journal/BGS) 

#### **Board Games Academics \(BGA\) \(Open Access\)** 

The BGA is a recent endeavor to collect academic articles on tabletop gaming that includes the journal, academic conferences, charity board game days, and nationwide presentations\. Their journal has published two issues with a wide range of subjects that mostly focus on modern games\.  

[https://boardgameacademics\.com/](https://boardgameacademics.com/) 

#### **Analog Game Studies \(Open Access\)**

Founded in March 2013, Analog Game Studies publishes articles on board games with prominent analog components\. They prioritize scholarship on “role\-playing games, traditional games \(chess, go, backgammon\), parlor games, strategy board games, collectible card games, LARP and similar material\.”  

[https://analoggamestudies\.org/](https://analoggamestudies.org/)
