The Pop Culture Cluster \(PopCC\) is pleased to share the outcomes of the ***Accessible Arts and Popular Culture Workshop***, a Pop Pedagogies Award–funded initiative that invites participants to reconsider a basic assumption: that art is something we only see\.

Held on November 13, 2025 as part of iSchool Community Learning Day, the workshop—led by PhD Candidate Victoria Rahbar \(iSchool\) —brought together collaborators from UBC Library alongside guest presenters Akiko Takesue and Kay Slater\. Through a panel, guided touch tour, and tactile art\-making session, participants explored how arts and popular culture can be engaged through non\-visual, embodied, and multisensory practices\.

The workshop provided a methodological intervention that centered touch, materiality, and accessibility, challenging long\-standing institutional norms that position art as untouchable—both physically and conceptually\. It thereby demonstrated how libraries, galleries, and museums can foster inclusive encounters with cultural materials, particularly in relation to Japanese popular media and Ainu craft traditions\.

A distinctive feature of the workshop is its afterlife and representation through multimodal means\. UBC iSchool students Care Barker and Miriam Rojas translated the event into graphic recordings, [now available](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/arts-based-research/aber-research-projects/accessible-arts-and-pop-culture-workshop/?_gl=1*1dhy04m*_ga*MTIyNDYxNjQ0Mi4xNzc4NjMyMzE1*_ga_T24XEY92ZE*czE3Nzg2MzIzNzIkbzEkZzAkdDE3Nzg2MzIzNzIkajYwJGwwJGgw#graphics) alongside detailed textual descriptions on the Pop Culture Cluster’s website\. These comics do more than simply document the workshop activities, they extend and embody the workshop’s commitment to accessibility through visual storytelling and multimodal knowledge mobilization\.

Readers are invited to explore the full set of [graphic recordings and instructor resources on the PopCC website](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/arts-based-research/aber-research-projects/accessible-arts-and-pop-culture-workshop/) and further reflect on the workshop’s role in advancing equity\-focused, arts\-based pedagogy, the focal point of [a companion news feature from the UBC iSchool](https://ischool.ubc.ca/news/graphic-recordings-of-accessible-arts-and-popular-culture-workshop-are-now-available/)\. Together, these materials offer a model for rethinking how we teach and experience popular culture, emphasizing engagement with art as less something to observe and more as something to encounter, collectively, through the senses\.

*To learn more about Rahbar’s *Accessible Arts and Popular Culture Workshop*, [read the recap of the PopCC’s recent Pop Pedagogies Lightning Talks](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/pop-pedagogies-lightning-talks-highlights/) on our website and [watch the event on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UMKcLc1Lavc)\.*
