### Created by Daniel F\. Moreno \(Independent Scholar\)

#### Intended Audience

High school students at the sophomore, junior and senior levels, who have ideally had at least a year of introduction to cinema and media studies and production\. Can be adapted for undergraduate and adult education\.

###### *This 14\-16 week workshop course was originally taught at an arts high school with a Media Arts and Film track \(the Ruth Asawa San Francisco School of the Arts, academic year 2024\-2025\)\. Students choosing the track would have a different course and instructor from the program each day of the week during their art block\. The specialized, preparatory nature of the school was such that advanced, college\-level and professional insights could be offered to teenagers and youth\. The theoretical framework incorporates insights from thinkers such as Thomas Schatz, Roland Barthes, Joseph Campbell, Mark J\.P\. Wolf, Virginia M\. Mecklenburg, Julian Hoxter and Jaimes Mairata\.*

#### Learning Objective

1.  Engage in the blockbuster motion picture production process of immersive storytelling and worldbuilding\.
2.  Gain insights into the narrative, art and technology turns that pushed classical U\.S\. film into the movies of today
3.  Test the boundaries of form in creating cinema and media pieces, while keeping these reachable by wide audiences\.

#### About the Creator

Daniel F\. Moreno is a Cinema and Media researcher, creator and educator\. As a motion picture editor and consultant, he focuses on the experience of time’s passage and permanence, while his research centers on storytelling and worldbuilding, production histories, media archaeology and spatiotemporal mapping through visual and aural montage techniques and technologies\. A Master of Arts from San Francisco State University and Master of Education from the University of the People, Daniel has taught post\-production, critical studies and screenwriting courses at the undergraduate and high school levels\.
