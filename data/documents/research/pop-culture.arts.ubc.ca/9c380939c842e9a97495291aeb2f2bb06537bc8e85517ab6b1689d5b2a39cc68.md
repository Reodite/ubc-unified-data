From Waylon Asp’s “Indigenous Collections and Repatriation” \(2025\) \| https://waylonasp\.com/icar

We’re excited to welcome [Waylon Asp](https://waylonasp.com/comics) as the selected artist for an upcoming Indigenous Comics Initiative, a community\-driven project centered on the Homalco Nation’s annual canoe journey\. Building on the success of [*Xwémalhkwu Hero Stories: A Graphic Novel*](https://www.homalco.com/press-release-xwemalhkwu-first-nation-celebrates-historic-graphic-novel/) 2024\), this second project, which kicks off this month, will document and celebrate contemporary cultural practices, intergenerational knowledge, and storytelling through comics\. Waylon will join returning artist [Valen Onstine](https://www.instagram.com/goatfangs/) on the project, which is spearheaded by Tchadas Leo of [**TSL Enterprises Ltd**](https://tchadasleo.ca/), and carried out in collaboration with the [**Xwémalhkwu \(Homalco\) First Nation**](https://www.homalco.com/) \(HFN\) and [**Education without Borders**](https://educationwithoutborders.co/)\. 

Ahead of the project’s start next week, we spoke with Waylon about his artistic background, what drew him to this initiative, and what he’s most looking forward to\. 

#### **Can you tell us a bit about your artistic background and what kinds of stories or themes you’re most drawn to in your work?** 

*Waylon Asp: Waylon Asp ūshye\! I am a mixed Tahltan/Settler/Ukrainian Jewish cartoonist with additional experience in illustration, painting, animation, sculpture and more\. Despite this wide variety of disciplines, I often return to comics as my most intuitive and exciting vehicle for storytelling\. Their ability to manipulate time, space, and emotion through the layout of a page calls to mind the rhythmic, undulating forms of Northwest Coast design and the expressive cadences of oral tradition\. There is a potent emotional undercurrent to much of the work that inspires my practice, and I have taken this passion for uncovering the secrets of form into my studies of both sequential narratives and of our ancestors’ artistic achievements\.* 

#### **What interested you in applying to this project, and what about it resonated with you personally or creatively?** 

*Waylon Asp: I was sent this application by a friend and fellow Tahltan I met in Dease Lake, BC, part of our home territory\. As an emerging Indigenous comics creator, discovering this opportunity felt too good to be true\! I have always found joy in translating my experiences into sequential art, and I am thoroughly excited to be continuing this process in collaboration with some incredible artists and a vibrant community right here on Vancouver Island\.* 

#### **How do you see this opportunity fitting into your broader artistic journey or goals at this moment?**  

*Waylon Asp: I find the immense visual and tonal ranges that comics can achieve allow for a breadth of stories to be told, audiences to be reached, and emotions to be captured\. With a project like this, the ability to adapt moments that are grounded and conversational, sweeping and cinematic, or some combination of the two, will not only develop my cartooning skills, but will immerse the reader in every aspect of the journey as it unfolds, from the humorous and human to the ceremonial and sacred\. In bringing this kind of storytelling to the community, I hope to fuel the passion of the next generation of Indigenous cartoonists in the same way I’ve been ignited by my love of comics\.* 

#### **This project involves close collaboration with community members and another artist\. What are you most looking forward to – or curious about – in that collaborative process?** 

*Waylon Asp: I have been working collaboratively on similar projects for a while now, most notably on a documentary graphic novel with Tahltan educator and language revitalization specialist Edōsdi about her maternal grandparents and their experiences of our home territory\. In this work, I have found that a mixture of close consultation and independent artistic license is key in authentically sharing intergenerational knowledge while also breathing new life into these stories with my own distinct voice\. I am thrilled to be working alongside other Indigenous cartoonists, learning from each other and discovering how our unique approaches can complement one another\.* 

#### **On a more personal note, are there particular artists, comics, or creative influences that inspire your work right now?** 

*Waylon Asp: The majority of my family are artists \(including my parents, who have both worked in the comics industry\.\) This is a fact I’m quite proud of, and has shaped how I view the world for as long as I can remember\. Pretty much any and everything I consume in my day\-to\-day life inspires my art\. That being said, I read a lot of comics in my day\-to\-day, so an ever\-expanding list of creators including Akira Toriyama, Jack Kirby, Osamu Tezuka, Bryan Lee O’Malley, Chris Samnee and more have embedded themselves into my artistic DNA\. I admire the work of Hayao Miyazaki and Hitoshi Ashinano as well as local artists Adam de Souza and Oliver Arthur for their shared ability to expand a single moment, a sensory experience or a fleeting natural occurrence into a broader phenomenological narrative\. Speaking of locals, I, of course, have to mention the works of Michael Nicoll Yahgulaanas and my Tahltan relative Cole Pauls, whose stories consistently inspire every aspect of my practice and assure me that there is a place for Indigenous comics to grow and thrive\. Beyond comics, my Indigenous artistic matriarchs include Tsēmā Igharas, Shoshannah Greene, Rachel Martin, Kelsey Mata Foote, and more\.*

**Artist Bio:** Waylon Asp \(b\. 2002\) is a Tahltan/Settler/Ukrainian Jewish cartoonist, interdisciplinary artist and curator based in the unceded territories of the W̱SÁNEĆ and Lək̓ʷ əŋən\-speaking peoples, colonially referred to as Victoria, BC\. In 2025, he graduated with honours from the University of Victoria’s Visual Art BFA Program and has since exhibited and sold numerous artworks and independent comics\.
