#### Event Details

-   **Start:** 12 March 2025 3:00 pm
-   **End:** 12 March 2025 4:00 pm
-   **Categories:** [Events](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/events/category/events/)

---

Join the UBC Comics Studies Cluster during UBC’s third annual Arts Multilingual Week for the comic book launch of ***Xwémalhkwu Hero Stories*** \(2024\), a groundbreaking graphic novel that brings to life the traditional stories and language of the Homalco Nation through comic art\.

**Date: Wednesday, March 12\[th\] @ 3 pm  
Location: Peña Room, IKBLC  
**[Register here to attend\.](https://ubc.ca1.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_cup7ilitjmkFwPA)

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/03/Xwe%CC%81malhkwu-Hero-Stories-COMIC-BOOK-LAUNCH-Landscape-1536x864.png)

*Led by media personality Tchadas Leo \(Homalco\) and in consultation with Homalco Knowledge Keepers, this graphic novel presents the work of Indigenous artists Alina Pete, Valen Onstine, and Gord Hill, who visually translated Coast Salish history and storytelling traditions into an engaging and educational format\.*

The project, developed in collaboration with the UBC Comics Studies Cluster and [Education without Borders \(EwB\)](https://educationwithoutborders.co/) exemplifies the power of comics to preserve cultural heritage and support Indigenous storytelling traditions\.

Copies of *Xwémalhkwu Hero Stories* will be available for purchase, and the team will share upcoming plans for educational resources and further community engagement\.

[**Register here to attend\.**](https://ubc.ca1.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_cup7ilitjmkFwPA) For more information or to inquire about book purchases, please contact the Homalco First Nation at [heroes@homalco\.com](mailto:heroes@homalco.com)\.

*This project was generously sponsored by* [*Education without Borders \(EwB\)*](https://educationwithoutborders.co/)*, the UBC Comics Studies Cluster, the UBC Popular Media for Social Change Research Excellence Cluster, the UBC Community Engagement Office, and the Narratives Group in the UBC Centre for Migration Studies\.*
