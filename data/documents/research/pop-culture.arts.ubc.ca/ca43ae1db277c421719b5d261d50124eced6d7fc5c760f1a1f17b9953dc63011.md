At the sold\-out *Climate Talks: Connecting Food, Health, and Biodiversity in a Changing Climate* symposium at the UBC Botanical Garden last October, artist Yumeng Chen–the selected artist from a [call in our comics\-to\-research program](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/comics-studies/comics-to-research/ "https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/comics-studies/comics-to-research/")–transformed conversations about insect decline, food systems, and environmental change into a compelling comics narrative\. Drawing on her background in Food Science, organic agriculture, and illustration, Chen documented the event through a series of watercolor and digital comics that translated complex scientific ideas into accessible visual storytelling\.

In this interview, Chen reflects on the challenge of adapting an interdisciplinary academic event into comics, the role of visual storytelling in climate communication, and why sequential art can make abstract environmental issues newly visible–and legible–to readers\. Her work from *Climate Talks* demonstrates how comics can function not only as documentation, but also as a form of public engagement and knowledge mobilization\.

#### Can you tell us a bit about yourself – what you study at UBC and what kinds of creative work you’re most passionate about?

I graduated from the Land and Food Systems at UBC and I studied Food Science\. I’m also an unclassified student taking some creative writing courses\. Drawing and painting have always been my passion and I usually create illustrations and paint watercolor landscapes\. I started exploring comics over the past two years, and later I took the Creative Writing in Graphic Forms course at UBC, and I’m very thankful to Biz for recommending it\!\! That was when I realized how interesting and expressive this medium can be\.

#### What inspired you to take part in Climate Talks, especially given its focus on insects, biodiversity, and food systems?

On one hand, my background is in food science, and I’ve had experience working in organic agriculture, so I’ve always been interested in food systems, biodiversity and environmental issues\. On the other hand, I’ve been exploring comics\. This event brought together science and art, which made it a great interdisciplinary opportunity and really motivated me to take part\!

#### The event brought together people from different fields and countries – what was the atmosphere like, and what moments stood out to you?

The atmosphere was very unique and dynamic, as both the speakers and the audience came from diverse backgrounds, and the topics ranged from well\-being to insects and various perspectives on climate issues\. In general, the discussion session stood out to me the most\. In the audience there were primary school teachers, university students, professionals in related fields, and also people who simply care deeply about our planet\. Everyone was very engaged in the discussion, and each person brought a different perspective\. Many people shared ideas based on their everyday life and it made the whole experience feel very touching and impactful\!

#### You translated the day’s conversations into comics – how did you decide what to highlight or include in your drawings?

First, I decided to document some moments and the overall atmosphere of the event in my drawings\. For example, things like the opening speech, the speakers, attentive audience members, discussions, and the setting as a whole\. Sally’s photographs really helped me in reconstructing those moments\. Second, as for the talks, I tried to extract the key ideas and underlying logic, like how ideas were connected and how conclusions were developed, and then translate them into visual scenes\. I also paid special attention to the concepts I didn’t fully understand\. I felt those were often the parts that could benefit from some visual support, so I noted them down and tried to rework them into something more visually understandable\.

#### What was it like creating both a longer narrative comic connecting the two talks, and shorter vignette pieces capturing group discussions? Did they feel different to make?

Yes, they felt quite different to me\! 

The longer narrative comic required me to connect different parts of the talks together, for example, integrating the lecture content with the overall atmosphere of the event\. This process involved a lot of thinking in terms of structure and selection\. 

The shorter pieces, on the other hand, felt more like quick sketches\. I could just capture specific moments and ideas in a more flexible and immediate way\. 

For me, the longer format was more challenging, especially in terms of deciding what to include, while the shorter pieces felt more spontaneous\. 

#### Were there any ideas or perspectives from the event that changed how you think about climate change or your role as an artist?

One idea that really stayed with me is that individuals can actually do quite a lot, and artworks can also play a role\! Sometimes it’s easy to feel powerless when facing large\-scale environmental issues, especially when we see how much waste exists on a systemic level\. But small individual actions can accumulate and make a difference\. I also learned during the discussions that engaging in environmental practices doesn’t always have to be about “changing the world\.” It can also be about creating a sense of personal reassurance or improving our own well\-being\. For example, eating more plant\-based food can significantly reduce energy consumption and is also healthier for us\. And artworks in this process can help as a form of advocacy\.

#### What do you think comics can do in a setting like this that other forms of communication might not?

I think the integration of images and text, as well as the interactions between panels, is something very unique to comics\. Compared to text or written articles, comics can make concepts clearer and more engaging by combining words with visual elements\. This helps readers understand complex ideas more intuitively, especially in scientific contexts where concepts can be abstract\. Compared to photography or video, comics are different in the way they use panels to build meaning\. A single page can bring together multiple scenes, moments, or ideas\. I try to create panels that are not only sequential, but also work together as a whole\. The panels on a page create a visual structure where ideas or scenes are presented simultaneously, while still allowing the readers to go through the flow at their own pace\.

#### Now that the project is complete, what are you taking away from the experience – and what’s next for you creatively or academically?

This was my first time transforming an academic event into a comic, so it was quite a challenge\! I came to admire even more the strong interdisciplinary potential of comics\. In the future, I’d love to continue exploring this direction and especially by adapting academic topics, scientific research, or books into comics, and using this medium to tell more stories\!

**Artist Bio:** Hi\! I’m Yumeng, a part\-time illustrator and comics artist, working mostly in watercolor and digital media\. My artwork often explores themes of nature, botany, and literature\. I hold a degree in Food Science from UBC Land and Food Systems, and since graduation, I’ve been working in organic farming and landscape gardening alongside my artistic practice\. Through work, learning and art, I’ve developed a strong appreciation for ecology, seasonal rhythms, and the ways people connect with land\. I often draw inspiration from these experiences when creating illustrations of plants, landscapes, or stories rooted in natural and historical themes\.
