### Created by Swati Arora \(Assistant Professor, Yokohama National University\)

#### Intended Audience

This 50\-minute interactive lesson uses a popular\-culture themed BINGO activity to help learners critically examine how media, advertising, and viral content influence attitudes towards diversity, stereotypes, and social issues\. The lesson is designed for high school or first\-year university students studying English, social studies, or intercultural communication\.

#### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Identify ways popular culture shapes beliefs, stereotypes, and opinions
2.  Distinguish between factual information and sensationalised media narratives
3.  Reflect on their own attitudes and external influences
4.  Collaboratively discuss diversity and cultural representation in media
5.  Develop basic media literacy and critical thinking skills

#### Materials

-   [PowerPoint](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/Aesthetics-or-Appropriation_-.pptx)
-   [Teaching Guide](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/Aesthetics-or-Appropriation-Swati-Arora-.pdf)

Students receive a BINGO sheet containing prompts such as:

“Seen a viral video that changed your opinion”  
“Recognised a stereotype in advertising”  
“Heard conflicting opinions about a celebrity or brand”  
“Experienced cultural appreciation vs appropriation debate”  
“Changed your view after learning facts”

Students move around the classroom asking peers questions to complete their BINGO grid\. This is followed by a whole\-class discussion connecting responses to themes such as media influence, fairness vs equality, diversity, and misinformation concepts drawn from real classroom case studies and media examples

The lesson concludes with a short reflection task where students identify one popular culture example that positively or negatively shaped social attitudes\.

#### About the Creator

Dr\. Swati Arora is an Assistant Professor at [Yokohama National University](https://www.ynu.ac.jp/english/) specialising in intercultural communication, English language pedagogy, and global education\. Her work examines how media, popular culture, and classroom practices shape learners’ critical thinking, identity formation, and social attitudes\. She has extensive experience teaching both International Baccalaureate \(IB\) and university\-level courses in Japan, and her research engages with multilingualism, translanguaging, and the role of education in fostering global citizenship\.
