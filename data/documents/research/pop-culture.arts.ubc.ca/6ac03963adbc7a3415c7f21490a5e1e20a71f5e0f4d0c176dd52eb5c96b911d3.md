### About

Welcome to the Comics Studies Cluster and Pop Culture Cluster’s **new** manga literacy resource\! We welcome folks new to comics from Japan by teaching manga literacy\.

Manga literacy is an example of multimodal literacy; the reader decodes both visual and textual elements as well as specific conventions unique to the medium for the purposes of being able to **read** and **create** both manga and manga **imaginations**\. Manga literate readers can replicate what they know to create their own manga\. They can also imagine new forms of manga, e\.g\., Michael Nicoll Yahgulanaas’ Haida Manga titles or Udon Entertainment’s Manga Classics series\.​

The key component of manga literacy is manga’s visual language, the various visual and textual elements present in mainstream manga\. When one has a better understanding of manga’s visual language, then their manga literacy should also increase\.

The manga literacy resource is a work\-in\-progress project by [V\. Rahbar](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/victoria-rahbar/), who leads the [Understanding Through Comics project](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/comics-studies/understanding-through-comics/) and is a member of the [PopCC‘s Arts\-Based Research](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/arts-based-research/) initiative titled [Arts\-Based Interventions in Graduate Research](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/phd-collab/)\.

### Online Resources

Want a quick guide about manga in libraries? Check out [Kodansha An Introduction to Manga for Librarians](https://d3nqfnht5m17hp.cloudfront.net/statics/uploads/2016/01/KAM_OverDrive_GuidetoManga_FINAL.pdf)\.

[Manga in Libraries](https://mangainlibraries.com/) is an educational organization focused on bringing Japanese comics to libraries in the United States\. Their [webinars](https://mangainlibraries.com/webinars/) and [booklists](https://mangainlibraries.com/book-lists/) are especially informative\.

[Ashley Hawkins of the Manga Librarian](https://mangalibrarian.com/) offers reviews and more about manga for school libraries\.

*[Publisher's Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/home/index.html)* is a great way to keep up on news about the manga world for English\-speaking readers\.

Concerned about censorship? [The Comic Book Legal Defense Fund](https://cbldf.org/) offers both resources and the latest news on censorship of all things comic, including manga\. Their work is mostly focused on events in the United States, however\.

### Reviews and Critiques

There are many, many reviewers of manga for readers, but who reviews manga for librarians?

[*Booklist*](https://www.booklistonline.com/) reviews individual manga series, but their [manga essentials features](https://www.booklistonline.com/Manga-Essentials-Fascinating-Nonfiction-Hawkins-Ashley/pid=9794566) and [annual guide's to graphic novels in libraries](https://booklist.booklistonline.com/html5/reader/production/default.aspx?pubname=&amp;edid=0be7d2c9-4078-459c-9d09-50dc24a15092) are especially notable\.

[*School Library Journal*](https://www.slj.com/) also provides reviews of individual series as well as features about manga including [best of lists](https://www.slj.com/story/best-manga-2024-slj-best-books)\.

### Citing Sources

When citing manga, consider the style guide of the publisher supporting your work\. However, when the publisher does not have established guidelines for manga and related media, we recommend the [Mechademia Second Arc Style Guide for Authors and Editors 2022](https://www.mechademia.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/mechademia-style-guide-2022.pdf)\.

When citing Japanese\-language sources and other sources from Asian languages in English\-language and Asian\-language documents, the [University of British Columbia Library provides an information page on how to cite for APA, MLA, and Chicago Style](https://guides.library.ubc.ca/c.php?g=707463&amp;p=5291936)\.
