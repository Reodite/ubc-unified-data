Arrieta Zamudio, A\., Morales Cruz, A\., Santiago Morales, D\., Morales Cruz, V\., López García, J\. and Tamayo Castelar, S\. \(2025\)\. Tùgûl̈ “Txì xtěn rǎ gǎmy” \[Graphic novel\]\. https://sites\.google\.com/view/tugul/

*In this interview, [Ana Laura Arrieta Zamudio,](https://linguistics.ubc.ca/profile/ana-laura-arrieta-zamudio/) a PhD candidate in Linguistics, reflects on creating a trilingual graphic novel with the San Pablo Güilá community\. Designed as both a linguistic resource and a cultural artifact, [Tùgûl̈](https://sites.google.com/view/tugul/versions-versiones?authuser=0) highlights how comics can make research accessible while supporting language preservation and education across generations\.* 

#### **Could you start by introducing yourself and your research?**  

My name is Ana Laura Arrieta Zamudio, and I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Linguistics\. My research focuses on modality, which in linguistics refers to how languages express what is possible, necessary, or desirable\. Part of my work also examines how modality interacts with tense and aspect across languages\. My dissertation investigates epistemic modality \(what is possible or necessary given the speaker’s knowledge\) and its interaction with tense and aspect in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec, an Otomanguean language spoken in Oaxaca, Mexico\. I also collaborate with Ryan Bochnak on projects related to modality, tense, and aspect in Ktunaxa, a language spoken in British Columbia and Idaho, USA\. 

#### **What drew you to this project, and how do you situate it within your broader scholarly and creative practice?**  

I have always been very interested in different cultural practices, and the Day of the Dead is a huge celebration in Mexico, which, depending on the region, involves different cultural practices\. Additionally, I have been working for several years with San Pablo Güilá Zapotec consultants, and some of the tasks I use in my fieldwork involve storyboards to gather semantic linguistic data\. Based on that, along with three members of the San Pablo Güilá community, Ana Mey Maricela Morales Cruz, Dulce Rocío Santiago Morales and Virginia Morales Cruz, we decided to create a graphic novel, based on the Day of the Dead traditions, while using a dialogue that includes linguistic expressions of modality and tense and aspect\. This [graphic novel](https://sites.google.com/view/tugul/versions-versiones?authuser=0) was created mainly for the community, so they could have a representation of their own community, places, people and traditions, written in their own language\. It will also serve as a tool \(like a storyboard\) for other researchers interested in modality, because it includes contexts that favour the emergence of modal expressions that could potentially be used in different languages\.  

#### **Can you tell us a bit about your PhD journey at UBC – i\.e\., were there key moments, influences, or challenges that led you toward developing** ***Tùgûl̈*** **\(Day of the Dead\) as an arts\-based, comics\-driven project \(or however you yourself define this project\!\)?**  

Some of the key moments of my PhD that influenced me to create *Tùgûl̈* were looking at pictures of the celebration in San Pablo Güilá and talking with various consultants about it\. Their excitement and respect for that celebration made me think we could do something together to preserve those cultural practices in some way\. Moreover, after speaking with Virginia Morales Cruz, a local teacher, she told me that local schools needed language\-teaching tools written in their own language\. Additionally, I have been working with Jose Froylan Lopez García, a Zapotec artist and community member\. From working and having these different conversations, I started to think of a way to transform part of my research into something that would be useful for the community and local teachers, and that would contribute to the language revitalization efforts that different members of the community are doing\.  

#### ***Tùgûl̈*** **translates dissertation research into a graphic narrative form\. In the process of this project, do you conceptualize comics not just as dissemination, but as a method of inquiry itself?**  

Yes, absolutely\. Comics and/or graphic novels can be extremely useful in different ways\. They are appealing to broader audiences because of their visual component, but also, researchers, such as linguists \(but also other researchers like anthropologists or sociologists, etc\.\), can use them as a tool to gather linguistic data in a fun way for the consultants, while also providing a specific product that is useful for the community\.   

#### **Could you walk us through your creative process? At what points does artistic decision\-making become analytical or theoretical work?**  

The first step was to ask a community member, Dulce Rocío Santiago Morales, to tell me about the whole celebration, what process it involved, what kind of preparations were needed, and, in general, how they celebrate the Day of the Dead\. After that, using the components Dulce gave me, I started creating the story, following a fictional family as they prepared for, celebrated, and concluded the celebration\.  When I created the story, I had in mind different contexts that favoured the appearance of different modal expressions \(based on the theory of modality\)\. After that, with the help of Dulce Rocío Santiago Morales, Ana Mey Maricela Morales Cruz, and Virginia Morales Cruz, we translated that story into San Pablo Güilá Zapotec\. It was a very long process, because while I was making sure that the contexts were adequate for a modal expression to appear, we were also making sure that any person who might read or hear the story could understand what was being said, could follow the whole story, and, more importantly, that the story was accurate, based on the actual celebration\. After that, Jose Froylan Lopez García created the illustrations of the characters, and Sergio Tamayo worked on the locations, the graphic design and putting together the three versions of the novel\. 

#### **You’ve framed the graphic novel as a means of documentation and cultural revitalization for the San Pablo Güilá community\. What affordances do comics offer that traditional academic writing cannot?**  

I think that comics or graphic novels are more accessible to broader audiences\. Traditional academic writing is often targeted to specific audiences, and research is often known only within academia because the way it is produced and even the language used are not accessible to others\. Comics are really engaging; they have a visual component and use simple language that makes them accessible to broader audiences, including children and teenagers, who are sometimes hard to reach\. 

#### **In transforming linguistic and cultural research into a visual narrative, how do you navigate issues of representation, authorship, and collaboration with the community?**  

These are issues that you need to take into account while you are working with different communities, especially a community that you are not a member of\. The whole process, from beginning to end, was carried out alongside various community members, always ensuring they felt represented and heard throughout\. All community members who contributed to the story are coauthors of the graphic novel, and the illustrator and graphic designer were given appropriate credit and payment for their work\. We also asked the various Collectives in the community and the authorities for their consent to release the graphic novel to the public\.      

#### **Can you speak more to the process of** ***Tùgûl̈*****’s publication and dissemination?**  

*Tùgûl̈ “Txì xtěn rǎ gǎmy”* was published in November 2025\. 100 copies of the graphic novel were published, and we had a presentation in San Pablo Güilá’s main square on December 18 th, 2025\.  All the copies were distributed among community members, local teachers and local authorities\. After that, I created a website to disseminate the three versions of the graphic novel, which is intended to reach broader audiences \([https://sites\.google\.com/view/tugul/inicio?pli\=1](https://sites.google.com/view/tugul/inicio?pli=1)\)\. 

#### **Your work engages with Zapotec language\(s\) and is published in three different languages \(San Pablo Güilá Zapotec, English, Spanish\)\. How does the comics form intersect with language revitalization or preservation, particularly in multilingual contexts?**  

I believe that comics and graphic novels are a great way to reach multiple audiences and to share the stories of a particular community or communities\. Our first goal was to create the graphic novel entirely in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec so that its speakers could read and understand it\. We also aim to reach other audiences, which is why we made Spanish and English versions available, allowing non\-native speakers of Zapotec to understand and follow the story and to compare the dialogues across the three languages\. Modal expressions are highlighted in all three versions so that readers can see how words like *must*, *might*, and *can* are expressed in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec\. 

#### **What’s next for you, and are there more avenues for employing arts\-based research as you complete your PhD program?** 

I hope to continue working with the San Pablo Güilá community, and I am currently a member of the group called *Creativos dì›ẍdò* where we created colouring books and a puzzle of the parts of the body in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec\. themymy

**Ana Laura Arrieta Zamudio, PhD Candidate in Linguistics**

My research focuses on epistemic modality and its interaction with tense and aspect in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec \(Otomanguean, Mexico\)\. To date, there has been no research on any type of modality in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec or in other Zapotec languages\. Using semantic fieldwork methodology with four consultants of San Pablo Güilá Zapotec, I have identified two epistemic modals \(*pǎt* ‘must/might’ and *nǒ’ ryěs* ‘it is possible that…’\)\. I also investigate how these two epistemic modals interact with tense and aspect\. My work is also focused on language revitalization, for which I created, with three consultants, the graphic novel *Tùgûl̈ “Txì xtěn rǎ gǎmy”* \(The celebration of the Day of the Dead: “The day of the souls”\)\.
