### **About the Project**

The UBC Comics Studies Cluster invites applications from **six Indigenous comics artists** to participate in a new collaborative project that critically and creatively recontextualizes the legacy of Franz Boas through Indigenous\-led visual storytelling\. 

Franz Boas profoundly shaped North American anthropology through his work on the Northwest Coast amongst Kwakwa̱ka̱ʼwakw Nations and Coast Salish peoples, as well as in BC’s interior with Nlaka’pamux peoples in collaboration with James Teit, and prior to this, the Inuit of Baffin Island\. While often celebrated as a foundational figure in the discipline, his legacy remains complex and entangled with extractive research practices, museum collecting, colonial knowledge systems\. This project seeks to engage Boas’s legacy not as settled history, but as a site for critical reflection, cultural resurgence, and Indigenous narrative sovereignty\. 

Rather than commissioning one extended work, we will commission **six short comics projects**, each responding to a theme identified by the Editorial Team\. Together, these works will form a curated collection that situates Boas within ongoing conversations about anthropology, cultural continuity, language revitalization, repatriation, and Indigenous intellectual traditions\. 

We especially encourage applications from artists with ties to the **Kwakwa̱ka̱ʼwakw** Nations and the broader Pacific Northwest, though Indigenous artists from other Nations whose work engages with these themes are warmly invited to apply\. 

### **Project Themes**

Artists will be invited to respond to one of six themes curated by the Editorial Team\. Themes may include \(subject to refinement\): 

-   Anthropology, Extraction, and Cultural Resurgence 
-   Museum Collections, Archives, and Repatriation 
-   Language Documentation and Language Sovereignty 
-   Visual Sovereignty and Indigenous Aesthetics 
-   Ethnography and Power 
-   Cultural Continuity and Refusal 

Applicants may indicate a preferred theme in their Letter of Interest\. 

### **Editorial Team**

This project is guided by an Editorial Team composed of Indigenous and settler scholars, artists, and community advisors with expertise in anthropology, Indigenous studies, museum studies, and visual storytelling\.

**Editorial Team**

-   Dr\. Bernard Perley \(Maliseet from Tobique First Nation\), Institute for Critical Indigenous Studies, UBC Vancouver 
-   Dr\. Rob Hancock \(Cree\-Metis\), Associate Professor of Anthropology, University of Victoria 
-   Dr\. Susan Rowley, Director, Museum of Anthropology, and Associate Professor of Anthropology, UBC Vancouver 
-   Dr\. Leslie Robertson, Professor Emeritus of Anthropology, UBC  
-   Dr\. Carole Blackburn, Associate Professor of Sociocultural Anthropology, UBC Vancouver 
-   G̱wuła̱las Claire Everson \(K’ómoks, Kwakwa̱ka̱’wakw, Mi’kmaq\), Graduate Student of Anthropology, UBC Vancouver 

**The Editorial Team will:**

-   Curate thematic directions
-   Review and adjudicate artist applications
-   Provide feedback on thumbnails and drafts
-   Support artists through research and contextualization

Honoraria will be provided to Editorial Team members in recognition of their advisory role\. 

#### **Artist Role &amp; Deliverables**

Each selected artist will: 

-   Participate in an initial virtual meeting with the Editorial Team\. 
-   Conduct independent research \(with support from UBC Library and the Comics Studies Cluster\)\. 
-   Participate in a funded site visit to Alert Bay \(travel, potential accommodation, and meals will be covered\)\. 
-   Create one **8–12 page comic** responding to their assigned theme\. 
-   Submit thumbnails/storyboards for feedback\. 
-   Deliver a final, print\-ready and web\-ready comic\. 
-   Provide a short \(500–750 word\) artist reflection situating their work\. 

Artists will retain copyright of their work\. The UBC Comics Studies Cluster will receive non\-exclusive rights for educational, exhibition, and knowledge mobilization purposes\. 

#### **Compensation**

Each artist will receive $3,000 CAD for their contribution, paid in installments:

-   50% upon approval of thumbnails
-   50% upon submission of final work

*All travel costs associated with the Alert Bay site visit will be fully covered*\.

#### **Eligibility**

Applicants must: 

-   Identify as Indigenous \(First Nations, Inuit, Métis, or Indigenous from other global contexts\)\. 
-   Demonstrate experience in comics or visual storytelling\. 
-   Be available for virtual meetings and the site visit\. 

*Artists working in Indigenous languages and/or multilingual formats are strongly encouraged to apply\.*

#### **Application Instructions**

Please submit a single PDF containing:

1.  **Letter of Interest** \(max\. 1 page\) describing your artistic practice, your interest in this project, and any experience with community\-engaged, research\-based, or socially impactful storytelling\.
2.  **3 examples of comic art** \(max\. 10 pages total\) demonstrating your approach to visual storytelling\.
3.  **OPTIONAL:** A brief proposal indicating which theme you are most interested in and why\.

### **About the Initiative**

The [**UBC Comics Studies Cluster**](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/comics-studies/) \(CSC\) supports academic and public\-facing scholarship in Comics Studies and comics production in Metro Vancouver\. We connect academics, non\-profits, and First Nations with cartoonists and artist\-practitioners across British Columbia to co\-create comics that advance our partners’ strategic, communication, and research goals\. 

This **Indigenous Comics Initiative** is part of the **CSC’s** [**Comics\-to\-Research program**](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/comics-studies/comics-to-research/) and is supported by Indigenous Strategic Initiatives funding through the “Comics and Cultural Continuity: Indigenous Visual Storytelling and Language Revitalization” project, co\-led by Dr\. Biz Nijdam \(settler\), Dr\. Shannon Leddy \(Métis\), Dr\. Strang Burton \(settler\), and Dr\. Tim Frandy \(Sámi\)\. This initiative builds on previous collaborative comics work with First Nations and Indigenous partners, including *Xwémalhkwu Hero Stories* \(2024\), and ongoing projects with the Homalco and Stó:lō Nations and the Arctic Winter Games International Committee\. 

We approach this work with humility and care, recognizing that Franz Boas’s legacy is not merely academic history, but entangled with living communities, ongoing cultural work, and questions of sovereignty, representation, and responsibility\.
