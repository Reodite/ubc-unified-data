### On Thursday, April 16th, the UBC Comics Studies Cluster Project Team joined artists and the Stó:lō Editorial Committee in Harrison Hot Springs and on Seabird Island to launch its newest Indigenous Comics Initiative, “Seabird Island Stories”\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/IMG_1715-768x623.jpeg)

Lunch at [Schi:ya Café](https://www.schiyacafe.ca/) with Seabird Island Stories Editorial Committee members [Xémóntalot Carrielynn Victor](https://carrielynnvictor.com/), Teqwótenot \(Roxanne Dool\), and Keanu Ritchie, artist Jared Deck, and UBC Project Team, Biz Nijdam, Strang Burton, and Jennifer Nagtegaal\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/04/IMG_1718-197x300.jpeg)Sq’éwqel \(Seabird Island\) is home to stories that carry the spirit, history, and lived experiences of the Stó:lō people\. In her final years, Elder Ts’ats’elexwot \(Elizabeth Herrling\), shared her memories of growing up on the island: from life in a reconstructed longhouse and encounters with the land and its creatures, to childhood adventures and everyday moments that capture the essence of community\. Stories include “The Doll Story,” “Maggots,” and several others, many of which have already been preliminarily storyboarded for this project\.  

In collaboration with [Stó:lō Shxweli,](https://stoloshxweli.org/) the **Seabird Island Stories Comics Project** will produce a bilingual graphic novel based on several stories by Elizabeth Herrling that were collected by the Stó:lō Shxweli Halq’emeylem Language Program\. The project prioritizes the Halq’eméylem language and will also include Herrling’s own English translations, preserving and amplifying the cultural knowledge and lived experience embedded in these narratives\.

Our recent gathering in Harrison Hot Springs brought together our creative and editorial teams to take a meaningful step forward on the project\. We connected with the project’s four artists—Natasha Donovan, Chelsea Forseth, and Gord Hill virtually, and Jared Deck in person—alongside our editorial board to map out story assignments and timelines\.

Fuelled by farm\-to\-table pastries and coffee from the family\-owned [Muddy Waters Cafe](https://muddywaterscafe.ca/), we met in the charming waterfront [Harrison Grand Motel](https://harrisongrandmotel.ca/) boardroom before sharing a memorable lunch next door at Schi:ya Café, where we were graciously hosted by Gary and his family and treated to a first taste of their new cold\-brew cake\.

The day concluded with a powerful tour led by Dianna Kay through Seabird Island and along the Fraser River, grounding our work in the histories, lands, and stories that continue to shape this project\.
