#### **For the second year in a row, the [UBC Critical Play Lab](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/critical-play-lab/) \(CPL\) returned to [Terminal City Tabletop Convention](https://terminalcitycon.ca/) with a strong mix of play\-based programming, community engagement, and a little bit of free swag\.**

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2853.png)

### [“Indigenous Game Design &amp; Decolonial Play: Lessons from the Arctic Winter Games”](https://tctc2026.sched.com/event/2Huvm/indigenous-game-design-decolonial-play-lessons-from-the-arctic-winter-games?iframe=no)

CPL Saturday programming included a well\-attended roundtable on **Indigenous Game Design and Decolonial Play** that brought together Indigenous designers and consultants to reflect on how tabletop games can support decolonial education\. Drawing on the team’s recent trip to the [Arctic Winter Games in Whitehorse](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/meet-the-awg-artists/), [**Luke Parnell**](https://www.instagram.com/luke_parnell_art/?hl=en) \(Haida and Nisga\), [**Chris Cooke**](https://chrisccooke.weebly.com/) \(Métis\), and game consultant [**David Plamondon**](https://pemetawe.com/) \(Cree\) emphasized the role of games in advancing Indigenous media representation and supporting Indigenous sovereignty in storytelling\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2586.png)

Plamondon provided a framework for understanding decolonization through an anti\-capitalist lens that is rooted in the Cree concept of Wâhkôhtowin, a kinship structure that functions through the interconnectedness of all living things\. He also spoke about the importance of Indigenous representation across media and of empowering Indigenous creators to make art\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2608.png)

Parnell reflected on his experience of the Arctic Winter Games \(AWG\) and revealed that he’s working on a concept for a new tabletop game with a roll\-and\-write *Yahtzee*\-like system that captures all the AWG games\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2640.png)

Cooke talked about his experiences working in the video game industry and as a hobbyist board game designer, sharing a few stories about the invaluable feedback that comes out of playtesting as a way to iterate and improve upon a game’s design and mechanics\.

### [“**UBC’s Critical Play Lab: A Place for Playful Research”**](https://tctc2026.sched.com/event/2CEj2?iframe=no)

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2689.png)

On Sunday, we hosted a second roundtable that reflected on the Critical Play Lab’s inaugural fellowship year and featured [Critical Play Lab Fellows](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/critical-play-lab/critical-play-fellowship/) **Stephanie Broder**, **Kayla McIntyre** and **Corey Wesley** in conversation about games as subjects, objects, and methodologies in their academic and community\-engaged work\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2748.png)

Beyond its Main Stage programming, the CPL maintained an active presence on the convention floor\. Over three days, Director Biz Nijdam and CPL Project Manager Sydney Lines, alongside volunteers from the Pop Culture Cluster, ran a booth that drew in BC educators, interdisciplinary researchers, game designers, and fellow tabletop enthusiasts\. The conversations were lively and wide\-ranging, often circling back to CPL’s core interest in how games can be used to rethink research, teaching, and community engagement\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2891.png) [Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2901.png)

A highlight for Nijdam was playtesting **“War Canoe,”** a board game prototype by **[Games for Decolonization](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/critical-play-lab/games-for-decolonization/) partner [Luke Parnell](https://www.instagram.com/kerf_cut_games/)\.** The game centers on recruiting and supporting a canoe racing team, using mechanics that emphasize collective energy, celebration, and momentum rather than conquest or domination\.

#### Playtesting is an essential process in the game design, and Nijdam is thrilled to report that she’s won playing various versions of Parnell’s game two years in a row\!

CPL’s return to Canada’s largest community\-driven tabletop game event reflects the Pop Culture Clusters continued work in the arena of publicly engaged game studies, where it continues to succeed in bringing scholars, students, designers, and communities to the literal and figurative table by turning to games as a way of innovating research and teaching at UBC Vancouver and beyond\.
