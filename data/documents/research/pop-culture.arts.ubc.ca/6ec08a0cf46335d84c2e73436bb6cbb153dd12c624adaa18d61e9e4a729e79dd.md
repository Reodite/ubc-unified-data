### Created by Alicia Matthews \(MA English Graduate\)

The target audience is upper\-level undergraduates in the Faculty of Arts, although the seminar is open to all students in third year and above\. It is for anybody interested in the evolution of media and propaganda, and the course structure is designed to allow for critical reflections on the influence of our media environments\. The syllabus is organized into five units or mini ‘arcs’:

Unit 1: Theory &amp; Foundations \(Jan 5—12\)  
Unit 2: Destabilizing the Media Empire \(Jan 19—26\)  
Unit 3: Medium, Mind &amp; Body \(Feb 2—9\)  
Unit 4: Afterlives of Truth \(Feb 23—Mar 9\)  
Unit 5: Mediating Temporalities \(Mar 16—30\)

#### Learning Objectives

Students will be able to…

1.  Apply theories from media studies, critical theory, and other fields to analyze contemporary media phenomena
2.  Evaluate various media\-related texts and assess their impacts on public perception, behaviour and discourse
3.  Compare the roles that popular media play in both catalyzing and neutralizing social change, especially in the context of \(de\)colonialism
4.  Develop integral, applicable research and communication skills
5.  Reflect on their own positionalities within digital information ecosystems

#### About the Creator

Alicia Matthews is a researcher in the areas of media theory, digital humanities and linguistics\. Her MA thesis traces contemporary non\-fictional discourses on artificial intelligence to nineteenth century semi\-satirical depictions of artificial consciousness\.
