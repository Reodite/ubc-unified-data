The following guide offers resources on teaching video games to students\. We hope the guide offers an opportunity to consider how digital games reflect cultural practices and ideas\. The guide includes lesson plans developed by the team, links to archives, academic journals on board games, and other useful material\.  

### Archives

#### **The Syd Bolton Collection** 

The Syd Bolton Collection, which includes almost 14,000 individual games and hundreds of consoles and systems, was amassed by Bolton over the past 20 years\. It also includes “over 5,000 issues of game magazines, hundreds of books and literature, peripherals, and related technology, materials, and documents\.” The collection includes games for popular consoles and niche or forgotten systems\. The University of Toronto’s library staff is currently working on compiling a complete finding aid\. Currently, the finding aid is accessible but incomplete\.  

[https://collections\.utm\.utoronto\.ca/collections/syd\-bolton\-collection](https://collections.utm.utoronto.ca/collections/syd-bolton-collection) 

#### **The Video Game History Foundation Library** 

The VGHFL was founded as a non\-profit in 2017 by video game preservationist Frank Cifaldi\. Their website includes access to posters, press kits, newspapers, and other video game ephemera such as manuals, and they’re currently digitalizing more of their physical collection\.  

[https://archive\.gamehistory\.org/](https://archive.gamehistory.org/) 

#### **The Strong: The National Museum of Play** 

The Museum of Play was founded in 1968 as the Margaret Woodbury Strong Museum of Fascination\. Strong was a wealthy, prolific collector of dolls and toys, and when she passed away in 1969, she left her collection and financial resources for the construction of a museum\. The Museum’s website features a searchable catalogue of video games as the Museum has museum has shifted its focus from Strong’s original collecting proclivities to a broader sense of “play” and “games\.” 

 [https://www\.museumofplay\.org/video\-game\-history\-timeline/](https://www.museumofplay.org/video-game-history-timeline/) 

### Academic Journals

#### **Game Studies**  

Game Studies features peer\-reviewed articles on video games\.  

[https://gamestudies\.org/2501/](https://gamestudies.org/2501/) 

#### **Games and Culture**  

*Games and Culture* is an international academic journal founded in 2006 that publishes peer\-reviewed articles, with a focus on the socio\-cultural, political, and economic dimensions of gaming\. The journal is interdisciplinary and encourages works that dialogue across fields\.  

[https://journals\.sagepub\.com/home/gac](https://journals.sagepub.com/home/gac) 

#### **Loading: The Journal of the Canadian Game Studies Association \(open access\)**  

Founded in 2007, *Loading* publishes issues twice a year and is run by professors at the University of British Columbia\. They “accept work from a variety of perspectives: from research on video game cultures and industries to educational approaches to the study and development of digital games, and from computer science driven papers on designing and testing games to psychological research on video games and their effects\.” 

[https://journals\.sfu\.ca/loading/index\.php/loading/index](https://journals.sfu.ca/loading/index.php/loading/index)
