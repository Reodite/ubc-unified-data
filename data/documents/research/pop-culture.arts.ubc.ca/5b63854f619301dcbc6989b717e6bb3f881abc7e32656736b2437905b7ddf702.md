Our games, digital and tabletop alike, are structured around a linear model of progress: As time passes, we get stronger, gain power and control over the game world\. Not only is this model of history rooted in colonialism, but it fails to reflect the non\-linear ways the body experiences trauma, queerness and disability\. How can we deconstruct the progress narrative and find ways of representing more complicated, fractured histories in play?

**Where: Dodson Room \(302\), Irving K\. Barber Learning Centre &amp; Online**

**Speaker Bio**

[**Anna Anthropy**](http://triple-a-games.biz/) is a game designer, writer, educator and cyborg\. She works in both the digital and tabletop spaces\. She currently lives in Chicago with a little black cat named Encyclopedia Frown\.

Several games referenced in the talk are free to download or play online:

-   [dys4ia](https://w.itch.io/dys4ia) \(15\-20 minute game about trans health care\) 
-   [Queers in Love at the End of the World](https://w.itch.io/end-of-the-world) \(10\-second game about queer temporality\) 
-   [Sonata in Zero G](https://w.itch.io/sonata-in-zero-g) \(30\-60 minute tabletop game about scars and disability\)
