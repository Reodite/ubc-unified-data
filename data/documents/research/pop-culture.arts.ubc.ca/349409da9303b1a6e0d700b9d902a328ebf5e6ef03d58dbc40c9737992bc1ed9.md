In *Critical Play: Radical Game Design* \(2009\), Mary Flanagan proposed a new theory of “avant\-garde game design \[…\] that focuses on the reworking of contemporary, popular game practices to propose an alternative, or ‘radical,’ game design” as a way of “understanding larger cultural issues as well as the games themselves\.” In th is spirit, t he Pop Culture Cluster is excited to announce the second annual call for the Critical Play Lab’s Fellowship Program for the 202 6\-202 7 academic year\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/03/182A2689.png)

### About the Fellowship

## About the Fellowship

The Critical Play Fellows comprise an interdisciplinary community of graduate students, faculty members, industry professionals, and independent designers for monthly programming that explores the intersections of digital, analog, and embodied gaming cultures, pedagogy, and critical theory\. The primary goal of the fellowship is to create and convene a local games\-based community\-of\-practice rooted in critical play\. 

Sessions will include monthly fellows' meetings, guest speakers and facilitators from the fields of game studies, game design, and/or community\-engaged gaming practice, as well as hands\-on workshops\. Through these gatherings, participants will engage deeply with questions around the politics of play, design ethics, decolonial mechanics, radical imagination, narrative interventions, and community\-centered gaming\. The gatherings encourage both critical reflection and practical experimentation, offering a unique space to build connections between academic research, game design, and social justice work\. Join us in reimagining what games can do—and who they’re for\.  

### Who Should Apply

## Who Should Apply

We invite applications from graduate students, faculty, game designers/developers \(digital and analog\), educators, and community members who are: 

-   in any discipline whose work intersects with the study of games, gaming cultures, game design, critical play, playful pedagogies, and/or justice\-oriented play 
-   looking to expand their research/professional networks and praxis in game studies 
-   interested in engaging with theory and critical pedagogy 
-   involved in gaming spaces, cultural production, or playful storytelling 

Benefits of the fellowship include: 

-   Developing skills in game analysis and game\-based research and design methods 
-   Learning from leaders in the field through talks and workshops 
-   Building and contributing to an interdisciplinary and intersectoral professional network and community of practice in critical play 
-   Exploring the role of games in social transformation, cultural resistance, and collaborative worldbuilding 
-   Contributing to collaborative work that could lead to larger grant\-funded projects 
-   Several free meals offered at various in\-person programming 
-   Receiving free or discounted registration at the [Canadian Game Studies Conference](https://gamestudies.ca/conference-conference/) \(faculty and students\) 
-   Opportunities for free participation/attendance at local gaming conventions like [ProtoCon BC](https://www.protoconbc.ca/) and [Terminal City Tabletop Convention](https://terminalcitycon.ca/)\. This may also include opportunities to present your work in associated programming\.  

### Expectations &amp; Requirements

## Expectations &amp; Requirements

As the goal of the fellowship is to foster an **active and generative community\-of\-practice in critical play,** **it is essential that we** **gather**\. “Communities of practice are groups of people who share a concern or a passion for something they do and learn how to do it better as they interact regularly” \([Wenger, 2015](https://www.wenger-trayner.com/introduction-to-communities-of-practice/)\)\. That said, s elected fellows must commit to attending monthly gatherings in Vancouver and participating in at least six Critical Play Lab events during the academic year \(any critical play event posted to our [events calendar](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/events/) is eligible\)\. E vents will take place on the UBC Vancouver campus and in the local Vancouver area and will be co\-hosted by various programs and units on campus and in the community, such as the UBC Green College Leading Scholars Program, the GRSJ Noted Lecture Series, and Science World\. Many of the events will be catered and feature local, n ational or international sp eakers presen ting on game studies, innovative approaches to game design, or other aspects of critical play\. Some of these events will be hybrid for better accessibility\. 

### Format of Fellow Gatherings

Fellows will meet monthly for a 90\-minute lunchtime gathering \(October 2026\-April 2027\)\. At the start of each meeting, three fellows will each give a 5\-minute lightning talk on an aspect of their critical play work or current critical play interests\. A Q&amp;A will follow, and the talks will inform the remaining discussion period for the gathering\. *The meeting times will be selected based on the availability of selected fellows\.*

We are, unfortunately, unable to offer honoraria for participation this year\.  

### Eligibility

## Eligibility

We welcome all local industry professionals, independent designers, and UBC faculty and graduate students from across the university in any faculty, school, or department\. We hope to foster an interdisciplinary, collaborative community\-of\-practice whose work engages in some way with justice\-oriented critical play, critical game studies, and/or game design\. Past fellows are welcome to apply again\. 

### Evaluation

## Evaluation

Fellows will be selected based on the relevance of their work and interests in critical play and their application's alignment with the goals of the fellowship to foster an active interdisciplinary community\-of\-practice engaged in critical play research, mentorship, and participation in Critical Play Lab activities\.  

### How to Apply

## How to Apply

Please fill out the application form linked below\. You will be asked to submit a short statement of interest describing your background, interest in games and social justice, and what you hope to gain from the fellowship as well as a short bio \(max 150 words\) and an abbreviated CV or résumé \(2 pages max\.\) highlighting any of your research, design, or teaching in critical play\. Collaborative, interdisciplinary, and community\-engaged work is highly encouraged\.
