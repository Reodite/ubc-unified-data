**This summer, *Xwémalhkwu \(Homalco\) Heroes: Tribal Journey* moved from the drawing board to the water \(and back again\!\)\.**

The second Indigenous Comics Initiative project with the Xwémalhkwu \(Homalco\) First Nation is taking shape through a very different kind of research process: participation\. Over the summer, lead comics artist Valen Onstine and co\-artist Waylon Asp, joined the Homalco community for its annual canoe journey, taking part in the paddling, launches, cultural events, and community\-building that will inform the new graphic narrative\.

Onstine, a two\-spirit Dane\-Zaa and Cree artist from Treaty 8, returns to the project after contributing to *Xwémalhkwu Hero Stories: A Graphic Novel*, published in 2024\. The first graphic novel began with archival recordings made with Homalco Elders in the early 1990s\. Those recordings, first developed into the *Remember: Our People, Our History* podcast series through 100\.7 FM The Raven, became the source material for three comics by Indigenous artists Alina Pete, Gord Hill and Onstine, who created the story centred on clam digging\.

[Read Tchadas Leo’s account of Xwémalhkwu Hero Stories](https://tchadasleo.ca/xwemalhkwu-hero-stories/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

*Tribal Journey* takes a different approach\. Rather than working primarily from archival recordings, the new comic is grounded in the contemporary canoe journey itself – a living expression of Homalco cultural knowledge, bringing together youth, Elders, skippers and support crews\. Onstine and Asp are documenting the preparations, protocols, teachings, relationships, and work that make the journey possible\.

[Meet Waylon Asp and read about his approach to the project](https://pop-culture.arts.ubc.ca/introducing-waylon-asp/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For the pair of comics artists, that has meant getting out of the studio and into the community and the experience itself\. From July 16\-18, Onstine and Asp, a mixed Tahltan/Settler/Ukrainian Jewish cartoonist and interdisciplinary artist based in Victoria, participated in paddling and launches, shared in meals with the community, and attended drumming and singing and dancing events as the journey unfolded\. A second trip on July 21 brought the project to Tsawwassen, where Onstine was present for the arrival\.

[Image](https://phh-comicstudies-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/homalco2.jpg)

A preliminary sketch of three panels in which Onstine documents the conditions and spirit of the launch\.

Those experiences, again with archival material from the *Remember* podcasts, are guiding their creative work\. Together, Onstine and Asp are developing the graphic novel with project manager **Tchadas Leo**, Homalco leadership and community members\. The aim of the project, which is funded by UBC’s Indigenous Strategic Initiatives, is not simply to illustrate a canoe trip after the fact, but to build a visual account from the experience of being there\. The project team hopes to have the finished comic ready for print with **Island Blue by the new year**\.
