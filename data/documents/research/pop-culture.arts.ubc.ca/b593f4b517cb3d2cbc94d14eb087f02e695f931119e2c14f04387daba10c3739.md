### Created by Dakota Brown \(UBC MA Children’s Literature\)

#### Intended Audience

This mini lesson is written keeping in mind an audience of 9/10th grade secondary students in an English Language Arts \(ELA\) classroom setting\.

#### Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Analyze themes/messages and interpret narrative techniques
2.  Examine key symbols \(e\.g\., machine, gold, memories, letter\)
3.  Develop and support personal and critical responses
4.  Evaluate how the story’s structure \(voiceover/letter format\) shapes meaning and mood
5.  Make connections to myths/literary traditions, e\.g\. compare Nicholas to figures from myths &amp; legends \(Icarus, Prometheus\) to understand concepts hubris and consequence
6.  Construct a persuasive argument using evidence from the film and personal reflection

#### About the Creator

Dakota Brown \(she/her\) is pursuing a MA in Children’s Literature at the University of British Columbia\. She holds a BS\.Ed\. in Secondary Education and a BA in English from the University of Idaho\. Dakota previously taught high school in her hometown before moving to begin a graduate program\. She has recently defended her thesis, which is a case study of performative heroics found in the animated series *My Hero Academia*, and is focused on creating pedagogical, pop culture content for secondary ELA classrooms\.
