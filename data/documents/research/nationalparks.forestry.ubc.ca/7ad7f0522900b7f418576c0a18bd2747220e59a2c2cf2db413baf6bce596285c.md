**Weiwei Wang**

wwwang28@mail\.ubc\.ca

Weiwei Wang earned her PhD degree at the Faculty of Forestry in the University of British Columbia\. She obtained her M\.Eng\. in Forest Remote Sensing at Beijing Normal University in 2019, and her B\.S\. in Applied Mathematics from the Communication University of China in 2016\. Her current research is focused on wildfires in national parks, including fire behaviour simulation, fire impact analysis, and fire management planning\. From her contact with nature, she perceives the close and complex interactions between various parts of ecosystems, including humans\. **Han Ling**

linghan0@mail\.ubc\.ca

Han Ling is a PhD student in the Faculty of Forestry at the University of British Columbia\. She pursued her master’s degree in Landscape Architecture at Peking University, and her undergraduate degree in the exceptional Zhang Zhidong program at Huazhong Agriculture University, China\. Han has been interested in endangered species conservation and landscape planning in protected areas\. Her master’s thesis at Peking University focused on the impacts of increased water temperature on the Chinese sturgeon \(*Acipenser sinensis*\) reproduction and potential locations for their spawning grounds\. Chinese sturgeon, a “national treasure” in the Yangtze River, is a relic of the dinosaur era and the subject of legends that she heard from her great\-grandmother\. After that, Han worked as a full\-time program assistant, participating in the spatial planning of giant panda \(*Ailuropoda melanoleuca*\) habitats in the Giant Panda National Park, China\. Currently, Han is working on the ecological resilience of grasslands to wildland fires, including fire impacts on aspen encroachment and wildlife distribution, and fire management planning in Canadian national parks\. She enjoys reading mythology books and jogging on weekends\.

**Dehui Geng**

dehui@mail\.ubc\.ca

Dehui Geng holds a PhD and is currently a postdoctoral researcher with the National Park Research Centre, with the focus on park, recreation, and tourism management\. Her research projects examine how large\-scale disruptions reshape human–nature interactions in national parks and the implications for adaptive tourism management\. Focusing on the Canadian Rocky Mountain national parks, her research integrates ground survey data and social media big data to investigate changes in visitor perceptions, behaviours, and spatial–temporal visitation patterns across the COVID\-19 pandemic timeline\. By combining traditional social science approaches with AI and big data analytics, including spatial analysis and Bayesian network modeling, the research analyzes how visitor demographics, travel characteristics, activity patterns, spatial and temporal visitation patterns, and satisfaction with park attributes interact within a complex tourism system\. The findings provide empirical insights into evolving visitor dynamics and highlight opportunities for evidence\-based and adaptive management strategies that balance visitor experiences with the long\-term sustainability of protected areas\.
