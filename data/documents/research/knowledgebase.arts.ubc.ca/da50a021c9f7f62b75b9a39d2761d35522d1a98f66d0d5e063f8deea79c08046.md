Unofficial Lakota Language Guide

## Language Name

Lakota\.

## Alternate Language Names

Lakȟótiyapi, Lakhota, Lakhóta, Teton, Teton Sioux\.

## Region

Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Ontario, Canada; Montana, Wyoming, Colorado, North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska, Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, Minnesota, and Iowa, United States\.

## Who

Steven YellowHand\.

## Others Involved

There is no information included in this resource about others involved\.

## Publishing Information

This is a digital dictionary that does not display any launch or update dates\. It was first published by the author on their personal website \(yellowhand\.tripod\.com\)\. It was then published and remains hosted by the WoLakota Project\. According to the information on the document properties of the PDF version hosted on the WoLakota Project’s website, the PDF was created in 2013\.

## Included Languages and Directionality

Lakota to English\.

## Dialects Included

No dialect is specified for this dictionary\.

## Type of Dictionary

This is a bilingual, mono\-directional word list\.

## How are Entries Organised

Entries are listed alphabetically by Lakota in two lists\. Entries in the first list contain the Lakota headword, the phonetic pronunciation of the Lakota word, and the English translation\. Entries in the second list contain the same information but in a different order, starting with the Lakota headword, then the English translation, and finally the phonetic spelling of the Lakota word\. No other information is given in any entries\. There is no apparent organization as to why the word lists are kept separate\. The second list is much more thorough than the first\.

## Other Notes

The WoLakota Project website that hosts two versions of the word list \(PDF and webpage\) was created to document the Oceti Sakouwin Essential Understandings \(OSEU\), which was created to give basic knowledge about the Oceti Sakouwin to school districts in South Dakota\. The website now functions as a resource for educators to build culturally\-informed curriculum\. The word list supplies a starting point for Lakota language learning to assist with cultural education goals\.
