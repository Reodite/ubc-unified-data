Western Gwich’in Topical Dictionary

## Language Name

Gwich’in\.

## Alternate Language Names

Gwitch’in, Kutchin, Kootchin, Loucheux, Loucheaux, Takudh, Tukudh\.

## Region

Northwest Territories, Yukon, Canada; Alaska, United States\.

## Who

Lillian James, Dick Mueller \(Compilers and editors\)\.

## Others Involved

Alaska Native Language Center, Summer Institute of Linguistics \(Heads of project\); James Kari \(Converted the dictionary to Word/Unicode\)\.

## Publishing Information

This dictionary has not been formally published, but the draft was completed March 1994\.

## How People are Cited

People are cited on the first page of the dictionary\.

## How Information is Cited

This resource does not cite the source of any of its information\.

## Where is Information Coming from

This resource does not indicate where information is coming from or what sources were used\.

## Tools and Framework used

This resource is available as a digital book\.

## Access

The digital book \(PDF scan\) is open access through the Alaska Native Language Archive\.

## Included Languages and Directionality

Gwich’in to English\.

## Dialects Included

This is a dictionary of the Western Gwich’in dialect\.

## Type of Dictionary

This is a bilingual, mono\-directional topical dictionary\.

## How are Entries Organised

Entries are organized topically\. Some of the main topics include: *Animals, Sewing and Beading,* and *Body Conditions, Sickness, Disease and Emotion*\. *Animals, Parts of the Body* and *Buildings* are the only three main topics that have sub\-categories, such as *Moose, Face and Head,* and *Furniture*\.

Some entries include more than one word as a translation and others have clarifications along with the English translations\.

This draft of the dictionary has some typed annotations like “???” and “Check\!”, which likely means that this rendition is not fully accurate or is missing some information on its entries\.  

The Alaska Native Language Archive includes three more files besides the draft of the dictionary\. One of them is the Word document version of the dictionary while the other two are typed notes and earlier versions\.
