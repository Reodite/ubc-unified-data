SENĆOŦEN Classified Word List

## Language Name

Northern Straits Salish\.

## Alternate Language Names

Saanich, SENĆOŦEN, Klallam\.

## Region

British Columbia, Canada; Washington, United States\.

## Who

Timothy Montler\.

## Others Involved

Elsie Claxton, Manson Pelkey, Earl Claxton, Chris Paul, Dave Elliott, Ivan Morris, Ray Sam, Anne Jimmy, Richard Harry, Lou Claxton, Lavina Charles \(Speakers\); Rebecca Gignac, Leslie Houston \(Compiling and Editing\); Elisha MENEŦIYE Elliott, Philip Kevin Paul, George David PENÁĆ Underwood, PENÁWEṈ Elliott, Madeline SIOLTENOT Bartleman, Jacqueline Jim \(Recording\); Janet Leonard \(Recording, Recording assistance, and Editing\); Randy Bouchard \(Recording, Editing\); Thom Hess \(Recording\); National Endowment for the Humanities, Jacobs Funds of the Whatcom Museum, University of North Texas \(Research funding\)\.

## Publishing Information

The web version of this wordlist was first published in 1999\. It was revised and expanded 2015\. It is published on Timothy Montler’s website\.

## How People are Cited

People are cited on the home page of the wordlist\.

## How Information is Cited

Information is cited on the home page of the wordlist\.

## Where is Information Coming from

Information in this dictionary comes from the 1991 dictionary *Saanich, North Straits Salish Classified Word List* and the 2018 dictionary *SENĆOŦEN: A Dictionary of the Saanich Language*, both compiled by Timothy Montler\.

## Tools and Framework used

This dictionary is a multimedia website which includes audio recordings\.

## Access

This dictionary is open access\.

## Included Languages and Directionality

English to Northern Straits Salish\.

## Dialects Included

This dictionary is of the Saanich \(SENĆOŦEN\) dialect\.

## Type of Dictionary

This is a bilingual, mono\-directional topical word list\.

## How are Entries Organised

Entries are organised topically and are numbered\. The topics are Numerals, Nature, Animals, Plants, Body, Spirit, Human Relations, Man\-made Things, Qualities, Pronominals/Demonstratives/Adverbials/etc, Events, and Place Names\. Each entry has three columns: the English headword, the SENĆOŦEN translation in the orthography developed by Dave Eliot Sr\., and the SENĆOŦEN word as written by Montler in his 1991 dictionary\.

The English and Saanich indexes, which are listed first, are organized alphabetically\.
