A Vocabulary of Susquehannock

## Language Name

Susquehannock\.

## Alternate Language Names

We are not aware of any alternative language names\.

## Region

New York, Pennsylvania, Maryland, and West Virginia, United States\.

## Who

Thomas Campanius Holm, Peter Stephen Du Ponceau, Claudio R\. Salvucci \(Authors\)\.

## Others Involved

Claudio R\. Salvucci \(Editor\); Evolution Publishing and Manufacturing \(Publisher\); The Long Now Foundation, PanLex Project \(Support\)\.

Besides these contributions, we are unable to access information about others who may have been involved in creating this dictionary\.

## Publishing Information

Published 1996 by Evolution Publishing and Manufacturing, Holland, Pennsylvania\. A second edition was published 2007, also by Evolution Publishing and Manufacturing\.

## Where is Information Coming from

The information in this dictionary comes from the original 1696 Swedish edition \(from New Sweden, now formally known as Pennsylvania\), and a translated version by Peter S\. Duponceau released in 1834\.

## Other Notes

Updates to the 2007 edition include additional data such as: a single word from George Alsop’s *A Character of the Province of Maryland* \(1666\), another single word from Benjamin Smith Barton’s *New Views of the Origin of the Tribes and Nations of America* \(1798\), which is allegedly from the Susquehannock remnant at Conestoga, and incidental statements from the Jesuits of Canada and Moravian Bishop Commerhoff which are the only evidence of the Iroquoian languages of Erie and Scahento, spoken in Northern Pennsylvania\.
