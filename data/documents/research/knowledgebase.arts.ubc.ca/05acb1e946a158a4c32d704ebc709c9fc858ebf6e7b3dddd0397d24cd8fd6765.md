Eastern Swampy &amp; Moose Cree Dictionary

## Language Name

Cree\-Montagnais\-Naskapi\.

## Alternate Language Names

Cree, Naskapi, Montagnais, ᓀᐦᐃᔭᐍᐏᐣ, Nēhiyawēwin\.

## Region

Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Northwest Territories, Canada\.

## Who

## Publishing Information

This dictionary was published in 2018 on atlas\-ling\.ca with the Algonquian Languages Project\. Previously, it was hosted on a website titled *Spoken Cree*, published digitally in 2012\. The *Spoken Cree* website is still up, but does not appear to be actively updated as of mid\-2024\.

## How People are Cited

Speakers and contributors are cited under the *Credits* tab\.

## How Information is Cited

Ellis’ previous works, *Spoken Cree* \(2000\) and *âtalôhkâna nêsta tipâpcimôwina, Cree Legends and Narratives from the West Coast of James Bay* \(1995\), are cited in entries\. Speakers are cited under *Credits\.*

## Where is Information Coming from

This dictionary is compiled from Ellis’ previous works with speakers which were previously published in the above 2000 and 1995 references\.

## Tools and Framework used

This is a web\-hosted dictionary\.

## Access

This dictionary is open access online\.

## Included Languages and Directionality

Cree to English; English to Cree\. It can be searched in English or Cree\.

## Dialects Included

This dictionary includes forms from Moose and Swampy Cree\. Each entry provides the form in both dialects, unless a word is specific to one dialect\.

## Type of Dictionary

This is a bilingual, bidirectional online dictionary\.

## How are Entries Organised

This dictionary can be searched by Cree headword or English definition, or browsed by either Cree headword or part of speech \(under *Browse*\)\. Each entry is listed by the Cree word in Roman script, followed by the syllabic spelling\(s\), the part of speech, and comparative forms\. Clicking on the entry opens a drop\-down menu that displays the stem and the source of the entry in Ellis’s previous work, as well as any example phrases that are known\. When browsed under *Part of Speech*, each entry is formatted the same as above, grouped by parts of speech \(eg: noun animate\)\.
