### Primary Outcome Paper

Peters S, Hung SH, Bayley MT, Best KL, Connell LA, Donkers SJ, Dukelow SP, Ezeugwu VE, Milot MH, Sakakibara BM, Sheehy L, Wong H, Yang Y, Yao J, Eng JJ\. **Safety and effectiveness of the Walk ‘n Watch structured, progressive exercise protocol delivered by physical therapists for inpatient stroke rehabilitation in Canada: a phase 3, multisite, pragmatic, stepped\-wedge, cluster\-randomised controlled trial\.** Lancet Neurology\. 2025:24:643\-655\. [pdf](https://med-fom-neurorehab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/07/Primary-Outcome-Lancet-Neurology-for-repository.pdf)  [Supplemental](https://med-fom-neurorehab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/07/Primary-Outcome-Supplemental-for-repository.pdf)

*This paper reports the primary outcomes of the Walk ‘n Watch trial across 12 Canadian inpatient stroke rehabilitation sites, demonstrating that Walk ‘n Watch significantly improved walking endurance, balance, and quality of life compared to usual care, with no serious adverse events\.*

### **Protocol Paper**

Peters S, Lim SB, Bayley MT, Best K, Connell LA, Corriveau H, Donkers SJ, Dukelow SP, Klassen TD, Milot MH, Sakakibara BM, Sheehy L, Wong H, Yao J, Eng JJ\. **Implementation of increased physical therapy intensity for improving walking after stroke: Walk ‘n Watch protocol for a multisite stepped\-wedge cluster\-randomized controlled trial\.** International Journal of Stroke\. 2023;18\(1\):117\-122\. doi: 10\.1177/17474930221129982\. Epub 2022 Oct 28\. PMID: 36129364\. \[[pdf](https://med-fom-neurorehab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Peters-et-al_2023.pdf)\] \[[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36129364/)\]

*Describes the rationale, design, and methodology of the Walk ‘n Watch implementation trial, including the stepped\-wedge cluster\-randomized design across 12 Canadian inpatient stroke rehabilitation sites\.*

### **Secondary Outcome &amp; Implementation Papers**

-   Yan Y, Eng JJ, Hung SH, Bayley MT, Best KL, Connell LA, Donkers SJ, Dukelow SP, Ezeugwu VE, Milot MH, Sakakibara BM, Sheehy L, Wong H, Yao J, Peters S\. **Aerobic minutes and step number remain low in inpatient stroke rehabilitation\.** PLoS One\. 2025 Jul 28;20\(7\):e0328930\. doi: 10\.1371/journal\.pone\.0328930\. PMID: 40720450\. \[[pdf](https://med-fom-neurorehab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Yan-et-al_2025.pdf)\] \[[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40720450/)\]  
    *An analysis of usual care physical therapy sessions showing that despite modest increases in step counts, aerobic intensity minutes remain critically low compared to clinical guideline recommendations across Canadian inpatient stroke rehabilitation units\.*

-   Hung SH, Ackerley S, Connell LA, Bayley MT, Best KL, Donkers SJ, Dukelow SP, Ezeugwu VE, Milot MH, Peters S, Sakakibara BM, Sheehy L, Yao J, Eng JJ\. **Real\-world experiences of therapy staff implementing an intensive rehabilitation protocol in Canadian stroke inpatient rehabilitation settings: a multi\-site survey study\.** Phys Ther\. 2025 Oct 1;105\(10\):pzaf111\. doi: 10\.1093/ptj/pzaf111\. PMID: 41139309\. \[[pdf](https://med-fom-neurorehab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Hung-et-al_2025.pdf)\] \[[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41139309/)\]  
    *A survey of 47 therapy staff delivering the Walk ‘n Watch protocol found that while most successfully implemented the intervention, key barriers included insufficient therapy time and device inaccuracies, with the majority supporting future use of the protocol\.*

-   Ezeugwu VE, Peters S, Hung SH, Bayley MT, Best KL, Connell LA, Donkers SJ, Dukelow SP, Milot MH, Sakakibara BM, Sheehy L, Wong H, Yang Y, Yao J, Eng JJ\. **Predictors of favorable modified Rankin Scale outcome during inpatient rehabilitation for subacute stroke: a secondary analysis of the Walk ‘n Watch trial\.** Disabil Rehabil\. 2026 Apr 24:1\-14\. doi: 10\.1080/09638288\.2026\.2661476\. Epub ahead of print\. PMID: 42028977\. \[[pdf](https://med-fom-neurorehab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/06/Accepted-mRS-paper_Disability-and-Rehabilitation.pdf)\] \[[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42028977/)\]  
    *A secondary analysis finding that admission characteristics associated with favourable functional outcomes differed between usual care and Walk ‘n Watch groups, with higher cognition linked to better outcomes in the Walk ‘n Watch group\.*

-   Ackerley S, Hung SH, Sheehy L, Donkers SJ, Timofeeva P, Best KL, Peters S, Park SS, Ouellet B, Ezeugwu VE, Milot MH, Sakakibara BM, Eng JJ, Connell LA\. **Exploring factors influencing implementation across the explanatory\-to\-pragmatic trial continuum: a sequential qualitative integration of delivering higher\-intensity walking exercise within inpatient stroke rehabilitation\.** Implement Sci Commun\. 2026 Jan 8;7\(1\):44\. doi: 10\.1186/s43058\-025\-00812\-y\. PMID: 41508158\. \[[pdf](https://med-fom-neurorehab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/05/Ackerley-et-al_2026.pdf)\] \[[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41508158/)\]  
    *A qualitative study comparing therapist and manager perspectives across two trials, identifying key implementation strategies and practical actions to support the adoption of higher\-intensity walking exercise in real\-world inpatient stroke rehabilitation\.*
