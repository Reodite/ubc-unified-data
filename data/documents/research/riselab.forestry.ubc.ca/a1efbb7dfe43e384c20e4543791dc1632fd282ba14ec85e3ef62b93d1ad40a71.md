**Timeline:** May 2026 – May 2026

**Committee:**

[Image](https://riselab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/09/IMG_0587-1024x683.jpeg)

As cities grow, we face a challenge: how can we make room for more people while still supporting urban wildlife? This project explores land sparing versus land sharing**:** whether wildlife benefits more from larger greenspaces or from smaller patches of habitat spread throughout the city\. We look at how birds and bats use public and private greenspaces across different urban densities in Metro Vancouver, and how vegetation and the surrounding landscape shape the species we find\.

We select sites across different housing densities and greenspace types and use autonomous recording units to listen for wildlife\. The recorders capture bird sounds around sunrise and ultrasonic bat calls around sunset\. We use automated tools to identify species in the recordings and compare bird and bat diversity with features such as plant diversity, vegetation structure, and nearby greenspace\.

The project is part of a larger collaboration exploring how cities like Vancouver, Montreal, and Quebec City can support biodiversity while accommodating housing and development\. By looking beyond large public parks to the smaller greenspaces woven through neighbourhoods, we aim to better understand how urban landscapes can be both spared and shared\.
