Project Snapshot:

On December 17th 2010, a Tunisian fruit vendor name Mohamed Bouazizi took a can of gasoline and set himself on fire in front of the local governor’s office\.  Bouaziz’s actions resulted from having his fruit cart confiscated by local police and his frustration at not obtaining an audience with the local governor\.  Similar examples of extreme actions can be found in other countries\.  With no other means of voicing discontent and lack of trust, citizens can embrace extreme forms of protest against institutions and governments that quickly escalate\.

This exploratory research project, aims to gain new insights into the relationship among citizens’ sentiment about governance institutions, trust in Government, and civil unrest\.  The approach will be to conduct sentiment analysis of social media over a one year period in one country \(e\.g\., Brazil\) looking at specific institutions \(e\.g\., transport, police, health\) to gain insight into how citizens are feeling about their governance institutions, how that translates into feelings about their Governments in the political sense, and how that corresponds to observed citizen behaviour\.

Project Snapshot:  Global LEI Watch is a research project aims to increase global financial transparency\. The Project integrates Legal Entity Identifier \(LEI\) data from LEI registration authorities – called Local Operating Units \(LOUs\) – in several different countries around the world and renders the data as interactive visualizations that can be explored “to detect the expected and discover the unexpected\.”

LEIs are designed to be a single, universal standard identifier for any organization or firm involved in a financial transaction internationally\. Tracking the different entities involved in financial transactions is of crucial importance in determining where the money flows\. Without understanding where the money flows, it is impossible to understand the financial system at a level to help prevent corruption and future financial crises\.

\(3\) Visual Analytics for Financial Systemic Risk

Dr\. Victoria Lemieux, Associate Professor, iSchool, UBC and Senior Public Sector Specialist, World Bank; Professor William Wong, Professor and Head of the Interaction Design Centre, Middlesex, University  and Payam S\. Rahmdel, Post Doctoral Fellow, MAGIC\.

The objective of this project is to design a Visual Systemic ‘Risk Map’ as one possible prototype to address some of the issues of risk identification and analysis in the context of global financial systems\.  We propose the design concept of the ‘Risk Map’ using principles of Cognitive Systems Engineering, which is suited to engineering for complex systems\.  Cognitive systems engineering addresses the issue of analysing and designing process control systems from a human factors perspective\. The Risk Map will show constraints and boundaries of the system and represent areas of high risk \(scenarios to be avoided\), and low risk \(but possibly unprofitable\), and places where it is profitable and safe \(e\.g\. enough liquidity to stabilize minor disruptions\)\. These areas will respond to changes in the market and behaviours, which are discussed in Brammertz\.  Work on the design will be carried out in consultation with Dr\. Mark Flood, US Treasury, Office of Financial Research\.

\(1\) Dr\. Victoria Lemieux, Associate Professor, iSchool, UBC and Senior Public Sector Specialist, World Bank

Project Snapshot: The ArchivIT Project focuses on how to exploit the value in this unstructured digital data by means of classification and organization using visual analytics, which is the science of analytic reasoning facilitated by interactive visual interfaces that seeks to leverage the strengths of both human and machine intelligence\.

As a first step, we undertook a study to understand the tasks and data of users in the target domain\. This is critical for the effective application of VA to any domain of interest and requires close collaboration between VA system designers and users in the domain of interest\.  From this study we learned, among other things, that archivists rely on spatial attributes of documents in the process of category learning\.

To take advantage of this classification strategy, our ArchivIT application is able to mine spatial attributes of \.pdf documents and scanned images of documents to create document categories\.  Our application permits the archivist to interact with the system to adjust the weight of specific spatial attributes within three regions of the documents to tune an initial unsupervised clustering of a sample of documents in a corpus\.
