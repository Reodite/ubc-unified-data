**ReROW Student Symposium 2026** 

The **StreetSmarts PhD Collaboratory** is a PhD Collab initiative supported by the **Rethinking the Right\-of\-Way \(ReROW\)** research cluster\. The project brings together faculty, PhD students, and civic partners to advance interdisciplinary research and knowledge mobilization focused on streets and the right\-of\-way\.

The Collaboratory supports doctoral research that examines how different disciplines and practice communities understand, study, and act on the right\-of\-way, with the goal of strengthening collaboration between academic research and real\-world decision\-making\.

### **Focus and approach**

The StreetSmarts PhD Collaboratory uses a transdisciplinary, action\-research approach to explore how knowledge about streets and the right\-of\-way is produced, integrated, and applied\. The work focuses on:

-   Understanding how goals for street transformation are defined across disciplines and sectors
-   Examining the assumptions, evidence, and methods used in academic and practitioner settings
-   Developing shared conceptual models and language to support collaboration and knowledge integration

### **Objectives**

The project has two core objectives:

1.  To develop an integrative and iterative framework for connecting and integrating knowledge across disciplines, anchored by the right\-of\-way as a shared focus\.
2.  To study how transdisciplinary knowledge integration and mobilization occurs in theory, method, and practice through collaborative workshops and PhD\-led case studies\.

### **Participants**

The StreetSmarts PhD Collaboratory brings together ReROW faculty across planning, civil engineering, forestry, and related fields, along with PhD students and civic partners from regional and municipal organizations\. The Collaboratory is designed to support doctoral research while also generating insights relevant to practitioners working on street and right\-of\-way challenges\.

### **Planned activities**

-   **Year 1:** A series of facilitated workshops to develop shared conceptual models of the right\-of\-way and a common glossary of key terms across disciplines and partner organizations\.
-   **Years 2–3:** PhD students develop and test right\-of\-way case studies connected to their dissertation research, with ongoing validation and evaluation in both academic and practitioner contexts\.

### **Timeline**

-   **Funding period:** Beginning January 2026
-   **Project duration:** 2026–2029
