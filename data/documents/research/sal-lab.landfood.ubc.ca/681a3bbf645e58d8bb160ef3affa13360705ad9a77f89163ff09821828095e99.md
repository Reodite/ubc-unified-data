### Overview

The purpose of this project is to help the BC Ministry of Agriculture and Food \(AF\) tailor programming and policies to increase uptake of evidence\-based Beneficial Management Practices \(BMPs\) to reduce greenhouse gas \(GHG\) emissions, increase carbon sequestration, and improve reporting on mitigation outcomes\. Outputs of this project will provide foundational information and recommendations for a cross\-sectoral subset of BMPs with the greatest potential to improve environmental sustainability, farm resiliency, and production, alongside net GHG mitigation outcomes\. This research will help AF and the agriculture sector prioritize and adopt these BMPs\.

### Research objectives

-   Increase understanding of the GHG and co\-benefit performance of climate change mitigation BMPs in the BC context, including refined emission factors and activity data datasets\.
-   Identify research, adoption support, and policy priorities for increasing adoption of climate change mitigation BMPs\.

### Approach

The UBC team worked in collaboration with the BC Ministry of Agriculture and Food to develop a literature review, a database, and a set of complementary reports\. In brief, the team:

-   Started with 183 potential practices and used a structured review process, developed with the Ministry and industry experts, to identify the most promising options for BC agriculture\.
-   Screened and refined the list based on scientific evidence, relevance to BC farming systems, practicality for implementation by 2030, and likelihood of producer adoption\.
-   Selected 30 priority Beneficial Management Practices with the greatest potential to reduce agricultural greenhouse gas emissions while also providing environmental and farm\-management co\-benefits\.
-   Conducted extensive literature reviews and compiled the available scientific evidence into an open\-access database and bibliography to support transparent decision\-making\.
-   Evaluated GHG reduction potential for each BMP using published research, Canadian greenhouse gas accounting methods, agricultural statistics, and expert knowledge\.
-   Estimated adoption opportunities across BC by considering biological, environmental, and operational constraints, as well as current levels of BMP adoption\.
-   Assessed multiple future adoption scenarios to understand how different levels of BMP uptake could contribute to provincial climate targets by 2030\.
-   Created a comprehensive BMP database that summarizes GHG benefits, co\-benefits, adoption potential, and key research gaps to support policy, industry, and farm\-level decision\-making\.

The prioritized BMPs varied widely in their estimated mitigation potential, level of uncertainty, and ability to provide additional co\-benefits for farm resilience\.

### Project outputs

**Project outputs:** This project produced two publicly available reports, an open\-access BMP database, and a supporting bibliography to help guide climate mitigation policy and practice in BC agriculture\.

#### Reports: *30 Agricultural Beneficial Management Practices for Climate Mitigation in British Columbia*

Together, these reports provide a comprehensive assessment of 30 priority Beneficial Management Practices \(BMPs\) for climate mitigation in British Columbia, including estimates of GHG reduction potential, co\-benefits, implementation challenges, adoption potential, uncertainty, and key research gaps\.

#### [Report 1: Assessment of Benefit Potential and Research Gaps](https://open.library.ubc.ca/soa/cIRcle/collections/facultyresearchandpublications/52383/items/1.0452583)

The first of two reports was designed to help the Ministry tailor programming and policies to increase uptake of evidence\-based BMPs\. The report identifies 30 BMPs with a high likelihood of GHG benefits for BC agriculture, compiles emission reduction factors, activity data, and co\-benefit information, and synthesizes key data gaps and recommendations for improving data quality and management\.

The analysis found that, at a 50% level of adoption by 2030, 19 stackable BMPs could reduce agricultural emissions by approximately **523 ± 172 kt CO\[subscript: 2\]e per year**\. At 25% and 10% levels of adoption, the same 19 BMPs could provide reductions of approximately **262 ± 86** and **105 ± 34 kt CO\[subscript: 2\]e per year**, respectively\.

When focusing on stackable BMPs with lower levels of associated uncertainty, the GHG emission reduction potential of 11 BMPs adds up to approximately **240 ± 65 kt CO\[subscript: 2\]e per year** at a 50% level of adoption\.

#### [Report 2: Benefits, Uncertainty, and Challenges](https://open.library.ubc.ca/soa/cIRcle/collections/facultyresearchandpublications/52383/items/1.0452582)

The second report provides detailed summaries of BMPs that can reduce GHG emissions and/or sequester carbon while enhancing farm resilience, profitability, and environmental sustainability\. Each BMP summary includes a description of the practice, its GHG mitigation mechanisms, likely conditions for success, estimates of current and future adoption potential, co\-benefits, implementation challenges, cost considerations, knowledge gaps, and uncertainty\.

### Project significance

This project supports more informed decision\-making for agricultural climate mitigation in British Columbia\. By bringing together scientific evidence, provincial agricultural data, and expert knowledge, the work helps identify BMPs that are most likely to provide meaningful GHG reductions while also supporting farm resilience, productivity, and environmental sustainability\.
