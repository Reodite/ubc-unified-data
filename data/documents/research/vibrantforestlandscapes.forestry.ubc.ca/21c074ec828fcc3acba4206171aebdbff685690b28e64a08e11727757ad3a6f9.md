The Kunming\-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework \(KM\-GBF\), adopted in December 2023, aims to protect 30% of the planet’s oceans, lands, and freshwater by 2030, representing a significant commitment to conservation\. It aligns with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals \(SDG\) and includes innovations such as expanding protected areas and introducing “Other Effective area\-based Conservation Measures” \(OECM\)\. However, the initiative has attracted controversy over Indigenous rights issues, especially where more forest lands are given back to traditional owners and Indigenous People &amp; Local Communities \(IPLC\) in many countries\.

Canada and Indonesia have been quite instrumental in handing back more forests to IPLCs and researching the best forest management system that could balance the goal of biodiversity conservation and thriving economic development\. Both countries based their economy on natural resources extraction \(logging, mining, liquefied natural gas/LNG etc\)\. Economic growth has to be balanced with biodiversity conservation to reach this idea of “healthy planet and healthy people”\.

OECMs could empower IPLCs in biodiversity stewardship, but their success depends on effective governance changes at national and local levels\. The proposed action research will look at optimal governance structures for OECMs in Indonesia and Canada, recognizing that solutions must be context\-specific\. The project will utilize “learning landscapes,” “transdisciplinary science,” and a “Landscape\-Seascape Approach” to understand and achieve balanced land stewardship with close engagement of local communities and other partners and collaborators\.
