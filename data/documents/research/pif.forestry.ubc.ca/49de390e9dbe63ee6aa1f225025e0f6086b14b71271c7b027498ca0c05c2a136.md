The Lil’wat Medicine Shed/Cultural Re\-Connection classroom is an Indigenous community\-led initiative working to bridge research around Indigenous health and land stewardship\.

This research came out of a partnership between Lil’wat Nation and the Peoples, Institutions and Forests lab that first started in 2015\. This research partnership supports Lil’wat Nation’s goals around land stewardship and cultural revitalization—bringing life and celebrating pride for Lil’wat community members’ connections and care for their territory\.

## The specific objectives of the Lil’wat Medicine Shed are to:

-   Support Indigenous\-led research related to gender and wellness;
-   Encourage knowledge creation that will raise awareness and improve understanding of Indigenous concepts of gender in a strengths\-based, wellness context;
-   Facilitate knowledge sharing as to how Indigenous concepts of gender can affect wellness, in a manner that is directly relevant to and for the benefit of Indigenous communities; and
-   Support the implementation of action\-oriented, community\-based projects that will improve wellness among Indigenous communities

## Some questions that this research looks at:

-   What role does traditional medicine play for the health and healing of Lil’wat First Nation members?
-   What role does gender play for community member wellness?
-   Why is this significant considering scientific/health literature?
-   How can service delivery of gender and traditional medicines be improved within the Lil’wat community?
-   What indicators are relevant?

For the Lil’wat Nation, traditional medicines are integral to individual and collective community health\. Traditional medicines prepared from plants of the territory provide culturally appropriate healing remedies and preventative medicines\. Some traditional medicines have a gendered component, as they are used at certain times of a persons’ development, such as coming of age \(puberty\) ceremonies for men and women and prenatal/postnatal care\. Yet, there is also a collective understanding that the gender binary of male/female have been imposed as a legacy of colonialism\. Therefore, there is a need to strengthen traditional medicine knowledge in a way that affirms the non\-binary gender of community members\.

This initiative aims to set the foundation for a lasting institution and supportive allyship network in the Lil’wat Nation that is centered on the goal of gathering and reinstating Lil’wat cultural knowledge of gender and sexual orientation and traditional medicines\. We are working to create a community hub– the Lil’wat Cultural Re\-Connection Classroom– that is a queer\-positive and gender\-inclusive space for hosting workshops, ceremonies, hands\-on practice, learning opportunities and storytelling events related to traditional healing and medicines\. This will be a community space to create what they see as the necessary steps, activities and resources for ensuring the transmission and enhancement of collective community knowledge around traditional medicines and gender\.

[Image](https://pif.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/03/20180511_133618-web-3.jpg)

Guided by the Lil’wat Research Protocol, the S7i’stken, all research activities are overseen by the Lil’wat Culture, Heritage and Language Authority, who provides the necessary guidance and ethical permission to do research with the Lil’wat Nation\.

[Image](https://pif.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2025/03/hide-2.jpg)

## As of early 2025, we have completed:

-   12 Community\-led workshops on the land, as a land\-based learning intervention rooted in cultural pedagogies and values
-   12 harvesting trips among research team and community co\-researchers to gather and share knowledge of Lil’wat plant medicines
-   8 planning group discussion with community members who had supporting roles in research\.
-   3 conference presentations
-   Publication of the Lil’wat Companion Book: a community\-requested publication with educational information about Lil’wat foods and medicines
-   Drafts of 2 other community outputs, expected for publication in 2025
