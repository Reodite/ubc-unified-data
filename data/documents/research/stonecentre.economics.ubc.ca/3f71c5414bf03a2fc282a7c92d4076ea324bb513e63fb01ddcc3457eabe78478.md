**ABOUT**

---

In 2018, the UN Office on Drugs and Crime declared that the home is the most dangerous place for women\. Most women who are victims of homicide are killed by an intimate partner or family member; and most women who are raped, are raped by someone they know rather than by a stranger\. Yet, strikingly, both scholars and the general public tend to view stranger rape as the paradigm example of rape\. Martial rape remains an understudied phenomena in ethics, political philosophy, and legal theory\. The project, conducted by Kimberley Brownlee and Ritu Sharma, will use the methods typical of analytic philosophy to show that marital rape differs meaningfully from stranger rape in light of the distinctive economic, social, and legal conditions within which a spouse perpetrates it — all within the secluded space of the home where a person shelters at her most vulnerable\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

This research will examine how the legal, social, and economic entanglements characteristic of marriage should inform both the conceptualization of marital rape and the analysis of its severity\. Drawing on feminist political philosophy, legal theory, and empirical studies of intimate partner violence, the project will also explore how economic subordination within marriage contributes to patterns of intergenerational, gendered disadvantage and wealth concentration\. Finally, the project will consider the merits of potential legal reforms and policy reforms, including changes to marital property law, enhanced welfare protections, and public education campaigns aimed at dismantling cultural myths about consent and male entitlement in marriage\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

In highlighting the role that wealth inequality plays in the context of marriage and marital rape, the project underscores from a new angle what feminists have long observed, that the home can be a critical site of injustice that both reflects and reinforces broader systems of gendered disadvantage\. While the project assesses the exacerbating effect that spousal wealth inequality has on persons’ experiences of marital rape it also argues that financial inequality is not a mere background condition but a key factor in the incidence and recurrence of sexual violence within marriage\. While marital rape is typically framed as a legal or moral violation, this project expands that framing by showing how it is also often an economic injustice\.
