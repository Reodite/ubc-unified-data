**ABOUT**

---

This research investigates how overwork— an increasing and defining feature of contemporary labour markets—contributes to changing gendered disparities in pay\. Men are more likely than women to work long hours, often due to gendered caregiving responsibilities at home\. Rising rewards to overwork may, in turn, disproportionately benefit men and deepen gender income inequality\. The research will produce critical new knowledge to help identify setbacks and advances on the path toward gender income equality\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

Previous research shows that men are more likely than women to work long hours, and that this gender difference, combined with rising wage returns to overwork, has widened the gender wage gap\. Using three decades of nationally representative data from Canada’s Labour Force Survey, this project will extend existing work to a Canadian context and examine whether overwork contributes to persistent gender pay disparities and slows broader progress toward gender equality\. While several patterns of labour market inequality documented in the U\.S\. are also observed in Canada, it remains unclear whether the same mechanisms apply\. In both countries, men are more likely than women to work long hours, and the narrowing of the gender wage gap has slowed in recent decades\. However, unlike the U\.S\., the prevalence of long hours has declined in Canada since the late 1990s, offering a potential contrast in how overwork shapes inequality\. 

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This project aligns with the Stone Centre’s mission of advancing research on income and wealth inequality by addressing a structural driver of persistent gender pay gaps: overwork\. Income inequality cannot be fully understood without recognizing gendered disparities in how work and caregiving are distributed\. By examining whether and how rising rewards to long hours of work disproportionately benefit men, this research sheds light on a mechanism that sustains inequality across households and over time\. Findings from this research will inform equitable labour policies and effective workplace reforms to help close the gender wage gap in Canada\.
