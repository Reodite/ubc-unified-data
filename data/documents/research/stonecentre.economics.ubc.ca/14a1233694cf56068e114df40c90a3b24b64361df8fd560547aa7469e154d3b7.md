**ABOUT**

---

Despite being illegal for over 50 years, caste\-based untouchability practices remain widespread in India, limiting access to basic economic activities and public services for over 200 million people\. This project studies the persistence and transformation of these discriminatory norms by combining historical survey data, contemporary field surveys, observational and lab\-in\-the\-field measures, and village\-level records of untouchability\-related violence\. By linking these sources to individual economic data, the research aims to understand how social and economic policies influence discriminatory practices and their impact on inequality\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

The project is expected to provide a comprehensive understanding of how caste\-based discriminatory norms have persisted and transformed over the past fifty years\. By linking historical and contemporary measures of untouchability to household\-level economic outcomes, it will shed light on the ways discrimination shapes wealth and income inequality\. The research will also identify the economic, political, and social factors that reduce discriminatory practices, offering evidence on which policies and interventions are effective\. Finally, the creation of a publicly available dataset will serve as a resource for scholars, journalists, and policymakers studying discrimination and inequality\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This project examines the links between discrimination and economic inequality\. By studying how caste\-based untouchability shapes wealth, income, and social outcomes—and how improvements in education and economic opportunities for lower\-caste groups influence discriminatory norms—the research addresses fundamental questions about inequality and social mobility\. The findings will provide evidence on the conditions under which discrimination can be reduced, offering insights relevant for policy interventions and broader debates on social and economic justice\.
