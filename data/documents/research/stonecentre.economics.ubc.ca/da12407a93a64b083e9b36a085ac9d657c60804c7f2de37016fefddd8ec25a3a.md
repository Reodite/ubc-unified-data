**ABOUT**

---

The academic and policy debate on Artificial Intelligence \(AI\) centers on a stark trade\-off: while AI can raise productivity and innovation, it may also displace workers, including those in high\-skilled occupations\. This paper investigates whether generative AI \(GenAI\) offers a countervailing force by reducing barriers to entrepreneurship and shifting bargaining power in labor and capital markets\. By automating creative and technical tasks and delivering capabilities through pay\-per\-use APIs, cloud services, and open\-source models, it reduces the need for employees, consultants, or large infrastructure\. Startups can therefore launch and scale with fewer workers and less upfront financing\. By lowering the costs of creative and technical work, GenAI allows founders with limited resources or specialized skills to enter markets that were once inaccessible\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

It is expected that exposure to GenAI facilitates entrepreneurial entry, particularly among individuals previously constrained by capital or skill barriers\. As automation and scalable AI tools reduce startup costs, new founders will be able to build viable firms with smaller teams and delayed reliance on external financing\. This democratization of entrepreneurship could lead to greater business dynamism and evenly distributed opportunities across sectors\. In turn, shifts in market structure are also anticipated: reduced industrial concentration, stronger outside options for workers, and a redistribution of bargaining power away from large incumbents and dominant investors\. These outcomes will help inform ongoing debates about whether AI ultimately deepens inequality or serves as a technological equalizer in modern economies\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This project evaluates whether GenAI can reduce market power, expand opportunity, and promote a fairer distribution of income and wealth, forces that have driven rising inequality over recent decades\. By lowering financial and skill\-related barriers to entrepreneurship, GenAI may democratize firm creation, challenging the dominance of “superstar firms” whose monopsony power has suppressed wages and contributed to declining labor shares\. At the same time, if employees can more easily transition to entrepreneurship, their outside options improve, reducing incumbents’ ability to set wages unilaterally\. Finally, by changing financing dynamics, this could reduce wealth concentration among capital providers and allow founders, particularly those from underrepresented groups or with limited financial resources, to retain greater ownership stakes\.
