To kick off the 2026–27 Stone Inequality Seminar Series, the Stone Centre is excited to welcome Juan Carlos Suarez Serrato, who will be presenting his paper titled, “Regulation Meets Public Infrastructure: Evidence from Environmental Water Policy in China\.”

**Abstract**

Improving water quality is costly and can often hinder industrial production\. The tradeoff between pollution control and manufacturing output is particularly salient in rapidly developing economies such as China and India\. We study whether and how public infrastructure investment can mitigate the economic costs of stricter environmental regulation\.

We study the South\-to\-North Water Transfer Project—one of China’s largest environmental water initiatives—as a quasi\-experiment\. The project significantly restricted pollution output by regulated firms, which incentivized firms to collaborate in funding the development of wastewater treatment plants\. By combining regulation and with added infrastructure, the project substantially reduced manufacturing pollution while causing only modest declines in sales\.  

We develop an industry\-equilibrium model linking environmental regulation, firm abatement decisions, and local infrastructure development to quantify the associated costs and benefits\. The model replicates our reduced\-form estimates closely and highlights the importance of coordinated infrastructure in improving the cost\-effectiveness of regulation\. The construction and pipeline costs of local wastewater treatment plants are significantly smaller than the profits preserved through improved abatement efficiency

**Juan Carlos Suárez Serrato** is The Edward P\. Rust Professor of Economics at Stanford Graduate School of Business, a Senior Fellow at the Stanford Institute for Economic Policy Research, and a Faculty Associate at the National Bureau of Economic Research\. He is also the José E\. Feliciano and Kwanza Jones SUPERCHARGED Initiative Faculty Fellow at Stanford GSB\.

His research studies how taxes, regulation, and government spending impact economic growth and welfare\. His recent projects have analyzed the effects of corporate tax policy on firm investment, evasion, and productivity growth; how capital investment impacts employment and workers’ wages; how multinational corporations respond to international tax rules and regulations; how to best design property taxes in developing countries; and how multi\-unit firms respond to energy and environmental regulations in the US and China\.

He was previously a professor of economics at Duke University and a postdoctoral fellow at the Stanford Institute for Economic Policy Research\. He received a PhD in economics from UC Berkeley and a BA in economics and mathematics from Trinity University; and he is a proud graduate of the AEA Summer Training Program in Economics\.
