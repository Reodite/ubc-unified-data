**ABOUT**

---

The project aims to understand the increasing income inequality in the U\.S\. economy by focusing on two of its characterizing features: Almost all of the increase happens at the top half of the income distribution and the increase gains pace at around 1980\. The average weekly wage of full\-time employed males remained steady across income quantiles until 1980\. While inequality below the median remained stable, inequality above the median rose considerably during the 1980s and 1990s\. The paper introduces a new explanatory mechanism for both patterns\. The rising utilization of high\-skilled labor through the growing prevalence of domestic labor outsourcing\. This process enabled firms to access specialized skills more flexibly, reshaping how labor was allocated across the economy\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

The growing use of labor outsourcing technologies can amplify or dampen existing differences in worker skills and their corresponding returns\. This process allows individuals to provide infrequently\-needed services to multiple firms\. At the same time, it allows new and smaller firms to avoid the fixed costs of hiring permanent employees or establishing in\-house departments, thereby reducing uncertainty and improving operational flexibility\. Together, these mechanisms capture how the rise of labor outsourcing can both influence income inequality and alter the composition of entrepreneurial activity in the economy\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This paper will help shed light on puzzling patterns of increasing income inequality in the U\.S\. Due to data limitations, existing research on outsourcing has either focused on worker outcomes using worker\-level datasets or has been limited to particular tasks that were outsourced\. Therefore, the discussion around labour outsourcing has been limited to its implications for outsourced workers’ wages and job security\. This research will combine the effects of labor outsourcing on the entire income distribution that includes low\-skilled and high\-skilled employees as well as entrepreneurs\. Hence, it allows the evaluation of policies that encourage and discourage outsourcing with regard to how they affect income inequality\.
