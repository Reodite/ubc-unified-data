**ABOUT**

---

For both Indigenous and Black populations in Canada, settler colonialism has had a historical and ongoing imprint on their lives and economic well\-being\. Settler colonialism and systemic racism facilitate structurally racist policies and practices, contributing to disparate wealth outcomes and racialized life experiences\. This research will investigate settler colonial governance in Canada that continues to perpetuate historic and ongoing dispossessions, shaping wealth inequality among Indigenous and Black populations\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

Despite the conceptual and theoretical understanding of the relationship between settler colonization and economic outcomes, there is a notable lack of measures that accurately capture the multifaceted lived experiences of settler colonialism\. Using community\-based approaches with Indigenous and Black communities and academic scholars, the project will develop survey measures that will be used to understand the historical and on\-going impact of settler colonialism on their lives and life experiences\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

Indigenous and Black populations in Canada experience wealth inequality with higher rates of poverty, lower median incomes, and lower likelihood of homeownership\. Within the current Canadian economic structure, land ownership, often through home ownership, is a main source of wealth for individuals and their families\. The project will center the role of settler colonialism and its major mechanism of land dispossession and displacement\.
