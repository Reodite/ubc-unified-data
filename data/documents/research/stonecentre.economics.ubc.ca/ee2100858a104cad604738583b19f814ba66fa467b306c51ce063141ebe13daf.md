**ABOUT**

---

This study explores how lowering tariffs through free trade agreements shapes businesses and workers\. It looks at how changes in trade policy can influence companies ’productivity, the types of jobs they offer, and differences in pay across employees\. By comparing firms that were more directly affected by new trade agreements with those that were less affected, the research aims to better understand how opening markets can create opportunities while also reshaping workplaces and earnings\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

Previous research has shown that globalisation and trade policy changes can affect firms and workers in uneven ways, with gains and adjustment costs often concentrated in particular industries, regions, or groups of employees\. By examining multiple trade agreements and linking firm and worker outcomes more closely to trade exposure, this study aims to offer a more comprehensive understanding of how trade policy shapes productivity, job opportunities, and inequality\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This study leverages detailed data to examine how trade policies impact individual workers and their earnings\. By connecting worker\-level records with firm productivity, it reveals how globalisation shapes income disparities both within and across firms\. The research also investigates whether trade\-driven changes disproportionately benefit high\-skill workers, providing evidence on how economic policies can influence inequality\.
