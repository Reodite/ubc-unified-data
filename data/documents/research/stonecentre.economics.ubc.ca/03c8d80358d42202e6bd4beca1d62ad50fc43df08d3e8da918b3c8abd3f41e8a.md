The Stone Centre is thrilled to host Felipe Lobel for our 2026\-27 Stone Inequality Seminar Series, who will be presenting his paper titled, “Regulation Meets Public Infrastructure: Evidence from Environmental Water Policy in China\.”

**Abstract**

We study the role of productive inclusion in shaping the effects of cash transfers\. Using rich individual\-level administrative microdata, we leverage an increase in the generosity of the world’s largest cash\-transfer program for the extremely poor\. Difference\-in\-differences estimates show that the reform increased employment by 14 percent, with similar responses for men and women\. At the same time, health improves substantially: hospitalization rates fall by 8 percent, public treatment costs decline by 15 percent, and household mortality falls by 14 percent, implying that the reform saved approximately 1,000 lives\. These results challenge neo\-classical predictions and point to direct productivity effects of transfers\. We show that they are consistent with a mechanism in which transfers relax binding subsistence and health constraints, raising productivity and enabling greater labor supply\. We formalize this mechanism in a model of productive inclusion and use it to evaluate welfare, showing that the program more than pays for itself and that accounting for health has first\-order implications for the design of antipoverty policy\.

—

**Felipe Lobel** combines theory and large administrative data to study how corporate behavior and public policy interact in the presence of market failures\. Building on this, his recent work also examines cash transfers, and housing policy\. He completed his PhD in Economics at UC Berkeley, is currently a postdoctoral fellow at Stanford \(SIEPR\), and will start as Assistant Professor of Economics at Duke University in the Fall 2026\.
