**ABOUT**

---

Over a period that begins roughly in the mid\-seventeenth century and stretches to the end of the Napoleonic Wars, in virtually every country in Western Europe, nominal wages for low\-skill occupations froze in place\. Unskilled wages had previously been quite flexible, successfully keeping up with inflation throughout the Price Revolution of the 1500s and early 1600s, and only became flexible again in the nineteenth century\. This study investigates the stagnation seen in the intervening period, roughly 1650\-1820, and the consequences for living standards and income inequality\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

By compiling new wage series disaggregated by skill across multiple European countries, along with data on coinage and labor statutes, this study will analyze the causes and long\-run implications of this prolonged period of extreme wage rigidity\. It will explore its roots in monetary constraints and institutional frameworks that limited labor mobility and the bargaining power of workers\. Findings will shed light on the deeper historical origins of income inequality and the institutional conditions that shaped labor markets prior to industrialization\. This study will also investigate how prolonged wage rigidity shaped income inequality and influenced the economic dynamics that led to modern growth\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This project contributes to understanding the relationship between wage rigidity, labour market institutions, and income inequality over a period of more than 150 years\. The study will shed light on the deeper historical roots of the link between inequality and modern economic growth, while illuminating the early conditions that gave rise to one of the most consequential political and social movements of our time\.
