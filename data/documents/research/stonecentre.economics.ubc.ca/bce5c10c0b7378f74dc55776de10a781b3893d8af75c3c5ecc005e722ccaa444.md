We’re delighted to announce that our next Stone Inequality Seminar will be featuring Melanie Meng Xue, who will be presenting her paper titled, “Women and Asylums\.”

**Abstract**

During the nineteenth century, England experienced a rapid expansion of public asylums: both the number of institutions and the confined population rose sharply\. Using the complete British population censuses, I document the characteristics of asylum residents and show that women were disproportionately represented among those recorded as “lunatic\.” To explain this gender imbalance, I analyze more than 140,000 admission records and the reported “causes of lunacy” recorded at entry, showing that the recorded pathways into confinement were strongly gendered and often referenced domestic and household circumstances for women\. I then study one concrete institutional channel that could shift these outcomes: women’s economic rights within marriage\. Leveraging the Married Women’s Property Act of 1882 as a natural experiment, I show that expanding married women’s control over earnings and property reduced women’s recorded lunacy, consistent with economic rights shaping women’s exposure to severe distress that escalated to confinement\.

**Melanie Meng Xue** is an Assistant Professor of Economic History at the London School of Economics and Political Science \(LSE\) and a Research Affiliate at the Centre for Economic Policy Research \(CEPR\)\. Her research interests include political economy, gender, culture and narrative, and early modern and modern China\. 

Her work examines the rise of women, the self\-perpetuation of authoritarian regimes, the expansion of mental asylums, and the long\-term effects of affirmative action policies\. By tracing the impact of historical events over time and in various institutional settings, her work centers on the role of values, beliefs, and norms in shaping economic and political disparities\. 

In her research on historical narratives, she has found that folklore is a crucial source of cultural values\. In a more recent project, she explores cultural change following the Industrial Revolution through the study of proverbs\. Another ongoing line of inquiry concerns cultural values in China across space and time, within and between ethnic groups\. A book expanding this line of research is currently in progress\.
