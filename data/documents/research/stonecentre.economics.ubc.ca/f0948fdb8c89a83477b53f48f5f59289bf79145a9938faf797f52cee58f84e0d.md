**ABOUT**

---

Persistent inequality is sustained not only by structural factors but also by the preferences of economic elites, who contribute much of the tax base yet often resist redistribution\. One explanation is isolation: elites live in social and spatial bubbles where private spending substitutes for public goods, limiting their understanding of the conditions under which redistribution matters most\. In unequal societies, the sustainability of taxation depends on elite support for redistribution\. This project examines whether breaking elites’ isolation from marginalized communities changes their perceptions of taxation and public goods\.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

By measuring changes in political orientation, support for taxation, perceptions of inequality, and behavioral allocation choices, the project provides causal evidence on how elite attitudes are shaped by contact\. The results will inform debates on both the consequences of elite isolation—weaker fiscal coalitions and underfunded public goods—and possible remedies through structured cross\-class contact\. More broadly, the study demonstrates the potential of targeting elites themselves as a lever for building support for inclusive taxation and reducing inequality\.

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This project investigates how social isolation among economic elites sustains unequal outcomes\. Increased awareness of inequality’s social costs may enhance elite support for redistributive policies, greater public investment, and fairer taxation systems\. Such changes could strengthen the fiscal foundations of welfare provision and improve social mobility, thereby mitigating the persistence of wealth inequality over time\. Through this lens, the project contributes to understanding not only the origins of resistance to redistribution but also the conditions under which that resistance can be overcome\.
