**ABOUT**

---

This study examines the occupational outcomes of agricultural workers during the early 20th century mechanization of American farming\. It investigates how the introduction of tractors and other mechanized technologies transformed the labour landscape, reshaping the types of jobs available and the skills required in rural areas\. By studying this period of economic and technological change, the research sheds light on how major shifts in production technology interact with broader social and economic forces to affect workers’ occupational mobility and earnings\. 

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**

---

This study is expected to provide new evidence on the distributional consequences of technological change during the early twentieth century\. By examining the mechanization of American agriculture, the research will clarify whether technological progress primarily benefitted displaced workers or exacerbated inequality\. The study anticipates that, unlike modern automation, early mechanization coincided with industrial expansion that enabled smooth occupational transitions and income gains for affected workers\. Overall, the findings are expected to deepen understanding of how the nature of technological revolutions shapes labour market mobility, skill transferability, and broader economic outcomes\. 

**HOW IT RELATES TO THE STONE CENTRE’S MISSION**

---

This research examines how technological change affects worker outcomes and income distribution during major economic transformations\. It provides insights into the conditions under which displacement can occur without worsening inequality, drawing lessons from the farm\-to\-factory transition, where workers could move into similar\-skill jobs without major geographic or skill barriers\. The findings are relevant to contemporary debates on AI, robotics, and intergenerational inequality, and can help inform policies for managing current automation trends\.
