We’re excited to welcome Jaime Arellano\-Bover for our 2026\-27 Stone Inequality Seminar Series\. Arellano\-Bover will be presenting his paper titled, “The Labor Demand Implications of Brand Capital: Evidence from Trademark Transactions\.”

**Abstract**

Brand capital–an intangible asset that differentiates a firm’s products–has grown in importance in recent decades\. Trademarks protect brand capital and are actively traded in firm\-to\-firm markets\. This paper studies how brand capital reshapes labor demand within firms, focusing on differences between production and marketing workers\. We link Italian administrative data on trademark ownership, firms’ financial statements, and employer–employee records, and use trademark transactions to identify the effects of brand\-capital investments\. We develop a model in which firms combine production labor, expansionary labor, and brand capital, and use its testable predictions to guide the empirical analysis\. Using a matched difference\-in\-differences design, we show that acquiring a trademark increases firms’ intangible assets by 19%, sales by 8%, and employment by 6%, while leaving average weekly earnings unchanged and reducing firms’ labor share\. Employment gains are concentrated among marketing and sales workers, indicating that brand capital is not skill\-neutral\. Accounting for both buyers and sellers, trademark transactions reallocate brand capital toward more productive firms, generating net output gains but a lower labor share\. A calibrated version of the model implies that the steady\-state firm\-to\-firm trademark market features an aggregate labor share 0\.6 percentage points below the observed level\.

**Jaime Arellano\-Bover** is a Lecturer and Senior Research Scientist at Yale University, and a research fellow at IZA\. Previously, he was a faculty member at the Einaudi Institute for Economics and Finance \(EIEF\) and Tor Vergata University of Rome\. He received his PhD in Economics from Stanford University in 2019, and was a Postdoctoral Associate at Yale University\. Originally from Madrid, Spain, his BA is from Universidad Carlos III de Madrid\. Jaime is a labor economist with research interests in earnings dynamics, migration, human capital, and how firms shape workers outcomes\. His research has studied the role of employers in skill formation, the long\-term impacts of first jobs and of entering the labor market in bad economic times, the labor demand for different college experiences, and the role of job mobility and climbing the firm ladder in immigrants’ integration\.
