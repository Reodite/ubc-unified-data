## FAQ: General Questions

### Can I complete the eligibility requirements to enter my chosen specializations over the summer?

No\. The specialization admission requirements \(i\.e\. courses\) must be completed before you apply\.

### What if I’m not placed in one of my three choices?

This is typically avoidable—ensure you apply to three choices and have done your research to confirm your choices are reasonable for your completed coursework and sessional average\. If you’re not placed in one of your three choices in Round 1, we’ll reach out via email regarding Round 2 of the application\. It’s important that you check your email and respond quickly—Round 2 is conducted on a first\-come, first\-served basis\.

Rarely, a student who is invited to Round 2 wishes to remain without a specialization and apply again the following year\. Students with fewer than 48 total attempted credits \(including advance or transfer credit\) are permitted to remain without a specialization and apply once more in the following year\. Students who have 48 or more total attempted credits must participate in the next application cycle and obtain a specialization, or they will be placed into a specialization determined by the Faculty of Science before winter registration opens in July\. 

Even though it is possible to participate in the specialization application up to two times, we strongly encourage students to complete Round 2 and register into courses for their specialization\. Students must receive a specialization through the coordinated application process before they are eligible to participate in future department\-run specialization application processes \(e\.g\., change of specialization or applying to honours\)\.

### What if the exact program specialization I want isn’t listed?

Some specializations, like some Honours, only admit students through departmental processes at a later stage of your degree\.

If a specialization that interests you isn’t listed as part of this application, review the current [UBC Calendar](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/faculties-colleges-and-schools/faculty-science/bachelor-science) to make sure it is still being offered\. Then, apply to a related major \(e\.g\., major in the same subject if you’re interested in honours\) as part of the BSc specialization application\. Once you are placed into that specialization, follow the instructions on the relevant department’s website to apply to the honours program\.

### What if the combined major I want isn’t listed?

Some combined majors only allow entry in third year and therefore aren’t part of this application\. Examples include the Combined Major in Mathematics and Economics and many combined honours specializations, such as Combined Honours in Mathematics and Statistics\. For now, apply to at least one of the two majors you’re interested in\. Check both departmental websites for instructions on how to apply to the combined program\.

### If I don’t get into the specialization I want, can I take the courses it requires during winter session \(while in another specialization\) and apply to switch for third year?

Maybe\. Depending on your registration date/time, you may be able to register in some or all of the courses required for your ideal specialization\. However, you’ll also want to look at the courses required for your current program specialization—if you’re not successful in gaining entry to your ideal specialization next year, you may find yourself in a situation where you’re not eligible to continue your studies\. For more information about preparing to apply to switch specializations, please read [How to Change Your Specialization](https://science.ubc.ca/students/change-specialization)\. 

### If I'm admitted to a specialization am I guaranteed to continue in this specialization?

Yes\. All students in the BSc need to meet the Faculty of Science continuation requirements in the calendar\. To stay in an honours specialization, you must meet the specialization and [Faculty of Science honours requirements](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/faculties-colleges-and-schools/faculty-science/bachelor-science/introduction-degree-options)\.

## FAQ: Transfer Questions

### I’m eligible to apply for a BSc specialization, but I've applied to transfer to another Faculty and I haven't heard back yet\. Should I still complete the BSc specialization application?

Yes\. Submit an application for a specialization so that if you don’t transfer, you’ll have a specialization\. If you receive the outcome for your request to transfer before we notify you of your specialization results, please email [yr2application@science\.ubc\.ca](mailto:yr2application@science.ubc.ca) to withdraw your application so that we can place another student instead\.

### I have been invited to apply for a BSc specialization, but my application to transfer to UBC Science is still pending\. Should I still apply for a specialization?

Yes\. If you’re waiting for an outcome regarding your UBC application, you still need to apply to the UBC BSc Specialization Application\. If you’re offered admission to UBC Science, it’s an important step you need to have completed\.

### If I’m not accepted as a transfer student into the Faculty of Science until after the application closes, will you still put me into one of the three specializations I chose in the application?

When the application closes, we consider the applications of all students who have accepted an offer to the Bachelor of Science\. If you are admitted after the application closes, please reach out to [yr2application@science\.ubc\.ca](mailto:yr2application@science.ubc.ca) right away to learn what your options are\. There may be a chance that we can consider your three choices if there is space in one of them \(and you meet the requirements\), but you may need to select from one of the specializations that still has room\.

## FAQ: Honours

### **What are the new BSc honours specialization requirements?** 

New BSc honours specialization requirements will be implemented in the 2026\-2027 academic year\. Starting in 2026, honours students will be required to maintain a cumulative average of 76%\. There is no longer a credit minimum for admission or continuation in an honours specialization\. Students cannot be admitted to or continue in an honours specialization if they have failed a named course in the specialization\. Named courses are those specifically listed in the calendar entry for the specialization \(e\.g\. not electives\)\. Refer to the UBC Calendar pages for the [Science honours requirements](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/faculties-colleges-and-schools/faculty-science/bachelor-science/introduction-degree-options) and the [Calendar](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/faculties-colleges-and-schools/faculty-science/bachelor-science) pages for each honours specialization for details\.

### **What is required to be admitted to an honours specialization in the BSc specialization admission process in 2026?** 

In this transition year, students will need to have a minimum 68% admission average for admission to an honours specialization \(70% for Honours Biotechnology and Combined Honours in Biochemistry and Forensic Science\)\. Failed elective courses will not disqualify a student, and there is no minimum credit load\. Some honours specializations have additional grade minimums in specific courses, please review the [Specialization Requirements](https://science.ubc.ca/students/spec-admission-requirements) page for details\.

### I don't see the honours specialization I want on the list\. How can I apply?

Most honours specializations aren't part of the BSc specialization admission process—you apply directly to departments after obtaining a related specialization\. For now, apply to the Major in the same field\(s\)\. While in second year, choose courses to meet the requirements of your desired honours\. To find out specifics about switching from a Major or Combined Major into the parallel Honours or Combined Honours, look for information about the timing and process on the relevant department website\(s\) before you contact the department advisor\(s\) to inquire or ask more questions\.
