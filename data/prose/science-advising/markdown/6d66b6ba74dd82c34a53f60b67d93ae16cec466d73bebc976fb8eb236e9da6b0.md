In the years between getting accepted into your specialization and your graduating year, you will have the opportunity to develop your experience and transferable skills as you build towards future careers\. 

By this point, the majority of students in UBC Science will be settled in their program specialization\. 

The start of third year is a good time to review [Faculty and program specialization requirements](https://science.ubc.ca/students/requirements) \(refer to your program specialization in the [UBC Academic Calendar](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/faculties-colleges-and-schools/faculty-science/bachelor-science)\)\. Both sets of requirements must be fulfilled before you graduate with a BSc\.

Things to consider during your middle years\.

-   [What Can I Do With My Science Degree?](https://students.ubc.ca/career/your-degree/science)
-   [Explore Science Co\-op](https://sciencecoop.ubc.ca/prospective)
-   [How to Change Your Specialization](https://science.ubc.ca/students/change-specialization)

### Careers and beyond

-   Figure out [how to tell your story to employers](https://students.ubc.ca/career/your-degree/science#use-your-toolkit), from your degree experience to your skill sets
-   Browse [on\-campus student jobs](https://science.ubc.ca/students/blog/find-work-on-campus) to gain work experience
-   Students considering graduate studies should get to know professors and [gain research experience](https://science.ubc.ca/students/undergrad-research) in labs\. You'll need solid academic references by first term of fourth year
-   [Read advice](https://science.ubc.ca/students/blog/career-year-three) on planning your third year and beyond
