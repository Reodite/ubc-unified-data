#### New\! An internet for your personal devices\.

UBC IoT Internet of Things wi\-fi network gives your personal devices their own private network — secure, encrypted, and invisible to everyone else on campus\. Your laptop, phone, gaming console, smart TV, speaker, and other personal devices see each other and work together, just like they do at home\.

UBC staff, faculty, and students can connect their devices to the network using their CWL account \(Multi\-factor Authentication required\)\.   
  
[**Learn More**](https://it.ubc.ca/services/email-voice-internet/ubc-internet-things-iot-wi-fi "UBC Internet of Things (IoT) Wi-Fi")

[**Log in and add your devices**](https://iot.ubc.ca/accounts/ubc/device/) 

**Heads\-up**: *for now, log in once every 4 weeks to keep your devices active\. It’s a temporary security measure while we finish automating personal devices this fall — after that, you’ll only log in to add or remove a device\.* 

**Note:** **The Wireless Device Registration System will be decommissioned in 2027\. To connect your personal devices to the UBC wi\-fi network, please use the** [Internet of Things \(IoT\) wireless network](https://it.ubc.ca/services/email-voice-internet/ubc-internet-things-iot-wi-fi)**\.**

The “Wireless Device Registration System” allows residential multimedia and gaming devices to be pre\-registered to connect to the ubcvisitor wireless network\. Once registered, these can connect to the Internet via ubcvisitor without the requirement to login\. These types of devices are typically unsupported on enterprise wireless networks like ubcsecure so this service was launched to allow seamless connectivity to the UBC Wireless Network\.

## Requirements and Eligibility

#### Who can use this?

Students residing in RESNET Wireless supported buildings listed on the [RESNET page](https://it.ubc.ca/services/email-voice-internet/resnet "ResNet") are permitted to use this service only

#### What type of devices can use this service?

Any device that supports Open Authentication is capable of connecting\. However, we do ask that if your device is capable of connecting to ubcsecure, or eduroam WiFi networks, you should configure them to do so\. Here are a few examples of devices that are able to connect to Wireless Device Registration Service;

-   Gaming Consoles
-   Set\-top TV boxes
-   Smart TVs
-   Other cloud\-connected devices that are unable to connect to [ubcsecure](https://it.ubc.ca/services/email-voice-internet/wireless-internet-access/ubcsecure-wireless-network "ubcsecure Wireless Network") or [eduroam](https://it.ubc.ca/services/email-voice-internet/wireless-internet-access/eduroam-wireless-network/how-access-eduroam "How to Access eduroam")

In order to register the device you will need to locate the wireless MAC address or wireless physical address\. This address is typically located physically on the device near the serial number or can be found in the menu system \(i\.e system information, about menu, etc\)\. The wireless MAC or physical address is formatted in six groups of two hexadecimal digits\. The address may be formatted like aa:bb:cc:11:22:33 or aa\-bb\-cc\-11\-22\-33\.

### Service Restrictions

-   This service is restricted to the registration of multimedia \(set top / smart TV\) and gaming console devices that are not capable of connecting to 802\.1x type wireless networks like "[ubcsecure](https://it.ubc.ca/node/30771)"\. Users are not permitted to register devices such as smart phones, laptops and desktop computers, rather these devices should be connecting to the "[ubcsecure](https://it.ubc.ca/node/30771)" wireless network\. Registered devices not meeting the above criteria may be scrutinized and permanently removed from the service by IT staff at any time\.
-   Each user is only permitted to register a maximum of three devices\.
-   Bandwidth is limited but is capable of streaming video and online gaming\.
-   You may not be able to access some UBC academic resources \(e\.g\. Library, Connect, etc\); use [ubcsecure](https://it.ubc.ca/node/30771) from a smartphone, laptop or desktop instead\.
-   Some devices such as Google Home and Chromecast that require the use of the Bonjour or mDNS protocol may not function as expected\. These devices and protocols were designed for a typical home WiFi network that is not scalable to service 1000's of users\.

### Appropriate Use

All clients must agree to abide by the [Appropriate Use Policy](https://it.ubc.ca/node/1569)\.
