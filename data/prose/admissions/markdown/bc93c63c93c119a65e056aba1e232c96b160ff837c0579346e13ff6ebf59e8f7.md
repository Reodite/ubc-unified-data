## One room\. Twenty teams\. A question worth solving\.

The Big Question is a case study competition where you’ll face real\-world problems, build a solution under time pressure, and present those solutions to a panel of UBC professors and notable alumni \(including business leaders based in India\)\. This is a genuine university experience\.

## Competition details

## How it works

1

**Learn from UBC professors in a pre\-competition masterclass**  
Before competition day, a UBC professor will run a live online session to sharpen your skills and introduce you to UBC thinking\. This online session is open to all Grade 11 and Grade 12 students – even if you are not on the team\. Think of it as a free masterclass from one of the world’s top 40 universities\.

2

**Your school selects a team**  
Your counsellor or teacher will select four students to represent your school at the competition\.

3

**The Big Question drops**  
On competition day, you’ll receive a sealed brief that contains a problem that none of the teams has seen before\. You’ll have hours – not weeks – to solve the problem\. You’ll work in a breakout room, with UBC faculty and alumni guiding the process\.

4

**You’ll present your solution**  
All teams will present, defend, and debate their ideas and their solutions to a panel of esteemed judges – made up of UBC faculty and accomplished UBC alumni\.

5

**The best solution wins**  
The panel of judges evaluate every team\. The top teams will present their solutions to the entire group of competition participants\. The winning team will walk away with The Big Question title and prizes for every team member\. And competing students will receive a certificate of participation\.

## Two engines that drive change\. Your team needs both\.

Engineering builds the systems that make things work\. Business builds the case that makes things happen\. The Big Question sits at the intersection of both disciplines – and this is where the best solutions emerge\.

### UBC Sauder School of Business

How do you turn a concept into a plan that works? Who pays for it? What’s the market? The Bachelor of Commerce teaches you how to take new ideas, shape them into business plans, and make them real\.

### UBC Faculty of Applied Science

Can it be built? How does it scale? What could go wrong? The Bachelor of Applied Science prepares students for a simple reality: The most complex challenges ahead will not be solved by improving what we have today\.

## Some answers to your questions about the competition

[Do you need a background in business or engineering to participate?](https://you.ubc.ca/big-question/#accordion-6-panel-1)

No\. The competition is designed to be accessible for students from any stream\. What matters is how you think, not what you’ve studied\.

[How do schools select their teams?](https://you.ubc.ca/big-question/#accordion-6-panel-2)

Your counsellor or teacher will decide how to select the four students to represent your school\. This may be done through an internal competition, by nomination, or some other method\.

[Is there a registration fee?](https://you.ubc.ca/big-question/#accordion-6-panel-3)

No\. The competition is free for those schools that have been invited to participate\.

[Can students who aren't on the team still benefit from the competition?](https://you.ubc.ca/big-question/#accordion-6-panel-4)

Yes\. The online session on May 5, 2026, is open to all Grade 11 and Grade 12 students from the participating schools\. This session will provide you with an opportunity to learn from world\-class faculty, regardless of whether you’re on a competing team\.

[How can schools get involved in future competitions?](https://you.ubc.ca/big-question/#accordion-6-panel-5)

As this is our inaugural year, we are working with a select number of schools\. If you’d like your school to be considered for future The Big Question competitions, you can email [south\.asia@ubc\.ca](mailto:south.asia@ubc.ca)\.
