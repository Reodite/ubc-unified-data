### **[Tùgûl̈ “Txì xtěn rǎ gǎmy” ‘The celebration of the Day of the Dead: “The day of the souls”’](https://sites.google.com/view/tugul/inicio)**

By: [Ana Laura Arrieta\-Zamudio](https://linguistics.ubc.ca/profile/ana-laura-arrieta-zamudio/), PhD Candidate, Department of Linguistics

**What is your book about?**

This is the first graphic novel written entirely in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec \(Spanish and English versions are also available\)\. It follows Juana \(Xwân\), José \(Sê\), and their children Dulce \(Nàx\) and Pedro \(Bêd\) as they prepare to celebrate the Day of the Dead in San Pablo Güilá\. It also focuses on modals \(that is, expressions about notions like possibility and necessity, like English ‘must’, ‘might’\) in San Pablo Güilá Zapotec\. The Day of the Dead is a major holiday in Mexico, and communities celebrate it in different ways\. This book provides insight into how it is celebrated in San Pablo Güilá \(a Zapotecan community in Oaxaca, Mexico\)\. It also allows other linguists interested in modals to see how they are expressed in this language\.

**Why did you write this book?**

This book is the result of my PSI grant\. It is a community\-based collaboration\. The co\-authors of the story, who are also members of the community and native speakers of San Pablo Güilá Zapotec, are Dulce Rocío Santiago Morales, Ana Mey Maricela Morales Cruz, and Virginia Morales Cruz\. The illustrations were made by the Zapotecan artist José Froylan López García and graphic designer Sergio Tamayo Castelar\. This book will serve as a useful resource for language documentation and a tool for language revitalization, since printed copies were distributed to local teachers, and the three versions are available to the general public for free in digital form\. This book combines community work with material design for linguists interested in modals, while also showcasing an important celebration\.

### **[Urban Indigenous Assemblages: Qom Mobilities and the Remaking of White Buenos Aires](https://www.vanderbiltuniversitypress.com/9780826508386/urban-indigenous-assemblages/)**

By: [Dr\. Ana Vivaldi](https://sociology.ubc.ca/profile/ana-vivaldi/), Sessional Lecturer, Departments of Sociology and Department of Anthropology

**What is this book about?**

*Urban Indigenous Assemblages* explores how Qom people navigate racialization in the city, remake urban space and Indigenous life in Buenos Aires\. In a city long imagined by Argentina’s elites as white and European, Qom people are simultaneously recognized as Indigenous and racialized as part of the non\-white urban poor\. Based on 18 months of ethnographic research, the book follows the movements that connect a Qom barrio in Greater Buenos Aires with rural communities in the Chaco, other Indigenous urban spaces, city institutions, and the city centre\. These mobilities create forms of Indigenous territoriality that extend beyond bounded places and conventional ideas of indigeneity\. Through the concept of Indigenous assemblages, the book examines how everyday practices, movements, and relationships produce Indigenous spaces while challenging the racial and spatial boundaries through which Buenos Aires has been imagined\. The book thus rethinks urban Indigenous life through mobility, race, and the remaking of territory\.

**Why did you write this book?**

I wrote this book out of a sense of the invisibility of Indigenous and non\-white people in Buenos Aires, the city where I grew up and lived for half of my life\. I was also struck by the fact that researchers working with Indigenous people generally travelled far from the city, to rural communities\. I wanted to challenge this separation between rural and urban and to unpack the unspoken and largely unrecognized racism embedded in the making of Buenos Aires as a supposedly race\-less city and society\.

I became particularly interested in Qom’s people movement, against the idea that Indigenous people are fixed in place and bounded within the territories the state recognizes as Indigenous\. I wanted to understand the connections between Indigenous people, rural\-to\-urban migrants, and wider histories of mass political mobilization, including Peronism and unionism\. Ultimately, the book asks us to see Indigenous people not as “ethnic minorities” at the margins of the city, but as key political actors who reshape space and create an Indigenous territoriality that exceeds the boundaries imposed by the state\.

### [**Grounds for Exclusion: Race, Health, and Disability in Argentine Immigration Policy, 1876\-1932**](https://uncpress.org/9781469695358/grounds-for-exclusion/)

By: [Dr\. Benjamin Bryce](https://history.ubc.ca/profile/benjamin-bryce/), Associate Professor, Department of History

**What is this book about?**

I stumbled into this project, but the more I dug the more I realized it was an important story that needed to be told\. Argentina’s immigration story has mostly focused on community histories of the six million Europeans who arrived between the 1870s and 1930\. When I came across a diplomatic row in 1912 between Argentine and British officials about 600 Punjabi immigrants, something felt very off\. If that incident could vanish so completely from national memory and the stories that historians and museums tell, I wanted to know what else had been left out\. The answer turned out to be a great deal\. Once I started looking for the machinery of exclusion, I found it everywhere — in health inspections, in the work of consuls in Europe and Asia, in small clauses in immigration law, in the quiet refusals at ports of departure that never generated the kind of paper trail that arrivals do\.

**Why did you write this book?**

I also wrote it because Argentina’s “nation of immigrants” story isn’t unique\. Canada and the United States have built self\-images around the arrival of millions of people while saying much less about the mechanisms that decided who counted as desirable and who they excluded\. As I argue in the book, the widespread inclusion of millions of people in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries – in Argentina and other settler societies – was predicated on the exclusion of others\.

### [**Sexual Heresies: Religion, Science, and Sexuality in Modern Britain**](https://www.sup.org/books/history/sexual-heresies)

By: [Dr\. Joy Dixon](https://history.ubc.ca/profile/joy-dixon/), Associate Professor, Department of History

**What is your book about?**

*Sexual Heresies* explores the ways that, in Britain at the turn of the last century, the new sexual sciences—from an anthropology that located the roots of religion in ancient fertility cults to psychoanalytic theories that explained religious experience in terms of psychosexual development—characterized religion as closely connected to or as a displacement of the sexual\. Those changing understandings of sexuality also transformed what was meant by “religion” and created a new concern with differentiating “normal” from “abnormal” forms of religiosity\. The book then goes on to track the impact of and response to that move across multiple sites: in liberal modernist theology in both the Catholic and Anglican churches, in occult or esoteric religions and “sexual magic,” among Anglican evangelicals and Anglo\-Catholics, and in secularist free\-love and free\-thought and “pagan” anti\-clericalism\. I argue that the increasing concern to police “sexual heresies” led to a narrowing of sexual possibilities inside “orthodox” religion and the association of alternative forms of religion with dissident sexualities that continues to shape both religion and secularism today\.

**Why did you write this book?**

Histories of religion and sexuality are often written as though they had nothing to do with each other, or as though they are always somehow in opposition to each other \(for example, in the idea that religion is inevitably sexually oppressive, and that sexual liberation is inherently secular\)\. I wanted to tell a more complicated story that does justice to the diversity of responses to changing ideas about sexuality from a variety of perspectives and traditions\. The book is, in the end, an effort to track the creative ways that people have found to make sense of the relationships between faith \(or its absence\) and desire in their own lives, and to clarify some of what is at stake in that process\.

### **[The Oxford Handbook of Global Realisms](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/56841)**

By: [Dr\. Katherine Bowers](https://cenes.ubc.ca/profile/katherine-bowers/), Associate Professor, Department of Central, Eastern and Northern European Studies

**What is this book about?**

Realism is an artistic practice that aims to faithfully represent reality\. Historically, it has been practiced across different media, from early pictorial art and epic oral narratives, through literature and visual arts, to film, music, and digital media\. By foregrounding diverse theories, practices, and forms of realism, this volume revises the Eurocentric geography of the concept\. It offers a broad chronology that overcomes the fixation in studies of realism on the nineteenth century as its starting point and offers, instead, a more flexible timeline of this artistic practice\. The handbook presents realism as a transnational, transhistorical, and intermedial global phenomenon\.

**Why did you write this book?**

We wanted to understand why our field’s conceptualization and historicization of realism seemed so different from that of other disciplines\. As we began to research this, we were struck by the diversity of realisms across different literatures, cinemas, and art forms, and the lack of resources that attempted to speak to this diversity as a whole\. *The Oxford Handbook of Global Realisms*, co\-edited with Dr\. Margarita Vaysman \(University of Oxford\), emerged from this line of inquiry and the conversations we had with colleagues across disciplinary lines\.

### **[The Language of Memes: Patterns of Meaning Emergence Across Image and Text](https://www.cambridge.org/ca/universitypress/subjects/languages-linguistics/cognitive-linguistics/language-memes-patterns-meaning-across-image-and-text?format=HB&amp;isbn=9781108844352)**

By: [Dr\. Barbara Dancygier](http://faculty.arts.ubc.ca/bdancygier), Professor, Department of English Language and Literatures

**What is this book about?**

The book proposes a cognitive linguistic analysis of internet memes\. We show how image\-plus\-text contemporary artifacts instantiate and develop patterns of meaning emergence we have been seeing in language\. As we show, memes not only rely on pre\-existing linguistic conventions, but also establish new conventions which are well\-adjusted to the needs of online communication\. Far from being simplistic coinages of image and text, they create new standards of internet discourse\.

**Why did you write this book?**

For a while now, we have been puzzled by the fact that memes use deceptively simple forms to express a variety of interesting and relevant meanings\. They are very good at expressing opinions online without lengthy explanations\. We also found that memes stand out among other multimodal artifacts \(advertisements, campaign materials, posters, etc\.\) in that they use linguistic forms in specific and well\-structured ways\. We intended to draw attention to online discourse and its rules by discussing the ways in which language adjusts to the dominance of visual forms of expression\.

### **[Austrian German: a 300\-year love story \[Österreichisches Deutsch: eine 300\-jährige Liebesgeschichte\]](https://www.newacademicpress.at/gesamtverzeichnis/unkategorisiert/oesterreichisches-deutsch/)**

By: [Dr\. Stefan Dollinger](https://english.ubc.ca/profile/stefan-dollinger/), Professor, Department of English Language and Literatures, Canadian Word Centre

**What is your book about?**

This book reflects on Standard Austrian German, the dominant standard variety in Austria, examining its 500\-year history alongside 300 years of neglect, suppression, and misunderstanding within German philology, dialectology, and linguistics\. It showcases, for readers aged 14 and up, the unexplored biases in linguistics—some of which remain entangled, to this day, with National\-Socialist stances that were carried over into the present without ever being critically examined\. It tells the story of the hegemonization, and at times weaponization, of “standard German” from Germany, and of the deliberate backgrounding of Standard Austrian and Standard Swiss German for sociopolitical reasons since the 1860s\. Large\-scale data shows that 94\.3% of its speakers \(n\=70,000\) see Standard Austrian German as the “more suitable” standard for Austria\.

**Why did you write this book?**

It’s a question of linguistic justice\. I noticed that what is done internationally in sociolinguistics is incompatible with the way German dialectologists and sociolinguists work on what they call “German in Austria\.” The name — it used to be “Austrian German” — is itself their program\. I had to speak up\. I started in 2016 with a methodological paper, then followed up in 2019 with a book\. The response was so negative and ad hominem that I decided to dig through the archives instead\. What I found was a story worse than I could have imagined: linguists who had acted as Nazi desk perpetrators, then went on to be celebrated after 1945\. What counts as “the German language” is an important question, and the answer looks very different in the 2020s — yet the field, by and large, still relies on a definition from the 1820s, straight out of the Grimms’ storybook\.

### **[The Long Rise of the British Novel, 1660\-1800](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-long-rise-of-the-british-novel-16601800-9780198987369?q=nicholas%20hudson&amp;lang=en&amp;cc=ca)**

By: [Dr\. Nicholas Hudson](https://english.ubc.ca/profile/nicholas-hudson/), Professor Emeritus, Department of English Language and Literatures

**What is your book about?**

*The Long Rise of the British Novel, 1660\-1800* presents a new thesis on the rise and development of the modern novel over the entirety of the eighteenth century\. It challenges the widely received notion that the rise of capitalism fostered the individualism of realist fiction in the Victorian age characteristic of the novel\. I argue instead that the novel evolved as a form typically opposed to capitalist greed and celebrating empathetic social interaction\. My book also argues that women writers, particularly Frances Burney and Charlotte Smith, created the first realist fiction in the late eighteenth century\. Though largely forgotten in the nineteenth century, these authors shaped the form of realist fiction from the Victorian age to the present day\.

**Why did you write this book?**

I believed that reigning theories on the origins of the novel were mistaken in important respects\.

### **[James Bay Memoirs and Other Stories](https://uofmpress.ca/books/james-bay-memoirs-and-other-stories)**

By: [Dr\. Isabella Huberman](https://fhis.ubc.ca/profile/isabella-huberman/), Assistant Professor, Department of French, Hispanic, and Italian Studies

**What is your book about?**

I had the pleasure of collaborating with Chisasibi Eeyou poet Margaret Sam\-Cromarty to put together this volume of her life works\. Born in 1936 on Fort George Island, Margaret began writing in her 50s, after her family was relocated to Chisasibi for the La Grande hydroelectric project\. This event informs much of her poetry, which explores family, community life, and her experiences of the land both before and after the move\. Thirty years after publishing her first book in 1992, Margaret revisited her poetry and stories to bring them into the hands of a new generation of readers\. **Why did you write this book?**

Margaret Sam\-Cromarty is the “Poet Laureate of Eeyou Istchee” but is little\-known outside of James Bay\. Though out of print for years, her books remained in high demand within her community\. As an Anglophone First Nations author in Quebec, writing at a time when the province’s literary scene gave little attention to Indigenous voices, her work fell into an in\-between and has, until now, been absent from scholarly work on this corpus\. We worked with the “First Voices First Texts” series at UMP, which is committed to reconnecting contemporary readers with unjustly neglected classics of Indigenous literature\. Without any doubt, Margaret’s poetry and stories make a significant contribution to the field of Indigenous literatures in Quebec and Canada\.

### **[Prison Song: Music and Incarceration in the United States](https://press.umich.edu/Books/P/Prison-Song2)**

By: [Dr\. David Metzer](https://music.ubc.ca/profile/david-metzer/), Professor, School of Music

 **What is your book about?**

In *Prison Song: Music and Incarceration in the United States*, I explore how musicians across genres—hip hop, country, blues, folk, rock, jazz, and classical—have confronted the prison system\. They have defied the dehumanization and silence of incarceration by telling the life stories of imprisoned individuals, creating empathetic bonds between listeners and those individuals, and attacking the racial and social inequalities embedded in incarceration\. I discuss the defiant songs of incarcerated, formerly incarcerated, and nonincarcerated musicians from diverse racial and ethnic backgrounds\. My book is the first to take a broad view of prison songs, revealing how they and their critiques of incarceration have changed and grown across the 20th and 21st centuries\.

**Why did you write this book?**

Over years of listening to Johnny Cash’s *At Folsom Prison*, Joan Baez’s “Prison Trilogy,” and J\. Cole’s “4 Your Eyez Only,” I didn’t realize that I was listening to a particular history, that of songs about incarceration\. Nor did I anticipate that I would one day chronicle that history\. One reason to write *Prison Song: Music and Incarceration in the United States* was to be the first to explore such a rich and important historical subject\. Scholars rarely get these opportunities\. I was also excited to further my studies of how musicians have challenged social and racial inequalities, just as the musicians that I discuss have been doing for more than a hundred years with their prison songs\.

### **[Canadian Literatures in English, Second Edition](https://broadviewpress.com/product/canadian-literatures-in-english-volume-1-and-2-package/#tab-description)**

By: [Dr\. Laura Moss](https://english.ubc.ca/profile/laura-moss/), Professor, Department of English Language and Literatures

 **What is this book about?**

Canadian Literatures in English is a scholarly anthology that traces the literary history of Canada over the past 400\+ years\. It reflects a commitment to historical contextualization, cultural inclusion, and a nuanced understanding of critical debates over the long literary and cultural history of what is now Canada\. The second edition has been updated throughout to include recent scholarship and fresh literary works\. It provides a diverse range of genres, including short fiction, poetry, and creative nonfiction, but also legal, government, and policy documents, contemporary art, journalism, and experimental work that crosses genres\. The first volume \(published in summer 2025\) covers the early writing to 1920 and the second volume \(published in summer 2026\) covers 1920\-2025\.

**Why did you write this book?**

Cynthia Sugars and I published the first edition of this anthology in 2009 to reflect the kind of texts and contexts we taught as postcolonial Canadian scholars\. We wrote extensive introductions to each section to situate culture in historical context\. A lot has changed in Canadian literary studies over the past 17 years so we knew it was time to overhaul the first edition and update everything\. Tracking the debates and advances in the field and with the inclusion of work by 36 new authors, the new edition is a wonderful overview of the cultural history of Canada over the centuries\. It is also a collection of some of the fantastic literature being produced here and now\. Our goal was to create a book for any reader interested in knowing the complex and fraught literary history of Canada\.

### **[Literate Workers and the Production of Early Christian Literature](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/abs/literate-workers-and-the-production-of-early-christian-literature/62051738DA0938D8308179085AC2D722)**

By: [Dr\. Isaac Soon](https://amne.ubc.ca/profile/isaac-soon/), Assistant Professor of Early Christianity, Department of Ancient Mediterranean and Near Eastern Studies

**What is your book about?**

My book gives a broad overview of the embodied nature of enslaved early Christian secretaries and stenographers in late antiquity\. In this short volume, I recover biographical snapshots from the lives of enslaved literate workers, who they were, what they looked like, their dreams, aspirations, and uses in early Christianity\.

**Why did you write this book?**

Recently, there has been a lot of important work on enslaved literate workers and their role in constructing early Christian literature like the New Testament \(see for example the work of Candida Moss and Chance Bonar\)\. I wanted to push this scholarship further by experimenting with how much we can know about the contributions of such workers to ancient translations, theological documents, and early Christian culture\. These enslaved workers were not just the instruments by which enslaving Christians produced Christian texts\. They should be considered authors and intellectuals in their own right, their voices relatively accessible from our sources\. At the same time, I wanted to highlight the ways in which literate work was also disabling, taking a physical toll on these ancient persons\.

### **[Racial Virtuality: Information Capitalism and the Suggestive Materiality of Asianness](https://nyupress.org/9781479838110/racial-virtuality/)**

By: [Dr\. Danielle Wong](https://english.ubc.ca/profile/danielle-wong/), Assistant Professor, Department of English Language and Literatures

**What is your book about?**

*Racial Virtuality* examines everyday experiences and experiments with virtuality in the information age that can feel pleasurable, humorous, therapeutic, or even tedious\. Specifically, the book theorizes Asianness as nonrepresentational processes of new media—interfaces, gestures, calibrations, calculations—and as forms and aesthetic practices that illuminate how Asianness is a specific mode of informational capital, and also a mode of relation and critique\. The book broadens our understanding of racialization in the digital age and challenges traditional notions of cultural production and subject formation\. In doing so, it demonstrates how Asianness circulates as a new media form in a digital marketplace of commodified affects, senses, gestures, and tastes\.

**Why did you write this book?**

This book extends theories of race to consider Asian racialization as not only occurring through media representation and other forms of figuration, but also through our interactions with media technologies and their unseen operations\. The racialization of Asians, who appeared to embody the model minority success story in the first decade of social media, is now implicated more in the racial logics of algorithms, interfaces, gestures, circulations, and affects, rather than individual representations of racialized subjects\. The book reveals the ways in which “Asianness” moves beyond a politics of recuperation and recognition to yield modes of fugitivity, illicit knowledge, and critique, all of which threaten existing relationships between capital, labor and information\.
