> Still need an elective for Winter Term 1? We’ve done some digging 👀

We’ve rounded up some hidden gems from across Arts, from language, literature and performance to politics, history and life’s big questions\. Take a look, find something that sparks your curiosity and add it to your timetable\.

[Language, literature and performance \(12\)](https://www.arts.ubc.ca/news/hidden-gems-winter-term-1-2026/#language-literature-and-perfor-40)

[**LING\_V 101: Languages of the World**](https://linguistics.ubc.ca/undergraduate/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=LING%20101)  
Trace how the world's thousands of languages are classified, evolve, and sometimes disappear, from writing systems to endangered languages\.  
*Multiple sections\. Offered both WT1 and WT2\.*

---

[**MUSC 165B/466B/565B: World Music Ensembles**](https://music.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=165B)  
Play Balinese gamelan instruments in a performance ensemble, no music reading or prior experience required, building toward an end\-of\-year concert\.  
*Wed/Fri, 1:00 PM–2:50 PM\. Full\-year course, both terms required\.*

---

[**SWAH\_V 101: Beginners Swahili I**](https://africanstudies.arts.ubc.ca/courses-2/2026-winter/)  
Start speaking Swahili, one of the world's most widely spoken languages, through everyday vocabulary and real cultural context\.  
*Mon/Wed/Fri, 11:00 AM–12:00 PM*

---

[**ASIA\_V 212: Writing about Japan**](https://asia.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=Writing%20about%20Japan%20212)  
Read everything from ancient myths to travel narratives to the "Cool Japan" phenomenon, and see how Japan has been imagined and re\-imagined across centuries through literature and pop culture\.  
*Tue/Thu, 12:30 PM–2:00 PM*

---

[**RMST\_V 201: Intro to Literatures and Cultures of the Romance World I**](https://fhis.ubc.ca/course-description/rmst201/)  
Read *Don Quixote*, *Dante's Inferno*, and *Madame Bovary* in translation, and trace 500 years of literary history across France, Spain, Portugal, and Italy\.  
*Tue/Thu, 12:30 PM–2:00 PM*

---

[**AMNE\_V 392: Introduction to Akkadian**](https://wd10.myworkday.com/ubc/d/inst/3$49941/13228$477174.htmld#TABTASKID=2998%2426057)  
Learn Akkadian, the language of the world's oldest surviving written story, and explore everything from royal epics and divine myths to treaties, oaths, and adoption contracts\.  
*Mon/Wed/Fri, 2:00 PM–3:00 PM*

---

[**ASIA\_V 357: Modern Korean Literature in Translation**](https://asia.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=ASIA%20357%20011)  
Discover Korea's modern history through fiction on gender, division, trauma, and LGBTQ experiences, no Korean language required\.  
*Mon/Wed, 3:30 PM–5:00 PM*

---

[**GMST\_V 341: Nineteenth\-Century German Literature and Social Media**](https://cenes.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=GMST%20341)  
Dig into 19th\-century scandals, fake news, and periodical press in German\-speaking Europe, and see how familiar the media chaos feels today\.  
*Mon/Wed, 12:30 PM–2:00 PM*

---

[**NORD\_V 334: Resilient Narratives: Anxiety and Grief in Contemporary Nordic Fiction**](https://cenes.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=334)  
Explore grief, anxiety, and the occasional scary troll through contemporary Nordic fiction and children's literature\.  
*Mon/Wed/Fri, 11:00 AM–12:00 PM*

---

[**THTR 340B: Studies in Asian Theatre**](https://theatrefilm.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=THTR%20340)  
Explore how modern theatre took shape across Asia, from colonial influence and nationalism to reforming existing theatrical traditions, through plays from China, Japan, India, Indonesia, Korea, and Vietnam\.  
*Mon/Wed, 2:00 PM–3:30 PM*

---

[**ASIA\_V 412: Pre\-Modern Chinese Poetry**](https://asia.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=ASIA%20412)  
Read early Chinese poetry by Qu Yuan, Cao Cao, and Tao Yuanming, plus anonymous voices, in translation, and build the tools to make sense of texts written centuries ago\.  
*Mon/Wed, 11:00 AM–12:30 PM*

---

[**FNEL 489A: Language, Landscape, and All Our Relations**](https://cis.arts.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=489A)  
Explore how ancestral languages and landscapes are inseparable, and what it means to document and reclaim a language through land\.  
*Mon/Wed, 11:00 AM–12:30 PM*

[Politics, History, and Ethics \(16\)](https://www.arts.ubc.ca/news/hidden-gems-winter-term-1-2026/#politics-history-and-ethics-16-41)

[**ANTH\_V 275: Anthropology of Migration and \(Im\)mobilities**](https://anth.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=ANTH%20275)  
Examine the lived, everyday realities of migration and border\-crossing, and the forces that constrain who gets to move freely\.  
*Tue/Thu, 9:30 AM–11:00 AM*

---

[**CENS\_V 201 \(Sec 101\): Controlling Objects**](https://cenes.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=Contrasts%20and%20Conflicts)  
Unpack how your phone, your clothes, and your commute quietly shape who you are, through a century of literature, film, and photography\.  
*Tue/Thu, 2:00 PM–3:30 PM*

---

[**CENS\_V 201 \(Sec 103\): Ukraine: Narratives We Didn't Know**](https://cenes.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=Contrasts%20and%20Conflicts)  
Trace Ukraine's story from the twentieth century to today through literature and film you probably haven't encountered yet\.  
*Mon/Wed/Fri, 11:00 AM–12:00 PM*

---

[**CSIS\_V 200: Introduction to Critical Studies in Gender and Sexuality**](https://grsj.arts.ubc.ca/courses/)  
Build a foundation in sexuality studies, and see how sexuality intersects with race, class, ability, and power rather than existing on its own\.  
*Tue/Thu, 11:00 AM–12:30 PM*

---

[**FNIS\_V 210: Indigenous Politics and Self\-Determination**](https://cis.arts.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=210)  
Get into the politics, land claims, and self\-government struggles that define the relationship between Indigenous peoples and the Canadian government\.  
*Mon/Wed, 9:30 AM–11:00 AM*

---

[**HIST 217: Global History Before 1500: Eurasia**](https://history.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=217)  
Rewrite your mental map of the ancient world, from Sumer to the Caliphate, without Europe at the centre\.  
*Mon/Wed/Fri, 11:00 AM–12:00 PM*

---

[**PHIL\_V 222: Enriched Symbolic Logic**](https://philosophy.ubc.ca/courses/)  
Build formal proofs from the ground up, from set theory to predicate logic, if you like puzzles with actual right answers\.  
*Mon/Wed, 11:00 AM–12:30 PM*

---

[**PHIL\_V 230: Introduction to Ethics**](https://philosophy.ubc.ca/courses/)  
Wrestle with what actually makes something right or wrong, through classic and contemporary readings on obligation and value\.  
*Mon/Wed, 11:00 AM–12:00 PM*

---

[**PSYC\_V 203: Career Exploration Using Psychology**](https://psych.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=PSYC%20203)  
Apply psychology to your own career planning, with real strategies for figuring out what's next, not just theory about other people\.  
*Sec 002: Mon, 2:00 PM–3:00 PM\. Sec 003: Mon, 3:00 PM–4:00 PM*

---

[**AMNE\_V 318: The Roman Republic**](https://wd10.myworkday.com/ubc/d/inst/3$49941/13228$477174.htmld#TABTASKID=2998%2426057)  
Follow Rome from its founding through the collapse of the Republic, and see how empire\-building and political dysfunction fed each other\.  
*Mon/Wed/Fri, 2:00 PM–3:00 PM*

---

[**AMNE\_V 361: The Origins of Judaism**](https://wd10.myworkday.com/ubc/d/inst/3$49941/13228$477119.htmld#TABTASKID=2998%2426057)  
Trace the history of Jews and Judaism from the destruction of the First Temple to the roots of the rabbinic tradition\.  
*Mon/Wed/Fri, 10:00 AM–11:00 AM*

---

[**ASIA\_V 366: Queer/Early/Asias**](https://asia.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=ASIA%20366C)  
Dig into queer life across pre\-modern Asia through texts, field trips, and film screenings, and help re\-imagine queer Asian futures\.  
*Tue/Thu, 2:00 PM–3:30 PM*

---

[**ASIA\_V 373: History of Hong Kong**](https://hksi.ubc.ca/asia-hist-373/)  
Trace Hong Kong's transformation from a 19th\-century colonial port to one of the world's most distinct global cities\.  
*Mon/Wed, 2:00 PM–3:30 PM*

---

[**ASIX\_V 300: News, Information, and Knowledge in Early Modern East Asia**](https://asia.ubc.ca/courses/?academic-period=2026w&amp;search=ASIX%20300V)  
See how "fake news" anxieties played out across Ming China, Tokugawa Japan, and Joseon Korea centuries before social media\.  
*Mon, 11:00 AM–12:00 PM / Wed, 11:00 AM–1:00 PM*

---

[**LAST\_V 315A: Food in Mexico and Central America**](https://las.arts.ubc.ca/last-315/)  
Study corn, cuisine, and identity in Mexico and Central America, from pre\-colonial cosmologies to the politics of your dinner plate today\.  
*Mon/Wed, 2:00 PM–3:30 PM*

---

[**ASIX\_V 430: Indigenous Environmentalism in Asia**](https://scarp.ubc.ca/news/2026/new-course-generative-ai-cities-and-society)  
Explore how Indigenous environmentalism is embodied, experienced, and expressed across Asia and beyond, beginning with the xʷməθkʷəy̓əm land where the course is taught\.  
*Tue, 2:00 PM–3:30 PM*
