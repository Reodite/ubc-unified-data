> Interested in learning how Indigenous histories, cultures, and communities shape the world around us? These courses explore Indigenous stories, resistance, creativity, and community—from right here on xʷməθkʷəy̓əm \(Musqueam\) territory to other Indigenous lands around the world\.

You’ll explore big topics like power, identity, and place, and come away with fresh perspectives on the world and your role within it\.

Below is a list of some of the Winter Session 2026/27 Arts courses you can take to learn more about Indigenous histories and cultures\. These courses are open to all Arts students without prerequisites, and many also fulfil the Place and Power credit requirement for [BA students who started in 2024/25 or later](https://www.arts.ubc.ca/degree-planning/degree-requirements/bachelor-of-arts/#place-and-power)\.

***The course add/drop deadline for Winter Term 1 is Monday, September 21, 2026\.***

### **Indigenous Literature: NDN Time: Past and Presents Erased, Futures Foreclosed**

*ENGL\_V 373 \| Mon/Wed/Fri 3:00–4:00 PM*

Who gets to imagine the future, and who gets written out of it? This course explores Indigenous futurisms and speculative fiction, examining how Indigenous, Black, and racialized communities envision radical alternatives to mainstream narratives of what's to come\. You'll engage with Indigenous notions of time, kinship, and continuity as tools for imagining genuinely shared futures\.

Taught by [Dr\. Dallas Hunt](https://english.ubc.ca/profile/dallas-hunt/)

### **Place, Power, Poetics and Narrative Discourse**

*ENGL\_V 376A \| Mon/Wed/Fri 12:00–1:00 PM*

What does it mean to read literature here, on unceded xʷməθkʷəy̓əm land? This course pairs works by Musqueam and local Indigenous authors with texts from elsewhere, asking how place, power, and the act of reading are always connected\. You'll develop new frameworks for literary analysis rooted in Indigenous, diasporic, and settler ways of knowing\.

Taught by [Miranda Burgess](https://english.ubc.ca/profile/Miranda-Burgess/)

Learn more about ENGL\_V 376A

### **Francophone Indigenous Literatures in Dialogue**

*RMST\_V 326 \| Tue/Thu 2:00–3:30 PM*

What happens when Indigenous Peoples are separated from their lands by resource extraction? This course explores the work of Indigenous writers, filmmakers, and artists in Quebec and BC, examining life\-writing, testimony, autofiction, and dystopian fiction as tools for understanding relationships to land, culture, and kin\.

Taught by [Isabella Huberman](https://fhis.ubc.ca/profile/isabella-huberman/)

### **Arctic Art and Activism**

*CENS\_V 203, Sect\. 201 \| Tue/Thu 9:30–11:00 AM*

Indigenous peoples across the Arctic are facing unprecedented environmental and economic pressures from outside their communities\. This course explores neocolonialism in circumpolar regions, examining how Indigenous communities have fought to retain sovereignty over their lands and ways of life through collective action, art, and cultural revitalization\.

Taught by [Dr\. Tim Frandy](https://cenes.ubc.ca/profile/tim-frandy/)

### **Gender, Race and Indigeneity in Canada**

*GRSJ\_V 301 \| Tue/Thu 9:30–11:00 AM*

What do documented histories leave out, and what do narrated lives reveal? This course examines gender and Indigeneity through the recorded histories and lived experiences of Indigenous peoples in Canada, exploring how race, gender, and colonial structures have shaped and continue to shape Indigenous life\.

*Recommended for students who have completed GRSJ\_V 101 and GRSJ\_V 102, or have third\-year standing\.*

### **Reading the Bible on Indigenous Lands**

*AMNE\_V 384 \| Mon/Wed/Fri 10:00–11:00 AM*

How were biblical texts used to justify European settler colonialism in North America? This course examines the reception and appropriation of the Bible on Turtle Island, with a focus on British Columbia, and traces the impact of Christianity on Indigenous communities through the Indian Act and Residential School system\.

Taught by [Dr\. Kurtis Peters](https://amne.ubc.ca/profile/kurtis-peters/)

*ENGL\_V 376B \| Tue/Thu 11:00 AM–12:30 PM*

What does it mean to study literature on unceded xʷməθkʷəy̓əm land? This course foregrounds Musqueam teachings, stories, and relations alongside works by other Indigenous authors, exploring how literature and storytelling articulate identity, resurgence, and self\-determination\. You'll come away with a reshaped sense of what reading can be when approached through Indigenous ways of knowing\.

Taught by [Dr\. Dallas Hunt](https://english.ubc.ca/profile/dallas-hunt/)

### **Indigenous Latin America**

*LAST\_V 303 \| Tue/Thu 2:00–3:30 PM*

From the conquest to the present, this course surveys Indigenous cultures across Latin America, from the United States to Tierra del Fuego\. You'll explore a rich range of sources including chronicles, visual art, legal documents, novels, and oral history, drawing connections with the experience of Indigenous nations in Canada and critically examining concepts like decolonization and subalternity\.

### **Sámi Histories and Cultures**

*NORD\_V 340 \| Mon/Wed, 3:30 PM–5:00 PM*

Explore the diverse histories and cultures of Sámi people—the Indigenous Peoples of Norway, Sweden, Finland, and the Kola peninsula of Russia\. From reindeer to religion, from folklore to *Frozen II*, this course examines Sámi traditional culture, the impacts of settler colonialism upon it, and the ways revitalization and activism work as agents of decolonization today\.

Taught by [Dr\. Tim Frandy](https://cenes.ubc.ca/profile/tim-frandy/)

Learn more about NORD\_V 340
