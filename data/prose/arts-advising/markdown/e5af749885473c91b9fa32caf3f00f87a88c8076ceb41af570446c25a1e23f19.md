*By Brenda Gonzalez Cordova BA ‘21, former Arts Peer Advisor*

> If you’re a first year student entering UBC Arts, start by choosing a first\-year study option\.

I was an international student, so the road to UBC was more complicated for me than some others\. Dealing with immigration documents, finding housing, and my nervousness of leaving home already had me overwhelmed — not to mention the stress and excitement browsing courses brought\. Every subject and course sparked my curiosity and I had no idea how I was going to choose only 10 courses\.

It wasn’t until I received an email from the Faculty of Arts informing me of different first\-year options that I felt some direction\. When I learned that the [Coordinated Arts Program \(CAP\)](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/) follows a cohort model where I’d get the chance to build a tight knit community with peers and faculty throughout the year, I thought to myself — “this is the stability I need\.”

---

## What are first\-year study options?

You start your academic journey in the Bachelor of Arts by choosing a first\-year study option\. You can choose from three options:

1.  [Coordinated Arts Program \(CAP\)](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/)
2.  [Arts One](https://artsone.arts.ubc.ca/)
3.  Custom Timetable

Both Arts One and CAP can help ease your transition into university because they offer bundled collections of courses, smaller communities of instructors and peers, and enable you to cover some degree requirements in your first year\. For those wanting more flexibility, you may prefer a custom timetable, where you build your own course schedule from scratch\.

## The benefits of CAP

Since I chose CAP for my first\-year study option, I’ll share my experience with you, including the benefits of the program and how to register for it\.

CAP offers coordinated assignments within a timetable worth 18 credits for the entire Winter Session \(2 terms, September – April\)\. This means that you can register for 12 more credits of courses outside the program \(6 credits per term\)\. These courses, called “electives”, allow you to enhance your CAP experience by exploring additional subjects beyond the topics covered within CAP\.

In my first year, I was looking for courses that would not only match my interests, but also allow me to make close, supportive connections with students and instructors\. In this way, CAP’s cohort model was the perfect option for me\. I knew that my workload would be balanced through the coordinated assignment schedule and that I would perform well at university if I was given the opportunity to engage with topics in small group discussions\.

## Choosing a CAP stream

Another reason CAP stood out to me as my preferred first\-year program was because it lets you choose a “stream” or academic theme\. There are [five CAP streams](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/our-streams/) to choose from:

-   [Individual &amp; Society](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/streams/individual-society/)
-   [Law &amp; Society](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/streams/law-society/)
-   [Environment &amp; Society](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/streams/environment-society/)
-   [Media Studies](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/streams/media-studies/)
-   [Political Science, Philosophy, Economics \(PPE\)](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/streams/political-science-philosophy-economics-ppe/)

My main interests were in disciplines that explore the distribution of power, the causes of inequalities, and the development of a social contract, so I chose the PPE stream\. Because I still had 12 credits left to enrol in during first year, along with three more years of university, I knew I would have ample time to take other courses that piqued my curiosity\.

For those of you trying to decide which stream to take, I have some advice:

1.  Reflect on which stream topic interests you the most and through which disciplines would you like to explore that topic\.
2.  Look at upper\-level courses that interest you to see which lower level courses you need to satisfy prerequisites\.
3.  Think of how the different courses in a stream complement each other and reflect on the different paths you could take for your future studies\.

## Registering for CAP

To get started with registration in CAP, complete the simplified registration form on the [CAP website](https://cap.arts.ubc.ca/program/registration) until registration opens in Workday, or sign up in Workday once first\-year registration opens by choosing courses from your selected CAP stream\.

You can visit [Arts Academic Advising](https://www.arts.ubc.ca/student-support/academic-support/academic-advising/) for registration tips and advice about what to do if a course is full\.
