> Introducing the inspiring group of students who have impacted communities within Arts, at UBC, and beyond\. In 2024, we received 102 nominations and celebrate 74 students who have brought forth a wealth of interdisciplinary skills, demonstrating the incredible leadership and capacity our students have to improve their communities and the world\.

These students come from over 32 different majors that are represented within the Faculty of Arts, ranging from First Nations and Endangered Languages to Media Studies, Philosophy, Visual Arts, and many more\.

These student leaders held senior positions in student associations including the [UBC Alma Mater Society](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/), the [Arts Undergraduate Society](https://www.ubcaus.ca/), and departmental student associations\. They supported research in the [Indigenous Undergraduate Research Mentorship Program](https://indigenous.ubc.ca/students/current-students/experiential-learning/research-mentorship/), developed advocacy campaigns through the [Human Rights Collective](https://humanrightscollective.ubc.ca/), conducted seminars, and jumpstarted new initiatives such as the [Ukrainian Student Union](https://amscampusbase.ubc.ca/usu/home/)\. They extended their service leadership through positions with the AMS Sexual Assault Centre, Peer Programs, Athletics, and beyond\.

To learn more about their experiences and reflections as student leaders, we asked the winners and nominees how these opportunities have impacted and shaped their undergraduate journeys in their time as UBC Arts students\.

### Kiran Bhana, History

[Image](https://www.arts.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2024/03/Bhana-Kiran.png)

**What student leadership opportunity had the most impact on you during your time at UBC?**

The student leadership experience that has shaped me most profoundly has been my time with the [Thunderbird Marching Band](https://www.thunderbirdband.ca/)\. I’ve had to learn a lot about organization, to keep track of moving pieces such as event requests and rehearsal planning\. I’ve also had to develop the ability to make long\-term plans, so that I can plan larger events a little at a time\. I’ve had the privilege of meeting, befriending, working with, and learning from people with a broad array of perspectives\. I’ve been able to interact with audiences ranging from sports crowds to fundraiser attendees, which has pushed me as a musician and a leader to learn how to plan for and perform to many different audiences\. 

**What advice would you give to incoming UBC Arts students?**

Do not be afraid to try new things that interest you\! If a club seems interesting, attend a meeting or an event\. If a course looks intriguing, try it out\! If your professor mentions something during a lecture that draws your attention, they are probably eager to discuss more with you at office hours\. University is about academics, but it is equally about the experiences you gain in meeting new people and learning from them\.
