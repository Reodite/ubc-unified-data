> This month, UBC Arts faculty were featured in media outlets including *CBC, BBC, Politico, The Washington Post*, and *Forbes*\. Their expertise informed stories ranging from the real\-world history behind *The Odyssey* and the global story of pepper to AI and online regulation, B\.C\. politics, and the science of happiness and well\-being\.

### **Department of Psychology**

**How to have a happier vacation  
**Dr\. Elizabeth Dunn said vacation happiness can begin before a trip starts, noting that anticipation, shared experiences, and planning can boost well\-being\. She also highlighted the benefits of local getaways, new experiences, and reducing phone use while travelling\.  
[The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/2026/07/24/5-surprisingly-simple-ways-enjoy-your-vacation-more/)

**Family who lost 12\-year\-old son to cancer says ‘we are choosing to live’ by sharing in his passion  
**Dr\. Ingrid Söchting said understandings of grief have shifted away from the idea of moving through fixed stages, highlighting the concept of “continuing loving bonds” as a way people can honour loved ones while living alongside loss\.  
[CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/family-son-birding-9.7268948)

**Stop shaming yourself into better habits\. Science says this emotion works better  
**Drs\. Jiaying Zhao and Elizabeth Dunn argued that positive emotions such as joy are more effective than guilt or shame for building lasting habits, including climate\-friendly behaviours, and suggested focusing on positive additions rather than restrictions\.  
[Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kevinkruse/2026/07/08/stop-shaming-yourself-into-better-habits-science-says-this-emotion-works-better/)
