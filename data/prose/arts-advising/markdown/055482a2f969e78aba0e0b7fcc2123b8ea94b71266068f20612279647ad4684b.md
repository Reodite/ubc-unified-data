## Immigration and Pre\-arrival

**Do I need a study permit for UBC?**  
If you’re not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident and plan to study for longer than six months, you’ll need a valid study permit before you arrive\.

**How do I register for courses?**  
Winter Session registration for first\-year students opens in late June, and your registration date will arrive by email, typically one to two weeks before you are eligible\. You’re encouraged to attend an advising workshop, so you’re ready to plan and register with confidence\.

## Your first weeks at UBC Arts

**How can I make friends and meet new people at UBC Arts?**  
Six\-Week StART is a welcome program designed to help new students feel at home at UBC\. Over the first six weeks of term, you’ll meet fellow students, build connections, and find your footing \(socially and academically\) as life at UBC starts\.  

Learn more about Six\-Week StART

## Support throughout your UBC Arts degree

**What can Arts Academic Advising help with?**  
Arts Academic Advisors can support you throughout your degree—not just in first year\. Whether you’re planning your courses, navigating degree requirements, or working through registration issues, advisors are there to help you stay on track\.  

Learn about the events

*arrow\_right\_alt*

**Where can international students get dedicated support?**  
International Student Advisors are Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultants \(RCICs\) or Regulated International Student Immigration Advisors \(RISIAs\) who can help support you in your experience as a UBC international student\.

**Are there dedicated spaces for commuters?**  
UBC Collegia is a welcoming space on campus for students who commute\. Drop in between classes to socialize, use the kitchen and microwaves, and relax\. It is designed to give commuter students a place to feel at home on campus\.
