The Practicing Kinesiology Pathway covers core competencies that define what it means to be a practicing kinesiologist\. Students will develop and apply knowledge of movement\-based interventions and multi\-disciplinary approaches to enhance health, performance, and overall well\-being\. The pathway is available to all Bachelor of Kinesiology students\.    

By completing courses in this path, students can pursue membership in professional kinesiology associations, preparing them to work with individuals who are either apparently healthy or living with stable chronic conditions or disabilities in community or private settings\. Kinesiologist certification is regulated or administered provincially\. Check provincial kinesiology association to confirm eligibility requirements\.

Students pursuing the Practicing Kinesiology Pathway must complete the following lower\-level courses by the end of second year:  

-   KIN\_V 140 \(3\) Lifespan Motor Development
-   KIN\_V 220 \(3\) Exercise Testing and Prescription for Healthy Individuals with Special Considerations 

Students must complete the following upper\-level courses in third year:  

-   KIN\_V 320 \(3\) Exercise Prescription and Behaviour Change for Individuals with Chronic Disease and Disability
-   KIN\_V 324 \(3\) Prevention of Sports Injuries  
-   KIN\_V 330 \(3\) Clinical Exercise Physiology

Students must complete the following upper\-level courses in fourth year:  

-   KIN\_V 422 \(3\) Professional Practice in Kinesiology
-   Students must complete three courses from the Practicing Kinesiology Pathway elective list, with each course drawn from a different subject area\. Students intending to pursue certification with the British Columbia Association of Kinesiologists \(BCAK\) must ensure that at least one elective meets the Special Populations Health criteria \(\*\)\. If KIN\_V 320 is used to satisfy both the Clinical Kinesiology Assessment and Clinical Exercise Prescription categories, an additional course must be completed from any of the 14 core subject areas recognized by the BCAK\.

Practicing Kinesiology Pathway electives:

-   Indigenous Perspectives in Kinesiology: KIN\_V 368
-   Leadership Education for Pedagogy and Physical Activity: KIN\_V 344\*, 345, 442
-   Neuromechanics: KIN\_V 316, 411\*, 413\*, 415\*

### Clinical Exercise Physiology Specialization  

Students pursuing the Practicing Kinesiology Pathway may choose to further specialize in Clinical Exercise Physiology\. Students will develop and apply knowledge of exercise and education as a prevention, treatment, and management modality of chronic disease and to support healthy lifestyles\.  

By completing the required courses and fieldwork, students will be well\-prepared to challenge the Clinical Exercise Physiologist certification exams offered by the Canadian Society for Exercise Physiology\. This will prepare graduates to work with individuals with more complex, symptomatic chronic conditions, and disabilities in clinical settings\.

Bachelor of Kinesiology students interested in specializing in Clinical Exercise Physiology must formally apply\. Applications are submitted at the end of third year following the completion of the 300\-level requirements in the Practicing Kinesiology Pathway\. Admission is competitive and considers both academic performance and the quality of the submitted application\.

In addition to the courses required to complete the Practicing Kinesiology Pathway, students admitted into the Clinical Exercise Physiology Specialization need to complete the following upper\-level courses in fourth year:  

-   KIN\_V 421 \(3\) Advanced Exercise Prescription for Chronic Conditions: Evidence\-Informed Practice
-   KIN\_V 423 \(3\) Clinical Exercise Assessment 

Students must complete a minimum of three credits of clinical exercise work\-integrated learning courses from those listed below:

-   KIN\_V 471 \(3\) Exercise for Neurological and Balance Conditions
-   KIN\_V 473 \(6\) Kinesiology Field Work in Primary Care Medicine
-   KIN\_V 475 \(6\) Physical Activity for People with Spinal Cord Injury  

Students must also take at least one KIN\_V elective course from the Clinical Exercise Physiology Specialization electives list that meets the criteria for Special Populations Exercise Physiology \(\*\*\)\.  Those courses include: 

Clinical Exercise Physiology Specialization electives:

-   Neuromechanics: KIN\_V 411\*\*, 413\*\*, 415\*\*  
-   Systems Biology, Exercise &amp; Health: KIN\_V 424\*\*, 435\*\*, 438\*\*
-   Psychology of Movement: KIN\_V 351\*\*

Students admitted to the specialization will be registered in the Neuromechanical and Physiological Sciences Option\. Completion of the Clinical Exercise Physiology Specialization satisfies a minimum of six of the seven required upper\-level KIN\_V option courses and at least 12 of the 15 upper\-level KIN\_V elective credits required for the B\.Kin\. degree\.
