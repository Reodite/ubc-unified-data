## About Credit/D/Fail[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail\-grading/\#about\-credit\-d\-fail](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail-grading/#about-credit-d-fail)

The [Credit/D/Fail \(CR/D/F\) grading policy](https://senate.ubc.ca/vancouver/policies/credit-d-fail-standing/) was created to encourage students’ curiosity and learning in subjects outside of their own programs of study, and to introduce them to diverse fields\.

The policy was supported by the AMS Council and approved by the UBC Vancouver Senate in March of 2009\.

Credit/D/Fail is available to students in some direct\-entry undergraduate programs of study\. They can take eligible elective courses for Credit, D, or Fail standing instead of percentage grades\.

## How it appears on your transcript[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail\-grading/\#how\-it\-appears\-on\-your\-transcript](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail-grading/#how-it-appears-on-your-transcript)

If you choose the Credit/D/Fail option, you’ll be expected to complete all of the coursework and exams\. You will be assigned a percentage grade, but only the standing will be displayed on your grades record or official transcript\. These documents won’t display any percentage or letter grade\. 

You’ll get credit for courses with a Credit or D standing, but not for a Fail standing\. Courses that you take for CR/D/F won’t be counted toward your grade average calculations used for the purpose of academic performance evaluations\. However, these courses will still be considered when determining your overall [academic standing](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/campus-wide-policies-and-regulations/academic-standing)\. If you receive a Fail standing in too many courses, including those taken as CR/D/F, you could be placed on academic probation or asked to withdraw\.

The standings are awarded for the following percentage grades:

-   **CR:** 55% or higher
-   **D:** 50 to 54\.9%
-   **F:** less than 50%, or below the passing grade

For courses or programs that define a passing grade as higher than 50%, only **CR** or **F** may be awarded\.

## Eligibility checklist[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail\-grading/\#eligibility\-checklist](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail-grading/#eligibility-checklist)

You’ll be eligible to take courses for Credit/D/Fail \(CR/D/F\) grading if all of the following apply:

-   You’re in a direct\-entry undergraduate program, with the exception of Applied Science\.
-   Your program allows CR/D/F courses \(check with the [Academic Advising office](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/) for your program or degree\)\.
-   You understand the possible [awards implications](https://students.ubc.ca/finances/awards-scholarships-bursaries/maintaining-awards-loan-eligibility/)\.
-   Your course doesn’t require a percentage grade as a prerequisite to a course you may want to take in the future\.
-   The course is an elective in your program and you are otherwise meeting all other degree requirements\.
-   You’re not taking more than 6 CR/D/F credits in one Session \(Winter or Summer\)\.
-   You don’t take more than 12 CR/D/F credits in total toward your degree program\.

### Future programs

If you’re thinking of applying to a particular graduate or other program in the future, you should consider the possible impact of CR/D/F grading on that program’s admission requirements\.

## Awards implications[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail\-grading/\#awards\-implications](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail-grading/#awards-implications)

### Scholarships

Students must meet the minimum course load requirements to maintain scholarship eligibility\. These requirements are based on percentage\-graded credits so Credit/D/Fail \(CR/D/F\) grading, which is not percentage graded, may affect your scholarship eligibility\.

Read [UBC’s Regulations Governing University Awards](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/campus-wide-policies-and-regulations/university-awards) to get full details on minimum requirements for your award type\.

If you’re not sure how CR/D/F might affect your award eligibility, contact an [Enrolment Services Advisor](https://students.ubc.ca/about-student-services/enrolment-services-advisors/) to understand your eligibility and options\.

### Bursaries 

Credit/D/Fail grading does not impact your eligibility for bursaries, which are awarded based on financial need\.

## Switching your course back to percentage grading[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail\-grading/\#switching\-your\-course\-back\-to\-percentage\-grading](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/courses/creditdfail-grading/#switching-your-course-back-to-percentage-grading)

Please contact the [Academic Advising office](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/) of your undergraduate degree program prior to the add/drop deadline to change your course from Credit/D/Fail to percentage grading\. You cannot complete this on your own in Workday\.

### Switching after the add/drop date

Normally, switching from CR/D/F to percentage grading after the add/drop deadline is not permitted\. If you are experiencing extenuating circumstances, reach out to your faculty to discuss your options\.
