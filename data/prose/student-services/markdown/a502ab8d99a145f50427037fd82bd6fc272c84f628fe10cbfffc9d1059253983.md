If you left UBC to deal with a family emergency, to focus on your health and mental well\-being, academic issues, a job opportunity, or to take time away from studies, it can be hard to know where to start to return to your studies\.  
  
It’s important to start planning for your return as early as possible so you can meet various application and program\-specific deadlines and apply for a study permit, housing, or other services you may need to resume your studies\.

## First, review your scenario[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/readmission/\#first\-review\-your\-scenario](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/readmission/#first-review-your-scenario)

Find guidance on common return scenarios to understand what your return might look like, whether you took a leave of absence, had to withdraw, or are enrolling as a mature student or alumnus\.

[Read the scenarios](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/readmission/return-scenarios/)

## Next, follow the returning checklist[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/readmission/\#next\-follow\-the\-returning\-checklist](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/readmission/#next-follow-the-returning-checklist)

Once you’ve reviewed your return scenario, use the [Return to UBC Checklist](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/readmission/return-checklist/) to help you plan your next steps\. 

## If you need help[https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/readmission/\#if\-you\-need\-help](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/readmission/#if-you-need-help)

Contact [Enrolment Services](https://students.ubc.ca/about-student-services/enrolment-services-advisors/) during opening hours\.  
  
If you are an international student, connect with [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) first\.
