## About gaps in studies[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/immigration/taking\-time\-away\-school\-international/\#about\-gaps\-in\-studies](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/taking-time-away-school-international/#about-gaps-in-studies)

You may need time away from studies, whether it be for health reasons, a family emergency, because you are in\-between programs, or other reasons\.

All reasons for taking time away from studies will be referred to as “gaps” in the sections below\.

As an international student, you have specific considerations to think about when taking a gap\. Taking a gap could negatively impact:

1.  Your current [student status](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/#maintaining-your-student-status) in Canada
2.  Your current [eligibility to work](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/) on and off campus
3.  Your future eligibility for the [Post\-Graduation Work Permit](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/post-graduation-work-permit/) \(PGWP\)

As of Nov 8, 2024, if you [change institutions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/change-schools.html), you must apply for a study permit extension for your new institution and most students need to receive the new study permit before beginning studies at your new institution\.

If you are thinking of withdrawing from courses, see [immigration impacts of part\-time studies and withdrawing from courses](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/immigration-impacts-part-time-studies-withdrawing-courses/)\.

## Types of gaps[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/immigration/taking\-time\-away\-school\-international/\#types\-of\-gaps](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/taking-time-away-school-international/#types-of-gaps)

You must “actively pursue studies” in order to [maintain your student status](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/#maintaining-your-student-status) in Canada and your eligibility for the [Post\-Graduation Work Permit](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/post-graduation-work-permit/)\. The length and type of gap determine whether you are considered to be actively pursuing studies during that time\.

### Gaps considered actively pursuing studies if 150 days or less

-   UBC authorized leaves of absences \(see below to learn more\)
-   [Deferring your program start date](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/ubc-definitions-immigration-purposes/#deferred-enrolment) after arrival in Canada 
-   Undergraduate students on a [scheduled break](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/ubc-definitions-immigration-purposes/#scheduled-break) in the summer session
-   Strike or school closure

Your 150\-day period begins during the following times depending on your situation:

-   **Undergraduate students on authorized leave**  
    If it is not your first term of study and you dropped all courses before the [add/drop deadline](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/course-change-dates/), your leave would start the first day of classes for the term\. If you withdraw with a “W”, your leave would start on the day you withdrew from all courses\. 
-   **Graduate students on an authorized leave**  
    Your leave begins on either the first day of classes of the term, or the day your leave was approved by G\+PS —whichever comes last\. For more details, review the [leave of absence policy](https://www.grad.ubc.ca/current-students/managing-your-program/leave-absence) for graduate students\.
-   **Changing programs at UBC after finishing your program**  
    The day your [letter of completion](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/post-graduation-work-permit/#when-to-apply-for-a-pgwp) first becomes available
-   **Deferring your start date after arrival in Canada**  
    Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\.

### Gaps which are not considered actively pursuing studies

-   Approved leaves that are longer than 150 days
-   Gaps beyond 150 days when deferring program start date after arrival in Canada 
-   Gaps as a result of changing programs at UBC without finishing your program
-   Any other gap not on the approved list above
-   Gaps beyond 150 days due to a strike or school closure

## Next steps[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/immigration/taking\-time\-away\-school\-international/\#next\-steps](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/taking-time-away-school-international/#next-steps)

**

If you have taken a gap in the past

**

If your study permit is [still valid](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/), no immediate action is necessary until you make your next immigration application, such as for a post\-graduation work permit\. However, if your study permit is no longer valid, or you are unsure if your study permit is still valid, contact International Student Advising for support\.

Collect as many documents as you can related to the reason for your gap in studies\. See “documents to prepare” below for more information\.

**If you took a gap in studies during Winter Term 2 2019 \(January to April 2020\) or Summer Session 2020 \(September to May 2020\)**

There was a temporary IRCC policy for these terms due to the onset of COVID\-19\. See our [FAQs for details](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-immigration-health-insurance-faq/#online-courses-and-pgwp)\.

**If you are an undergraduate student who took a gap in studies in Winter Session 2020 \(September 2020 to April 2021\) or earlier**

Authorized leave for undergraduate students was not available at this time and you would not have been considered actively pursuing studies\. Connect with [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\.

**If you are an undergraduate student who took a gap in studies in **or after September** 2021**

Starting September 2021, under the International Undergraduate Leave Procedure, students in good academic standing could request a leave letter to show they were on leave for one or both winter terms\. If you took one or both terms off, request a letter through your academic advising office as soon as possible\. If you have a leave letter, keep it until you need to show it to IRCC\. If you are unable to get a leave letter, or your leave was longer than 150 days, contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\.

**

If you take a gap which is considered actively pursuing studies

**

**Status in Canada and eligibility to stay**

You are considered to be [maintaining your student status](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/#maintaining-your-student-status) and can stay in Canada for up to 150 days if your gap is considered to be active pursuit of studies\. No immediate action is necessary if you will be on a gap that is less than 150 days or before your study permit expires, whichever comes first\. 

Summer is considered a [scheduled break](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/ubc-definitions-immigration-purposes/#scheduled-break) for undergraduate students, and no enrolment in courses is required\. You do not need to request an approved leave in order to take a gap during the summer, and you may be eligible to work full time during the summer scheduled break if you meet the eligibility criteria\. The summer scheduled break is considered an active pursuit of study for undergraduate students\.

**Working during your gap**

As soon as you are no longer enrolled in full\-time studies, you are not eligible to [work in Canada](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/) during an academic term, and also not eligible to work during scheduled breaks before and after the term in which you are taking a gap\. If you have a co\-op work permit, you cannot use it for work unrelated to studies\. To be eligible to work while you are on a gap, you must have a valid work permit which is unrelated to studies, which in general can be difficult to obtain\. 

**Impact on your Post\-Graduation Work Permit \(PGWP\) in the future**

In general, your eligibility for the [PGWP](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/post-graduation-work-permit/) should not be impacted if you are on a gap which is considered actively pursuing studies for less than 150 days, however the officer who reviews your application will make the final decision\. You should collect as many documents as you can related to the reason for your gap in studies\. See “documents to prepare” below for more information\. 

When you apply for the PGWP, write a letter of explanation about the reason for your gap in studies and provide as many supporting documents as possible\. Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for assistance\.

**

If you take a gap that is an authorized leave of absence longer than 150 days

**

**Status in Canada and eligibility to stay**

A gap that is longer than 150 days is not considered to be actively pursuing studies\. 

This applies even if you have a UBC authorized leave of absence that is longer than 150 days, for example, an authorized leave for one year\. Because a gap that is longer than 150 days is not considered to be an active pursuit of studies, you must take action in order to remain compliant with the conditions of your study permit\. 

Before the first 150\-day period is over, you must do **one** of the following:

-   Apply for a [Visitor Record](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/extend-stay.html) to stay in Canada as a visitor, which does not allow you to work\.
-   Hold a [work permit](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/permit.html) unrelated to studies \(if eligible\)\. In general, these are difficult to qualify for and you may find an [authorized immigration representative](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/find-regulated-immigration-consultant-or-lawyer/) outside UBC for advice\.
-   Leave Canada\.
-   Start or resume studies\. If you [change institutions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/change-schools.html), as of Nov 8, 2024, you must apply for a study permit extension for your new institution and most students need to receive the new study permit before beginning studies at your new institution\. Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\. If you plan to return to UBC, check with an [Academic Advisor](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/) from your faculty to see if your credits can be transferred back to UBC or count towards your program requirements\.

If your authorized leave is longer than 150 days, your study permit may become invalid\. This could impact your eligibility to remain in Canada, as well as your ability to return to your studies\. Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) to discuss your situation and options\.

**Exception**: UBC undergraduate students on authorized leave beginning in Winter Term 2 do not need to take further action on your status in the summer scheduled break since UBC considers undergraduate students during summer to be on a [scheduled break](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/ubc-definitions-immigration-purposes/#scheduled-break) for immigration purposes\.

**Working during your gap**

As soon as you are no longer enrolled in full\-time studies, you are not eligible to [work in Canada](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/) during an academic term, and also not eligible to work during scheduled breaks before and after the term in which you are taking a gap\. If you have a co\-op work permit, you cannot use it for work unrelated to studies\. To be eligible to work while you are on a gap, you must have a valid work permit which is unrelated to studies, which in general can be difficult to obtain\. 

**Impact on your Post\-Graduation Work Permit \(PGWP\) in the future**

Taking gaps which are not considered actively pursuing studies can impact your PGWP eligibility\. You should collect as many documents as you can related to the reason for your gap in studies\. See “documents to prepare” below for more information\. 

When you apply for the PGWP, write a letter of explanation about the reason for your gap in studies and provide as many supporting documents as possible\. You can still submit a PGWP application; however, unfortunately, there is no guarantee the application will be approved\. The officer who reviews your application will decide whether or not to approve the application on a case\-by\-case basis based on your individual circumstances and will make the final decision\.

**

If you take a gap which is not considered actively pursuing studies

**

**Status in Canada and eligibility to stay**

You are not considered to be meeting your requirements as a student and cannot remain in Canada during the gap without taking further action\.

You must do **one** of the following before your gap begins:

-   Apply for a [Visitor Record](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/extend-stay.html) to stay in Canada as a visitor, which does not allow you to work or study in Canada\. You should submit your application for a visitor record before your gap begins\.
-   Hold a [work permit](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/permit.html) unrelated to studies \(if eligible\)\. In general, these are difficult to qualify for and you may find an [authorized immigration representative](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/find-regulated-immigration-consultant-or-lawyer/) outside UBC for advice\.
-   Leave Canada for the duration of the gap\.
-   Start or resume studies\. As of **Nov 8, 2024**, if you [change institutions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/change-schools.html), you must apply for a study permit extension for your new institution and most students need to receive the new study permit before beginning studies at your new institution\. Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\. If you plan to return to UBC, ask an [Academic Advisor](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/) from your faculty if your credits can be transferred back to UBC or count towards your program requirements\. If you will need to take a gap because you have been required to withdraw from UBC due to a failed year, contact International Student Advising as soon as possible for support\. 

If you are not actively enrolled at UBC or on an approved academic leave, your study permit may become invalid\. This could impact your eligibility to remain in Canada, as well as your ability to return to your studies\. Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) to discuss your situation and options\.

**Work eligibility**

As soon as you are no longer enrolled in full\-time studies, you are not eligible to [work in Canada](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/) during the academic term, and are also not eligible to work during scheduled breaks before and after the term in which you are taking a gap\. If you have a co\-op work permit, you cannot use it for work unrelated to studies\. To be eligible to work you must have a work permit which is unrelated to studies, which in general can be difficult to obtain\.

**Impact on your Post\-Graduation Work Permit \(PGWP\) in the future**

Taking gaps which are not considered actively pursuing studies can impact your PGWP eligibility\. You should collect as many documents as you can related to the reason for your gap in studies\. See “documents to prepare” below for more information\. 

When you apply for the PGWP, write a letter of explanation about the reason for your gap in studies and provide as many supporting documents as possible\. You can still submit a PGWP application; however, unfortunately, there is no guarantee the application will be approved\. The officer who reviews your application will decide whether or not to approve the application on a case\-by\-case basis based on your individual circumstances and will make the final decision\.

**

If you might take a gap in the future: Authorized Leave

**

**Undergraduate students**

Starting from the 2021/22 Winter Session, UBC offers a procedure for international undergraduate students to document their authorized leaves\. The undergraduate leave procedure allows you to take time away from studies and still be considered to be actively pursuing studies, maintaining the conditions of your study permit and maintaining eligibility for the Post\-Graduation Work Permit program for leaves that are up to 150 days\.

UBC Vancouver Senate’s  [Academic Leave Policy](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/campus-wide-policies-and-regulations/academic-leave) enables students in good academic standing to take up to a year away from studies without having to reapply for admission, though note that leaves longer than 150 days may have an immigration impact\.

If you want to prove to IRCC that you were on an authorized leave, you must request the leave and receive a letter from UBC confirming that you are authorized to take the leave\.

**When to drop or withdraw from courses**

-   If it is your first term in your program:
    -   If Winter Term 1 is your first term in your program and you drop all courses before the [last day to drop courses without a W](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/course-change-dates/), and you are registered in Term 2, you would be considered to have deferred enrolment for immigration purposes\. You would not need and would not be eligible for an authorized leave in Term 1\.
    -   If in your first term in your program you withdraw from all courses after the [Course change dates](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/course-change-dates/) with a W, you might qualify for the Academic Leave Policy\. The leave would start on the day you withdraw\.
-   If it is not your first term in your program:
    -   If you drop all courses before the [last day to drop courses without a W](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/course-change-dates/), then your leave would start on the first day of classes for that term, or 
    -   If you withdraw from courses before the [deadline to drop a course with a W](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/course-change-dates/), then your leave would start on the day you withdrew from all courses\.

**If you withdraw** ***after*** **the deadline to drop a course with a W**, you will not be eligible for an authorized leave for that term\.

**When to request a leave letter**

You can request a leave letter as soon as registration opens for the term\(s\) for which you are requesting a leave letter\. We recommend you request a leave letter by mid\-October for leaves starting Term 1, or mid\-February for leaves starting Term 2, so your leave will be recorded accurately in the Immigration Report which IRCC requires from UBC each winter term\.

**Eligibility for a leave letter**

To be eligible to request a leave letter, you must meet all of the following requirements:

-   Request a leave letter for one or more winter terms in Winter Session 2021 \(starting September 2021\) or later
-   Be eligible to register as of the first day of classes for each term for which you’re requesting a leave letter
-   Have no active registration in the term\(s\) for which you’re requesting a leave letter
-   Request a leave letter for a maximum of one full academic session \(Winter Term 1 \+ 2\) plus any number of one\-term leaves per program; however, programs reserve the right to deny leave letters if sufficient academic progress is not being made
-   Be an international student in a program that leads to a UBC degree, diploma or certificate and to which *graduate* student leave policies don’t apply
-   Have withdrawn from courses in time \(read “when to drop or withdraw from courses” above\)
-   Meet eligibility as specified by your faculty’s academic advising office

**How to request a leave letter**

If you are considering taking a gap in studies, follow these steps:

1.  After reading this page, carefully consider whether you will take Term 1, Term 2, or both terms away from studies\.
2.  Talk to an [Academic Advisor](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/) to check your program requirements and discuss your options for taking a gap and returning to studies later\. You must meet all academic regulations of your program\. Taking a leave does not guarantee courses will be available when you return to studies\. 
3.  Carefully review how taking time away from studies impacts your immigration and other potential impacts on this page, particularly if you will be on leave for more than 150 days and will be in Canada during your leave\. Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) if you have any questions about the immigration and health insurance impacts of taking a gap\.
4.  After you withdraw from all courses in Term 1, Term 2, or both terms, send a request to your [Academic advising office](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/)\. Tell them you are requesting a leave and for which term\(s\)\.
5.  If they agree, the authorized leave letter will be emailed to you\. The leave will not appear on your transcript\. Keep the authorized leave letter on file, and include it with future IRCC applications as needed\.

Your authorized leave letter will say when your leave began\. You can remain in Canada for 150 days after that date\.

Leaves longer than 150 days affect your Post\-Graduation Work Permit eligibility and status in Canada\.

**If you are not eligible for an authorized leave**

If you take a gap in your studies and are not eligible for an authorized leave, there could be serious impacts to your status in Canada and the validity of your study permit\. Please connect with [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) as soon as possible for support\.

**Graduate students and students in the Diploma in Accounting and Juris Doctor programs**

If your leave of absence is approved, you could remain in Canada for 150 days after:

-   The day your leave was approved, or
-   The first day of classes of the term in which your leave starts, whichever comes last\.

If you plan to take a gap:

1.  After reading this page, carefully consider which in term\(s\) you plan to take time away from studies\.
2.  If you are a graduate student, review UBC’s [Graduate Student Leaves of Absence policy](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/faculties-colleges-and-schools/faculty-graduate-and-postdoctoral-studies/classification-students/leave-status)\.
3.  Talk to your [program staff](https://www.grad.ubc.ca/current-students/supervision-advising/program-advising) to discuss your options for taking a gap and returning to studies later, as well as any impacts on funding\.
4.  Contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) if you have any questions about the immigration and health insurance impacts of taking a gap\. 

## Documents to prepare[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/immigration/taking\-time\-away\-school\-international/\#documents\-to\-prepare](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/taking-time-away-school-international/#documents-to-prepare)

Whether you have had a gap in studies in the past, are currently on a gap, or plan to take a gap in studies in the future, you are **not** required to notify IRCC right away\. 

However, no matter the reason or length of your gap, you **must keep documentation** in case you are asked to show why you took the leave and/or include them in your future Post\-Graduation Work Permit application\. Prepare the below documents and connect with an International Student Advisor before submitting your application\.

### Step 1\. Download the UBC Acknowledgment Letter

This [UBC Acknowledgement Letter](https://students.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/ubc_acknowledgment_letter.pdf) \(pdf\) affirms UBC’s policy towards time taken away \(e\.g\., gaps\) from studies\.

### Step 2\. Create a timeline

Create a list in chronological order of all decisions, events, and action steps leading up to your gap in studies\. Include key dates which would be helpful for the officer to understand your circumstances; for example, the date that you withdrew from courses, received approval for a deferral, left and returned to Canada, received medical care, had a family emergency occur, etc\. Keep this timeline for your records in case you are asked for information from IRCC or need to write a letter of explanation for a future immigration application\.  

### Step 3\. Gather supporting documents

Gather as many documents as possible related to your reason for your gap in studies and keep scanned and original paper copies \(if applicable\) in an accessible, safe place\. 

Examples include a doctor’s recommendation for treatment, hospital documents, flight tickets showing when you left and returned to Canada, letters issued by your program or faculty, documents relating to a family emergency, confirmation of your internship or military service, etc\. Any documents which are not in English or French will need an official translation if you submit them to IRCC\.

#### Letter of support

If possible, request a letter of support with the below guidelines:

-   The person should be aware of the reasons you took a gap, for example a doctor, counsellor, or advisor\.
-   The letter should contain factual information to attest to the reason for your gap in studies\.
-   Individuals can only attest to things they have not observed or witnessed themselves\.

## Potential impacts of taking a gap[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/immigration/taking\-time\-away\-school\-international/\#potential\-impacts\-of\-taking\-a\-gap](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/taking-time-away-school-international/#potential-impacts-of-taking-a-gap)

### Traveling on a gap

Ensure your [study permit is valid](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/) and that your entry document \([Temporary Resident Visa](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/temporary-resident-visa-trv/) or [Electronic Travel Authorization](https://onlineservices-servicesenligne.cic.gc.ca/eta/applicationQuery)\) is valid to return to Canada\. See our [Travelling to Canada guide](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/travelling-to-canada/) to learn about what documents to prepare before you travel\. If you had a gap that is not considered “actively pursuing studies”, are changing institutions, or had a gap of more than 150 days, contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\. 

If you have not registered for courses before returning to Canada, request a letter from your academic advisor or graduate staff before re\-entering Canada\. This letter should state that you are eligible to return to your program of studies at the end of your gap\. Upon return to Canada, if you are asked by an officer, present this letter along with any documentation of your break \(e\.g\., leave approval from G\+PS if applicable, copies of plane tickets proving when you left Canada, etc\.\)\.

### Study permit validity and extension during a gap

After your gap, you can resume studies on your current study permit if it is [still valid](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/)\. If you had a gap that is not considered “actively pursuing studies”, are changing institutions, or had a gap of more than 150 days, contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\.

If your study permit will expire or become invalid during your gap, contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) to discuss your options to apply for a study permit from inside or outside Canada\. You may need to provide additional documents to explain how you plan to resume your studies\.

### Medical Services Plan \(MSP\)

It is very important to maintain valid health insurance at all times to avoid medical bills and to help you access health support when you need it\. Your MSP coverage is usually valid for the same length as your study permit\.

If you will leave Canada for an extended time or your study permit will expire during your gap, see our [MSP page](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/medical-services-plan-msp-international/) for information on next steps\.

### AMS/GSS/UBCSUO Health and Dental

Check if you maintain eligibility for the [Alma Mater Society \(AMS\)](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/student-services/health-dental/), [Graduate Student Society \(GSS\)](http://gss.ubc.ca/health-dental/), or [UBC Students’ Union Okanagan \(UBCSUO\)](https://www.suo.ca/health-dental/) Health and Dental Plan\. Coverage ends on August 31 each year and you must have basic health insurance, such as MSP, to be able to make a claim\. 

### Social Insurance Number

Your [Social Insurance Number \(SIN\)](https://students.ubc.ca/finances/taxes/social-insurance-number-sin/) remains valid until the expiry date, however you can only [work in Canada](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/) if you are enrolled full\-time for immigration purposes, or are eligible to work on a scheduled break\. Having a valid SIN is not sufficient to work in Canada\.

### Dependents’ status

If your common\-law partner or spouse has a work permit as a result of your study permit, it remains valid until the expiry date indicated on the work permit unless there is a condition which says otherwise\.

If you have children, they may continue to study with or without a study permit while your study permit is valid, even if you have applied for visitor status\. 

However, if you are on visitor status due to taking a gap of 150 days or more and your study permit and your spouse’s open study or work permit will expire, your children must obtain their own study permit\.

See [documentation for your family](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/documentation-family/) for more information and contact [International Student Advising](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/international-student-advising/) for support\.

### Scholarships/awards or other funding

If you are currently receiving funding, such as a fellowship, scholarship or award, check with your funding agency to see if taking a gap will impact your eligibility\.

If you are an undergraduate student and have financial assistance or awards, check with an [Enrolment Services Advisor](https://students.ubc.ca/about-student-services/enrolment-services-advisors/) to see if taking a gap will affect your funding\.

If you are a graduate student and receive funding, check with your [graduate program staff](https://www.grad.ubc.ca/current-students/supervision-advising/program-advising) to see if taking a gap will affect your funding\.

### Eligibility for on\-campus housing

Ask about your eligibility to remain in on\-campus housing with [Student Housing and Community Services](https://vancouver.housing.ubc.ca/applications/eligibility/)\.
