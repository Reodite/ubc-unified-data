Many substances, including alcohol, cannabis, nicotine \(in tobacco and some vapes\), and caffeine can alter someone’s mental, emotional, or physical state\. All substance use carries some risk, and it’s essential to consider the short\-term and long\-term effects on your wellbeing as a student\.  

No unregulated drug is safe, and prescription drugs not dispensed directly from a pharmacy also come with risks\. People should use caution with taking any unfamiliar prescription medications\.

Fentanyl and carfentanyl are strong opioid painkillers that are in the unregulated drug supply in Vancouver\. A miniscule amount of fentanyl or analogs \(carfentanyl\) can be fatal\. Fentanyl and benzodiazepines are two categories of drugs that are often found in unregulated drugs\.

You can learn more about [fentanyl](https://www.camh.ca/en/health-info/mental-illness-and-addiction-index/street-fentanyl) and about [benzodiazepines](https://www.camh.ca/en/health-info/mental-illness-and-addiction-index/anti-anxiety-medications-benzodiazepines) on the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health website\.

Other unregulated sedatives including veterinary tranquilizers like [Xylazine](https://towardtheheart.com/assets/uploads/1660762743kH775J4lVykS90oUsfpve5BeVpxIoJ9ZqpgPp3e.pdf) \(pdf\) have also been found in the unregulated drug supply\. For more information on toxic drug alerts, visit [Toward the Heart](https://towardtheheart.com/alerts), where you can also sign\-up for text message alerts\.

When using unregulated drugs, there is no way to know the exact contents of those drugs\. One form of harm reduction is [drug checking](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-topics/substance-use-health-harm-reduction#drug-checking-testing), which can provide you with more information about the contents of those drugs\. 

Not using substances altogether has the lowest risk\. If you are using drugs, you can make a plan to reduce possible harms\. 

## Harm reduction and safer drug use[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/substance\-use\-health\-harm\-reduction/\#harm\-reduction\-and\-safer\-drug\-use](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/#harm-reduction-and-safer-drug-use)

If you use or intend to use unregulated drugs, including prescription drugs not directly dispensed from a pharmacy, there are steps you can take to reduce potential harms: 

-   Go slow, start with a low dosage\.
-   Make a plan [for safer uses](https://towardtheheart.com/safer-use)\.
-   Have your [drugs checked](https://wellbeing.gov.bc.ca/substance-use/harm-reduction/drug-checking)\.
-   Sign up for [toxic drug and public health alerts](https://towardtheheart.com/alerts)\.  
-   Know how to recognize and respond to the signs of an overdose or toxic drug poisoning [below](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/#recognize-the-signs-of-an-overdose-or-drug-poisoning)\.
-   Carry [naloxone](https://vancouver.housing.ubc.ca/residence-life/health-safety/safety-security/#naloxone) and other [harm reduction supplies](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/support-services/student-resources/ubc-harm-reduction-resources/)\. 
-   Do not use alone\. When possible, go to a [supervised consumption site](https://www.vch.ca/en/service/supervised-consumption-and-overdose-prevention-sites) in\-person, or use virtual supports like [NORS](https://www.nors.ca/) or [LifeGuard](https://lifeguarddh.com/)\.

Find out more by taking the [Harm Reduction 101 Canvas course](https://canvas.ubc.ca/enroll/JEAEHK)\. You can also request a workshop from [UBC Wellness Centre](https://students.ubc.ca/health/wellness-resources/wellness-centre/) and [AMS Peer Support](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/support-services/student-services/peer-support/)\. You can also [access the Naloxone 101 Training course](https://learninghub.phsa.ca/Courses/8458) from Toward the Heart\.

## Recognize the signs of an overdose or drug poisoning[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/substance\-use\-health\-harm\-reduction/\#recognize\-the\-signs\-of\-an\-overdose\-or\-drug\-poisoning](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/#recognize-the-signs-of-an-overdose-or-drug-poisoning)

Look for the [following signs](https://respondtooverdose.gov.bc.ca/#recognize): 

-   Not moving and can’t be woken up, or unresponsive 
-   Breathing is slow or stopped 
-   Choking, gurgling or snoring sounds 
-   Lips and nails may be a blue or ashy colour 
-   Skin is cold and clammy 
-   Pupils are tiny\.

## Responding to an overdose or drug poisoning[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/substance\-use\-health\-harm\-reduction/\#responding\-to\-an\-overdose\-or\-drug\-poisoning](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/#responding-to-an-overdose-or-drug-poisoning)

If someone is experiencing a known or suspected overdose, there is no time to waste\. 

1.  Immediately call [911](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-topics/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/) 
2.  Give Naloxone 
3.  Call [Community Safety](http://communitysafety.ubc.ca/): [604 822 2222](tel:604%20822%202222) 

You are protected by the [Good Samaritan Drug Overdose Act](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/opioids/about-good-samaritan-drug-overdose-act.html) even if you have a small amount of substance on you\. You will not get in trouble with UBC, Student Housing, the police, or Citizenship and Immigration Canada for calling 911\. 

To learn more about the Good Samaritan Act and to find out how to respond to accidental drug poisoning, enrol in the [Harm Reduction 101 Canvas course](https://canvas.ubc.ca/enroll/JEAEHK)\.

You can pick up free nasal and intramuscular \(injectable\) naloxone kit to carry with you, as well as training on how to administer it, at the following locations:

**

**

AMS Peer Support

**

**

BC pharmacies

**

## When to seek help for problematic substance use[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/substance\-use\-health\-harm\-reduction/\#when\-to\-seek\-help\-for\-problematic\-substance\-use](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/#when-to-seek-help-for-problematic-substance-use)

If you are curious about seeking help, consider the following questions, and explore support options available to UBC students below\. 

-   Are you curious to explore your relationship to substance use in a way that can positively impact your wellbeing? 
-   Do you wish to change your relationship with alcohol and/or other substances? 
-   Has your relationship with substances impacted your daily life and/or relationships? 
-   Are you looking to explore resources or reach out to someone to talk to about substance use?

## Peer support[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/substance\-use\-health\-harm\-reduction/\#peer\-support](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/#peer-support)

It might be easier to talk with a trained peer\-support student staff about substance use\. They may understand what you’re going through and can offer helpful resources\.

The [UBC Student Recovery Community \(SRC\)](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/ubc-student-recovery-community/) is a safe, confidential, and inclusive space for students who are in recovery or curious to explore their relationship with substances and/or non\-substance addictive behaviours such as disordered eating, pornography, gaming, gambling, and more\.

You can also visit [AMS Peer Support](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/student-services/peer-support/) for free, confidential one\-on\-one peer support, naloxone training for individuals and groups, fentanyl test strips, peer support group sessions, and workshops on mental health and harm reduction\.

## The intentional use of substances to facilitate sexual assault[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/substance\-use\-health\-harm\-reduction/\#the\-intentional\-use\-of\-substances\-to\-facilitate\-sexual\-assault](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/#the-intentional-use-of-substances-to-facilitate-sexual-assault)

Using drugs, including alcohol, to deliberately create a situation in which a person is incapacitated \(and therefore cannot give consent\) to pursue sexual contact or attention is [substance\-facilitated sexual assault](https://students.ubc.ca/health/substance-use-health-harm-reduction/intentional-use-substances-facilitate-sexual-assault/)\. 

Anyone who has been impacted by substance\-facilitated sexual assault deserves support\. Confidential, non\-judgmental support is available at the [Sexual Violence Prevention and Response Office](https://svpro.ubc.ca/support/) \(SVPRO\)\.
