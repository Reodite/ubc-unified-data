## About student work placements[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/working\-canada/co\-op\-or\-student\-work\-placements/\#about\-student\-work\-placements](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#about-student-work-placements)

A [student work placement](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/intern.html), often called a Co\-op, is a work experience, such as a UBC Co\-op, practicum, or work\-integrated learning experience, that is approved by your UBC program or UBC Co\-op office and required for the completion of your degree program\. These can be paid or unpaid positions or volunteer opportunities, and cannot make up more than 50% or your program of study\.

In most cases, you are enrolled in a formal Co\-op, practicum, or work\-integrated learning course or term in order for the student work placement to be considered authorized\. 

If you are not sure whether your work is required for your academic program, please contact an [Academic Advisor](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/) from your faculty or your [graduate program advisor](https://www.grad.ubc.ca/current-students/supervision-advising/program-advising)\. You will need a letter from your faculty or program to prove that the work placement is a program requirement\. Learn more about [UBC Co\-op programs](https://coop.ubc.ca/students/how-co-op-works)\.

### What’s not considered a student work placement

A work placement or internship is not considered a student work placement for immigration purposes if **all** of the following is true:

-   It is arranged independently by the student\. 
-   It isn’t a mandatory requirement of the academic program\.
-   It does not appear on the student’s transcript\.

### Changes to the Co\-op work permit requirement

As of April 1, 2026, you do not need a work permit for required student work placements, such as a UBC Co\-op, internship, or practicum, provided you meet the [eligibility requirements below](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#confirm-eligibility)\. 

While a work permit is no longer required, you still need to be admitted into a Co\-op program or registered in a formal required practicum term, work\-integrated learning course, or similar program\-required student work placement course or term\.

If you submitted an application for a Co\-op work permit before April 1, 2026, IRCC will likely withdraw your application and provide you with a letter stating that a Co\-op work permit is not required\.

## Confirm your eligibility for a student work placement[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/working\-canada/co\-op\-or\-student\-work\-placements/\#confirm\-your\-eligibility\-for\-a\-student\-work\-placement](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#confirm-your-eligibility-for-a-student-work-placement)

To be eligible for a student work placement, you must meet **all** of the following requirements:

-   You are enrolled as a full\-time student and not currently on leave from your studies\.
-   You have a valid study permit, or you applied to extend your study permit before it expired\.
-   You have conditions printed on your initial study permit that indicate you are allowed to work **on campus\.**
-   You have a UBC letter from your Co\-op office or program indicating that the work is a requirement for your program of study\.
-   Your employer is approved by your Co\-op office or program\.
-   You are registered in a UBC\-approved Co\-op, practicum, work\-integrated learning course, or another similar program\-required student work placement course or term, in which case you are considered [full\-time for immigration purposes](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/ubc-definitions-immigration-purposes/#full-time)\.
-   Your program of study is at least 6 months in duration, and will lead to a degree, diploma, or certificate from UBC, unless you are a visiting or exchange student who will not receive a credential from UBC\.
-   You are **not** studying as an Access or Unclassified student\.
-   Your primary program of study is **not** English or French as a second language\.

If you’re not eligible for a student work placement, you may be authorized to work on and off campus during your studies for other types of employment, based on your study permit’s work authorization if you meet the [eligibility requirements](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/#permits-required-to-work-in-canada)\.

Usually, [off\-campus work](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/off-campus-work/) is limited to 24 hours per week during academic terms\.

## Complete a medical exam, if applicable[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/working\-canada/co\-op\-or\-student\-work\-placements/\#complete\-a\-medical\-exam\-if\-applicable](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#complete-a-medical-exam-if-applicable)

If you will be working in jobs which require a [medical exam](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/#if-you-work-with-children-or-seniors-in-healthcare-settings-or-in-agriculture), such as working with children or seniors, in healthcare settings, clinical laboratories, or in agriculture, you must complete a medical exam and have the right conditions on your study permit before starting your position\. 

Even if you do not work directly with vulnerable populations, you might require a medical exam based on your place of work, no matter what your position is\. Examples include in\-person positions in a hospital \(even if you do not directly care for patients, such as research, administrative, or janitorial positions\) or in a daycare \(even if you do not directly care for children, such as administrative positions\)\. 

If you will likely work in these kinds of positions, or you would like to keep your options open, it is best to complete the medical exam before you apply for your study permit\. Include a brief explanation letter with your study permit application stating that you would like proper conditions on your study permit to work in restricted occupations\.

It’s also possible to take the medical exam after you’ve received your study permit, then apply to change the conditions on your permit afterwards\. However, you will not be able to start the position until you have received a study permit with appropriate conditions\.

## When you can start working[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/working\-canada/co\-op\-or\-student\-work\-placements/\#when\-you\-can\-start\-working](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#when-you-can-start-working)

You can only start your UBC student work placement once you meet all of the following requirements: 

-   You fulfill the eligibility requirements [listed above](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#confirm-eligibility)\.
-   You have received a UBC letter of acceptance or approval from your Co\-op office or program\. Keep it for your records as you may have to provide it to IRCC later\.
-   You are enrolled in a UBC\-approved Co\-op term, practicum, work\-integrated learning course, or similar\.
-   You have completed a medical exam and have a valid study permit with proper conditions on it, if applicable\.
-   Your student work placement term has begun\.

## When you must stop working[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/working\-canada/co\-op\-or\-student\-work\-placements/\#when\-you\-must\-stop\-working](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#when-you-must-stop-working)

You must stop working immediately in any of the following situations: 

-   You no longer meet the eligibility requirements for a student work placement\.
-   Your study permit expires and you did not apply to extend it\.
-   Your [Program Completion Letter](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/post-graduation-work-permit/#when-to-apply-for-a-pgwp) becomes available\.

## On\-campus and off\-campus work eligibility[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/working\-canada/co\-op\-or\-student\-work\-placements/\#on\-campus\-and\-off\-campus\-work\-eligibility](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/co-op-or-student-work-placements/#on-campus-and-off-campus-work-eligibility)

Student work placements or Co\-ops are separate from the eligibility to work on and off campus and refer only to work required to complete your UBC program\. 

If you are eligible for a student work placement, you can work for any employer that is approved by your Co\-op office or program, even if the work takes place off campus\. There is no weekly hour limit while participating in student work placements, but your work placement cannot make up more than 50% of your program of study\. 

If you are enrolled in a UBC Co\-op, practicum, or work\-integrated learning course, you are considered [full\-time for immigration purposes](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/ubc-definitions-immigration-purposes/#full-time)\. You may work on a UBC student work placement and, at the same time, [work on and/or off campus](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/) in another type of employment if you meet the requirements for each\.
