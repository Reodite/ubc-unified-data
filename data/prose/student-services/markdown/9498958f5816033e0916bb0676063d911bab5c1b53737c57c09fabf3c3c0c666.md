## Documents to bring[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/travelling\-to\-canada/what\-bring\-canada/\#documents\-to\-bring](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/travelling-to-canada/what-bring-canada/#documents-to-bring)

There are important documents you should be sure to have in your carry\-on luggage as you travel\.

### All students

-   **UBC Proof of Enrolment Letter**  
    If you’ve already registered in courses, you can bring a current [Letter of Enrolment](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/extend-study-permit-within-canada/gather-required-documents-study-permit-extension/#:~:text=Letter%20of%20Enrolment%20%28listed%20as%20%E2%80%98Letter%20of%20Acceptance%E2%80%99%20on%20your%20MyCIC%20checklist%29) as proof that you are a current, enrolled student\. This letter should ideally be issued the week before you travel\.
-   **Advance Declaration \(optional\)**  
    You can bring your completed [Advance Declaration](https://www.canada.ca/en/border-services-agency/services/arrivecan.html) on your phone or bring a printout\.
-   **Passport**  
    Always keep [your passport](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/passports/) with you when travelling\. 
-   **Visa or eTA**  
    Depending on your country of citizenship, you will need a valid [Temporary Resident Visa](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/temporary-resident-visa-trv/) \(TRV\) or [Electronic Travel Authorization](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/eta.html) \(eTA\) — [US citizens and lawful US permanent residents](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/entry-requirements-country.html#lawful-pr-us:~:text=American%20citizens,how%20you%E2%80%99re%20travelling.) are exempt\.
-   **Proof of health insurance**  
    Bring proof of health insurance\. This might include your MSP card, virtual [iMED card](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/) or other basic insurance\. See [Before you travel to Canada](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/travelling-to-canada/before-you-travel/) for details\. If your iMED card does not arrive in time for your travels, you can show a Border Services Officer the [iMED page](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international#when-am-i-covered) with details on coverage\.
-   **Your spouse/common law partner and/or children’s documents \(if applicable and you are travelling together\)**  
    Bring relevant [documents for your family](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/documentation-family/), such as Letters of Introduction for their study or work permits \(if applicable\), proof of relationship, their passport with valid TRV or eTA, and proof of health insurance\. Check additional documentation you may need when travelling with a [minor](http://cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/travel-voyage/td-dv-eng.html#children)\. 

### Current students with study permits

In addition to the above list, all students continuing to study at UBC who already have a study permit must bring with them:

-   **Study permit**  
    You need to have your original, [valid study permit](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/study-permits#maintaining-your-student-status) with you\.
-   **A letter from your program explaining any required student work placements \(if applicable\)**  
    You will need this letter if your required work placement is not already mentioned in your letter of admission\. If you have one, you should also bring a current, valid job offer\.
-   **Proof of funds for yourself and any accompanying dependents**  
    We recommend your bank documents be issued within 30 days before traveling\. See [proof of means of financial support](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/extend-study-permit-within-canada/gather-required-documents-study-permit-extension/) for sample documents\. If you have paid for tuition, download your [proof of payment](https://workday.students.ubc.ca/finances/downloading-your-proof-of-tuition-payment-account-statement/) in Workday*\.* There is no specific minimum amount required\. However, ideally you would show you have access to **at least $22,895**\. The amount required for [additional family members](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html#:~:text=If%20you%20apply%20on%20or%20after%20September%201%2C%202025) depends on how many dependents you have\.
-   **Medical exam, if required**  
    If you’ve been in a [designated country](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/medical-police/medical-exams/requirements-temporary-residents/country-requirements.html) for 6 months or more consecutively in the past year, are a medical student or will work in [jobs which require a medical exam](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/#if-you-work-with-children-or-seniors-in-healthcare-settings-or-in-agriculture), bring a copy of your medical exam \(“e\-medical”\) completed within 12 months before travelling\. See the [Before you travel to Canada](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/travelling-to-canada/before-you-travel/) page for details\. 

### New students and current students who need a new study permit

In addition to the above list, if you are a new student, or a current student who applied for a new study permit from outside of Canada, you’ll also need to bring:

-   **A Letter of Introduction**  
    This is your electronic study permit approval, or [Letter of Introduction](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/apply-initial-study-permit/#get-your-study-permit-approval)\. 
-   **Your UBC Letter of Admission \(new students only\)**  
    This is also known as your offer letter or Letter of Acceptance\. How you [get your Letter of Admission](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/apply-initial-study-permit/tutorial-apply-initial-study-permit-required-documents/) depends on your program\.
-   **Proof of funds for yourself and any accompanying dependents**  
    We recommend you bring copies of the financial documents you provided in your study permit application\. In addition, we recommend you bring recent financial documents which are issued within 30 days before traveling and proof of tuition payment, if you have paid for tuition\. See [proof of means of financial support](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/study-permits/apply-initial-study-permit/tutorial-apply-initial-study-permit-required-documents/) for sample documents\. Download your [proof of tuition payment](https://workday.students.ubc.ca/finances/downloading-your-proof-of-tuition-payment-account-statement/) from Workday*\.* You should be able to show proof of **at least $22,895**\. The amount required for [additional family members](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html#:~:text=If%20you%20apply%20on%20or%20after%20September%201%2C%202025) depends on how many dependents you have\.
-   **Valid medical exam, if required**  
    Bring a copy of your medical exam \(e\-medical\) if you required one for your study permit application, or get a medical exam if your situation changed since you applied and you now [require one](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/medical-police/medical-exams/requirements-temporary-residents.html#needs)\. Your medical exam should be completed within 12 months before travelling\. If it has been more than 12 months since you took your exam, you must take another medical exam before travelling\. See the [Before you travel to Canada](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/travelling-to-canada/before-you-travel/) page for details\. 
-   **English language test results, if you have them \(recommended\)**  
    If you provided English language test results in your study permit application, bring a copy of your results, if applicable\.

### Other helpful documents to carry on the plane

-   Driver’s license
-   Medical or immunization records, including proof of COVID\-19 vaccination\(s\)
-   Marriage certificate and notarized copies
-   Documents for dependents accompanying you: passports, birth certificates, children’s immunization and school records
-   National identity document from your country

## What to pack[https://students\.ubc\.ca/international\-student\-guide/travelling\-to\-canada/what\-bring\-canada/\#what\-to\-pack](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/travelling-to-canada/what-bring-canada/#what-to-pack)

There are some things you cannot bring to Canada or will need to declare when you arrive, such as cash over CAD $10,000 and food or animal products\.

Refer to the Canada Border Services Agency’s [visitors to Canada](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/travel-voyage/ivc-rnc-eng.html) and [restricted and prohibited goods](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/travel-voyage/rpg-mrp-eng.html) pages for details\.

Learn what to do if you plan to [travel with your pet\(s\)](https://inspection.canada.ca/importing-food-plants-or-animals/pets/eng/1326600389775/1326600500578)\.

Also, we recommend you bring snacks in case of a long wait upon arrival\.

### Medication

Keep your medication in your carry\-on bag in its original packaging, along with a prescription from the doctor\. If you are planning on bringing medication or health products with you, check that the quantity or type is not [restricted](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/importation-exportation/personal-use-health-products-guidance/document.html) or if they have [controlled substances](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/health-concerns/controlled-substances-precursor-chemicals/controlled-substances/compliance-monitoring/travelling-into-and-out-of-canada-with-prescription-medication-containing-controlled-substances.html)\. 

It’s a good idea to bring a copy of your medical records for you and the family members who travel with you\. If you have a chronic medical condition, have your doctor write a short medical history, preferably in English, for your new doctor in Vancouver\. Bring records including the dates of any immunizations and medical tests you have done\.
