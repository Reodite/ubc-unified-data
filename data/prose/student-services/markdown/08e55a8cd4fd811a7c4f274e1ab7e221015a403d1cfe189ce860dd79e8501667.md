GuardMe Enhanced@UBC iMED is a temporary basic health insurance plan for international students who are new to UBC\. At UBC, the plan is referred to simply as iMED\. Managed by [GuardMe](https://guard.me/en.html), iMED provides international students with basic health insurance during the three\-month waiting period before you qualify for the British Columbia government\-run [Medical Services Plan](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/medical-services-plan-msp-international/) \(MSP\), or for the duration of your studies if your program is shorter than 6 months, such as a one\-term exchange program\.

iMED covers emergency hospitalization and required medical services for an unexpected sickness or injury, including treatment at a doctor’s clinic\. Full details of what is covered by iMED can be found on the Policy Wording document in your [GuardMe account](https://www.guard.me/login.php)\.

Learn more about [health insurance for international students](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/), including the differences between iMED, MSP, and the AMS/GSS Health and Dental Plan\.

## Eligibility for iMED[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#eligibility\-for\-imed](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#eligibility-for-imed)

All new international students who are charged AMS/GSS student fees, as well as Go Global exchange and Visiting International Research students \(VIRS\), are eligible and automatically enrolled in iMED when they register in their first on\-campus, in\-person course\. 

Domestic students, including Canadian citizens and permanent residents, and students who are registered only in off\-campus courses are not eligible to be covered by iMED\.

Check your [financial account activity](https://workday.students.ubc.ca/finances/viewing-your-financial-account-activity-tuition-amount-and-payments/) in Workday to confirm you are charged for iMED\. You will receive a welcome email from GuardMe with detailed information about the plan before your coverage begins\. 

If you’re enrolled at UBC, but are not eligible for iMED, you may need to purchase private temporary insurance to cover you until you qualify for MSP\. Contact [GuardMe](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/contact) to discuss your options\.  

## iMED costs[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#imed\-costs](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#imed-costs)

The cost for your iMED coverage depends on the length of your program or studies at UBC\.

Your iMED fee will be charged as a part of your student fees, which you can [view in your Workday account](https://workday.students.ubc.ca/finances/viewing-your-financial-account-activity-tuition-amount-and-payments/)\. Please note that the direct billing fee applies only to UBC Vancouver students\.

**

Students in programs of study that are longer than 6 months

**

iMED costs **$204\.30** for three months of coverage, plus a one\-time direct billing fee of $11\.15\.

This amount also applies to degree\-seeking students, two\-term exchange students, and VIRS students studying more than 6 months\.

**

One\-term exchange or one\-term non\-degree students

**

For the 2026/27 academic year, iMED costs **$295\.10** for 130 days of coverage, plus a one\-time direct billing fee of $11\.15\.

**

Visiting International Research Students \(VIRS\) studying 6 months or less

**

iMED is available on a monthly basis and costs **$68\.10** per month, plus a one\-time direct billing fee of $11\.15\.

## Coverage dates[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#coverage\-dates](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#coverage-dates)

The details of your coverage dates and how to change them depend on the length of your program or studies at UBC\.

**

Students in programs of study that are longer than 6 months

**

This information also applies to degree\-seeking students, two\-term exchange students, and VIRS students studying more than 6 months\.

iMED is designed to cover you during the waiting period before you become eligible for the British Columbia government\-run [Medical Services Plan](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/medical-services-plan-msp-international/) \(MSP\)\. The MSP waiting period includes the remainder of the month you arrive in BC, plus the next 2 calendar months\. For example, if you arrived in BC on Aug 19, you would be eligible for MSP on Nov 1\.

Your iMED coverage dates depend on when you start your program:

-   Winter Term 1 \(Sep to Dec\): coverage starts on Aug 10
-   Winter Term 2 \(Jan to Apr\): coverage starts on Dec 10
-   Summer Session \(May to Aug\): coverage starts on Apr 10

Check the iMED coverage dates on your iMED card to ensure they’re consistent with your planned arrival date\.

Note that VIRS students’ coverage will depend on when your research period begins\.

If you are a Go Global exchange student or a VIRS student, please connect directly with [Go Global](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/contact) if you need to change your coverage dates\. 

For all other students, if you plan to arrive in Canada before the earliest iMED coverage start date available for your UBC program \(e\.g\., your coverage starts Aug 10, but you arrive in July\), you should purchase [Early Arrival coverage](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/department/international-students/early-arrivals) directly from GuardMe\.

If you plan to arrive in Canada in the calendar month **after** your iMED coverage starts \(e\.g\., your coverage starts Aug 10 but you arrive in September\), you must [submit a date change request](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/page/international-students/date-change-request) to have your iMED coverage shifted to cover your 3\-month waiting period for MSP\.

**

One\-term exchange or one\-term non\-degree students

**

One\-term exchange or non\-degree students have iMED coverage for a period of 130 days, equivalent to one academic term\. The duration of iMED coverage takes the move\-in date for UBC residence into consideration\.

Your iMED coverage dates are based on the academic term you’re enrolled in:

-   Winter Session Term 1 \(Sep to Dec 2026\): Aug 26, 2026 to Jan 2, 2027
-   Winter Session Term 2 \(Jan to Apr 2027\): Dec 23, 2026 to May 1, 2027

Your iMED coverage dates are fixed and cannot be changed\. However, you can purchase additional days of coverage directly from GuardMe if you will arrive in BC before, or leave after, the dates indicated above\. 

To discuss your options and purchase additional days of coverage, [contact GuardMe](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/contact) directly or log into your [GuardMe account](https://myaccount.guard.me/login.php)\.

**

Visiting International Research Students \(VIRS\) studying 6 months or less

**

A monthly rate is available for VIRS students, and you will have iMED coverage for the duration of the research period\. 

The start date of your iMED coverage depends on when your research period begins\. UBC will automatically assess the iMED coverage period based on your program and your research period at UBC\. Check your iMED coverage dates in [your account](https://myaccount.guard.me/login.php) and ensure they are correct\.

If you need to shift your program start or end dates, or you plan to arrive in Canada before your coverage start date, please reach out directly to [Go Global](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/contact) for assistance\. 

## Logging into your GuardMe account[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#logging\-into\-your\-guardme\-account](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#logging-into-your-guardme-account)

Once you register for your courses, you will be automatically enrolled in iMED and receive a welcome email from GuardMe within 2 to 4 weeks of your program start date\. Be sure to check your spam folder\.

Follow the instructions in your welcome email to log into your account and download your card\. In your GuardMe account for iMED, you can review your coverage, submit a claim, and view other important information\.

During your coverage period, print your iMED card and keep it with you in case you need medical attention, along with your student ID and a copy of your study permit, if you have one\. You can also access your card through the [GuardMe mobile app](https://news.guard.me/GuardMe_Mobile_App.html)\. 

## Accessing health care services with iMED[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#accessing\-health\-care\-services\-with\-imed](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#accessing-health-care-services-with-imed)

Students covered under iMED may seek medical care at any licensed medical clinic or hospital\. Medical expenses that are eligible under the iMED policy can be submitted to GuardMe either directly by the medical facility, or by you\. You are usually billed for treatment at a hospital after you are discharged from the hospital \(e\.g\., when you have finished treatment\), while you will likely be asked to pay up front for consultations with a doctor\.

If you need care, you should always show your iMED card and your study permit \(if you have one\) when you’re being admitted and ask if the hospital or clinic will agree to bill expenses to GuardMe\. You must also [contact GuardMe](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/contact) immediately with the policy number on your iMED card if you are being admitted to a hospital\.

If you need to pay out of pocket for medical services, or you receive an invoice from a clinic or hospital, you will need to [submit a claim](https://guard.me/en/submit-a-claim.html) through your account or the GuardMe mobile app to request reimbursement\.

On the mobile app or [GuardMe site](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/clinicSearch/), you can search for a clinic that bills to GuardMe directly, as well as view their live wait times, hours, and contact details\.

### Additional health care services

GuardMe provides a [Mobile Doctor](https://www.guard.me/en/mobile-doctor.html) service which offers you 24/7 access to Canadian doctors via Maple on your phone, tablet, or computer\. This is a fast and convenient way to access medical care in minutes, even when outside of Canada\.

You can also access the [Student Support Program](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/gmssp) for 24/7, culturally\-matched, and confidential mental health support for any challenges you may be experiencing\. This program is available during your entire first year at UBC, even after your iMED coverage ends\.

### Important tips

-   If you are hospitalized, you or someone on your behalf must [contact GuardMe customer care](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/contact) immediately with the policy number on your iMED card\. Coverage for certain medical services requires that you contact GuardMe to request pre\-authorization\. Examples include surgery, specialist consultation, and specialized diagnostic tests like an MRI or CT scan\.
-   Carry a photocopy or digital copy of your study permit, if you have one\. You must present a copy of your study permit with your iMED card if you are treated at a hospital to ensure you are billed fees as a British Columbia resident\.
-   You don’t have to hold a study permit to use your iMED plan\. If you and/or your dependents are in Canada as a visitor or worker and you and/or your dependents are enrolled in iMED, bring your iMED card, photo ID, and any immigration documents you have when seeking medical services\.

## If you withdraw from or defer your program[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#if\-you\-withdraw\-from\-or\-defer\-your\-program](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#if-you-withdraw-from-or-defer-your-program)

If you decide to withdraw from your studies, or defer your program to another academic term, it’s important to [drop your courses](https://workday.students.ubc.ca/course-registration/) before the [registration add/drop deadline](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/dates-deadlines/) for the term in order to receive a refund on your tuition and student fees\. 

If you drop your courses before the add/drop deadline, your iMED fees will be refunded to you along with your other fees\. If you register for courses in a future term, you will automatically be enrolled in iMED once you register in courses\.

## Enrolling your family in iMED[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#enrolling\-your\-family\-in\-imed](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#enrolling-your-family-in-imed)

Your spouse, partner, or dependent children can also be covered by iMED for the duration of their stay or until they qualify for MSP\. You can [purchase iMED for them](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/dependents/dependents/current-students) directly through GuardMe\. If you aren’t currently covered by iMED, GuardMe offers other [health insurance options](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/department/dependents/students-with-msp) for your dependents\. 

## Opting out of iMED[https://students\.ubc\.ca/health/health\-insurance/health\-insurance\-international/imed\-temporary\-health\-insurance\-international/\#opting\-out\-of\-imed](https://students.ubc.ca/health/health-insurance/health-insurance-international/imed-temporary-health-insurance-international/#opting-out-of-imed)

It is mandatory to have basic health insurance coverage while enrolled at UBC\. If you meet the eligibility criteria, you may be able to [opt out of iMED](https://sites.guard.me/ubc/optout/international-students/opt-out) by submitting a request before the deadline\. Go Global exchange and VIRS students should contact [Go Global](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/contact) directly for information about opting out\.
