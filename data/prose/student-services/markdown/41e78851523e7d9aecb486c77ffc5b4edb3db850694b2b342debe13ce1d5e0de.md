Review commonly used transfer credit terms and their definitions below\. 

- **Row 1**

  - **Header cell 1:**
  
    Term
  
  - **Header cell 2:**
  
    Definition

- **Row 2**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    Articulation
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    Articulation is the formal process of determining if coursework completed at another institution is eligible for transfer credit at UBC\. It is a collaborative process that involves Admissions Advisors and Transfer Credit staff who determine the appropriate subject and transfer credit values, as well as faculty articulators who compare the original course content to specific UBC courses\.

- **Row 3**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    BC Transfer Guide
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    The [BC Transfer Guide](https://www.bctransferguide.ca/) is an online tool that allows you to search for courses you took in another institution—within or outside of BC—and how they can be transferred to UBC\.

- **Row 4**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    Credit value
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    UBC courses are based on semester credits\. Courses from different credit systems such as quarter credits are converted to equivalent UBC semester credits in the [BC Transfer Guide](https://www.bctransferguide.ca/)\.

- **Row 5**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    Effective date
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    The time period within which the course must be completed to receive transfer credit\.

- **Row 6**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    Elective
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    Elective or “ELEV” transfer credit indicates a general form of transfer credit that has no specific equivalent course at UBC\.

- **Row 7**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    First\-year credit
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    AP, IB or A\-levels exam results that meet the minimum requirements listed on the [Undergraduate Programs and Admissions website](https://you.ubc.ca/applying-ubc/applied/first-year-credit/) are eligible for first\-year credit\. Official final exam results need to be sent to the UBC Undergraduate Admissions Office by examination boards for credit to be added on the student’s record in Workday\.

- **Row 8**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    General Credit
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    When an exact UBC equivalent course does not exist within the same department, general transfer credit is granted as close as possible to the course completed elsewhere\. General Credit will indicate the equivalent subject code, year level, and credit value\.  
      
    Example: *HIST\_V 1st*

- **Row 9**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    General Credit with Exemption
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    Courses that do not match a specific UBC course but have sufficient overlap in learning outcomes may be assigned General Credit with an Exemption for a specific UBC course\.  
      
    Students with General Credit with Exemption are considered to have completed the Exempted course\.  
      
    Example: MATH\_O 1st \(3\), exempt MATH\_O 100

- **Row 10**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    Letter of Permission \(LOP\)
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    A [Letter of Permission \(LOP\)](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/letter-permission/) is an approval from your faculty that the course\(s\) you are planning on taking at another institution will transfer back to your UBC degree\.  
      
    Current UBC students who wish to register in any course at another institution must obtain prior permission from their faculty, in the form of an LOP, before taking courses elsewhere\.  
      
    Requests for an LOP are reviewed on a case\-by\-case basis, and approval is not guaranteed\.

- **Row 11**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    No Credit
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    Courses that have been evaluated and deemed not transferable to UBC such as high school equivalency, pre\-university, foundational, professional, applied, technical or other non\-academic courses\.  
      
    English literature and English writing and composition courses taken at institutions located in countries where English is not the principal language are not transferable to UBC, regardless of the language of instruction at the institution\.

- **Row 12**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    Not for Credit in Science
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    Some transfer credit agreements on the [BC Transfer Guide](https://www.bctransferguide.ca/) indicate that another institution’s course is eligible for transfer credit, but not in the Faculty of Science or in other Science\-based programs, such as Engineering, or Land and Food Systems\.  
      
    The following is an example of how it may be displayed:  
    *CHEM\_V 1NC*  
    Although the course may be considered for credit in a science subject, such as CHEM with corresponding credit value under the “Credits” column indicating the number of transfer credits of general first\-year chemistry, the original course cannot be used to satisfy Faculty of Science degree requirements\. Other faculties may apply such transfer credit differently\.

- **Row 13**

  - **Cell 1:**
  
    Specific Credit
  
  - **Cell 2:**
  
    Courses that have significant overlap with a specific UBC course are granted Specific Credit\.  
      
    Specific transfer credit will be used as if the course was taken at UBC\. Unless approved by your faculty, you should not register in the same course at UBC for which you have already received specific transfer credit, as you cannot receive the same credit twice\.
