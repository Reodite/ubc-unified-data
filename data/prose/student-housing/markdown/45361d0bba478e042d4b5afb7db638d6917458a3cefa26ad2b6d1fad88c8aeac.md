## Residence safety strategies

-   **Lock it up\.** Each time you leave and when you are sleeping, lock your unit and room door, plus any accessible windows\.
-   **Don’t hold the door for strangers\.** Please don’t let people follow you into a locked residence building\. If they have permission to be there, they will have their own key—or a host\.
-   **Stop before you prop\.** Never prop open a building door, even if you are stepping outside for just a moment\.
-   **Theft happens\.** Don’t leave valuables, like your phone, laptop or wallet unattended in a study room, lounge or classroom—or even your unlocked room\. Learn more about [protecting your valuables](https://vancouver.housing.ubc.ca/residence-life/health-safety/safety-security/#protect-valuables)\. Have a bike? Follow these [bicycle security tips](https://vancouver.housing.ubc.ca/residence-life/health-safety/safety-security/#bicycle-security)\.
-   **Trust your instincts\.** If you feel like you or another resident is in danger, or if you witness suspicious activity, first call 911, then contact Community Safety \(604 822 2222\) and your [residence Front Desk](https://vancouver.housing.ubc.ca/contact-us/) or the Overnight Mobile Team\.

## On campus after dark

To stay safe when walking on campus after dark:

-   Walk with a friend or classmate\.
-   Ride the [Translink Community Shuttle](https://planning.ubc.ca/vancouver/transportation-planning/transportation-options/transit/ubc-community-shuttle-routes) \(Route 68\), which stops near student residences\. Learn more about taking [transit on campus](https://vancouver.housing.ubc.ca/getting-around-ubc/)\.
-   Use the [AMS Safewalk program](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/support-services/student-services/safewalk/)—from 8 pm to 2 am \(September–April\) or 9 pm to 2 am \(May–August\)\. Phone Safewalk and a driving or walking team will accompany you to and from campus destinations after dark\.
-   After 2 am, [Community Safety](https://communitysafety.ubc.ca/) can accompany you across campus\. Call 604 822 2222 for assistance\.

For more information about your safety on campus, visit [UBC Community Safety](https://communitysafety.ubc.ca/)\.

### Emergency naloxone kits

The street drug supply in BC is more toxic and unpredictable than ever\. It’s important to know how to prevent, recognize and respond to an overdose\.

Naloxone is a fast\-acting medication used to temporarily reverse the effects of an opioid overdose\. It can be delivered via injection or as a nasal spray for the emergency treatment of a known *or suspected* opioid overdose\.

*Naloxone will not cause harm when administered, and it will not have an effect if opioids are absent\.*

#### Where are naloxone kits located in residence?

Emergency naloxone kits, each containing two doses of nasal naloxone, are available in wall\-mounted cabinets in every residence building for 24/7 access:

-   **Residence houses:** naloxone cabinets are located in the ground floor lobby of every building\.
-   **Residence commonsblocks:** naloxone cabinets are located near your Residence Front Desk, alongside an Automated External Defibrillator \(AED\)\.

#### Where are naloxone kits located elsewhere on campus?

Across campus, emergency naloxone kits are installed alongside many AEDs\. [Learn more](https://communitysafety.ubc.ca/safety-resources/overdose-prevention-and-response-program/) and [visit the AED and naloxone locations map](https://portal.gis.ubc.ca/arcgis/apps/instant/minimalist/index.html?appid=4efed2f9f35442fa8f65f815669fd585)\.

Overdose response

Knowing how to respond to an overdose, even if you don’t think you’ll need to, makes the residence community safer for everyone\.

If someone is experiencing a known or suspected overdose, there’s no time to waste\.

1.  **Immediately call 911**
2.  **Give naloxone**
3.  **Call Community Safety: 604 822 2222**

You will not get in trouble with UBC, Student Housing, the police, or Citizenship and Immigration Canada for calling 911\. You are protected by the [Good Samaritan Drug Overdose Act](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/opioids/about-good-samaritan-drug-overdose-act.html)—even if you have a small amount of substances on you\.

#### Additional info about the naloxone cabinets

##### Alarm

Opening the naloxone cabinet triggers an alarm\. The purpose of the alarm is to prevent misuse of the product, and it sounds locally only; it does not notify UBC, Student Housing or emergency services\. If you encounter a known or suspected overdose, call 911 immediately\.

##### Replacement

If naloxone is missing or a cabinet has been tampered with, please contact Community Safety \(604 822 2222\) to let them know\.

### AEDs

Automated External Defibrillators \(AEDs\) are critical lifesaving devices that can provide a quick response in the event of a cardiac arrest\.

#### How to access AEDs and emergency naloxone

-   In case of emergencies 24/7, immediately call 911\.
-   During commonsblock open hours, and if you have access to your commonsblock 24/7, you can access an AED mounted to the wall near your Residence Front Desk\.
-   AEDs are also [available in publicly accessible buildings across campus](https://communitysafety.ubc.ca/safety-resources/automated-external-defibrillators/)\.

## Tips and additional resources

### Coexisting with wildlife on campus

UBC’s Vancouver campus is home to a rich diversity of wildlife—coyotes, raccoons, birds, and more\. Find out how to [share campus safely with wildlife and report a wildlife conflict or incident](https://facilities.ubc.ca/services/pest-management/wildlife-on-campus/)\.

### Ways to protect your valuables

Laptops, tablets, smartphones, wallets, purses and personal identification documents can be an easy target for thieves in residence\. Keep them safe by securing your room/unit every time you leave, even if it is just for a minute\.

-   Keep photocopies of all your identification, credit cards, serial numbers—anything that would have to be replaced if your belongings were taken\.
-   [Get insurance](https://vancouver.housing.ubc.ca/residence-life/health-safety/renters-insurance/) to protect your belongings from theft or damage due to an unforeseen cause like a fire or flood\. UBC is not responsible for lost, stolen or damaged property\. Some students may already be covered under their family’s plan\. [BCAA](http://www.bcaa.com/) is also an option\.
-   Rent a safety deposit box at a nearby bank to secure valuables such as large amounts of cash\.

### Bicycle security

[Cycling at UBC](https://planning.ubc.ca/transportation/cycling) is a quick and healthy way to get around campus\. Consider these tips for keeping your bike safe from thieves:

-   UBC Community Safety recommends using a good quality steel U\-lock and heavy cable lock for the front wheel\.  
    
-   Don’t bring an expensive bike to campus\.
-   If possible, keep your bike in your room, but not in hallways or common areas due to fire safety regulations\.
-   Never leave an unlocked bike on a balcony, even if you are above the ground floor\.
-   Consider leaving your bike at home and using UBC’s convenient, affordable [Bike Share](https://planning.ubc.ca/transportation/cycling/bike-share-programs) program instead\.

### Vehicle parking

-   Do not leave valuables in any vehicle parked on campus\.
-   All vehicles parked on campus must have insurance\.

### Online safety

Your digital identity matters\. [Digital Tattoo](https://digitaltattoo.ubc.ca/) is a great UBC resource for everything from staying safe online and outwitting [phishing](https://privacymatters.ubc.ca/content/phishing-emails) \(and pharming\) to understanding how your online activity affects your career or getting tips for effective online research\.

*Remember that UBC will never send you an email asking for your Campus\-Wide Login or password\.*
