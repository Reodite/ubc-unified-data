UBC Student Tiffany Guan reflects on how her unforgettable exchange experience at Yonsei University helped her learn more about Asian history and culture, while embracing spontaneity and stepping out of her comfort zone along the way\.

May 20, 2026

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)*  
  
*Meet Tiffany Guan, a 5th year UBC undergraduate student in Sauder, specializing in Accounting with a concentration in Business Analytics\. She grew up in Vancouver, Canada\.*

**Why did you pick Yonsei University for your exchange? How did you hear about it?** 

I chose to study abroad at Yonsei University in the Spring semester of my fourth year, and it was the best time of my undergrad experience\! I’ve always wanted to be more well\-versed in different cultures and be able to easily travel between countries, so I knew I wanted to study in Asia or Europe\. I went on the partner universities page on the Go Global website to filter for schools that had transferable business courses\. I was drawn to Asian schools, especially since I had planned to travel there after my high school graduation, but missed my chance due to COVID\. My friend went to Yonsei a few semesters before me, and it looked so fun from his Instagram stories\. I asked him about his experience, and that was enough convincing for me to choose Yonsei\.

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**

Before applying, I made sure to understand what I wanted to gain from this experience\. I researched a few places I wanted to spend time exploring \(Korea, Singapore, Hong Kong\) to be certain of where I wanted to study\. I regularly asked friends who’d gone abroad for advice, and went to academic advisors for concerns regarding transfer credits\. With a budget planner now part of the application process, I’d recommend trying to accurately fill this out, especially since it’s easy to spend more than expected when travelling\. Overall, the Go Global application process was really straightforward, especially with the help available from Go Global advisors\!

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of? \(travel, cultural differences, classes, etc\.\)** 

Before flying to Korea, I asked friends who had gone to study abroad for travel tips and day\-to\-day advice\. This was really helpful because I had installed a bunch of apps for finances \(Wise, Splitwise\), navigation \(Naver Maps, Kakao T\), and communication \(KakaoTalk, Papago\), which allowed me to smoothly get around the city\. 

A big tip is to watch some YouTube videos beforehand by someone else who’s been to the same school for an exchange semester\. Yonsei also had videos for prospective students about campus life, course registration, and many other important topics\! \(*My YouTube watch history was flooded with these videos, and my Instagram Explore page was filled with posts about Korea\.*\)  

Another important thing to look into is the weather\. I severely underpacked for the cold because it was snowing and even reached \-10 degrees\! \(*Not all bad though, this was an incentive to shop more\)*

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome the anxiety?**

Although I was excited to live out my dream of travelling and exploring new countries, I didn’t know anyone applying to Yonsei and was worried about making friends\. Thankfully, I was able to meet my first “exchange friend” at the UBC Welcome and Mingle event for Asian countries\! It felt comforting to know someone going through the same experience and from the same school\. We were also constantly updating each other on school deadlines and application processes \(*thank you, Erin*\)\. Because Yonsei was such a welcoming school, I was able to meet close friends in classes and clubs, eventually flying to different places with them\! If I hadn’t applied to Go Global alone, I wouldn’t have needed to step out of my comfort zone as much\. I’m glad I was able to make so many unforgettable memories on exchange\!

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?**

Studying abroad has been life\-changing and eye\-opening in many ways\. Being able to fly out of the country to spend the weekend in Vietnam on a moped bike, or celebrate my birthday in Hong Kong, were experiences I’d never imagined possible before\. I was able to connect with the Asian customs and cultural norms, which were different from life in Canada\. I learned how important it was to seize any opportunity to meet new people and try new things\. In many ways, exchange felt like I was reliving my first year again, except without COVID guidelines\. Saying yes to things I’d never initially planned made everything seem new and fun\. I grew to love the spontaneity of every idea and surrounded myself with friends who were just as adventurous\!

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change/do?**

Being in Asia, I really wanted to take advantage of the quick flights between countries and ended up spending the month of May flying to a new country every week and living out of my suitcase\. Travelling all over Asia was one of my main goals on exchange, so I have no regrets about that\. However, if I could’ve done something differently on exchange, that would’ve been to explore more of Korea\. I mainly stayed within Seoul, with a few trips to the popular tourist destinations of Busan and Jeju\. I wish I had travelled to more rural cities and taken in the cultural and scenic landmarks\. I definitely could’ve gone on some fun day trips\!

**How were the academics?**

Yonsei’s Spring school semester was from early March to the end of June, and had really similar aspects to UBC’s own courses \(i\.e\., participation marks, exams, projects, papers, presentations\)\. Yonsei also had courses designated for study abroad students, so the professors were extremely flexible and lenient, and the course loads were lighter\. I took four courses, three being general electives for exchange students and one being a business course with local students \(*thankfully taught in English*\)\. I made sure all my courses would transfer to UBC credits, and it was especially interesting to take a required course \(COMM 491 \- Strategic Management\) in the context of the Korean business landscape while working with local students\. As well, I scheduled my classes from Monday to Wednesday so I could use the rest of my week to frolic around Seoul\!

**How was the social life/social aspect of the trip \(excursions, sightseeing, etc\.\)?**

Yonsei had a huge exchange program, so it was easy to meet many people from different countries\. The dorms were also a fun way to connect with other students, whether that meant having a roommate or meeting floormates\. Yonsei’s extensive club list was open to anyone, some clubs of which catered to international students\. It was an extremely welcoming and fun environment, and I was able to meet some of my close friends through random encounters \(i\.e\., playing Mafia at the convenience store by the dorms\)\! Outside of school, Korea’s abundance of trendy cafes and shopping areas, historical landmarks, and nighttime activities made every day exciting\. Located in a very central location within Seoul, Yonsei was near many bus stops and train stations, making it convenient to go around the city\. I remember taking the electric scooters home after spending the whole day \(and night\) in Hongdae\.

**Would you recommend this experience to other students? What can they look forward to?**

I’d definitely recommend this experience to everyone, even those who are still unsure\! Going on exchange has been one of the most rewarding experiences in my undergraduate degree, especially helping me feel comfortable being on my own to explore unknown paths\. Putting yourself out there is the first step to uncovering a wealth of knowledge\. Look forward to the great level of personal growth and appreciation for what the world has to offer\!

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**

Having grown up in Vancouver all my life, surrounded by family and friends, I had never considered leaving until experiencing life abroad and meeting people from all over the world\. Not only did I learn more about Asian history and culture, but I also learned how to navigate unfamiliar situations alone\. Stepping out of my comfort zone allowed me to feel more confident in connecting with others, regardless of our backgrounds and past experiences\. I feel extremely fortunate to have been presented with this unique opportunity\. I’m excited to see what life has in store for me after graduating from UBC\!
