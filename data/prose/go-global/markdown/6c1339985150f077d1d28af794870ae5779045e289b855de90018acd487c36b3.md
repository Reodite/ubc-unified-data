UBC student Ghita Adnane reflects on how her Global Seminar experience in Paris has inspired future career plans, a greater sense of global exploration, and transformative personal growth\.

May 14, 2026

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)* 

**Program Featured:** Paris, Capital of Modernity, HIST 390 taught by Dr\. Michael Lanthier, Department of History\.  
  
This program was generously funded by the Faculty of Arts Cultural Experience in France Grant thanks to an anonymous donor   
  
*Ghita Adnane is a 3\[rd\] year undergraduate student in the Urban Studies program in the Faculty of Arts\. She was born in Morocco and raised in Surrey, BC\.*

#### Before you go   

**Why did you pick** [**Paris: Capital of Modernity \(HIST 390\)**](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/ubc-student-programs/global-seminars/programs/paris-capital-modernity-hist-390-3-credits) **for your Global Seminar?** **How did you hear about it?**  
I have always wanted to live in Paris, ever since my one\-day layover trip there in 2024\. I fell in love with the city, and seeing the Eiffel Tower for the first time felt unreal and magical\. When I got to UBC, I learned about Go Global during Imagine Day—that’s when I found out about [Global Seminars](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/global-seminars)\. It stood out to me because it’s different from a traditional student exchange\. 

That fall, I started researching the [seminars available](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/global-seminars/programs), and Paris HIST 390 caught my attention because I was interested in the topic of modernity and how Paris transformed into the city we know today\.  

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**  
The application process was quite straightforward\. I applied during the Round 1 deadline in December 2024\. The application consisted of several written questions about your motivation and previous experiences traveling abroad or working in a team\. 

In January, I received an email inviting me to an interview with Professor Lanthier\. This was an opportunity to talk about why I wanted to participate in the global seminar and what I hoped to learn or gain from the experience\. The interview was conversational and included a few questions about group dynamics and previous international experiences\. 

One tip I would give is to research the seminar topics beforehand and connect them to your academic interests or experiences\. This helps show your genuine interest and engagement\. A few weeks later, I received my acceptance\.

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of?**   
I prepared by researching Paris extensively on social media\. Since it was my first time traveling abroad alone, I wanted to be ready for any situation\. It was also helpful to talk to people who had previously traveled there to get tips and recommendations\. This helped me plan what to pack based on the weather and safety considerations\. 

I recommend bringing good sneakers, since the lectures were all walking tours\. I averaged around 20,000 steps a day, so comfortable footwear is essential\. I also suggest bringing a fanny pack, water, and sun protection\. Be sure to check the weather before going, as it can be unpredictable\. Bring warm layers like jackets or hoodies, as it tends to get cold at night\.

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome anxiety?**  
Paris has a reputation for pickpocketing, so I was quite nervous about that, especially since it was my first time traveling alone\. I did a lot of research on ways to protect myself and prevent pickpocketing\. I bought a phone chain so I could keep my phone attached to my wrist, which helped me feel more secure\. I also used a fanny pack and a zippered shoulder bag to keep my belongings safe\. 

Thankfully, I didn’t have any negative experiences on the metro or while exploring the city\. I found Paris to be quite safe overall, but I would still recommend staying alert\. 

#### During the experience

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?**   
My overall experience was amazing, I still think about my trip to this day\. It changed me for the better\. I learned a lot about myself and how independent I can be\. Although I was nervous about traveling alone, I ended up having an incredible time and felt safe throughout the trip\. 

I learned to be adaptable, open\-minded, and comfortable navigating a new environment\. I also learned a lot about the culture and felt very comfortable by the end of the three weeks\. The experience solidified my decision to move to Paris, and I’m now planning to do an exchange semester in France during Term 1 because I loved it so much\.

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change/do?**  
I would create a list of places I wanted to visit and things I wanted to try\. Paris is such a large city that it can feel overwhelming at times, and it’s impossible to do everything\. Having a plan and setting priorities before arriving would help make the most of your time, especially since three weeks go by quickly\. 

I wish I had done that, because there were times when I felt indecisive and ended up not doing much, which I regretted afterward\.

**How were the academics?**  
The academics were similar to a regular UBC course but more condensed and fast\-paced, like summer courses\. However, I really enjoyed the structure and assignments\. 

The course was very interactive\. It included a pre\-departure component in Vancouver, where we covered key topics and readings\. In Paris, the lectures were essentially walking tours in different parts of the city, depending on the topics we were studying\.

**How was the social aspect of the trip?**  
The social aspect was one of the highlights of the trip\. Since Global Seminars involve a group of UBC students, it was easy to connect with others\. We created a group chat before meeting together, which helped break the ice\. 

During the pre\-departure session, we met in person for the first time and quickly got along\. There were about 14 students, and many of us were roommates\. We cooked together, went on small trips, and explored the city as a group\. The walking tours also gave us plenty of time to bond and create lasting memories\. 

One of the best parts of Global Seminars is the friendships you build as they often continue even after returning to UBC\.

#### After the experience

**Would you recommend this experience to other students? What can they look forward to?**  
I would definitely recommend doing a Global Seminar at least once during your undergraduate degree\. It’s a unique opportunity that you won’t have after graduation\. Not only do you get to explore a new city, but the experience also counts toward your degree\. 

It was the highlight of my undergrad\. I learned so much about myself, experienced Paris in a meaningful way, and made friendships and memories that I will always cherish\.

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**  
Academically, I gained a deeper understanding of Paris’s history through walking tours, museums, and lectures\. It was incredible to see history come to life in front of me\. The hands\-on learning experience is something you don’t get in a traditional classroom\. The walking tours also allowed us to explore parts of Paris we might not have seen otherwise, especially with guidance from the professor\. It changed the way I viewed the city and added depth to my understanding of it\. 

On a personal level, this was my first time traveling alone for an extended period\. I did feel homesick during the first few days, but I gradually adapted to the new environment with the support of my peers\. I also tried solo travel for the first time, which helped build my confidence and independence\. Overall, I learned a lot about myself and pushed outside of my comfort zone by trying new things\.
