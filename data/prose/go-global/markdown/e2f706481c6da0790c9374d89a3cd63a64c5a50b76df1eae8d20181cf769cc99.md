UBC graduate Emma Martin\-Rousselle shares how an exchange at UNSW in Sydney sparked spontaneity, global exploration, and a deeper sense of academic and personal growth\.

January 14, 2026

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)* 

*Meet Emma Martin\-Rousselle, a recent UBC B\+MM graduate\. She completed her M\.M\. dual\-degree in December 2025 and B\.A\. Sociology in May 2025\. She is originally from Deep River, ON, Canada\.*

It was 7am in Sydney, Australia, and I had just gotten off one of the longest flights I had ever taken in my life\. After finishing my summer internship Friday, packing up Saturday, and sharing tearful goodbyes, I boarded my flight to head to exchange\. Then, following a day of travel \(including a Hong Kong layover adventure\), I finally made it to Sydney\!

Jet lagged, I got into a taxi and, after arriving at my accommodation, dropped my bags and slept for a few hours\. When I woke up, I met my new housemates, who quickly convinced me to join their trip to Cairns the next week\. Eleven of them were planning to spend the week snorkeling or scuba diving in the Great Barrier Reef and exploring the Daintree Rainforest\. That same day I arrived in Sydney, I booked my flights, along with a spot in a scuba diving certification course, so I could join a few roommates on an overnight scuba tour to the reef\.

This type of spontaneity is exactly what I wanted to achieve in my exchange\. After a busy three years of university, I hoped this would be a chance to explore the world – even though I’d be studying at [UNSW](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/exchange/university-new-south-wales "University of New South Wales"), I knew there’d also be many opportunities to check items off my bucket list\. With my new roommates, I travelled across New Zealand, hiked the Blue Mountains\. I even met up with one of my roommates in Vietnam over Christmas to ride the Ha Giang Loop\. I also travelled solo for the first time, learning to surf, exploring new places, and meeting incredible people along the way\.

So, here’s a bit about my exchange experience: before, during, and after\!

#### Before you go   

**Why did you pick the** [**University of New South Wales**](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/exchange/university-new-south-wales "University of New South Wales") **for your exchange? How did you hear about it?**    
I knew I wanted to go to Australia for my exchange: it was a far\-away place I had never been to before and I wasn’t sure if I’d ever have the opportunity to visit there long\-term other than doing an exchange\. There were a lot of things that appealed to me about Australia, such as the beautiful nature, the beaches and the cultural similarities to Canada\. UNSW was my top choice because of its trimester system\. Most Australian universities have their spring term from July \- November, but UNSW’s third trimester runs from September \- November, matching with UBC Term 1\. I personally needed a university whose term dates aligned with UBC so I could complete a summer internship and still graduate on time\.

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**   
The application process was straightforward: GoGlobal helped make the process very easy\! The only thing I had to submit to UNSW was an official transcript with my final grades, which GoGlobal provided\. Course registration was a bit different from UBC’s: we first selected our classes and then, after approval of those courses by UNSW admin, could select our timetable\.

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of? \(travel, cultural differences, classes, etc\.\)**   
One thing that students interested in UNSW should be aware of is the housing situation\. When I went to UNSW, most exchange students were not eligible to live in [on\-campus residence\.](https://www.unsw.edu.au/accommodation) This required us to find our own housing off\-campus, usually only after arriving in Sydney\. Personally, I found housing through a privately operated company that caters specifically to exchange students\. They also allowed me to sign my lease before I got to Sydney\. I had a good experience living with others studying abroad and my roommate was a fellow UBC student who I met at a pre\-departure mixer event\! However, housing through this private company was really overpriced compared to other options\.

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome the anxiety?**  
I was lucky that, going into the experience, this wasn’t my first exchange – I did a high school exchange in France and, with that experience, knew what it was like to pick up and move to a new country\. That said, moving didn’t feel any less daunting\. I still worried about making friends, academics, and the long trip\. I overcame my anxiety by speaking to friends who had had similar experiences\. Before exchange, I reconnected with Australian friends who I met on my high school exchange, and I also spoke  to previous GoGlobal students who went to Australia\. I found it helpful to hear about their experiences\. I also connected with my housemates and roommate on social media before arriving, to help ease some of the pressure about living with new people, and was really glad to have done so\!

#### During the experience

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?**   
I loved Australia: the weather, the travel opportunities, the lifestyle\. I enjoyed my experience so much that I hope to move back to Australia in the next few years; and, I learned how much I thrive in the Australian lifestyle and sunny weather\!

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change or do?**   
I wish I’d explored Sydney more instead of focusing on weekend trips or my routine in my neighbourhood\. I often forgot that I could spend my weekend travelling around Sydney, too, since it’s such a big city\! I returned to Australia six months after my exchange ended and had time to explore Sydney a bit more, and that’s what made me realize I missed out on so much during my exchange\.

**How were the academics?**   
UNSW felt similar to UBC, if not more relaxed\. I took all electives and, because of the trimester layout, only took 3 courses \(worth 12 UBC credits\)\. All of my lectures were recorded, so most students \- even non\-exchange students \- seemed to only attend the mandatory tutorials, and I only went to campus a couple of days a week\. I also took courses that I felt I wouldn’t have been able to take at UBC: Media in the Asia\-Pacific; Sydney History, Landscapes and People; and Migrants and Refugees in Australia and the World\. These courses gave me a new and unique perspective on the world\.

**How was the social aspect?**  
As mentioned above, I had a great social life because of my roommates, as well as local friends I met\. Travel was a huge part of my exchange, including a six\-week backpacking trip I took at the end of my exchange, and 10 days spent in New Zealand over my reading week\. I also hiked the Blue Mountains, went camping, celebrated the AFL Grand Final in Melbourne, and even attended an opera at the Sydney Opera House\. I honestly made so many great memories\.

#### After the experience

**Would you recommend this experience to others? What can they look forward to?**   
Absolutely yes\! Australia is an amazing country and there are so many things to look forward to\. Sydney offers vibrant city life, culture, and beaches, plus endless opportunities to travel around nearby regions and enjoy hiking and the outdoors \- it’s very similar to Vancouver, but with nicer weather year\-round\. Though you might be less likely to explore neighboring countries compared to going on exchange to Europe or Asia, I still got to travel to five countries in Asia, as well as seeing New Zealand and three states of Australia\. I also could have seen so much more of Australia, and definitely want to go back to see other parts of the country I missed\.

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**    
I wanted to see if I could picture myself living and working in Australia in the future, and, after my exchange, the answer is yes\! The experience also made me more spontaneous, learning to say ‘yes’ to opportunities from impromptu group trips to solo travel, and has inspired me to hopefully do more backpacking/solo travel in the future\.
