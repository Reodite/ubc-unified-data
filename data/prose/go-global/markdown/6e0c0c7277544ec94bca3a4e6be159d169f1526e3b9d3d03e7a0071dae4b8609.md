UBC student Ariane Huang reflects on how her exchange at the National University of Singapore sparked cultural discovery, unforgettable friendships, and a transformative journey of academic and personal growth\.

February 18, 2026

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)* 

*Meet Ariane Huang a 4th year UBC undergraduate student in the Integrated Sciences program studying Neuroscience, Physiology, and Public Health\. She grew up in British Columbia, Canada\.*

#### Before you go   

**Why did you pick the**  **The National University of Singapore** **for your exchange? How did you hear about it?**    
When I first started looking into exchange programs, I had three main criteria in mind:

1.  The country needed to be culturally different from Canada, where I’ve lived my whole life\. This is because I wanted to push myself out of my comfort zone\.
2.  It needed to be English\- or Mandarin\-speaking \(so classes and navigating my way around wouldn’t be overwhelming\)\.
3.  It had to offer plenty of science courses \(so I could check off degree requirements while abroad\)\.  
    Initially, my heart was set on Taiwan\. I have family there and have always wanted to spend more time connecting with my roots\. Language wouldn’t be a barrier since I speak Taiwanese at home and use Mandarin often too\. But having been born and raised in Canada, I feel very assimilated, and so whenever I visit Taiwan, I’m often seen as a foreigner\. Living there would’ve been such a  
    meaningful way to learn more about my heritage\. Unfortunately, when I dug into the details, I realized Taiwan’s exchange options in the life sciences were more limited, which made it hard to align with my academic goals\.

Regardless, I continued my search for the perfect university for my exchange\. I started by researching partner universities listed on the Go Global website\. That’s when the National University of Singapore came onto my radar\. It had a wide variety of course offerings, English as a primary language, and a culture that felt refreshingly different from Canada\. I spoke to some of my friends who had already gone on exchange to Singapore, and they couldn’t stop raving about it\. From the incredible food to the people they met to the cultural experiences and easy travel opportunities, their enthusiasm was contagious\. After hearing them, I started seriously considering Singapore and started doing more research\. The more I learned, the more excited I became\.

I loved Singlish, a lively blend of English with Malay, Hokkien, and other languages\. I was impressed by how safe the country is, which not only reassured me but also put my family at ease about me living independently abroad\. The efficient public transportation system meant I could get anywhere in the country easily without needing a car, though car shares were also available for the occasional trip\. It was also nice that Singapore is in Asia, so if I wanted to visit my family in Taiwan, it would only be a short flight away\. And then there was the food \- endlessly diverse, affordable, and accessible\. I could already imagine never getting tired of it\. All of these things together made Singapore feel like the perfect fit for me academically, culturally, and personally\. 

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**   
The application process honestly wasn’t too bad\! Go Global advising was super helpful, and the Pre\-Departure Session/Modules made things much clearer\. As a Science student, there are a few extra details to watch for around eligibility and requirements\. My biggest tips would be: keep a close eye on your emails, stay on top of deadlines, do plenty of research, and most importantly, don’t be afraid to ask lots of questions\.

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of? \(travel, cultural differences, classes, etc\.\)**   
I asked several questions and did a ton of research\. I made a bucket list of places to explore and foods to try, and picked up a bit of Singlish from my Singaporean friends\. I tried to plan as many logistics as I could prior to arriving in Singapore, such as transportation, phone plan, visa, insurance, budgeting, and which classes to take\. The number one “warning” people gave me before going to Singapore was that chewing gum is banned\. So now, dear reader, consider yourself warned\.

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome the anxiety?**  
I was definitely nervous about making friends\. I already had a solid circle back home, and it had been a while since I’d had to make friends “from scratch”\. But attending the Welcome Mingle eased my anxiety as I met great people right away\! NUS’s Global Buddy Programme was also fantastic; I made wonderful friends there who I still talk to almost every day\. I also started attending a church in Singapore, where I met even more amazing friends that I still keep in touch with\. Putting myself out there was daunting, especially as an introvert, but I’m so glad I did\.

#### During the experience

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?** 

> "The best way to describe my exchange is that it felt like a dream\." \- Ariane Huang

 It still feels unreal that I lived across the world for almost half a year, surrounded by new sights, flavors, and traditions every day\. Those months flew by, but were filled with moments of discovery and everlasting memories\. From late\-night conversations with friends from all over the world, to exploring hidden corners of Singapore, to feeling the thrill of independence, I learned how meaningful it is to embrace new cultures and experiences with an open mind\. I also learned how connections can form across different backgrounds and stories\.

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change or do?**   
Looking back, I wish I had joined more clubs and attended more NUS events\. It would have been such a fun way to meet people with similar interests and maybe even discover new ones\. I might have tried something completely different like rowing, scuba diving, or crocheting, all of which NUS had clubs for that I never got around to exploring\.

**How were the academics?**   
Everyone at NUS seemed really studious, and many of the local students I spoke to were very focused on doing well academically and career\-wise\. Thus, I felt motivated to keep up too\. The courses I took were exam\-/group project\-heavy, and they were bell\-curved, which added a bit of challenge since success meant not only passing grades, but performance above the class average\. It was tricky to find reviews on the courses or professors, but thankfully, all my professors were incredibly kind and supportive\. I met with each of my professors one\-on\-one to prepare for exams and get to know them better, and they were all incredibly encouraging\. 

**How was the social aspect?**  
The social side of my exchange was easily my favourite part\. I met friends from all over the world and plenty of locals who welcomed me with incredible hospitality\. They introduced me to their go\-to hawker stalls and, when my family visited, even treated us to a beautiful Singaporean restaurant, which remains one of my most cherished memories\. I’ll never forget the late\-night ice cream runs, spontaneous day trips, and hours spent wandering Singapore’s hidden corners just for the fun of it\. Those shared adventures made Singapore feel like home and turned strangers into lifelong friends\.

#### After the experience

**Would you recommend this experience to others? What can they look forward to?**   
Absolutely, I would recommend this experience to any student\. Singapore offers the perfect mix of vibrant city life, rich culture, and incredible food, all while being safe and easy to navigate\. NUS provides a welcoming environment with supportive professors, a beautifully diverse community, and countless events and excursions\. Future students can look forward to exploring different neighborhoods, weekend getaways to nearby countries, delicious hawker centre meals, and friendships that cross cultures and last long after the semester ends\. It’s the kind of once\-in\-a\-lifetime experience that opens your world and leaves you with memories you’ll treasure forever\.

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**    
Going on exchange was one of the most transformative experiences I’ve ever had\. Early into my exchange, I went on my first solo trip to Malaysia over Lunar New Year, hoping it would help me learn more about myself, and it did\. Navigating the city and planning everything on my own pushed me out of my comfort zone\. But what happened next taught me even more\. Shortly after returning, I fell seriously ill and was hospitalized for a few days\. Not only did my friends show up for me, bringing comfort items and support, but strangers did too\. A doctor gave me a slice of chocolate cake he bought himself, someone prayed for me, another comforted me when I felt overwhelmed, and even brought me a little box of washed blueberries the next day\. Through this, I realized just how amazing my support system is and that I am never truly alone\.

This all happened during midterm season, so I was also stressed about keeping up with schoolwork and making friends\. It took about a month to fully recover, but I did my best to bounce back and stay on top of my classes while still making the most of my time by exploring with friends\. After recovering, I promised myself I’d take better care of my health\. I started going to the gym almost every day and focused on eating more balanced meals, little but steady changes that made me feel stronger and more grounded\. It’s in uncertain times like these that I learn the most about myself, and that I undergo the most self\-growth\. I discovered how resilient I am and how much I’m capable of handling\.
