UBC student Soyoung Jeong reflects on how her exchange at Universidad de Granada immersed her in Spanish language, culture, and daily life, while opening doors to personal growth, friendships, and unforgettable travel experiences across Spain and beyond\.

March 31, 2026

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)* 

*Soyoung Jeong is a 4th year undergraduate student in Cognitive Systems in the Faculty of Arts\. She grew up in British Columbia, Canada\.*

#### Before you go   

**Why did you pick**  [**Universidad de Granada CLM**](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/exchange/universidad-de-granada "Universidad de Granada") **for your exchange? How did you hear about it?**    
The seed was planted back in high school when I started watching Community, a sitcom about a group of wacky individuals who take Spanish 101 in college and bond over being in the same study group\. The show brought so much joy that I was inspired to take the same course when I entered UBC\. SPAN101 led to SPAN102 which led me to choose to go to a Spanish\-speaking country for exchange\. I did have some trouble deciding which one, but ultimately the fact that Spain was located in Europe meant I could explore other countries that I wanted to visit\. Then the mountains, history, culture, and last but not least, free tapas, are what led me to hone in on the city of Granada\.

Everyone will have their own unique reason for why they pick their exchange destination, but it definitely helps to talk with people who have been on exchange and to also bounce ideas around with friends and family\.

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**   
The application process was pretty straightforward, but you should definitely carve out lots of time or else you’ll likely be scrambling to complete it\. The earlier you start, definitely the better\. [Check the deadlines](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/new-programs-ubc-students/new-student-resources/go-global-deadlines "Go Global deadlines") and info sessions for what you’re interested in, especially if you’re looking into bursaries/awards\! 

I’d also recommend [booking a meeting](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/contact "Contact us") with a Go Global advisor so they can help you with any questions or if you have trouble deciding on where to go\. They are there to help you and can also connect you to other resources and people\.

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of? \(travel, cultural differences, classes, etc\.\)**   
After I got accepted, I started working on all the logistics surrounding exchange \(visa, insurance, finances, etc\.\)\. I created a folder for all my important documents which helped with organization\.

One of the best decisions I made was reaching out for a coffee chat to a student who had recently been on exchange to Granada\. I was able to get a clear idea of what school and daily life would look like and she also gave me very useful links and contacts\. Since coming back, I’ve also been asked for coffee chats, and it’s really fun to recount the experience so don’t be shy \- ask away\!

Another tip is to sort out as much admin as you can before leaving and know which things can easily be figured out once you’re there \(like your phone plan\)\. Make sure anything that needs 2\-SV with your phone number is dealt with in advance \- or else you’ll end up like me and unable to access money for two weeks\!  
For accommodation, I chose to stay with a host family because I wanted as much immersion as possible, while some students chose dormitories and others rented their own places\.

One thing that really caught me off guard was the Andalusian accent\. Spain has many different regional accents, and Andalusian is one of the trickiest\. For example, “¿Cómo estás?” sounds more like “¿Cometah?”\. I recommend watching local videos of your destination to be prepared\.

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome the anxiety?**  
My main worry was the financial side of going on exchange, but after applying to funding opportunities, working part\-time, and budgeting, that anxiety was gone by the time I was on the plane\.

With any big anxieties beforehand, I recommend reaching out, either to advisors, someone who has gone on exchange, friends, or online\. There are people out there who have had similar worries and can share insightful experiences and advice\. 

#### During the experience

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?**   
I had a blast living with my host family, even going on a trip with their children\. I improved my Spanish to a level I never could have imagined, experienced “last night was a movie” nights out, ate delicious local food, drank plenty of sangria, learned fascinating history, saw beautiful sights, and, most importantly, met amazing people\.  
Of course, I also ran into many problems, very many: being severely sick for a month, bed bugs, language barrier, flight cancellations, etc\. But now I can laugh about it\. To me, that’s what exchange is all about\. Facing whatever comes your way, learning from it, and growing through those experiences\.

On the school side, the learning was very hands\-on, especially with cultural and historical topics with guided trips\. I was fully immersed, and I also connected with students from all over the world, even traveling with them after class\.

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change or do?**   
For the first month, I became really sick, and my routine would be coming home straight after classes and sleeping until dinner\. I was missing out on core first\-month experiences of settling in, making friends and exploring the city\. After being sick of being sick, I went to the doctors to see what they could do\. I started improving immediately after taking medicine and was able to catch up on everything\. If I could, I’d give my past self a good kick for not taking better care of my health and going to get medicine asap\. 

**How were the academics?**   
In my program, I was placed at a higher level of Spanish than I thought I would be, so the first month I could understand only about 10% of what the teachers were saying\. I kept telling myself that saying, “If you’re the smartest person in the room, you’re in the wrong room” \(Confucius\)\.

Through small class sizes that emphasized speaking and presentation projects, I improved my Spanish quickly, and caught up to my classmates\!

The program was very hands\-on, with lots of trips built into the schedule and energetic teachers who packed as much learning as they could\. It wasn’t encouraged to miss school, so I’d definitely recommend planning personal trips around school\.

One trip I remember distinctly was to Pampaneira, a small town high up in the Alpujarras\. It was so unique, welcoming, and full of friendly people\. I really enjoyed it, and it’s a place I never would have discovered on my own\. Other trips included hiking, visits to nearby towns, exploring cathedrals and museums, and wandering through historic streets\.

**How was the social aspect?**  
In terms of social life, Granada is one of the major European exchange destinations, so it’s buzzing with university\-age students\. The city has an incredible energy to it\. In one night, you can go from enjoying tapas with friends, to a cozy flamenco bar, to a packed two\-floor nightclub, and then finish with churros in the morning, all in the same day\.

Granada’s specialty is free tapas, which are basically small snacks or appetizers that come with the drink you order, and they are different every time\. It’s such a fun way to spend time with friends and meet new people, and you can still enjoy them even if you don’t drink alcohol\.

Spain also has a big coffee culture, with tons of unique cafés to explore, each with its own vibe\. I’d definitely recommend making a list on Google Maps and checking them out one by one\.

#### After the experience

**Would you recommend this experience to others? What can they look forward to?**   
UBC’s Go Global program is actually the main reason I chose to attend UBC, so I may be biased, but I really believe it’s something students should definitely take advantage of\. The program is connected to universities all over the world, so there’s a good chance you can go somewhere that excites you\.

While on exchange, you’re still learning, but you also gain an incredible network and foundation of support as a student\. The resources and funding available make the experience much more accessible than traveling on your own\. It’s also a great change of pace from regular university life\. When I came back, I felt refreshed and ready to start a new year\.

Students can look forward to exploring a new country, trying amazing food, seeing beautiful sights, learning about history and culture, and meeting new people from around the world\. You also grow a lot personally, becoming more independent, open\-minded, and confident\.

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**    
I expected that exchange would be just an all\-out fun experience, full of parties, meeting friends, and exploring\. I did get to do all of that, but it also gave me so much more\. I faced challenges I never would have back in Vancouver, and without my family or a familiar environment to lean on, I had to adapt on my own\. Situations I found myself in taught me to handle the unexpected and to stop setting expectations for people, plans, or experiences\. The experience refreshed my outlook on life, gave me a greater sense of perspective, and built my confidence to step into new situations without worrying so much about small things\.

On the academic side, I can now say I am conversationally fluent in a new language, and I learned how to adapt to new learning environments\. Beyond all of this, exchange sparked a curiosity to explore and learn from the world\. Since then, I have completed a global seminar in Indonesia, and I am now taking a semester off in Sydney to experience life abroad from a new perspective\.
