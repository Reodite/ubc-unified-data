UBC student Deborah Ojo reflects on how her exchange at Sciences Po Reims opened the door to European travel, meaningful friendships, and a new global academic perspective\.

February 3, 2026

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)* 

*Meet Deborah Ojo, a fourth year Commerce student studying Accounting and International Business\. She is originally from the Toronto, ON, Canada\.*

#### Before you go   

**Why did you pick the** [**Sciences Po Reims**](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/exchange/sciences-po-reims-and-paris "Sciences Po Reims and Paris") **for your exchange? How did you hear about it?**    
Ever since high school, I had dreamed of seeing Europe\. I had never travelled beyond North America and my home country of Nigeria, so when the chance came to apply for an exchange, I knew exactly what I wanted: a school that would let me experience Europe to the fullest, both in and out of the classroom\. As an avid reader of French and British writers such as Austen and Camus, I also knew I wanted to apply to programs that would allow me to study literature through a European lens, an opportunity my Accounting degree rarely afforded me\. During this search, I came across two schools: [Bocconi University](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/exchange/universita-commerciale-luigi-bocconi "Università Commerciale Luigi Bocconi") and [Sciences Po](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/exchange/sciences-po-reims-and-paris "Sciences Po Reims and Paris")\. My first choice was actually Bocconi, but I ended up getting matched to Sciences Po Paris instead\. You can imagine my initial disappointment at not being matched to my first choice\. However, I reminded myself that Paris could be just as fun\. I mean it's Paris, right?

Funnily enough, after being matched to Paris, I came across the Sciences Po Reims campus and one night at 2:00AM, I spontaneously requested to be switched\. Why? I saw that they offered a class on Jane Austen\. Little did I know, that random late\-night choice would ended up changing my life\! 

As one would expect, soon after this late night spontaneous decision, I had my doubts\. I became quite worried that I wouldn't like Reims, and even tried to switch back to Paris\. When switching back wasn't a possibility, I felt afraid I wouldn't enjoy my exchange\. While I felt slight regret for not sticking with Paris, I tried to remind myself that everything happens for a reason\. To calm my nerves I did a lot of research about Reims and I soon discovered what an amazing location it truly was\. 

Firstly, living in Reims made life just a bit cheaper in terms of rent and groceries\. It was also the heart of the Champagne District, making it a small town with a rich and unique culture\. Additionally, the courses offered at Sciences Po Reims were very unique, ranging from classes on African film and British literature, to global politics\. The thing that made the Sciences Po Reims campus stand out the most was the campus itself\. The Sciences Po Reims campus was smaller than the Paris campus, making it easier to navigate, especially for someone like me who is quite bad with directions\. The smaller campus also ended up making it easier for me to meet people, since the student body was very small\. All of this made the Sciences Po Reims campus an unexpected yet perfect location to live out my high school dreams\.

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**   
The application process was fairly straightforward, and Go Global offered many supporting resources to assist during the process, so my tip is to not stress, as long as you ensure you are adhering to the deadlines and doing your due diligence\. The hardest part was actually choosing the school I wanted to go to and the courses I wanted to take for the study plan\. There are so many options through exchange and it can feel overwhelming if you don’t have a clear direction\. My tip is to consider feasibility: how will going to the school affect your degree planning? How will the cost of living in that country impact you? How difficult will it be to get the needed travel documents?

As a Sauder student I could either go to a school which offered commerce courses, or only take electives while abroad\. In order to fully experience Europe, fulfil my dream of studying literature, and to avoid any complications with transferring credits, I opted to only take electives at Sciences Po\. Secondly, the cost of living in Reims seemed to be similar to, if not less than Vancouver, which made the location more appealing\. Lastly, through speaking to previous students who’d gone to France for exchange, I learned that getting the needed travel documents was very feasible if I planned properly\. The hardest of the three to evaluate for me was definitely course selection\. Transferring credits back to UBC can get complicated, so my recommendation is to do the appropriate preparations and meet with Go Global before applying\.

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of? \(travel, cultural differences, classes, etc\.\)**   
The biggest part of preparation for me was finding housing and applying for a visa\. To find my housing I relied on the resources provided to me by Go Global and Sciences Po\. They offered a list of common residences and apartments that exchange students used, and I spent a lot of time vetting each location and applying for tenancy\. For Reims, be aware that student housing is very limited, so I would highly recommend finding accommodation through an external residency such as [MGEL Logement](https://mgellogement.fr/), [Sharies](https://sharies.co/en/city/reims) or [KLEY](https://www.kley.fr/en/student-residence/reims)\. You can also utilise the Sciences Po housing portal to find a student friendly landlord, as there are many nice apartments around campus that are closer to the Sciences Po Reims Campus\. As for the Visa application, the hardest part is getting an appointment\. Be aware that appointments are limited, so get ahead and start looking for slots as soon as you can\. When it comes time to attend your appointment, make sure to be prepared, and print absolutely everything\! I made the mistake of forgetting to print a photocopy of my passport and other identification documents, and ended up paying extra to have them do it for me\.

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome the anxiety?**  
My biggest worry was about making friends\. My French isn’t very strong and so I feared the language barrier would limit my ability to socialize\. I feared feeling homesick and missing out on things as a lot of my friends were still at UBC when I decided to go on exchange\. To overcome my anxiety I made sure to put myself out there, and to say yes to as many opportunities as possible, whether that was taking a spontaneous trip to Brussels, sending a random text in the exchange group chat inviting people to lunch, or simply saying yes to a night out with new friends\. Through getting out of my comfort zone, I met an amazing group of girls who I travelled with, and who made my exchange experience a million times better\. I also made sure to put in effort in keeping up with what was going on back home\. I felt less homesick through doing weekly calls with my friends\!

#### During the experience

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?**   
The overall experience was unforgettable\. Being able to experience more than 5 countries and more than 10 different cities was new, exciting and eye opening, especially for someone who hadn't travelled much before\. The ease and affordability of travelling across Europe from France, paired with my light courseload made it very easy for me to take weekend trips with friends to places like Brussels, Porto, London, and Barcelona, while still engaging myself in my coursework\.

Contrary to some assumptions, living in Reims did not hinder my ability to experience the rest of France, in particular, Paris\. In fact, I travelled to Paris quite often, sometimes to visit friends, other times just to get my hair done or enjoy the pastries and hot chocolate at the popular café, [Carette](https://paris-carette.fr/)\. There was a 30 minute train that went straight to the heart of Paris directly from Reims, and if that didn’t work, I took what quickly became my best friend on exchange, a Flixbus\.

Additionally, the exchange community at Sciences Po Reims was extremely friendly, outgoing and tight\-knit\. I was able to make friends with people from all across Europe and the globe\! Looking back, the best memories I made were through exploring Reims itself and experiencing what everyday life is like in a French town\. The morning coffee with friends, afternoon picnics in the park, midnight snacks at Le Mediterranée, and long walks under the Christmas lights down Rue Gambetta created lifelong memories that I’ll never forget\. I wouldn’t have been able to experience any of these things had I not said yes to new friendships and opportunities, and so I think the biggest thing I learned was not being afraid to say yes\!

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change or do?**   
Looking back, I wish I did more solo trips\! I had so much fun travelling with my friends, but I also ended up doing a solo trip to Wales and London near the end of my exchange and had so much fun\! During this trip I got to visit family, do a boat load of shopping, try different restaurants and even catch up with old high school friends\. That experience made me realize that solo tripping is such a unique and fun experience, and depending on the city, it can be much easier to find hotels or get tickets to certain excursions when going solo\. I didn’t do as much solo tripping out of fear, but looking back as long as you plan, there isn’t much to worry about\. I’d say if you get the chance while on exchange, try and go on at least one trip by yourself\.

**How were the academics?**   
I found the academics at Sciences Po Reims to be lighter than that of UBC\. The classes were much smaller and heavily participation based, with the exams being mainly essay writing or a final presentation\. We didn’t get much homework, if any, and when we did receive homework it was usually light reading\. As someone who enjoys reading and writing, I found this to be very manageable\. The content of the classes were also very unique\. I was able to learn about the history of Global Sociological Debates, Feminist European literature, and African American history, all topics I hadn’t had the chance to dive deeper into due to my major in Accounting\. I found this different learning experience to be extremely engaging\. Two classes in particular stuck out to me: Documenting Africa, which allowed me to study films by filmmakers from my home country, Nigeria, and Power, Professions, and Politics, where I explored Victorian\-era feminist British literature and even studied my favourite author, Jane Austen\. Due to the smaller class sizes, I was also able to bond with my professors, and make close friends through my classes\. Additionally, my course schedule worked out so that I only had classes on Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays\! This gave me a four\-day weekend, which I often spent exploring both the city and Europe as a whole\.

**How was the social aspect?**  
Through exchange I was able to meet so many amazing friends\. I found that the strongest bonds were with fellow exchange students\. On one of my first days at Sciences Po I went to a party, and spontaneously met another exchange student who added me to a group chat with other exchange students\. The next day I invited members of the group chat to grab lunch, and through the lunch I met some of my closest friends from my exchange experience\!

A unique aspect of the Sciences Po Reims campus is that the non\-exchange students are all in either their first or second year of university\. The Reims campus offers a distinctive three\-year program where students spend their first two years at Sciences Po and their final year abroad\. This structure, paired with how small the campus is, created many orientation events that were designed for first years, but were also welcome to and well attended by exchange students\. All the fun orientation events filled my first few weeks at Science Po with exciting events such as parties, scavenger hunts, competitions and city tours\. Through these events it was incredibly easy to meet fellow exchange students and from close bonds\. We created weekly routines and went to hang\-out spots, like Tuesdays at Ma Boutielle, or Thursdays at Lescale, and spent our weekends planning which city we would travel to next\. This routine made being in such a new environment feel less daunting\.

#### After the experience

**Would you recommend this experience to others? What can they look forward to?**   
I would definitely recommend exchange to others\! My exchange experience was an amazing opportunity to integrate myself in a new environment, engage in a new learning style and make new friends\. Seeing a different side of the world was life changing\!

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**    
I gained a lot of confidence and independence on exchange\. Going to a totally new country, let alone a continent, all by myself, was daunting, but oftentimes we learn the most when pushed out of our comfort zone\. Experiences such as lugging my three massive suitcases by myself from Paris to Reims, moving into a new home alone, and navigating transit systems in a different language alone, strengthened my independence and thus confidence in myself\. I now feel more comfortable in new environments and more confident in my ability to take on new things\. I also feel more eager to travel the world\! Experiencing even a little of how diverse and vibrant the world is has sparked a dream in me to one day travel to at least two countries in every continent\. I also learned so much about global politics, cultures and issues while on exchange through befriending such a diverse group of people\. This, compounded with the impact of being in an academically rigorous environment which focused on shaping a strong international lens in students, has made me look at what I learn at UBC from a more global lens\.
