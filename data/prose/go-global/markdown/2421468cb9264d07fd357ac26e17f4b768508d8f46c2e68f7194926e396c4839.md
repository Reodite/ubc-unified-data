Selected students from UBC participated in this year's Association of Pacific Rim Universities \(APRU\) Undergraduate Leadership Program to explore how social robotics and embedded AI will transform human life, industry, and society\.

July 24, 2026

*A small group of UBC students participated in this year’s* [*Association of Pacific Rim Universities \(APRU\) Undergraduate Leadership Program, “Future Ready: Leadership in the Age of AI and Robotics”*](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/global-conferences-special-programs/apru-undergraduate-leaders?https://science.ubc.ca/future&amp;gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=24025368364&amp;gbraid=0AAAAADzypeiNVbi0UFQ2XOpq7AAdi6rqt&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjwmdLSBhANEiwAkREMN2T5OF_Ia-Ozhr0AOsSNfn253M_oJoiYOrqmBLC1sUt7rUWvg6dnrRoCebEQAvD_BwE)*\. This unique opportunity for global learning was available through UBC’s membership in the APRU network, a network of leading research universities from around the Pacific Rim region that collaborate on research, education, and policy initiatives to address shared challenges\. Hosted by Southern University of Science and Technology \(SUSTech\) and located in Shenzhen, China, the program brought together future leaders from across the Pacific Rim to explore how social robotics and embedded AI will transform human life, industry, and society\.* 

[UBC students proudly display their certificates of completion \(Photo Credits: Milena Durcheva\)](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-images/Milena%20%288%29.jpeg) 

*UBC students proudly display their certificates of completion \(Photo Credits: Milena Durcheva\)*  
 

The selected students from UBC were supported by the Go Global office and participated amongst a cohort of 50\-60 undergraduate students from across APRU\-member universities\. The program which ran from June 22 to July 3, 2026, included design workshops, seminars on the ethical and social dimensions of artificial intelligence, site visits to leading technology companies in Shenzhen, and cross\-cultural exchanges with scholars and practitioners\.  

Hear from students on the success and positive impact of the program: 

-   Zoey Ma, Computer Science and Statistics  

-   Milena Durcheva, Business and Computer Science  

-   Hannah Kim, International Relations

**What drew you to apply for the APRU Undergraduate Leadership Program?**

**Zoey:** As a student studying Computer Science and Statistics at UBC, I am often deeply immersed in the technical intricacies of machine learning, statistical modeling, and data science\. I was drawn to the APRU Undergraduate Leaders' Program because it offered a rare opportunity to bridge the gap between technical expertise and global leadership\. The program's themes, particularly those focused on AI, presented the perfect environment to explore how the theoretical concepts I study in the classroom can be applied to complex, real\-world, interdisciplinary challenges alongside international peers\.

*Zoey Ma engaging in interactive conference activities \(Photo Credit: Zoey Ma\)*  
 

**Milena:** Seeing that the program was taking place in Shenzhen, China, it felt like such a phenomenal opportunity to learn about a new culture and lifestyle\. I'd never been to China before, and though it was a bit nerve\-wracking knowing that I couldn't speak the language, wasn't accustomed to the food, and didn't know what to expect overall, I'm always up for a challenge and an adventure, so there was no other option but to apply\!

**Hannah:** I was drawn towards the APRU Undergraduate Leaders' Program because it seemed like a fantastic opportunity to connect with young leaders from all over the world and gain valuable insights on one of the most pertinent issues today, AI and robotics\. Especially as an International Relations major, I find it important to consider the various global perspectives on how to properly navigate an emerging world with AI, so that we can coexist and grow peacefully with these new technologies and use them in an equitable way to create a better world\.

**What was the most memorable or unexpected moment from the program, for example a conversation, workshop, or cultural experience that stuck with you?** 

**Zoey:** Two specific moments really stuck with me\. The first was an unexpected, yet profound, conversation right at the opening of the program where someone shared, "Diversity does not come from where you are from, but what you think\." That statement completely shifted my mindset and set the tone for how I approached collaboration for the rest of the event\. The second was the final capstone project day\. Working through the intensive schedule, our team came together to propose a comprehensive design for a Shenzhen landscape design project\. Seeing our diverse perspectives merge into a cohesive, final presentation was an incredibly rewarding experience\.

**Milena:** The most memorable moment from the program was when two new friends from the program and I ordered our lunches via a drone\. One of the program's peer mentors helped us place the order, and then we walked about five minutes from the School of Design to this little vending machine\. Drones flying overhead weren’t uncommon, but seeing the drone carrying our box of food, slowly land on top of the machine, and taking the box from the drone was incredibly cool\. It made me realize just how many things were normal on the streets of Shenzhen that people from the rest of the world \[might find\] so futuristic\. Once we received our food, we collapsed the box and placed it in a section right next to the station \- so the system was also mindful of its waste production\!

**Hannah:** I had the most enlightening conversations with my roommate during the program who was from Kazakhstan studying in South Korea\. Together, we discussed the lack of gender representation in the field of AI, which made me consider how existing biases in AI are continually shaped by those who have power over it\.

**How has this experience changed the way you think about your studies, career, or future plans?**

**Zoey**: The emphasis on cognitive diversity and our work on the Shenzhen landscape design really highlighted the importance of interdisciplinary problem\-solving\. It made me realize that building effective technical or data\-driven solutions requires more than just good architecture or algorithms; it requires integrating those tools with design, urban landscapes, and diverse human perspectives\. Moving forward, I am much more focused on seeking career opportunities and projects that allow me to collaborate across different fields rather than working strictly in a technical silo\. Most importantly, I met so many friends from different backgrounds through this program\. It’s a program that only lasted 11 days, but I’ve met lifelong friends here\.

[Exploring Shenzhen, China on the Metro during free time](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-images/Zoey%20%281%29.jpeg)

*Exploring Shenzhen, China on the Metro during free time \(Photo Credit: Zoey Ma\)*  
 

**Milena**: This experience has made me realize that technology and systems that we still think of as part of the future are already being used today\. There is so much potential to utilize AI and robotics for good and to help with issues North America \(and the rest of the world\) faces that China has managed to eliminate\! For example, in Shenzhen, sanitation robots rumble up and down the streets, picking up any garbage and washing the roads\. Something like that in Canada or the United States would drastically help keep our cities clean\. So, in application to my studies and future plans, I now think \- even more than I did before \- that it's so important to draw from international inventions and innovations, and look at the successes of other countries that could be applied to our own\. There is no need to reinvent the wheel if it's already been done, and I will be keeping this at the forefront of my contributions to my academic and professional career\.

**Hannah**: This experience has made me realize how important it is to consult a multitude of perspectives, whether it'd be humanities, science, technology to move towards a sustainable future where AI can be integrated safely into the world\.

**What would you say to a fellow UBC Student who is considering applying for a program like this?**

**Zoey**: I would tell them to absolutely go for it\! It is very easy to get caught up in the routine of standard coursework, midterms, and technical projects\. A program like this forces you to step outside your comfort zone and apply your knowledge in entirely new contexts\. You will engage with incredible peers, encounter completely new ways of thinking, and return to UBC with a refreshed and broadened perspective on what you can ultimately achieve with your degree\. 

**Milena**: I would say don't hesitate\! 120% apply\. The experiences you gain will be so unique and far from anything you'd expect\. On top of that, APRU provides really cool opportunities that are hard to find elsewhere\. Lectures by field experts, insider tours of companies, cultural excursions outside the city\.\.\.the list goes on\. And the friends you make are the ultimate cherry on top\. With such a diverse group of people, all hungry to learn and experience, you make connections that will truly last a lifetime\. I had such a great time in China with students from all over the world, and I can't wait to see them again, wherever it may be\!

**Hannah**: Definitely go for it\! These experiences are a formative part of intellectual growth and you will create both professional and personal connections that can truly go a long way\.
