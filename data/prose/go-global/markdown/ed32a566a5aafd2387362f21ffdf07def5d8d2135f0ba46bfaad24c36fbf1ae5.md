UBC undergrad Stephanie Chen reflects on how an exchange at the National University of Singapore shaped her academic journey and personal growth\.

December 5, 2025

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)* 

*Meet Stephanie Chen, a 5th UBC undergraduate student in the Sociology program in the Faculty of Arts\. She was born and raised in Vancouver, BC\.*

#### Before you go   

**Why did you pick the** [**National University of Singapore**](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/exchange/national-university-singapore) **for your exchange? How did you hear about it?**    
I chose National University of Singapore \(NUS\) for several reasons\. I have always wanted to visit Singapore and explore Southeast Asia, and watching films such as “Seven Letters”, “Crazy Rich Asians”, and “Ilo Ilo” deepened my interest in the country’s culture and urban landscapes\. These stories ignited a desire to experience Singapore’s rich cultural blend firsthand\. Moreover, choosing NUS as my exchange destination felt natural as it is one of Asia’s top universities known for its academic excellence, diverse international community, and vibrant campus life\. Altogether, it felt like the perfect place to learn, grow, and immerse myself in a new environment\.

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**   
The process was smooth and straightforward\! As the Go Global application format changed, I would heavily advise students to actively seek guidance and help with our Go Global advisors to soothe any concerns\.

When it comes to course selection at NUS, I would recommend that students research courses carefully to ensure they align with their academic interests and study\-abroad goals\. At NUS, it can be quite challenging to switch or change modules once the semester begins\. There are specific deadlines for adding or dropping courses, and missing them can make adjustments difficult\. However, with proper planning and research beforehand, navigating the system becomes much easier\.

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of? \(travel, cultural differences, classes, etc\.\)**   
From my own experience, I wish I had learned Singlish before going to Singapore\. Singlish is a mix of English with influences from languages such as Malay, Mandarin, Tamil, and Hokkien\. Although Singapore is an English\-speaking country, most people speak Singlish in their day\-to\-day lives\. This can create a language barrier with your peers, professors, and daily interactions with staff workers\. However, many local students at NUS are happy to help teach you Singlish slang and phrases to help you navigate through the country\.

*Here are some Singlish terms I learned while I was abroad:*

-   Ex: A shortened word for expensive\.
-   Sian: A feeling of boredom or frustration\.
-   Aiyoh\!: An exclamation to express concern, shock, or frustration\.
-   Can/Cannot: People like to use “can or cannot,” when they are either available or unavailable to attend or do something\. For example: “Would you like to study tonight?” “Can\!”

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome the anxiety?**  
Before my exchange, I knew I would experience homesickness\. Although I was excited for this new chapter of my life in Singapore, Vancouver was my safe space\. Most of my friends knew how nervous I was for my exchange, so they prepared handwritten letters and previous photographs of us together to encourage me throughout my journey\. I decided to bring those letters and photographs as a token that symbolized home\. During my exchange, their support and kind words helped me through the hardest days, allowing me to step out of my comfort zone and explore Singapore, as well as make new friends\.

#### During the experience

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?**   
The overall experience was truly an unforgettable memory of mine\. On my exchange, I had the privilege of staying at [Tembusu Residential College](https://tembusu.nus.edu.sg/), which had its own tight\-knit community with a variety of students from different disciplines\. I am grateful that I stayed at Tembusu as it not only gave me the opportunity to enrich my studies but also make life\-long friends\. The friends I made at Tembusu carried me through every high and low of exchange\. We built our own little world together like eating dinners side by side, scrubbing mold out of my room, wandering through Singapore, and having those late\-night hallway conversations that somehow made everything feel lighter\. Those moments made Tembusu feel like home\. Staying at Tembusu College taught me the importance of community, especially during challenging times\. Everyone there was so kind and supportive and always willing to help and befriend you\.

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change or do?**   
If I could do anything differently, it would be to be more aware of potential mold infestation\. Due to its tropical climate, mold infestation is common in Singapore\. During my exchange, I experienced health issues due to the growing mold infestation in my room\. Although students are advised to take caution, it sometimes can occur when you least expect it\. If I had the opportunity to do something different, I would invest in a portable air conditioner \(AC\) or invest in a room with AC\.

**How were the academics?**   
Personally, I found the academic system at NUS to be much easier than at UBC\. The classes were once a week alongside tutorials for each class, which allowed students to learn materials both independently and collectively during tutorial sessions\. I found this system refreshing because it empowered me to express my ideas creatively and learn from my peers\. It also gave me flexibility to explore the country and take part in activities, helping me maintain a healthy balance between academic success and having fun\.

**How was the social aspect?**  
NUS had a vibrant and close\-knit campus community that made it easy to meet people from all over the world\. During my exchange, I stayed at UTown, where many international students lived\. Since the residences were all located close to one another, it was easy to run into familiar faces and stay connected\. Living in UTown also kept me up to date with campus events and group activities, which made socializing more fun and engaging\. Moreover, each residence offered a range of extracurricular clubs, making it accessible to find groups that matched my hobbies and interests\. Through these experiences, I met new people and formed lasting friendships\.

Outside of campus, my local friends showed me around Singapore and introduced me to some of the city's most popular spots, like Haji Lane\. One memorable outing was when we went thrifting, sang karaoke, and enjoyed delicious Thai food together during semester break\! Most of my friends were excited to share their favourite spots and foods, which was not only fun, but also gave me the opportunity to immerse myself into their cultures\.

I also had the privilege to receive funding from the Go Global Award which made it easier for me to travel around Asia \- Tokyo, Japan, Bali, Indonesia, and Krabi, Thailand\. From kayaking in lagoons in Krabi and exploring beautiful temples in Bali, to spending time with my family in Tokyo, these trips were unforgettable and highlights of my exchange\.

#### After the experience

**Would you recommend this experience to others? What can they look forward to?**   
Absolutely\! My time abroad offered a meaningful opportunity to reflect on my personal growth as an adult, student, friend, and daughter\. The exchange experience was both deeply fulfilling and, at times, challenging, pushing me to confront unfamiliar situations and adapt to new environments\. Through these experiences, I developed a stronger sense of resilience and self\-assurance\. More importantly, being away from home made me deeply appreciative of the love and support of my family and friends, whose presence and encouragement I had often taken for granted\. 

> "Looking back, I am proud of how this journey strengthened not only my independence but also my gratitude for the people who have shaped and supported me along the way\. "

Therefore, students can look forward to several meaningful and rewarding aspects such as personal growth, deeper appreciation for loved ones \(home and from exchange\), and of course make memorable experiences\.

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**    
My exchange at NUS had a profound impact on both my academic and personal development\. Academically, studying in a new environment encouraged me to think more critically and creatively, as I learned to adapt to different teaching styles and collaborate with peers from diverse backgrounds\. It has strengthened my ability to engage with new perspectives and apply knowledge in more global and interdisciplinary ways\.

On a personal level, the experience pushed me to step out of my comfort zone and become more resilient, adaptable, and self\-assured\. Living independently in a different country taught me valuable lessons about responsibility and balance, while also deepening my appreciation for the people and support systems back home\. Overall, my time abroad not only expanded my worldview but also shaped me into a more confident, open\-minded, and empathetic individual\.
