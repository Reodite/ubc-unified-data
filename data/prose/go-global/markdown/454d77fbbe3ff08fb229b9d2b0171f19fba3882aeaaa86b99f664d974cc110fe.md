Sharon Kim reflects on how hands‑on learning in Iceland transformed her understanding of sustainability and inspired newfound confidence in travelling independently\.

March 10, 2026

*Are you a UBC undergrad thinking about going on a student exchange? It can be a daunting decision\. You might wonder, what's it like spending a year or a semester in another country? How can you prepare? And how do you handle it when things go sideways? We talked with a group of* [*UBC Global Ambassadors*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/new-student-experiences/global-ambassadors-and-global-cultural-ambassadors) *who studied abroad with a* [*Go Global program*](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global)*\. They spoke with us candidly, sharing their personal impressions, fears and misgivings, best memories, top tips and advice\. \(This story is part of a series of Global Exchange Insider interviews\.\)* 

**Program Featured:** Iceland Field Course, Systems Approaches to Regional Sustainability, ISCI 361/461 taught by Dr\. Denise Gabriel, Dr\. Steven Quane, and Dr\. Lee Groat

*Sharon Kim is a 3rd year UBC undergraduate student in the Bachelor of Arts majoring in Economics\. She was born and raised in Langley, BC, Canada\.*

#### Before you go   

**Why did you pick the** [**Iceland Field Course**](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/programs-ubc-students/global-seminars/programs/iceland-field-course-systems-approaches "Iceland Field Course - Systems Approaches to Regional Sustainability: ISCI 361/461 (3 credits)") **for your Global Seminar? How did you hear about it?**    
  
First of all, how could I not choose Iceland? I mean, come on\. How could I say no to its breathtaking scenery? But more seriously, for a long time, Iceland was one of those places I had always dreamed of visiting, but never knew how or when I’d be able to make it happen\. One day, I was on the Go Global website, just looking into study abroad exchange opportunities, when I came across the Global Seminar listings\. That's when I stumbled upon the ISCI 361 Go Global Seminar \(Iceland Field Course: System’s Approaches to Regional Sustainability\), which sent students to learn hands\-on in Iceland, and I instantly knew I had to apply\. You really never know where the universe might lead you\! Before this trip, I hadn’t had any experience travelling without my family, so I thought a two\-week seminar would be the perfect way to dip my toes into the water\. The course topic was also a big determining factor for me\. The bottom line is, you’re taking a course and paying the full tuition for it, so naturally, I wanted to take a class I was interested in\. I’ve always been passionate about the environment, and this particular seminar covered topics about the environment and sustainability in Iceland\. The credits also happened to fulfill degree requirements for my graduation, so it was a perfect fit\.

**What was the application process like? Any tips?**   
  
Overall, it was a very easy process\. The application itself is super straightforward and consists mainly of written questions about your interest in the program and any experience travelling or working with a group\. If your application passes the first round, you may be contacted for an interview with a Go Global Advisor, where you can further expand on these questions \(being interviewed depends on your faculty\)\. ISCI 361 is an Integrated Sciences course about systems\-thinking on all three levels of sustainability: environmental, economic, and social\. As an Arts student, I had pretty low expectations of getting accepted to the program \-\- but never say never\! In my written application, I chose to highlight the uniqueness I brought as a student outside of the faculty and the new ideas and perspectives I could contribute as a student who is not from any science\-related program\. Being passionate about the environment, I also emphasized my genuine interest in the program’s scope\. With all that in mind, the best tip I could give is to talk about your unique experiences and how they make you stand out\. And, don’t be afraid to write with personality\! The application does not have to be a formal essay\. Crack jokes, be witty; our advisors are humans too\!

**How did you prepare? Anything to be aware of? \(travel, cultural differences, classes, etc\.\)**   
  
I think that when travelling, there is only so much preparation you can do at home\. The adventures will flood into your life the moment you step off the plane if you allow them to, regardless of how prepared/underprepared you are\. Since I was only away for about two weeks, there weren’t many logistics to plan for compared to a longer study abroad experience\. That being said, there are a few things I would suggest you consider in preparation for your trip\. I think some of the worst things that can happen when travelling are losing your luggage, losing your passport, or getting sick\. So as long as you do whatever you need to do to avoid those situations \(air tags, vitamins, etc\.\), you’ll already be halfway there\. The next thing is mentally and emotionally preparing yourself for your trip\. For me, that means researching the place and culture I’m planning to visit\. This could look like familiarizing yourself with traditional food, learning a few words in the local language, or doing a quick search on cultural norms in that area\. Not only does this help you be a responsible and respectful traveler, but it also enriches your experience tenfold while you’re actually there\. Everything else, like how many socks to pack \(7 or 700?\) or whether you should bring your rain jacket or your puffer jacket \(or both\), will sort itself out in the end\.

**What worries did you have beforehand? How did you overcome the anxiety?**   
  
My biggest worry before the trip was finances\. Travelling can be expensive, and I hoped my time in Iceland would be as stress\-free as possible\. Go Global does its best to support students in my exact position by offering a generous range of awards, many of which students are eligible to apply for\. I was able to cover most of my flight costs with the award, which was a huge weight off my shoulders\. Preparing a realistic budget for my personal spending also helped alleviate a lot of the stress\. For many Global Seminars, the program fees will cover most \(if not all\) of the costs for accommodation and transportation, and sometimes meals, depending on which seminar you take\. In my case, most of my spending went toward groceries, which my friends and I split\. We cooked many of our meals back at our hostels\. Cooking meals instead of eating out most nights was one of the most effective ways I kept the trip affordable for me\. With that, I was able to spend some money on personal items and any souvenirs that I wanted to bring home worry\-free\.

#### During the experience

**How was the overall experience? What did you learn?**   
  
I truly loved every second of this trip, even the harder moments \(which, to be fair, were not many\)\. Seeing all the beautiful sights in Iceland hit me with a storm of emotions\. I am incredibly grateful for the privilege of witnessing the Icelandic nature that is slowly disappearing from our world, and I truly hope we learn to protect it better so future generations\-\-and all of you\-\-can experience it too\. This trip challenged me to think and learn from different perspectives, both academically and personally\. I really appreciated the emphasis on hands\-on learning\. Our classrooms consisted of local beaches, active volcano sites, national parks, renewable energy plants, and so much more\. I learned a lot about sustainability and how different micro\-systems interact and depend on one another to sustain the larger whole\. As an economics student, seeing all these lessons on sustainability being practically implemented by Iceland’s government and its large corporations was extremely inspiring\. Ultimately, this experience made me feel more connected to the topics I was learning and helped me discover new ways of engaging with ideas and dialogue\.

**If you could change something or do it differently, what would you change or do?**   
  
I wish I had done more individual research on Iceland and its culture before the trip\. While our pre\-departure modules included lessons on Iceland’s culture and some basic Icelandic language skills, I still think that extra personal effort really amplifies the experience\. This next change was something my friend, who went on a different Go Global seminar, told me, and I thought, man, that was a good idea\. Why didn’t I think of that? She brought a journal and committed to writing detailed entries every day on her trip, regardless of how tired she was at the end of each day\. I think this is a brilliant way to capture and sustain some of those quieter yet still beautiful moments\. I took thousands of photos and videos, but there’s only so much you can capture visually\. Some of the smaller, quieter moments from my trip to Iceland are already fading, and that honestly breaks my heart a little\! So the next time I travel, a journal and pen are non\-negotiables\.

**How were the academics as a student outside of the Faculty?**   
  
When I was accepted into the seminar, 99% of me was ecstatic, and the remaining 1% was slightly nervous because I’m an economics major who hadn’t taken a science class in years\. To my relief there were no titrations in sight\. The teaching team made the course content very approachable for students from different academic backgrounds\. The course load itself was very manageable, consisting of light pre\-travel modules, some mid\-travel assignments, and a post\-travel final paper, which students were given about a month after returning to complete\. During the trip itself, learning was hands\-on and interactive, which I loved\. One of the most valuable things about Go Global Seminars is getting access to the small, close\-knit learning environment\. Working closely alongside the professors and other instructors leading the program \(Dr\. Denise Gabriel and Dr\. Lee Groat\) was so much fun and a great way to expand my horizon of opportunities within and outside of UBC\. So huge shout out to the instructing team \(Dr\. Denise Gabriel and Dr\. Lee Groat\) and all the staff at Go Global for supporting our big group of students and making all the content so exciting to learn\. None of the trip would have been at all possible without them\!

**How was the social aspect?**   
  
For Global Seminars, you’re placed in a group of students led by a group of instructors, and you spend pretty much the entire trip together \-\- which for some can sound like either a dream, or for others a nightmare\. I really like meeting new people, but I also deeply value my alone time, so I was a bit nervous about living with 16 strangers for two weeks\. Little did I know that all the students felt the same way and were all very friendly\. I was surprised by how close we’d all gotten in such a short amount of time\. If making friends on these trips concerns you, I promise that you have nothing to worry about\. Something else that surprised me was Iceland’s city life\. Before I knew more about Iceland, I mostly thought of endless plains, cool rocks, and waterfalls everywhere \-\- which definitely do exist, and are the majority of Iceland’s landscape\. But the capital city, Reykjavík, has a totally different vibe\. It was vibrant and busy and full of so much life\. During the days we spent in Reykjavík, my friends and I spent our free time perusing the streets and visiting shops and exhibitions\. There is so much to see, I have already vowed to return someday\.

#### After the experience

**Would you recommend this experience to others? What can they look forward to?**   
  
I would absolutely recommend this experience to other students\. It’s been nearly a year since the trip, and I still talk about it all the time\. This experience was what made me want to become a Go Global Ambassador\! I want to reach more people beyond my own circle and encourage them to go for it\. Whether you apply to ISCI 361 or another Go Global program, I strongly encourage students to pursue some kind of exchange or travel experience while in university if they have the privilege to\. Travelling forced me to learn so much about myself and my identity that I otherwise never would have discovered\. It taught me the joy of stepping out of my comfort zone, and I think that’s something that anyone would benefit from\.

**What was the impact on your academic and personal development?**    
  
My academic experience in this seminar as an Arts student was very fruitful and allowed me to challenge my ways of thinking and learning\. It helped me engage with students in other faculties that I might not have met otherwise\. I was able to synthesize knowledge from my own academic background, learning about topics I would never encounter within my degree\. It also gave me a renewed appreciation for other specializations at UBC\. Our group had students from Engineering, Business, Forestry, Arts, and Science backgrounds\. It was a real mixing pot\! Being in such a diverse group really helped foster some truly interesting and insightful conversations, and we were all able to help each other grow in new ways\.

I hate to say this trip changed my life\! \-\- but this trip totally changed my life\. As I already mentioned, this experience was my first time travelling alone and was sort of a trial run for future study abroad plans\. One of the biggest personal takeaways from this trip for me was a lifestyle shift\. You know that feeling when you go somewhere new, and you’re suddenly filled with so much curiosity and energy to explore the place and engage with the people? I felt that way in Iceland, and it made me realize how\-\-dare I say\-\-lazy I’d gotten about my own hometown\. And this is Vancouver\! In British Columbia\! One of the most beautiful places in the world\! So since returning, I challenged myself to sustain that same sense of curiosity and intention in my daily life\. With that, whenever I think back on this trip, it reminds me to always stay grateful and seek joy, learning, and growth no matter where I am or who I’m with\.
