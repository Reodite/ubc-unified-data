There is nothing worse than waking up sick the week of midterms\. I have been there more times than I’d like to admit, sniffling through a lecture and surviving on cold medicine\. After one particularly rough flu season in second year, I started paying much more attention to what I was actually eating, and it made such a noticeable difference\. Here are seven foods I started adding into my routine that helped me get through the semester without completely falling apart\!

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/ben-ashby-TAqEFzBEKbY-unsplash-940x629.jpg)

## **Citrus Fruits**

They are going to be your body’s best friend when cold season hits\. I kept a bag of clementines on my desk during study sessions because they are cheap, easy to peel, and you can eat them without getting your keyboard sticky\. Squeezing lemon into hot water in the morning is also something I started doing almost by accident and it somehow became my favourite part of my morning\. If eating a whole orange feels like too much effort, just start there\.

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/sara-cervera-4caIPcmVDII-unsplash-940x625.jpg)

## **Yogurt**

This was an easy sell because who doesn’t like yogurt\! Studies show that your gut health has a lot to do with how often you get sick, and yogurt has probiotics that help with that\. I switched to plain Greek yogurt with honey and some fruit on top, and it became my go\-to breakfast on mornings when I had an 8am and no time to cook\. If plain yogurt sounds boring, adding a spoonful of peanut butter or some granola genuinely makes it feel like a real meal\.

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/ji-jiali-r8ySJfxvESQ-unsplash-940x528.jpg)

## **Garlic**

I know, I know, but hear me out\. I started throwing a clove or two into basically EVERYTHING I cooked\. Be it pasta sauce, stir fry or even roasted vegetables \(and after a while I stopped noticing it was even there\)\. The smell is honestly the biggest barrier, but cooking it down almost completely gets rid of it\. 

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/prchi-palwe-YUIMwavk7AQ-unsplash-940x627.jpg)

## **Turmeric**

This one is very easy to sneak into things\. I add a small pinch to rice, soups, and scrambled eggs, and I genuinely cannot taste it most of the time\. Turmeric golden milk before bed is also something I tried during finals and it became oddly calming, kind of like a warm hug in a mug\. Pro Tip: Add black pepper when you cook with it because it helps your body actually absorb it\.

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/kelly-sikkema-VQWiBp63eJQ-unsplash-940x627.jpg)

## **Ginger**

Ginger surprised me the most honestly\. I started making ginger tea on cold Vancouver mornings \(just a few slices boiled in water with honey\), and it became one of my favourite rituals\. It also settles your stomach if you are the type to stress\-eat your way through finals \(no judgment, same\)\. If you are feeling extra funky, ginger and turmeric shots are a thing at most grocery stores\. They taste intense for about three seconds and then you feel weirdly invincible, I promise\. 

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/aleksei-zhivilov-jim_U8LfJOw-unsplash-940x529.jpg)

## **Leafy Greens**

Okay, yes\. Obviously, leafy greens are on this list\. Nobody is surprised\. But the reason I am including them is because they are genuinely one of the easiest things to add without actually changing what you eat\. A handful of spinach in a smoothie? You cannot taste it\. Tossed into instant ramen? Takes two seconds\. Mixed into scrambled eggs? Barely noticeable\. You do not have to become a salad person, you just have to add a handful somewhere\!

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/07/maksim-shutov-pUa1On18Jno-unsplash-940x627.jpg)

## **Nuts and Seeds**

I started keeping a little container of mixed nuts in my bag after I realized I was getting to 3pm lectures, having eaten nothing since 9am\. Almonds, pumpkin seeds, sunflower seeds, all genuinely good for keeping your immune system supported\. They also stopped me from spending four dollars on a granola bar from the vending machine every single day, which was a bonus\!

---

None of these are miracle cures, and I am definitely not a nutritionist\. But making small, consistent swaps, a handful of spinach here, a ginger tea there, has truly helped me feel more resilient during the parts of the semester when everyone around me was getting sick\. Even one or two of these added into your routine makes a real difference\!

What are your go\-to foods when you feel a cold coming on? Share them in the comments below\!
