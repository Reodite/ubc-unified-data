Every September, new students flock to beautiful British Columbia\. I moved to Vancouver two years ago from Saskatoon, SK\. At first, Vancouver felt nothing like home, making my transition nerve\-wracking\. I was fearful of living away from home as well as excited for the next chapter\. It took me some time to find a new community here and settle in\. Whether moving from another province or a different country, here are my tips for moving to BC to make your transition as easy as possible\!

### Transportation:

During my first bus ride in Vancouver, I was so scared I would miss my stop\! Thankfully, using [tr](https://learningcommons.ubc.ca/tips-for-first-time-transit-users/?login)[ansit](https://learningcommons.ubc.ca/tips-for-first-time-transit-users/?login) is super easy and convenient in Vancouver\. UBC connects to almost all parts of the city by bus, with a large [bus depot](https://www.google.com/maps/place/UBC+Bus+Exchange/@49.2672397,-123.2507645,17z/data=!4m6!3m5!1s0x548673eaeb14557f:0x1e9152a90dadee1a!8m2!3d49.2672398!4d-123.2479321!16s%2Fg%2F11fdx25kg6?entry=tts) on campus\. [UPass](https://learningcommons.ubc.ca/tips-for-first-time-transit-users/?login) is included in our student fees, and allows you to ride any bus, Skytrain, or Seabus around Metro Vancouver\.

Car\-share programs like [Evo](https://evo.ca/search?gclid=Cj0KCQjwmvSoBhDOARIsAK6aV7g2W4e3O5uL3ItMQvkB1ScLhIp7EQDyBGgtz8CLVEc-FL6N1DSDSbAaAs0sEALw_wcB&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds) and [Modo](https://www.modo.coop/) are great if I am in a hurry\. Having a Saskatchewan driver’s license, I did not have to get a new one at [ICBC](https://www.icbc.com/) \(the office that handles driver’s licenses, car insurance, and Provincial Health Cards\) when I moved, since [I’m a student](https://www.icbc.com/insurance/moving-travelling/insurance-students-military-tourists)\. This also holds true for international students while their study permit is valid, although international students will need to get a Provincial Health Card while students from other provinces do not\.

There are even more ways to get around the city, like [biking, rollerskating, e\-scooters etc](https://learningcommons.ubc.ca/bus-blues-explore-the-alternatives/)\. Vancouver has a ton of [protected bike lanes](https://vancouver.ca/streets-transportation/cycling-routes-maps-and-trip-planner.aspx) \(meaning you don’t have to cross paths with any cars\!\) and they often connect with transportation hubs, like Skytrain stations, for longer trips too\.

### Employment Opportunities:

One of the first things I did when I moved to BC was find a part\-time job\. UBC has a plethora of opportunities in a variety of fields\. The [AMS job site](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/opportunities/jobs/) is particularly useful, since most of the jobs are located right on campus\. [WorkLearn positions](https://ubc-csm.symplicity.com/) also provide excellent opportunities for on\-campus jobs in research, library services and much more\. Fun fact: the Chapman Learning Commons Help Desk is staffed by fellow students in WorkLearn positions\!

It can be hard to land a first job, I know\! I found it helpful to go to [job fairs](https://hireastudent.ubc.ca/campus/career-fairs/?login) on campus to connect with recruiters and fast\-tracking the hiring process\. There are also lots of [resources](https://learningcommons.ubc.ca/preparing-for-an-interview/) and [workshops](https://students.ubc.ca/career/events-workshops) on everything from writing effective cover letters to how to [prep for an interview](https://learningcommons.ubc.ca/preparing-for-an-interview/)\.
