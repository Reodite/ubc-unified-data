Does waking up at 5 AM actually change your life, or is it just Instagram hype? We’ve all seen those reels and read those self\-help books that swear by it, the secret of billionaires, athletes, and traditionally “successful” people\. I’ll admit, I got curious\. So, I decided to try it myself: waking up at 5 AM for a week and seeing if it really lives up to the hype\.

## Day 1: The Messy Start

Let’s be real, my first day was rough\. I went to bed at midnight \(rookie mistake\) and got maybe 5 hours of sleep\. Technically, I did wake up at 5… but all that meant was opening my eyes, scrolling on my phone in bed, and maybe sneaking in a nap around 7\. By 8:30, I realised this wasn’t the kind of “transformative” morning I had in mind\.

## Day 2: Learning that Routine Starts at Night

That day I did some research and learned something important: a 5 AM morning starts the night before\. Our bodies need at least 7 hours of sleep \(if not more\), otherwise the early wake\-up just backfires\. So I made myself go to sleep by 10 PM\. I also made a simple morning plan so I wouldn’t just end up doom\-scrolling again\. 

The next morning felt different\. Sure, I was still groggy, but I got up, meditated, did some yoga, and actually made a proper breakfast\. That slow start meant I wasn’t rushing through my day, I felt more prepared, calmer, and surprisingly organized\. \(Yes, I was still yawning a lot, but at least I wasn’t sprinting from one task to another\.\) 

## Day 3: Structure, Wins and…Slips

By Day 3, I added time stamps to my routine\. Having a mini\-schedule helped me get so much done before 9 AM\. I studied, worked on extracurriculars, even sent emails that I’d been procrastinating to my professors\. Plus, I ate more than just a banana for breakfast\.   

Then came Day 4\. Let’s just say a friend’s birthday dinner \+ me pretending I can survive on less than 5 hours of sleep \= the alarm had no chance\. I woke up at 9, and honestly? The cake was worth it\.

## Day 5\-7: Motivation, Mindfulness, and Finding Balance

Getting back into routine on Day 5 was tough\. I had to remind myself why I was doing this: those peaceful, slow mornings where I didn’t feel rushed\. That thought kept me going\. For the last couple of days, I mixed in some mindfulness activities\. One morning, I tried a puzzle \(spoiler: it stressed me out more than it calmed me\), and the next I did some painting, which I’d *highly* recommend\. It was surprisingly grounding and definitely took the edge off midterm stress\.

## So, would I do it again?

Honestly? As a university student, 5 AM feels a little extreme\. Between late\-night assignments, 11:59 PM deadlines, and just hanging out with friends, it’s not always realistic\. But here’s what I *did* learn: slow, intentional mornings are worth it\.

I’ve decided I’ll start waking up around 7 instead of 9\. Those extra two hours still give me the space to breathe, eat, plan, and not feel like I’m chasing the day\. And that, in itself, feels like a win\. 

Maybe I’m not cut out for the 5 AM club just yet, but I’ve found my own version that works, and I think that’s the point\. Everyone’s “ideal” looks a little different\. So, comment down below, what’s yours? What’s the wake\-up time that makes you feel your best?
