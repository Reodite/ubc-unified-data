When I first started university, reading research papers felt impossible\. I remember opening a paper for a class, seeing dense paragraphs and complicated figures, and immediately feeling overwhelmed\. I thought I was supposed to understand everything on the first read, but when I inevitably did not, it just made me avoid them even more\. However, after a couple of years of trial and error, and lots of confusion, I’ve found a few approaches that make reading research papers feel way more manageable\.

## Starting with the Big Picture

One of the biggest changes I made was how I began reading a paper\. Instead of jumping straight into the first paragraph of the introduction, now I begin with the abstract and then skim the conclusion\. This gives me a general sense of what the researchers were trying to do and what they found\. Having that overview helps me feel more confident diving into the details\. Even if I don’t fully grasp the method section yet, at least I understand what the paper is aiming to achieve\.

## Breaking the Paper into Smaller Sections

Reading an entire paper in one sitting completely drains my energy\. Instead, I treat each section as its own task\. I’ll focus on the introduction one day, then come back later to look at the methods or results\. This makes the process feels less intimidating and easier to fit into my schedule\. I’ve also noticed that taking breaks helps me absorb the information better instead of zoning out halfway through\.

## Talking it through with others

Discussing or going through research with classmates is one of my favourite ways to approach a new paper\. When I explain a section out loud, it becomes clear which parts I actually understand and which parts I need to go over again\. Group discussions or study sessions have helped me catch things I completely missed on my own\. Hearing how others interpret the same paper has also provided me with different perspectives and ideas that help me further understand what I’m reading\.

## Accepting that I Won’t Know Everything

Probably the mos t important thing I’ve realized is that it’s okay not to understand everything\. For every paper, I read, there are always sections I skim over or concepts I need to look up later\. To help with this, I’ve found highlighting unfamiliar words or concepts and then researching them has been helpful\. However, I always remember that research papers are written by experts – for experts – so struggling with them doesn’t mean I’m doing something wrong\. I’ve now learned to focus on understanding the main ideas rather than every single detail\. Looking back, reading research papers has become less about “getting it right” and more about building familiarity with the subject over time\. The more I practice, the more confident I feel, even when things are still confusing\.
