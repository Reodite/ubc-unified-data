Leaving the life you’ve known, the people you grew up with, and your family to settle into another country is not an easy task\. Being an international student myself, I have gone through phases of extreme homesickness, missing friends back home and feeling left out of the hangouts/events in my hometown\. Watching through a screen is a blessing in today’s day and age, but it still doesn’t make the feeling of homesickness go away\.

Establishing a life in another country is time\-consuming, and sometimes you might get so caught up that you won’t realise when your friendships and relationships back home have started to drift a little\. I have been there as well, and now that I’m almost graduating, I hope my experiences help you navigate being in a long\-distance relationship with your loved ones\.

## Understanding the time\-difference

This might seem obvious, but I want to emphasise the importance of it\. Having a mutual understanding of the time zone between you and your loved ones is the first step in maintaining a bond\.

Sometimes my friends would forget this and call me around 5 am, or I would forget and call them late at night\. It might take some time to get used to it\. A widget showing the time in your loved one’s city on your lockscreen might help in reminding you, as it helps me\. Eventually, though, planning your calls will become much easier\.

## Planning calls and hangouts

This might sound silly, but now that you’re not a car ride away and both you and your loved ones have opposing and busy schedules, planning a time for calls is probably the way to go\. I try to book some time off at least once every two weeks for catching up, where we would either just talk, study, watch a movie, or even play an online game together\! This has worked well in maintaining my relationships\. This way, my friends and I are always in the loop of what’s happening in our lives without disrupting our own schedules\. 

## Putting in the effort

Now that seeing your friends is not as easy, and you can’t tell if something is bothering them just by meeting them, putting in the effort to check\-in is always appreciated\. If I knew my friends or family might be going through a hard time, just sending a quick text or calling to remind them that I’m thinking of them goes a long way in keeping that bond intact\. We all get busy, especially in this phase of our lives, so being open\-minded and understanding of the changes that your relationships are bound to go through is extremely important\. 

Your relationships will not be the same as they used to be before you moved away, which is completely normal\. Growing together in this new phase of your lives, where you plan calls, text more often, and don’t communicate as much as you used to, is all part of the parcel of being an international student\. When you do go back home to visit, the feeling of nostalgia might linger to the time before you left, except with a hint of growth and exposure that will be worth it in the end\. 

## Every relationship is different

Lastly, I would like to point out that every relationship is different and everyone has their own unique way of continuing their bond even if they’re far apart\. These tips worked for me but might not work for you and that is alright\. Taking the time to talk it out with your loved one on how you want this to work might help you navigate the long\-distance and avoid the ever\-chasing feeling of growing distant\. Even if you do grow apart, which has happened to some of my relationships, being understanding of that will ironically also help maintain the bond\.
