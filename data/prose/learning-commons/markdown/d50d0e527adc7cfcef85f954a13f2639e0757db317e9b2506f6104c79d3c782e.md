When I first came to UBC as an international student, taxes felt like an impossible puzzle\. I had no idea where to start, what forms I needed, or how to ensure I was doing everything correctly\. The language of T4s, tax credits, and slips like T2202 was totally new to me\. I remember feeling overwhelmed and worried I might make mistakes that could cause trouble later\. I spent hours researching online, trying to piece everything together, but it never really clicked\.

That’s when I heard about the [**UBC Tax Assistance Clinic for Students \(TACS\)**](https://www.ubctacs.com/)\. I’d seen it mentioned a few times in student forums, but never thought I’d actually need it\. But after realizing how confusing taxes can be, I decided to give it a shot\. Honestly, it turned out to be one of the best decisions I made during my first year here\. 

This clinic is run by students who understand the Canadian tax system\. Its main purpose is to help students and others who have simple and modest incomes file their tax returns properly\. It is completely free and primarily targets individuals with straightforward tax situations, such as students, new immigrants, or those with income from part\-time jobs or scholarships\. The volunteers are trained and motivated to make the process easy to understand and less intimidating\.

I visited the clinic during tax season, which runs from late February to mid\-April\. Booking an appointment online was simple, though they also welcome drop\-ins during their open hours\. When I went, I brought the following items: 

-   A valid government ID 
-   Social Insurance Number \(SIN\) 
-   Tax documents: T2202, T4A, T4, T5 T4 

One of the volunteers sat down with me one\-on\-one, and helped me understand what each form was for and guided me through filling each one out step\-by\-step\. What I appreciated most was that they explained everything clearly and in language that was easy to understand\. They answered all my questions patiently, which helped me feel a lot more confident and less overwhelmed\. The entire process felt manageable because I had someone to walk me through it\. 

If your income is pretty simple, maybe a part\-time job, a few scholarships, or some basic benefits, this clinic is a great fit for you\. It’s a hands\-on way to learn more about your finances and get comfortable managing your own tax situation\. On the other hand, if things are a bit more complicated, like having self\-employment income, rental properties, or earnings from abroad, this might not be the right service\. In that case, working with an accountant or other services could be a better option to make sure everything’s done right\.

What surprised me most was how much I learned about the Canadian tax system during my appointment\. It was not just about filing forms\. I gained a better understanding of how taxes work in Canada and what deductions I might be eligible for\. Learning this made me feel more confident about managing my finances\. 

If you are feeling unsure about doing your taxes, I recommend trying the clinic\. It’s a friendly, supportive environment, and having someone guide you through the process makes a big difference\. Once you get the hang of it, volunteering there can also be a good way to gain experience and help other students navigate the same stress as I did\! 

Also, if you’re okay with a DIY approach, plenty of students I know use [**Wealthsimple Tax**](https://www.wealthsimple.com/en-ca/tax), which is pretty easy to navigate online\.
