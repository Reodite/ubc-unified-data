Somewhere between online lectures, digital planners, group chats, and endless tabs open on my laptop, I recently realized how much of my day was spent staring at a screen\. Not just for school, but for *everything*\. Studying, planning, procrastinating, unwinding; it all happened on different glowing rectangles\.

I wasn’t necessarily *unproductive*\. I was getting things done\. But I constantly felt behind, overstimulated, and weirdly exhausted; even on days when I hadn’t physically done much at all\. That’s when I started noticing how closely my screen time and time management were tied together\. Spending more time on screens made it harder to focus, plan my time, and fully commit to one task at a time\.

With the start of a new year, I found myself thinking a lot about the kinds of goals I wanted to set, not the dramatic, all\-or\-nothing kind, but ones that felt realistic and sustainable\. Being more intentional with my screen time and how I manage my time overall became one of those goals\. I wasn’t aiming to cut screens out completely; I just wanted to be more aware of how often I was reaching for them and whether they were actually supporting what I needed at that moment\.

## Productivity vs\. Presence

One of the biggest shifts for me was noticing how often I equated *being busy* with *being* *productive*\. I’d spend hours switching between tabs and tasks, convincing myself I was making progress, even when my focus was scattered\.

Screen time made it harder to feel present in what I was doing\. I wasn’t fully resting when I took breaks, because those breaks usually involved more scrolling\. And I wasn’t fully focused when I worked, because distractions were always one click away\.

Over time, this constant half\-focus made managing my time feel harder than it needed to be\.

## Small Changes That Actually Helped

I didn’t overhaul my entire routine overnight\. Instead, I focused on small, realistic changes that felt sustainable\.

-   **Being intentional with how I opened my laptop:** I started sitting down with one clear task in mind instead of opening everything at once and hoping for the best\.
-   **Working in shorter blocks with non\-screen breaks:** Stepping away, even for five minutes, helped reset my focus far better than scrolling ever did\.
-   **Not starting my mornings with my phone:** This became one of my goals for the year\. As tempting as it is to scroll after hitting snooze for the millionth time, skipping that habit actually made waking up feel easier and helped me feel more grounded\.
-   **Checking in with why I was reaching for a screen:** Sometimes scrolling was a genuine way to unwind and at other times, it was just procrastination dressed up as “a break\.”
-   **Create one screen\-free buffer in the day:** I picked just one moment— either the first hour of my morning or an hour before bed — to stay off my phone\. Keeping it small helped me ease into the habit without feeling overwhelmed\.
-   **Separate “work screens” from “rest screens”:** I tried to keep my laptop strictly for schoolwork and my phone for personal use, so my work time felt more contained\. When I needed a break, I avoided opening new apps on my laptop and instead gave myself a proper pause\.
-   **End work sessions with a clear stopping point:** Instead of working until I felt exhausted, I started stopping after finishing one specific task\. This gave my work sessions a clearer end, rather than letting them stretch on without structure\.

## Letting Go of “Perfect” Time Management

What I’ve learned is that time management isn’t about squeezing as much productivity as possible into a day\. It’s about creating enough structure to support your energy, focus, and well\-being\.

Screens aren’t going anywhere, especially in student life, but learning how to work *with* them instead of letting them dictate your time has helped me feel more in control of my days\.

I’m still figuring it out\. Some weeks are better than others\. But being more mindful of my screen habits has made managing my time feel less overwhelming and more intentional\.

I’m still learning what works best for me, so if you have any tips or habits that help you limit screen time, feel free to share them in the comments\.
