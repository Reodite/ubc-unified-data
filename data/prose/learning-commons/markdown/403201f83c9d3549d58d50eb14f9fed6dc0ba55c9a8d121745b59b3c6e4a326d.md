Have you ever felt like you’re walking a path that doesn’t quite feel like yours?

At university, it can get incredibly easy to believe there is a “right” or a “perfect” way to do things\. Surrounded by high achievers, I always felt like everyone else had it figured out and I started making choices based on what looked good on a resume or what I thought would make others proud\.

But even when my life made perfect sense on paper, something felt off to me\.

## The Comparison Trap

My biggest hurdle was comparison\. I looked up to people I admired, and I unconsciously tried to follow their exact paths\. Somewhere along the way, I lost track of what I actually wanted and I didn’t realize I had started to live a life driven by someone else’s goals\.

## Shifting My Perspective

What changed things for me was taking a step back\. I decided to speak with a university counsellor and career advisor, and answering just a few simple questions made me pause and that’s when I realized the path I was on wasn’t mine\.

Of course I still don’t have everything figured out, but I’ve started being more intentional\. If you’re feeling “stuck” in a plan that doesn’t seem like *you*, here is what helped me:

-   **Ask “Why?”:** I started questioning if I was making decisions for myself or for the resume\.
-   **Follow Your Interest:** I started to pay closer attention to the topics that actually kept me engaged in lectures\.
-   **Embrace Newness:** I tried small things like taking electives and joining clubs that felt right, even if they didn’t fit my five\-year plan\.
-   **Talk It Out:** I stopped keeping my doubts in my head and started talking to my mentors\.

## Trusting the process

It’s easy to feel like you need a perfectly figured out life in university, but that isn’t realistic\. Figuring things out *is* the process and the sooner we realise, the more we can use our resources to the fullest extent\. Deciding to follow my own path didn’t happen overnight, it started with small changes and a lot of honesty\. It still feels uncertain sometimes, but it feels a lot more *real* than following a path that was never mine\.
