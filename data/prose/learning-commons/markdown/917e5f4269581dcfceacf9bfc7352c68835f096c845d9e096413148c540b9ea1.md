When I first started at UBC, I genuinely thought I had it all figured out, and let me tell you, I really did not\. There are just so many small things that nobody warns you about\! So, here are ten things I wish someone had actually told me before my first week, rather than just the generic information every orientation video repeats\.

[Image](https://lc2023.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2026/08/vancouver-campus-looking-west_54142651638_o-edited-scaled.jpg)

### Nobody actually has it all figured out

Walking around campus in my first week, I genuinely believed everyone else had read the syllabus, planned their four\-year degree, and already made best friends\. Spoiler alert: they had not\. Almost everyone is just as lost and overwhelmed as you are; they are just better at hiding it\. The sooner I accepted that we were all winging it together, the less alone I felt\.

### Your professors actually want you to come to office hours

I avoided office hours my entire first semester because I was convinced I would be wasting their time\. The reality is that most professors are sitting alone, hoping someone will show up to talk to them about the course\. Going even once made my professor remember my name, and that genuinely helped when I needed a reference letter later or found summer internships in their department or even to TA their course\!

### The Chapman Learning Commons is a hidden gem

So much tech you can borrow, study spaces, computer stations, and student staff who genuinely want to help you figure things out\. I had no idea any of this existed until my second year, and I struggled alone for way too long before I found it\.

### AMS Health and Dental is opt\-out, not opt\-in

You are automatically enrolled and charged for it, and if you already have coverage through your parents or another plan, you can opt out and get a refund\. I did not know this for a full year and basically donated money for no reason\.

### Group projects will humble you

You will inevitably end up in a group with someone who disappears until the night before the deadline\. The best thing I learned was to set up a shared document early and assign roles in writing, so it is clear who is responsible for what\. It does not always prevent the chaos, but it gives you receipts and keeps you clear\-headed\.

### Vancouver weather requires a real raincoat

A cute jacket from a fast fashion store will not survive a Vancouver winter, and umbrellas are basically useless when the rain is sideways\. Invest in something properly waterproof with a hood, because you will be outside more than you expect, especially when running between classes\.

### The bus loop is a battlefield during exam season

Nobody tells you that the 99 B\-Line at 8:30 in the morning is basically a contact sport\. I started leaving 20 minutes earlier than I thought I needed to, and even then, I sometimes had to wait for two or three buses to pass before I could squeeze on\. If you commute, plan accordingly\.

### Joining six clubs in September is a trap

I signed up for everything at Imagine Day because I wanted to “make the most of university,” and by October, I was burnt out and ghosting all of them\. It is much better to commit to one or two things you actually care about than to spread yourself thin across clubs you will never attend\.

### The friends you make in first year might not be your friends forever

I felt so much pressure to lock in my friend group during the first month, and I held onto some friendships way longer than I should have because I was scared of being alone\. The truth is that people grow in different directions during university, and some of the best friendships I have now started in second or third year\.

### You are allowed to change literally everything

Your major, your friend group, your study spot, your routine, all of it is changeable\. I thought every decision I made in my first year was permanent, and that pressure made me miserable\. The people who seem the most put\-together are usually the ones who have changed their minds the most\.
