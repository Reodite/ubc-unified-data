## First, what is a union? 

A union is composed of a group of workers who join to negotiate better working conditions with employers\. Let me put it this way – it’s a bunch of people trying to make sure working professionals aren’t taken advantage of\. This includes advocating for different kinds of benefits like fairer and higher wages\! 

## How do unions work? 

Unions are formed when a worker majority in a workspace vote for a representing party, or when an employer voluntarily recognises a group of worker representatives as a union\. The group must then be recognised by a board, after which they become official\! After all of this, employers and union members come together to create a [Collective Bargaining Agreement](https://www.lrb.bc.ca/collective-bargaining) \(CBA\) that is used by both parties to negotiate provisions like health insurance benefits, wages, and vacation time\. 

## Unions at UBC\! 

Undergraduates figuring out what to do with their degrees are exposed to so many opportunities it can be overwhelming\. At UBC, research assistant positions and part\-time work are incredibly inviting opportunities\. However, my experiences working part\-time around Vancouver and at UBC have taught me that it’s important to know where we stand as young professionals – if only, so that the ideas and innovation we bring with us are compensated fairly\. 

Now, I’ve learnt that at UBC, there’s a considerable union presence\. The [UBC Faculty Association](https://www.facultyassociation.ubc.ca/) is the biggest amongst these and is committed to creating academic freedom and equity at the institution\. Nowadays, there are even more voices determined to be heard\. For instance, graduate students working as Research Assistants at UBC have expressed their commitment towards unionising as well\. Over [3000](https://www.cupe.bc.ca/2023/05/15/ubc-research-assistants-to-join-cupe-in-the-largest-successful-union-organizing-campaign-in-recent-b-c-history/) graduate researchers have [petitioned](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/ubc-research-assistant-certification-1.6845948) to join the [Canadian Union of Public Employees](https://cupe.ca/) \(CUPE\), British Columbia’s largest union\!

## So, why are unions important? 

There’s a couple of reasons why I think unions are such an integral part of creating an equitable work environment\. The most important reason I could think of is fairly obvious – avoiding exploitation\. I believe that even undergraduate students contribute to work and research spaces in 

a way that impacts how institutions function\. So even if you don’t plan on joining a union at this very moment, I do think it’s important to know at least the basics of how they work, since I believe they’re an essential part of today’s professional world\!
