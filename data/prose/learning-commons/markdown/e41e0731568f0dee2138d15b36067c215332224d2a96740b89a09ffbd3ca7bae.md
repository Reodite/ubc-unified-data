I have always had the weirdest dreams\. Like one time, I was running late for class on a flying toaster, and this other time I was trapped in an orange juice box in the fridge, tormenting the vegetables\. I never thought much of my dreams, and honestly, I forget most of the story within thirty minutes of waking up\.

Recently,I thought to myself, “Wait, what if my brain is actually hiding something smart?” Basically, I convinced myself my dreams could make me a millionaire\. So I decided to try keeping a dream journal for a week to see if I’d unlock my subconscious… or just find out I dream about cereal way more than the average person\.

I decided to record my dreams in a cute little notebook, but you can use literally anything: notes app, sticky notes, voice memos, whatever works\. The real struggle was actually remembering the dream before it vanished\. I swear I’d wake up and five seconds later, I’d be like, “Wait… why was there a horse in my kitchen?” On Day 1, I just wrote one messy line: “something about the Great Wall of China??” No context\. But even that tiny line made me realise something\. I forget everything WAY faster than I think I do\. That was my first mini\-lesson\. Remembering things isn’t just about memory; it’s about slowing down long enough to notice them\.

By the third or fourth morning, I started spotting actual patterns\. Apparently, my brain loves movement, running, chasing, being chased, and climbing stairs for no reason\. I also kept dreaming about food \(like… a concerning amount\)\. But what surprised me was how these themes reflected what was happening in my real life\. On days I was stressed, the dreams were chaotic\. On days I went to bed happy or tired, the dreams were softer\. I’m not saying dreams reveal your destiny or anything, but they definitely mirror the mood you’ve been carrying around\.

Doing this little ritual every morning felt strange at first\. I’d sit there half\-asleep, struggling to write\. But after a few days, it became grounding in a way I didn’t expect\. It forced me to start my day slowly instead of immediately reaching for my phone\. It was weirdly calming to write something down before getting on with my day, and it made me realize I never give myself a moment to reflect, even just on silly things like dreams\. It might sound small, but it made my mornings feel… gentler\. A little more human\.

Keeping a dream journal didn’t turn me into some dream expert, but it made me appreciate how busy the brain is even when we’re “resting”\. Fun fact: we dream for about two hours every night, even if we remember none of it\. So your brain is literally storytelling while you sleep\. Recording my dreams helped me see tiny emotional patterns I wouldn’t notice otherwise, how stress shows up, how little fears sneak in, how my mind processes things I didn’t even realize I was holding onto\.

If you’re someone who likes understanding yourself better, or you’re curious about why your brain does what it does, you might genuinely enjoy dream journaling\. It’s not just “writing weird stuff”, it’s like checking in with a part of your mind that never gets to speak\. And if you’re not into analysing anything, it’s still a fun way to get a free laugh every morning\.
