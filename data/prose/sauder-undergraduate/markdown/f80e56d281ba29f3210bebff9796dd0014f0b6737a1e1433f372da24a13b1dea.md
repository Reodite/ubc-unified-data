## Going on exchange is an excellent opportunity to gain first\-hand experience of another country's business practices, language, and culture\. You’ll make new friends, valuable connections, and life\-long memories\.

### Getting started

Where to begin? Start with research\.

1.  Review eligibility requirements\.
2.  Search through UBC Sauder's business partner schools and UBC's partner universities\.
3.  Explore the **[UBC Go Global website](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/)**, attend an info session, and talk to students who have already been on exchange\.
4.  Make a plan and discuss it with our exchange advisor\.

### Eligibility and course requirements

To go on exchange, you must have attended UBC for one year and have completed Year 2 of the BCom program\. While on exchange, you'll take a full\-time course load, for which you can earn up to 15 UBC credits per term\. You’ll pay 15 credits worth of tuition to UBC per term\.

While on exchange, you can take up to **two courses** towards the requirements of **each** option or concentration you are pursuing\. For example:  
\- If you're completing one option and one concentration, you can take two courses towards your option and two courses towards your concentration\.  
\- If you're doing a double option or two concentrations, you can take two courses towards each option or concentration\.  
Keep in mind, these courses are part of your 15\-credit allowance for the exchange term\.

### Course equivalents and transfer credits

Check the **[Commerce Course Equivalency Chart](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/file/6701/download?token=8YRd-cX6) f**or a list of exchange courses that have been approved as UBC Sauder course equivalents\. Courses taken on exchange that are not equivalent to UBC courses are assigned as Commerce or non\-Commerce elective credits\. The year level and type of credit assigned is dependent on the course taken\.

### Frequently asked questions about exchange

What is a commerce\-specific exchange?

The UBC Sauder School of Business has partnerships with 62 schools in 30 countries around the world\. Going on exchange to one of our partner schools will enable you to take business courses to use toward your degree\. As well, spaces available at our partner schools are for BCom students only\. You are able to participate in university\-wide exchanges, but should be aware that access to business courses may be limited\.

When can I go on exchange?

You must have attended UBC for one year and have completed Year 2 of the BCom program\. Most exchanges are available for one or two terms during the academic year, while some also offer summer programs\. You must return to UBC after an exchange for at least one term, which includes at least one required course, prior to graduation, however, co\-op students may be exempted from this rule and other cases may be considered \(to go on exchange in your final term you must save 9\-15 credits of required electives to take on exchange\)\.

Are there courses I can't take on exchange?

A maximum of two courses for an option or concentration may be completed on exchange\. Given the content of the material, courses such as COMM\_V 393 Commercial Law, COMM\_V 394 Environment, Society, and Government and COMM\_V 396 Business Communication II must be taken at UBC\.

Can I afford to go on exchange?

Going on exchange is not as expensive as you might think\. As an exchange student, you remain registered at UBC, which means you are eligible for scholarships, awards, bursaries, and student loans\. You pay tuition directly to UBC and not the partner institution and may be eligible for an exchange\-related scholarship\. 

For a list of exchange awards available see [international learning awards on the UBC Go Global website](https://goglobal.ubc.ca/go-global/new-programs-ubc-students/new-student-resources/awards-students-participating-go-global#:~:text=About%20International%20Learning%20Awards,UBC-affiliated%20international%20learning%20award.)\.

How does exchange affect my grades?

**For students participating in exchange in the 2025 Winter session or prior:**  
While you receive credit for course work done abroad, you will not see grades earned on your UBC transcript\. Your exchange grades therefore do not affect the average on your UBC transcript\. Your exchange grades are however used internally to determine eligibility to continue in the BCom program, Dean's List standing, scholarships, and degree standing at graduation\. Your UBC transcripts indicates where you were on exchange, when you were on exchange, and how many transfer credits you earned\. If applying to another institution in the future you may be required to submit transcripts from all schools attended, including exchange schools\.

Grades obtained on exchange are not considered Credit/D/Fail unless your partner offers courses in that category\. If this category \(sometimes referred to as pass/fail\) is available, the UBC rules regarding Credit/D/Fail apply\. See Credit/D/Fail details [here](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/courses-money-enrolment/exams-and-grades/grades#creditdfail)\. 

**For students participating in exchange in 2026 Summer session or beyond:**  
While you receive credit for course work done abroad, your UBC transcript only indicates where and when you were on exchange and the number of transfer credits earned\. Grades obtained on exchange are not listed on your UBC transcript and therefore do not affect your UBC grade point average\. The number of credits earned on exchange in a Winter Session will be used in determining eligibility for scholarships, Dean’s List and Degree with Honours standing\. If applying to another institution in future you may be required to submit transcripts from all schools attended, including exchange schools\.

Grades obtained on exchange are not considered Credit/D/Fail unless your partner offers courses in that category\. If this category \(sometimes referred to as pass/fail\) is available, the UBC rules regarding Credit/D/Fail apply\. See Credit/D/Fail details [here](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/courses-money-enrolment/exams-and-grades/grades#creditdfail)\.

Will going on exchange delay my graduation?

As long as you plan accordingly and work closely with an advisor, going on exchange should not delay your graduation\. However, if you are going on exchange in your final term of study in the BCom program in Term 2 of the winter session, you will not graduate in May, but will instead graduate in November\.

Can I go on more than one exchange?

Students may participate in one full\-year or two single\-term \(winter session\) exchange experiences during their degree\. If you have been on a term exchange previously it is not possible to do a second exchange to a competitive commerce partner school\.

Where can I find more information?

Visit [UBC Go Global](https://global.ubc.ca/go-global/) to research schools, find out about upcoming information sessions, and learn about all of the resources that can help make your exchange a rewarding experience\.

### Registration

If you're studying abroad, do not register in any UBC courses during your exchange term\(s\); only register in courses for the term that you will be attending UBC\. Go Global will register you in an exchange code to indicate that you are on exchange, which will trigger your tuition for the term and enable awards staff to review your eligibility for the Go Global International Learning Award\. If you do register in any courses in the term\(s\) that you are scheduled to be on exchange, you will be dropped from these courses\. If for any reason you do not go on exchange, OSAS will assist you to register in any required COMM/COHR courses\.
