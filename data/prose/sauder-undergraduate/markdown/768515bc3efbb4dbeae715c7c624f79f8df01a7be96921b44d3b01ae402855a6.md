### Course Basics

**What is the difference between a course and a section?**

A course is a unit of study covering a particular topic or group of topics, whereas a section is an individual instance of a course that is scheduled for set dates, days, and times\. Each course has a subject \(e\.g\., COMM\_V\) and a course number \(e\.g\., 290\)\. Many courses have multiple sections that are offered at different times\. Each section has a corresponding section number \(e\.g\., 101, 102, 103, etc\.\)\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What do the abbreviations LEC, DIS, LAB, etc\. mean?**

These abbreviations refer to the format of a section’s meeting activity\. Descriptions of common course formats can be found on the Introduction to UBC classes webpage: **[students\.ubc\.ca/new\-to\-ubc/introduction\-ubc\-classes](https://students.ubc.ca/new-to-ubc/introduction-ubc-classes%20)** 

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What are prerequisites?**

A prerequisite is a requirement \(typically a course or list of courses\) that must be successfully completed prior to taking a specific course\. All pre\-requisites are enforced\. For example, COMM\_V 293 is a prerequisite to COMM\_V 294 so it must be completed prior to taking COMM\_V 294\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What are corequisites?**

A corequisite is a requirement \(typically a course or list of courses\) that must either be taken concurrently \(at the same time\) or have been successfully completed prior to taking a specific course\. All corequisites are enforced\. When registering for courses that require a corequisite, remember to also register for the corequisite for the same term, if not already completed\. 

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What is the difference between reserved and unreserved seats in a course?**

Unreserved seats in a course section are open to any students who are eligible to take the course \(i\.e\., they satisfy all restrictions—if any—that are set at the course level\)\. Reserved seats in a course section are open only to those students who satisfy an additional set of restrictions \(e\.g\., enrolment in a particular year level, program, etc\.\)\. For example, the majority of seats in sections of COMM\_V 353 are restricted to students in the Accounting option, but some sections may also include a limited number of general seats for Year 3 BCom students in other options\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What is transfer credit?**

Transfer credit is a recognition of course work completed at another institution which is equivalent or comparable to UBC course credit\. In many cases, you can use the credit you have earned at a previous institution to fulfill your BCom requirements\. Read more about transfer credit at **[students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/registration/transfer\-credits](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/transfer-credits)**

If your high school studies included Advanced Placement \(AP\), International Baccalaureate \(IB\), or British\-patterned education \(A\-Levels\), you may be eligible to receive transfer credit\. Learn more at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/registration/\#firstyeartransfercredit](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/registration/#firstyeartransfercredit)**

Note that at least 50% of your program \(i\.e\., 61 credits in BCOM; 64 credits in BUCS\) must be completed while enrolled at UBC Sauder\. See **[Requirements to Receive a Degree or Diploma](http://www.calendar.ubc.ca/vancouver/index.cfm?tree=3,40,0,0)** in the UBC Calendar\.

Transfer credit is awarded at the university level rather than for a particular program\. It’s possible that not all of the transfer credit you received can be applied toward the BCom program\. If you have questions about how your transfer credit may apply to your degree requirements, please contact the **[UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/node/1888)**\.

Note that credit minima may differ for other considerations, such as eligibility to receive and retain scholarships, to live in UBC residence, or to apply for a post\-graduation work permit\. Carefully review any conditions relevant to your personal circumstances to ensure your continued eligibility\. Learn more: **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/registration/\#courseload](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/registration/#courseload)**\.

**Where can I see my transfer credit and how do I use it toward my degree?**

A summary of your transfer credit can be viewed through your Workday Student profile under Academics &gt; Transfer Credit\. Your transfer credit will also be reflected on your Academic History showing TR instead of a grade\. Eligible transfer credit will apply towards your degree requirements automatically\. Please ensure to request the offering institution sends your official transcript directly to UBC\.

Transfer credits can also be viewed on your Academic Progress Report \(APR\), viewable in Workday\. Please note that staff in the UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services will be making manual adjustments to your APR after you are admitted to the program\. 

The APR has limitations and must be used in combination with our **[Course Planning Worksheets](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/bcom-program-planning-tool)** and **[Elective Checklist](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/elective-requirements)** for accurate degree planning\. To learn more about APR, please visit: [**mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/apr**\.](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/apr)

**I have high school transfer credit for a required course\. What if I take an equivalent course at UBC? Can I use both?**

You cannot use credit for both courses\. For example, MATH\_V 100, MATH\_V 110, and MATH\_V 180 are equivalent courses\. If you have transfer credit for MATH\_V 100 and retake either MATH\_V 100 or take an equivalent course such as MATH\_V 180 at UBC, MATH\_V 100 credit can no longer be used\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What does ‘NC’ mean if it’s part of the course number in transfer credit that I’ve received?**

'NC’, such as in COMM\_V 1NC, indicates that the credits are not for credit in Commerce\. Any not\-for\-credit COMM\_V credit you receive cannot be used to fulfill your BCom requirements unless noted on your Academic Progress Report in the form of an override\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What is an exemption \(EX\)?**

An exemption is the waiving of a prerequisite or a required course\. An exemption is typically granted together with a general credit, which covers the required course in the degree requirements\. For example, if a student is granted an exemption from COMM\_V 293 \(0\) and COMM\_V 2nd \(3\), the COMM\_V 2nd \(3\) credit covers the COMM\_V 293 \(3\) requirement\.

You will receive additional registration information specific to your year level in the BCom program approximately 1\-2 weeks prior to your registration open date for the winter session\. This information will also be available at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/registration](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/registration)**

### Electives

**What’s an elective and how many do I need to take?**

Electives are general course requirements taken in addition to the specific core courses that all BCom students complete\. The total number of elective credits you must complete will depend on which year level you entered the program \(as a direct entry student in Year 1 or as a transfer student in Year 2 or Year 3\) and which option you choose to complete\.

While you are not required to complete specific courses to fulfill elective requirements, a minimum number of your electives must be non\-Commerce and at the 300 or 400\-level\.

The Academic Progress Report in Workday Student may not accurately track your progress towards completing all of your elective requirements \(e\.g\., three credits from both humanities and science/social science\)\. After reviewing your elective requirements at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/elective\-requirements](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/elective-requirements)** we recommend tracking your elective requirements by using the BCom Elective Checklist available for download on that page\. 

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**How do I choose which electives to take?**

Take some time to research the different subject areas and topics that are available to study at UBC\. What you choose is up to you\. Consider taking courses to:

-   explore other areas of interest
-   complement your choice of option or intended career path
-   develop expertise in a specific subject area

Keep in mind that you may need to complete 100/200\-level prerequisites to take 300/400\-level courses in some subjects so plan accordingly\. Before you register for electives, check that those courses are not listed as “not\-for\-credit in the BCom program” in the tables found at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/elective\-requirements](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/elective-requirements)**

Note that if you started Year 1 in 2022W or later, your non\-Commerce electives must include at least 3 credits in the humanities, and at least 3 credits in science or social science\. Language courses are not eligible for the humanities requirement, but literature courses are\.

**What’s the difference between an option and a specialization? An option and a concentration?**

The terms option and specialization are synonymous\. A concentration is an optional set of related courses beyond your core degree and option requirements\. Courses taken for a concentration count toward elective requirements\. **How can I complete a double option?**

You may only be officially registered in one option\. If you're planning to complete a second option, it's up to you to register yourself in any requirements for that option\. You'll only be eligible to register in seats that are reserved for students in other options, if any are available\. Often these limited seats are taken by Year 4 students who register before Year 3 students\. **Can I double count courses between concentrations, options, and minors?**

Double counting is an allowance to use one course to fulfill two requirements but there are limits to the number of credits that can be double counted\. Some concentrations, options, and minors share common courses among their requirements\. For example, COMM\_V 414 Data Visualization and Business Analytics can be taken to fulfill requirements for the Global Supply Chain and Logistics Management, Marketing, or Operations and Logistics option, as well as the Business Analytics concentration\.

If the courses you take for a concentration overlap with the courses you take for an option or minor, you can double count a maximum of 3 credits of coursework in your degree toward both the concentration and one of either one option or one minor\.

Courses cannot be double counted between options\. For example, if you are pursuing a double option in Marketing and Operations and Logistics, COMM\_V 414 can be taken to fulfill requirements for one of the two options, but not both\.

Double counting a course does not reduce the number of courses you are required to take\. You must still complete the total degree credits required for your program to complete your degree\.

First, know that you’re not alone\. More likely than not, there are other students in your class who also find it difficult\. Don’t wait to seek help\. A few places to start:

-   Consult your course syllabus for study resources\.
-   Talk to your instructor or TA during office hours to review challenging content and ask about additional resources\.
-   Sign up for academic support programming at the Canaccord Learning Commons[https://mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/get\-better\-grades](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/get-better-grades) and/or the Chapman Learning Commons: [**mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/clc**](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/node/1317)[https://learningcommons\.ubc\.ca/academic\-support/](https://learningcommons.ubc.ca/academic-support/)
-   Visit the Learning and Studying Resources: [**mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/learning\-and\-studying\-resources**](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/learning-and-studying-resources)
-   Sign up for AMS Tutoring: **[ams\.ubc\.ca/student\-services/tutoring](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/student-services/tutoring/)**
-   Sign up for a review session offered by the Commerce Mentorship Program: [**ubccmp\.ca**](https://www.ubccmp.ca/)
-   Search the web for other learning materials—you may find some concepts easier to understand if they are presented in a different format\.

Remember to take care of yourself too\. Make use of the health and wellness supports that are available to you: **[students\.ubc\.ca/health](https://students.ubc.ca/health)**

### Academic concession

**What can I do if an illness or another personal challenge/conflict is impacting my ability to attend classes and complete coursework?**

At UBC, we care about your wellbeing and are committed to supporting you in your academic pursuits\. We recognize that sometimes unanticipated events or circumstances can affect your ability to complete your academic responsibilities\.

You may be eligible to submit a petition for an academic concession if you meet the certain eligibility criteria, and in some circumstances, provide supporting documentation\.  An academic concession is a form of support that allows for deferment of marked work so that you can complete a course's requirements\. Review the criteria for academic concession at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/academic\-concession](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession)**

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**How do I submit a petition for academic concession?**

Review instructions for how to submit your petition for academic concession at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/academic\-concession](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession)**

You are expected to submit your petition within 48 hours of the missed coursework, exam, or deadline\. Supporting documentation and/or a discussion with an academic advisor may be required before a concession is granted\. You must be in good standing \(regular attendance, completed all coursework and midterm exams\) in your course to be eligible for academic concession\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**I am a non\-BCom student\. How do I submit my petition for academic concession****?**

You should follow the academic concession process as outlined by your home faculty advising office\. Each faculty has its own process for in\-term concessions and it is important for you to follow those guidelines in order for the petition to be approved\. The UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services \(OSAS\) will assist you if your home faculty advising office asks you to connect with your instructor regarding your petition, and your instructor then asks you to contact OSAS\. We anticipate that many COMM\_V, COHR\_V, COMR\_V, and COEC\_V instructors will follow this process\. If this is what you have been asked to do, then you should review the information in [**mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/academic\-concession**](http://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession) and can fill out the academic concession petition form for BCom students\. Please disregard the note that says the form is only for BCom students and submit the form for an in\-term petitions\. We will confirm with both you and the instructor that the information we receive is correct\. Note that this process only applies to in\-term petitions\. You must connect with your home faculty advising office for academic concession for final exams\. OSAS will **NOT** process academic concessions for final exams for non\-BCom students\.

### Exams

**When will the exam schedule be published?**

The exam schedules for the winter session will be published on Workday in mid\-October and mid\-February for Term 1 and Term 2, respectively\. During the summer session, the exam schedule will typically be published three weeks prior to the final exam period in each term\.

Do not make travel arrangements or personal commitments until the exam schedule has been published or you have confirmed your final exam date with your instructor\. Resulting conflicts will not merit academic concession\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What is an exam hardship?**

An exam hardship is when you have three final exams scheduled within a 24\-hour period\. If the third exam falls on the 25th hour, it is not considered a hardship\. For example, if the first exam is on Monday at 8:30am, the second exam is at 3:30pm, and the third exam is on Tuesday at 8:30am, there is no official hardship because the third exam starts just after the 24\-hour period expires\.

If you face an exam hardship, you will be given an alternative exam date for the second exam\. Contact the instructor of your second exam no later than one month prior to the scheduled exam date for courses in the winter session and no later than two weeks prior to the scheduled exam date for courses in the summer session\. If the exam is for a Commerce course, your instructor may redirect you to the [**UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services**](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/node/1888) to report the hardship\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**Can I retake an exam that I've already written?**

No\. If you start an exam, the grade earned will stand regardless of whether or not you're able to attend the entire duration of the exam or to complete it\. You will not be permitted to rewrite the exam at a later date\.

If you are unwell or have other extenuating circumstances that will prevent you from writing an exam as scheduled, please review the academic concession policy at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/academic\-concession](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession)** 

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What do I do if I miss my exam?**

If you have extenuating circumstances that cause you to miss an exam, consult your course syllabus for any instructions provided by your instructor, AND self\-report your absence to the [**UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services \(OSAS\)**](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession)\. Note that you are expected to report your absence to OSAS by submitting an online academic concession petition form at your earliest convenience, but within 48 hours of the missed exam\.

Regardless of whether the exam you miss is for a Commerce or non\-Commerce course \(e\.g\., MATH\_V, ECON\_V\), all BCom students are expected to complete the UBC Sauder academic concession petition form found at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/academic\-concession](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession)**

Please note that submitting the form does not mean that your petition for a concession will be granted\. You must be in good standing \(regular attendance, completed all coursework and midterm exams\) in your course to be eligible for an academic concession\.

If you start an exam, the grade earned will stand regardless of whether or not you're able to attend the entire duration of the exam or to complete it\. You will not be permitted to rewrite the exam at a later date\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**What do I do if I experienced technical issues during an online exam that prevented me from completing it?**

If you experience unresolvable technical issues during an online exam that prevent you from completing it, please follow the instructions provided by your instructor\.  As soon as possible, email a detailed summary of your experience during the exam to [**bcomquestions@sauder\.ubc\.ca**](mailto:bcomquestions@sauder.ubc.ca)\. Include the nature of the technical issues you encountered, when they happened, and what steps you took to attempt to resolve them\. If you were in contact with any form of technical support per the instructions provided by your professor, include this in your summary as well\. Each situation will be reviewed on a case\-by\-case basis, however, there are no guarantees that any form of academic concession will be granted\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**I’ve been granted Deferred Standing \(SD\)\. When will my deferred exam be scheduled?**

If you’ve been granted a Deferred Standing \(SD\) for a missed final exam, you’ll normally be expected to write the exam during the official deferred exam period set by Enrolment Services unless otherwise indicated by your instructor\. While some instructors may be able to offer you an earlier opportunity to take a deferred exam, they are under no obligation to do so\.

The deferred exam period is in late July for courses taken during the previous winter session \(September to April\) and in November or courses taken during the previous summer session \(May to August\)\. Exam dates and times will be scheduled by Enrolment Services \(not the UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services\)\.

Note that some course instructors set alternate exam dates that differ from the official deferred exam period \(for example, MATH\_V deferred exams must be written with the next offering of the course—refer to *Exceptions* on the **[Standing Deferred and Supplemental exams page of the Student Services website](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/exams/standing-deferred-supplemental-exams)**\)\. We strongly recommend to contact your instructor immediately after missing your exam to find out if an earlier exam sitting will be offered and how to register for it\.

### Grades

**When will my grades be available after I have completed a course?**

Your final official grades will be released on Workday Student\. There may be a delay in posting your final grade if your final exam for the course was held late in the exam period\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**Are there minimum grade requirements in courses?**

Most of your courses require a minimum grade of 50% to successfully complete them\. There are a few notable exceptions\. You must achieve a 60% in both ENGL\_V 112/WRDS\_V 150B \(or equivalent course\) and COMM\_V 396 to satisfy these BCom degree requirements\. If you started Year 1 in 2022W or later, you must achieve a 60% in both COMM\_V 196 and COMM\_V 396 to satisfy these BCom degree requirements\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**How will my academic performance for the year be evaluated?**

Each year during the month of May, the UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services reviews your academic progress and calculates your winter session average based on all courses taken from September to April\. Your overall average must be of at least 60% to be eligible to continue in the BCom program\.

The sessional average is a weighted average, taking course credit values into consideration, and does include any failing grades\. If you participate in a study abroad and exchange program prior to May 2026, grades earned on exchange will be included in the calculation\. Courses taken during the summer session \(May to August\) are not included in the winter session average\.

[Back To Top](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/faqs#)

**If I am asked to discontinue from the program because of my academic performance, can I appeal the decision?**

If you did not achieve the minimum 60% winter session average required to continue in the BCom program, you may request that your circumstances be evaluated on their own merits by submitting a letter of appeal to the UBC Sauder Office of Student Academic Services\. Learn more at **[mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/courses\-money\-enrolment/exams\-and\-grades/failed\-year](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/courses-money-enrolment/exams-and-grades/failed-year)**
