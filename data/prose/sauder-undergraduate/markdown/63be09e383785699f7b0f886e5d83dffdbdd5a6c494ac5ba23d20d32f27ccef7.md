## Failed Year Standing  

All students in the Bachelor of Commerce program are required to maintain a sessional average of 60%\. This average is calculated on your grades from September to April\. Grades from courses taken in the summer session are not used in calculating a sessional average\.  

If your sessional average for the Winter Session falls below 60%, you are assigned a ‘Failed Year’ standing\. While students with a Failed Year standing are required to leave the BCom program, they may submit an appeal requesting permission to continue\. The appeal is an opportunity for students to present any extenuating circumstances that significantly affected them during the session\. The appeal can include documentary proof, where appropriate, that you connected with professionals \(doctors, counselors, etc\.\) to help resolve the extenuating circumstances\.

**If you have questions about your situation or need support, consider reaching out to an Academic Advisor at OSAS or consulting other on\-campus resources related to health, wellness, and financial planning:**

**[Support Resources](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/support)** 

### If this is: 

-   The first time you have a Failed Year standing and your appeal is successful: this means that you have been given permission to continue in the program\. OSAS will provide you with a letter that includes details on expectations that you must meet in order to be permitted to continue in the program\. This can include being placed on Academic Probation\.  
    

-   The first time you have a Failed Year standing and your appeal is NOT successful: you are required to withdraw from UBC for a period of at least one year\. OSAS will provide you with a letter that details the requirements you must meet in order to be eligible to apply for re\-admission\.  
    

-   The second time you have a Failed Year standing: you are required to [withdraw](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/campus-wide-policies-and-regulations/advancement-regulations) from UBC for a period of one year\. Re\-admission to the BCom program after a second failed year is not granted\. \([UBC Calendar](https://vancouver.calendar.ubc.ca/campus-wide-policies-and-regulations/advancement-regulations)\)\.
    

### If you intend to submit an appeal: 

The appeal form is open\. Appeals should be submitted by **May 27, 2026** in order to ensure you receive a response before registration for 2026W opens\. We cannot guarantee that appeals received after this date will receive a response before registration opens\.

**Requirements for submitting** **an appeal:**   

-   **Letter of appeal**   
    
-   **Supporting documentation** – If appropriate, provide doctors’ notes, official medical documentation, or other evidence that supports the grounds of your appeal\. If you are uncomfortable providing documentation, please connect with an Academic Advisor to discuss your situation\. Documentation and information provided will only be seen by the Academic Advisor preparing the appeal package and the Academic Review Committee for the purpose of reviewing your appeal\.
    

-   **Reference campus services** – If you have accessed campus services such as academic advising, peer programs, Counselling Services, the Centre for Accessibility, etc\., please include this in your appeal\. If you did not make use of campus resources, but intend to do so moving forward, please make specific reference to the resources you plan to use\. There are fields in the appeal form to include this information\.   
    

*If you have questions about the process or need support to structure your appeal, consider reaching out for additional support to an [Academic Advisor at OSAS](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/ugo) , the UBC Ombudsperson Office \(see [Appeal Guidelines](https://ombudsoffice-2016.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2024/06/Ombuds-Failed-Resources-Appeal-Guidelines-for-Students_Permission-to-Continue_UBCV-and-UBCO.pdf)\), or reach out to [AMS Advocacy](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/support-services/student-services/advocacy/)\.* [Appeal Form](https://sauder.jotform.com/260484797928073)

### FAQs:

Are sessional averages rounded up?

  
Averages are not rounded, so if your Winter Session average is below 60% \(e\.g\., 59\.9%\), you are considered to have failed the year\.  

I'm an international student\. What does being asked to discontinue mean for me?

  
As an international student, being required to discontinue the BCom program or withdrawing from UBC may affect your study permit and legal status in Canada\. It is imperative for you to review the International Student Advising website and seek advising from an International Student Advisor, as they are Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultants \(RCICs\) or Regulated International Student Immigration Advisors \(RISIA\)\. Academic Advisors at the UGO cannot advise on study permits or immigration\-related matters\.  

What will show on my Workday record?

  
Your Workday standing will reflect the status "Failed, Required to Withdraw" if you are not permitted to continue, regardless of whether it is your first or second failed year\.  

I am a fourth\-year student intending to graduate\. How does having a sessional average below 60% affect me?

  
If you are a graduating student and your final winter session average is below 60%, you will still be permitted to graduate\. However, your academic transcript will include the status "Failed, Permitted to Continue" for that Winter session\.

Note, however, that if you are a non\-graduating Year 4 student with a winter session average below 60%, your status will be "Failed, Required to Withdraw\." If this scenario applies to you, then we encourage you to connect with an Academic Advisor if you have not already done so\.  

I am registered in courses this summer\. Can I stay registered in them?

  
Because Term 1 classes start in May and appeal decisions are reached in June, you can stay registered in summer classes while your appeal is being reviewed\.

If your appeal is approved and the conditions of your appeal allow for you to remain registered in Term 2 courses, then you may keep those courses\.

If your appeal is not approved and you are not granted permission to continue in the Bachelor of Commerce program, then you may not stay registered in Term 2 summer courses\.
