## Start with a plan

Before you register, carefully review your degree requirements and make a plan \(and a backup plan\) that includes all of your required and elective courses\. Think ahead beyond the current year to make sure that you're taking any prerequisites now for courses you wish to take in later years\.

When planning your studies, keep in mind that you must complete all of your degree requirements within six years of entering the program\.

## Find your registration appointment

UBC Enrolment Services will notify you by email—in June for the winter session and February for the summer session—once your registration appointment is available in Workday\. Your specific registration date and time is determined by your class standing \(year level\) and cumulative GPA\. The higher your average, the earlier your registration date and time will be\.

[Registration dates](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/registration-dates)  

## Pay your acceptance deposit if new to UBC

If you were newly admitted to UBC this year, you'll be prompted to pay your [acceptance deposit](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/finances/paying-tuition/acceptance-registration-deposits) when you accept your offer of admission\. Continuing UBC students are not required to pay a deposit prior to registration\.

Video: [How to view your course schedule &amp; registration appointment](https://youtu.be/V2jCSMa49s8)   
Video: [How to create a saved schedule](https://youtu.be/jqkzrKfWksU)

In Workday, you’ll create saved schedules for each term\. If you’re planning to take a two\-term course, the course must be added to a Term 1 saved schedule\. Be mindful of potential conflicts with courses in your subsequent Term 2 saved schedules\.

Workday will allow you to add any courses to your saved schedules, even those for which you do not meet prerequisite, co\-requisite, or seat restrictions to register\. Review and resolve any alerts and error messages in your saved schedules before you attempt to register\. One common alert is *Your saved schedule includes course sections that you’re not eligible for*\. You should expect to see this alert in a Term 2 schedule if you plan to take its prerequisite\(s\) in Term 1\. The alert will disappear once you have registered for the prerequisite\(s\)\.

## Manage your registration

### Add, drop, and switch sections in a course

You can make changes to your registered courses without a withdrawal \(W\) standing on your transcript until the deadline posted on the course schedule\. When making changes to your registration, be sure that you’re updating your list of registered courses and not just a worklist\.

**Important:** Review each course section’s withdrawal deadlines carefully\. They vary depending on the duration and credit value of the course\.

### Withdraw from a course with a W standing

If you withdraw from a course after the first withdrawal deadline, a W standing will be noted on your transcript and refunds will be processed according to the [tuition refund schedule](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/finances/tuition-fees/tuition-refund-dates)\. You can withdraw with a W standing until the second withdrawal date posted for the section on the course schedule\. Beyond then, you cannot drop the course\.

[Official course change dates](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/registration/course-change-dates)  

If you need to withdraw from a course after the published deadline due to extenuating circumstances, speak with an advisor\. A change to your registration after the second withdrawal deadline is an [academic concession](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession) that requires approval from our office\. Supporting documentation is required\.

### Impacts of course withdrawal

It is your responsibility to consider the possible impacts of a course withdrawal, and weigh the benefits and disadvantages before making a final decision\.

Any of the following may be affected by a course withdrawal:

Your academic record

You will receive a “W” standing on your academic record\. A “W” standing is not included in the calculation of your overall average for the winter session\.

Tuition fees

Tuition fees for courses with a “W” standing will be assessed according to the UBC [tuition refund schedule](https://students.ubc.ca/finances/tuition-fees/tuition-refunds/)\.

Full\-time status as an international student

International students must actively pursue full\-time studies in each winter session term to be eligible to apply for a Post\-Graduation Work Permit after graduation\. Consult the International Student Guide for details:

-   [UBC definitions for immigration purposes](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/immigration/ubc-definitions-immigration-purposes/)
-   [Immigration impacts of part\-time studies and withdrawing from courses](https://students.ubc.ca/finances/tuition-fees/tuition-refunds/)[https://students\.ubc\.ca/finances/tuition\-fees/tuition\-refunds/](https://students.ubc.ca/finances/tuition-fees/tuition-refunds/)
-   [Post\-Graduation Work Permit](https://students.ubc.ca/international-student-guide/working-canada/post-graduation-work-permit/)

If you have questions, we encourage you to connect with an advisor at International Student Advising: [students\.ubc\.ca/about\-student\-services/international\-student\-advising](https://students.ubc.ca/about-student-services/international-student-advising)

Courses with prerequisites and option declaration

A “W” standing may impact your eligibility to take sequenced courses \(e\.g\., COMM 190\_V is a prerequisite to COMM 191\_V—if you drop COMM\_V 190 in Term 1, you cannot take COMM\_V 191 in Term 2\)\. Review course prerequisites on [Workday](http://myworkday.ubc.ca/) or the [UBC Calendar](http://www.calendar.ubc.ca/vancouver/courses.cfm?page=code)\. Prerequisite courses for option declaration are listed on our [Options webpage](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/options)\.

Promotion to the next year level and graduation

A “W” standing may impact your eligibility for promotion\. Before you withdraw from a course, carefully review [promotion requirements](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/courses-money-and-enrolment/exams-and-grades/promotion-requirements)\.

If you are in your final year of studies, withdrawal from a course will delay your graduation if you need it to complete your degree requirements\.

Academic distinctions

A “W’ standing may affect eligibility for [Dean’s Honour Roll and Degree with Honours](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/courses-money-and-enrolment/exams-and-grades/academic-honours)\.

Varsity sports

A “W” standing may impact your eligibility to participate in some Varsity sports or qualify for participation in some competitions\. Contact [student\.varsity@ubc\.ca](mailto:student.varsity@ubc.ca) for clarification of your requirements as a student athlete\.

Eligibility for funding

Awards, scholarships, student loans, private health insurance, and other financial supports often require that you are registered in a minimum number of credits\. A reduction in your total registered credits resulting from a withdrawal may affect your eligibility to access and retain these resources\. Please contact your [Enrolment Services advisor](https://students.ubc.ca/about-student-services/enrolment-services-advisors) to better understand any impacts on student loans and awards\.

Postgraduate academic programs and employment

Different institutions have different admissions criteria, so it’s important to refer to their published requirements\. For most programs, it is unlikely that a “W” standing will have a significant impact on the evaluation of your application\.

Similarly, potential employers who request to see your academic transcript will likely be more interested in your courses that have final grades, than in any courses with “W” standings\.

A pattern of multiple withdrawals throughout your degree may be more closely scrutinized and solicit requests for an explanation\.

 

## Course waitlists 

Some courses may offer waitlists once they are full\. If you try to register for a course that is full with an open waitlist, you will be added to the waitlist\. Being on a waitlist does not guarantee a seat in the course\. You receive an [invitation to register](https://student-workday-2024.sites.olt.ubc.ca/course-registration/registering-in-a-course-from-the-waitlist/) if a seat becomes available\.  

Monitor your email frequently if you are on a waitlist\. If a seat becomes available in the course, you will receive an automated email from Workday and will have **24 hours** to accept the seat before it is offered to the next student on the waitlist\. Make sure that your [email is up to date](https://workday.students.ubc.ca/personal-information/adding-changing-your-contact-information/) in Workday and that you have not disabled notifications\.

Note that many Commerce courses scheduled in 2026W will not offer waitlists\. For courses without waitlists, we recommend that you monitor enrolment regularly for available seats\.

Understanding section status

-   Waitlist: If the section status is “Waitlist,” this means there are available waitlist spots for that section\.
-   Open: If the status is “Open,” you can register directly into the course, provided you meet the eligibility criteria\.
-   Closed: A section marked as “Closed” indicates that the course is full and no additional seats, including waitlist seats, are available\. 

### Waitlist movement and position

Your chances of registering for a course from a waitlist will depend on demand for the course and whether or not current registrants drop it before the registration deadline\. Waitlists may be managed differently by each department/Faculty responsible for administering the course\. In some instances, waitlists may be processed in order of registration\. For others, students may be prioritized by program or class standing \(year level\)\. Neither academic advisors nor instructors are able to confirm your current position on the waitlist\.

### Requesting registration assistance

If you are unable to register for a **required Commerce course** that cannot be deferred to a future term, you may request registration assistance using the form at [mybcom\.sauder\.ubc\.ca/regi\-assist](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/courses-money-and-enrolment/courses-and-registration/registration-assistance)\. If you meet the valid criteria, our office will review and process your request\. Note that you may be registered in an alternate section or course that fulfills the same requirement, not necessarily your requested preferred section or course\. If you do not meet the valid criteria to submit a request, we recommend monitoring enrolment regularly for any available seats\. Please note that Commerce instructors cannot register you in their courses; you will be referred to our office\.

For **non\-Commerce** **courses**, please consult the relevant faculty offering the course to learn the correct procedure for requesting registration\.

### How does a 'W' affect my academic standing?

Don’t be concerned about having a W standing on your transcript\. It simply indicates that you withdrew from a course\. Course withdrawals are not included in calculating your sessional, cumulative, or graduating averages\. Remember though that you will not receive a full refund of your tuition past the first withdrawal deadline\. \(Please note if you're accessing resources that have credit requirements, a W may impact your eligibility\.\)

Are you meeting promotion requirements?

To be promoted to the next year level, you must complete a set number of total credits\. For example, to be promoted to Year 2, you must complete at least 24 credits\. Eligible transfer credits are included in this total\.

### Maintain your eligibility for student loans

Government loans, grants, and interest\-free status all require students to be enrolled in at least 60% \(80% for Newfoundland and Labrador loans\) of a full\-time course load in each term\. Given the credit structure of the BCom program, the minimum number of credits you must be enrolled in varies at different year levels\. Consult the course load guide at students\.ubc\.ca to ensure that you meet the requirements to maintain your funding\. Note that eligibility to receive scholarships and to live in UBC residence is determined by separate registration criteria\.

## Absences and Academic Concessions

At UBC, we care about your wellbeing and are committed to supporting you in your academic pursuits\. We recognize that sometimes unanticipated events or circumstances can affect your ability to complete your academic responsibilities\.

An academic concession is a form of support that allows for deferment of marked work so that you can complete a course's requirements\. Academic concessions are granted if you meet certain eligibility criteria\. In some circumstances, you may be asked to provide supporting documentation\.   

If you are experiencing circumstances that are affecting your academic work or your ability to attend classes regularly, support is available\. 

We encourage you to review the course syllabus and follow the process that the course instructor and your home faculty's academic advising office for guidance on available options and next steps\. Academic Advisors are familiar with university policies and processes and can provide guidance, support, and referrals to appropriate resources\.

Contact information for all advising offices can be found in [https://students\.ubc\.ca/enrolment/academic\-learning\-resources/academic\-advising/](https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/ "https://students.ubc.ca/enrolment/academic-learning-resources/academic-advising/")\. BCOM students can contact the Office of Student Academic Services \(OSAS\)\. 

[Learn more about academic concession](https://mybcom.sauder.ubc.ca/academic-concession)

Please know that if your absence is related to an experience of sexual violence, you have the option of contacting UBC’s [Sexual Violence Prevention and Response Office \(SVPRO\)](https://svpro.ubc.ca/) \(604 822 1588\) rather than OSAS\. SVPRO can provide you with supports and resources, as well as assisting you with the academic concession process\.
